## Canvas Build, Decided 24 November 2026

Alex King asked on 24 November 2026 for the plan in `prompts/canvas-build.md` to be carried out in full. The ten decisions of its Part 3 are decided as ours, as recommended there. Each can be changed by Alex King; a change is recorded here with its date.

1. **Approvals.** An organisation may hold Part-CAMO and Part-145 as well as Part-CAO. A second sample organisation holds Part-CAMO and Part-145, so every board can be shown. Aircraft stay Part-ML; Part-M aircraft come later.
2. **People.** Everyone in an organisation has a person record. A person who uses the system also has a sign-in, linked to that record.
3. **Customers.** Each owner or operator is a customer record. The free text Owned By on an aircraft gives way to it, and what was typed is kept in the history.
4. **Lead times.** The defaults stay as recorded under Interface: 10.0 hours, 10 cycles and 30 days. A 60-day alert for review certificates is a setting the organisation may turn on. The Settings board's 15 hours is not taken.
5. **Management System for Part-CAO.** Hazard Log and Safety Reports show to Part-CAO as well as Part-CAMO and Part-145, as Compliance Monitoring and Actions already do. The section is named to fit Part-CAO's quality system.
6. **Rules with no reference.** Each is a setting the organisation keeps, with the canvas value as its default, and is shown as the organisation's, not the regulation's: the load margin of 85% and 50.0 other hours a week; the experience logbook's rules and 10-year window; on the job training retakes; the competency minimum of 5 on a scale of 1 to 10; the enquiry reply within 2 working days; quote validity; invoicing on release.
7. **Money.** Invoices and payments are recorded only. The system takes no payment and connects to no payment service. Value Added Tax is a rate the organisation sets.
8. **Marketplace.** Built last. Shown only to signed-in users. The email values in the Civil Aviation Authority data are left out. Before anyone outside the organisation sees it, its data protection is checked, and Alex King decides the licence of the open data (Issue 25).
9. **Questions still open from the private beta brief.** Each is decided as ours in the phase that needs it, recorded here, and put to Alex King in that phase's summary.
10. **Part 147 and Part 21 Subpart G.** Not in this build (Issues 15 and 16).

Phase 0, Foundations, decided as ours on 24 November 2026 while building (REQ-200 to REQ-219). Each can be changed by Alex King:

- **An approval is a record.** Each approval an organisation holds is a record with its reference (REQ-200). The approval an organisation is set up with stays on the organisation record as well: its reference is the one its certificates carry, and only Airworthiness Limited changes it. The Administrator may record another approval and record that it has ended; nothing is removed
- **Privileges of the other approvals.** The four privileges recorded are those of a Part-CAO (CAO.A.095) and are asked only of an organisation set up as one. The privileges of a Part-CAMO (CAMO.A.125) and of a Part-145 (145.A.75) are not recorded yet; they come with Approval and Scope in Phase 5
- **The Owner of the Sidebar board is not built.** The board shows items to Owner, Part-CAO, Part-CAMO and Part-145. An Owner is not an approval, and every organisation in the system holds one, so there is nothing yet to show an Owner's sidebar to
- **The sidebar is recorded whole and shown as it is built.** `interface/navigation.py` holds every group, item and tab of the Sidebar and Tabs boards with the approvals each is for. One appears when its page is built. `tests/test_navigation.py` holds the boards written out by hand. Records and Users stay as ours, under Organisation
- **Not built from the Sidebar board:** the count beside an item, and the search. The board does not say what each count counts (Aircraft shows every aircraft, Maintenance Data only what needs attention), and gives the search no behaviour. Icons are still not built, as recorded under Interface
- **The tabs of Aircraft follow the Tabs board:** All Aircraft, Airworthiness Reviews, Transfers. Airworthiness Directives stays a tab of Aircraft until Aircraft Configuration is built in Phase 1, where the board puts it. This replaces the tab names recorded under Interface on 19 November 2026 (Fleet, Airworthiness Review). A section with one view built shows no tabs
- **A migration that must give existing records something new** goes through the organisations one at a time, as the owner of the tables, with that organisation set (`history.sql.for_each_organisation`). The owner sees no rows otherwise (ADR-0002). The forcing of row-level security is lifted from the organisation table alone, inside the migration's own transaction, and put back before it ends. The history records the work against the database role
- **A person record** holds name, role, staff group, who they report to, employment, licence, and the days they joined and left (REQ-203). The staff groups are those of the Staff board. Authorisation and recurrent training are not on it yet: they come with Phases 2 and 7
- **A sign-in and its person.** Adding a user makes their person record. If one person of that exact name is already recorded with no sign-in and has not left, the sign-in is linked to that person instead. The database refuses a sign-in with no person (REQ-204). A change of a person's name changes the name of their sign-in. Corrected on 24 November 2026, below: nothing is linked by name
- **Who adds or changes a person:** the Administrator, who already manages users (role matrix below). A person with an active sign-in is not recorded as having left: the sign-in is made inactive first. Corrected on 24 November 2026, below: recording the day makes the sign-in inactive
- **How long a former person's record is kept** (REQ-206). 3 years where the organisation holds Part-CAMO or Part-145 (CAMO.A.220 and 145.A.55, Personnel Records); otherwise 2 years for airworthiness review staff (CAO.A.045(e)). Ours: the 3 years are applied to every person of such an organisation; the longer period is shown where two apply; a person who left on 29 February is kept until 1 March. Part-CAO gives no period for its other staff, and the page says so. The system deletes nothing either way
- **The sample's people.** The 13 people of the Staff board and the former review staff member are person records. The Read Only sign-in of the sample is a fourteenth person, not in `SampleData.md`'s table. The L2 certifying staff member is written [Certifying Staff Name] and the former review staff member [Former Review Name]
- **A customer record** holds the name, the kind of owner, the members of a syndicate and the day they became a customer (REQ-207). The kinds are Pilot-Owner, Syndicate and Flying Club, as Writing.md gives owners, and Operator, because decision 3 names operators. A customer made from text typed before has no kind. Contract, open work and balance are not on it yet: they come with Phases 1 and 8
- **Owned By shows the kind of owner,** as the Fleet table of `SampleData.md` and the Fleet board do (Flying Club, Syndicate of 6), and the customer's name where no kind is recorded. The aircraft page shows the customer by name beside it
- **An owner given by name** (the Add an Aircraft page, an import): the customer of exactly that name is used if there is one, one is made if there is none, and the name is refused if two customers share it. The Add an Aircraft page also lets a customer be chosen
- **Existing aircraft.** The migration makes one customer for each different text typed in an organisation, so two aircraft with the same text share a customer. Where the text was the kind of owner, as in the sample (Pilot-Owner on three aircraft), they share one customer named Pilot-Owner until each is given its own. The aircraft's history keeps the text
- **Who adds or changes a customer:** the Continuing Airworthiness Manager and Planning and Records, as for aircraft. The owner of an aircraft is changed under Add or Change Aircraft
- **The sample's customers** are named apart, so that they can be told from one another: [Flying Club Name], [Syndicate Name G-BXYZ], [Owner Name G-ABCD] and so on. The board writes [Owner Name] for four of them. Customer Since is the first day of the month the board gives. G-HKLM's Owned By in `SampleData.md` is corrected from [Owner Name] to Pilot-Owner, as the Customers board has it
- **Settings** is one page with four parts: Organisation Details, Approvals Held, Deferral Limits and Alert Lead Times (REQ-211 to REQ-214). Everyone may read it; the Administrator changes it, under Change Organisation Record. Whether the deferral limits and lead times should be the Continuing Airworthiness Manager's to set is for Alex King

Not built from the Settings board: Telephone and Email for Alerts, because the system sends no email and a place to type an address would promise that it does; the count of sidebar items shown; Invite a User and the Users and Roles table, which stay on the Users page with its own rules; and the Owner tick, as above

The record has one, set on 18 November 2026, and it is kept. A subject is given its own when a phase needs it. Cycles have a lead time though the board shows none

While on, a review certificate is Due Soon from that many days before expiry, wherever it is shown: the Fleet, the review schedule, the aircraft's page and the Dashboard. It must be more days than the calendar lead time. The board shows two alerts, at 60 and 30 days; the system has one mark for Due Soon, so the earlier alert moves where it starts

The 30 days of the Settings board are kept as the organisation's own defaults, from 1 to 365 days, for points (b)(1), (b)(3) and (b)(4). For (b)(2) the board says Category Limit in the List; the system says Limit Given in the Minimum Equipment List, because the categories of a minimum equipment list have not been read in the regulation. The limits are held now and used when defects are built in Phase 1

Corrections after the independent review of 24 November 2026 (`assurance/independent-verification/code-review-2026-11-24.md`). Phase 0 was reviewed and recomputed that day by reviewers that did not write it. Each correction was decided as ours on 24 November 2026 and built by 27 November 2026 (pull requests 116 to 120). Each can be changed by Alex King:

- **An issued certificate keeps what it showed** (finding 1; REQ-110). A certificate of the organisation's own keeps the organisation's name, address and approval reference and the signatory's name as they stood on the day it was recorded. An extension keeps who extended it and the organisation's name and approval reference. The database writes them, whatever the application sends, and never lets them be altered. The certificate page shows only what was kept; later changes to the organisation or the person never change it. Certificates and extensions that existed were given the values as they stood on the day of the migration, which is the earliest day the records can speak for; the history of each has an entry by the database role naming what was set
- **Renaming a person who has a sign-in** (finding 2; REQ-204) needs a fresh sign-in or the code, on the code page the Users page uses, which carries what was sent. A person with no sign-in needs neither
- **The top-up adds once and changes nothing** (finding 3; REQ-218). It adds the people of the sample to an organisation once and keeps a record that it has (`sample.Added`). After that a start adds and changes nobody, whatever has been renamed. A sample given its people before that record was kept, as the test site was on 24 November 2026, is known by having people with no sign-in, and is taken as done. Nobody is found by name; no reporting line is written onto a person who has a record; every write goes through the services the pages use
- **Nothing is linked by name** (finding 4; REQ-204). A new sign-in is given a new person. On Add a User the Administrator may instead choose a person of the organisation who has no sign-in and has not left; the sign-in then takes that person's name. The sample's own sign-ins are known by their user names, which the sample gave them. A sign-in joined to the wrong person before this correction cannot be unlinked on a page: it is made inactive on the Users page and a new one is added
- **Leaving ends the sign-in** (finding 5; REQ-205). Recording the day a person left makes their sign-in inactive at once and ends their sessions, as REQ-010. Nobody records themselves as having left, and the last active Administrator is not recorded as having left. A sign-in is not made active while its person is recorded as having left: the day is taken off the person's record first
- **Record Kept Until does not shorten** (finding 6; REQ-206). It uses the approvals the records say the organisation held on the day the person left. The first period’s start is unknown, so an approval counts before its first recorded end. A re-recorded approval starts a new period on the UK date in its audit history. The previous reference and end remain in that history, and checks of dated activity and retention recognise any gap. The day changes only if the record of an approval is itself corrected to say it had ended by the day the person left. A person counts as airworthiness review staff if they have a review staff record, are in that staff group, or their record ever said so. Recording the day a person left records it on their review staff record too, if they have one and it has none
- **Three more rows on the Dashboard** (finding 7; REQ-216, REQ-217). A maintenance programme whose annual review has never been recorded, as the programme page says Not Recorded; a Non-Part 21 aircraft, in the words its own pages use, Calculations Not Yet Supported for Non-Part 21 Aircraft; and, in Documentation, each type rating of an aircraft for which no maintenance data is recorded, naming the aircraft. Each comes with what cannot be placed by a day
- **What each role is shown** (finding 8; REQ-205). The Staff pages show a user name only to a role that may manage users; others see Has a Sign-In. A link or a form for an action is shown only to a role that may take it: Add a Person, Add a Customer and the forms that change a person or a customer. The check on the server stays the control. Other pages are brought to the same rule as each is next built
- **The second Administrator's first password is its own** (finding 9; REQ-218). `render.yaml` generates `AIRWORTHINESS_TEST_SITE_SECOND_ADMINISTRATOR_PASSWORD`. With no value, with the first Administrator's value, or with one of the second sample's user names taken, the start sets up nothing for the second organisation, says so in its log, and carries on. No password is written anywhere. A second organisation set up before the correction keeps the password it was given
- **Airworthiness review is for a Part-CAO with the privilege** (finding 10; REQ-200). Until Phase 5 records the privileges of a Part-CAMO and their rules, review staff, starting a review, issuing, extending and recording certificates are offered only to an organisation that holds Part-CAO and whose approval includes airworthiness review (CAO.A.095(c)). Any other is refused with the reason: every action on the server, entered in the access log as other refusals are, and the pages say the reason and show no form. What is already recorded can still be read. So the second sample organisation cannot record a review certificate for its aircraft until Phase 5
- **Two more rules held by the database** (finding 11; REQ-200, REQ-204). Every sign-in has a person after every change, not only when it is made. The type and reference of the approval an organisation was set up with are changed only by the owner of the tables. The privileges of the approval, and the day the set-up approval was no longer held from, are not held this way: the decision names the type and the reference, and tests of other rules change the other two as the application. Whether to hold them too is for Alex King
- **The export of an aircraft** (finding 12; REQ-208) holds every customer that has ever owned it, as its history shows, with their names
- **Migration aircraft 0003** (finding 13; REQ-209) takes the spaces off the ends of owner text before customers are made. An aircraft with no text is given a customer named Not Recorded. It cannot be reversed, and says so with the reason before it changes anything: the text that was typed is no longer in a column to put back
- **A form sent twice** (finding 14). Recording an approval and naming a new customer take the organisation's row first, so the second waits for the first: it is refused with the reason, or given the customer already made. Changing an owner with a choice that is not valid shows the reason on the aircraft's page
- **The most a setting may be** (the recompute; REQ-212 to REQ-214) is held by the service and by the database as well as the form: 365 days, 999.9 hours, 9,999 cycles

- **The latest amendment check sets the next check deadline** (REQ-083, pull request 132). A check dated before the latest stays in the history. If the dates are equal, the last entry sets the next deadline.

Phase 1, Continuing Airworthiness, the Rest, decided as ours while building from 27 November 2026 (REQ-220 onward). Each can be changed by Alex King:

- **A defect is recorded with its decision** (REQ-220). Not Deferrable or Deferred, with who decided and the day. The decision is not changed afterwards: if a deferred defect can no longer be deferred, it is closed or a new defect is recorded. A defect already rectified is recorded with its rectification and changes nothing of the aircraft
- **Not Deferrable grounds the aircraft** (REQ-221; ML.A.403(a)). Recording such a defect makes the aircraft Unserviceable, with the defect as the reason and the day it was found, unless it already is. The aircraft is not made Serviceable while such a defect is open: the service and the database both refuse. Closing the defect does not itself make the aircraft Serviceable; that is done on the aircraft's page by whoever may
- **The limit of a deferred defect** (REQ-222). New default allowances start on the date the defect was first identified. An earlier maintenance-data limit takes precedence and is recorded even when it has already passed by the decision date. Under the minimum equipment list, the stated list limit applies. Previously recorded limits retain their dates and their calculation from the decision. The organisation sets the default duration and the 365-day cap measured after the decision.
- **Who is named** (REQ-222, REQ-225). Certifying staff who defer under ML.A.403(b)(4) are a person of the staff group Certifying Staff who had not left by the day. Whether they hold a current authorisation is not checked until authorisations are built (Phase 2). A pilot-in-command is not a person record and is written Pilot-in-Command. The name and licence of whoever decided are kept as they stood, written by the database, and shown as recorded: Sam Patel, Part-66 B1.2
- **A deferred defect is Due Soon from the calendar lead time** (REQ-223), Due on its last day and Overdue after it. This settles the open point that the board's amber dot did not follow its own lead time. A limit that has passed is shown red and changes nothing by itself: the system never changes an aircraft's status on its own
- **Closed defects are listed for 30 days** (REQ-224), as the board's heading says. Every defect stays in the record and on its own page
- **What counts as the same defect** (REQ-226; the open point of the plan). A defect names the chapter of its system as a number from 1 to 99 and the system in words, as the board writes them: Chapter 33, Lights. The same defect is one in the same chapter on aircraft of the same type rating, matched exactly. This counts two different faults in one chapter together, which errs toward looking. The 12 months are those ending today: from the day after the same day a year before. 3 is a Repeat, 2 is Watching, 1 is No Action, as the board gives them. The system holds no list of chapter names: none was found on the canvas beyond the four the board shows
- **The action on a repeat** (REQ-227). The Continuing Airworthiness Manager adds an action for the type rating and chapter. Earlier actions stay visible. A Repeat is To Assess if the latest counted occurrence is later than the last action. The same action can be recorded again if later occurrences warrant it. Group membership continues to use the aircraft’s recorded type rating.
- **Not built from the Defects and Repeat Defects boards:** the Work Package column, until work packages are built (Phase 3); the Export button of Repeat Defects, because the records export already holds every defect
- **A contract is a document** (REQ-240, REQ-241). It is recorded for an aircraft and its owner with what Appendix I to Part-ML has it contain: the aircraft's registration, type and serial number, the owner's name and address, the organisation's name, address and approval reference, and the type of operation. Those are kept as they stood when it was recorded, written by the database, and never altered. The text of the contract, its statement and the obligations of each party are not held: the signed contract is the organisation's own document
- **Tasks contracted** are every continuing airworthiness management task, or limited tasks that are named, as the board shows for G-ABCD. An aircraft has one contract in force. Whether the aircraft's type is within the scope of the approval is not checked: the scope of work is free text until Approval and Scope is built (Phase 5)
- **Ending a contract** (REQ-242, REQ-243). The reasons are Appendix I's two, non-conformity and denounced by either party, and Other. After non-conformity the owner tells the Civil Aviation Authority within 2 weeks: counted as 14 days from the day the contract ended, and shown with how it was worked out. The board says the owner tells the authority "if a Contract Ends"; the appendix says it of non-conformity, and the page follows the appendix. The organisation tells the authority too, of non-conformity and of a contract denounced; the appendix gives it no period, so the Dashboard shows the contract with no day until the day it was told is recorded. Each is recorded once
- **A work order** (REQ-244, REQ-245) is the written request for maintenance, ordered by the owner or by the Continuing Airworthiness Manager. It sits under the contract in force for the aircraft on the day it was agreed, if there was one, and keeps the owner's name as it stood. It is closed once, as Completed, with the work done if recorded, or Withdrawn. Its work package, and the Dashboard's Work Order Not Planned, wait for work packages (Phase 3)
- **Active has no green dot.** The board gives an Active contract one. Green is for Serviceable alone (REQ-193), so the word stands with no dot
- **The system records a certificate of release to service; it does not issue one** (REQ-260). Certifying staff sign their own certificate and have no sign-in. The board's button says Issue a Certificate; the page says Record the Certificate. A certificate is recorded for one piece of work done, with what ML.A.801(e) has it contain, and is added and never changed. What it says of the work, the certifying staff and the approval reference is written by the database as they stood (REQ-261). There is no way yet to void a certificate recorded wrongly: it is for Alex King whether one is needed
- **Recording the certificate enters it in the records** (REQ-260, REQ-051), on the day given or today. Work already recorded with a release reference keeps the day it had
- **Who may issue** (REQ-262, REQ-263; ML.A.801(b)). Certifying staff of the organisation, where it holds Part-145 or Part-CAO with the maintenance privilege: a person of the staff group Certifying Staff who had not left by the day of issue. Phase 2 checks their recorded authorisation at the recording step (REQ-302). Independent certifying staff, named, with a licence number where there is one. The pilot-owner, for work recorded as pilot-owner maintenance and for nothing else, with the pilot licence number (ML.A.803(d)). Whether a task is one a pilot-owner or independent certifying staff may release (Appendices II and III to Part-ML) is not checked
- **No certificate while a grounding defect is open** (REQ-263). The app applies ML.A.801(g) to a Not Deferrable defect found by the issue date and still open on it. An earlier signed document from that period can be retained as Unverified Historical Evidence. The evidence is immutable and linked to both the completed work and the grounding defect. It leaves the release entry and any outstanding deadline as they stood.
- **Who May Release on the page** states what was read in ML.A.801 and ML.A.803 on 27 November 2026. The board's Within Scope of Approval, All Tasks Within Authorisation, Authorisation Current, Not Complex Maintenance Tasks and Part-66 Licence Privileges were not read and are left out until they are

### Phase 1 review corrections

- **Issued certificates and completed airworthiness reviews** show the aircraft identity and authorisation saved with the certificate. The upgrade gives existing certificates the values available on the day it runs. The backfill is in their history.
- **Recording a release** uses the Release to Service form. Recording work or directive compliance creates the work entry. Its release reference and entry date are recorded together and retained. The release board counts certificates by their issue date.
- **Management contracts and releases** keep the approval that covers the activity. Where two held approvals qualify, the organisation's original approval takes precedence if it qualifies. Approval records have an end date. An approval whose start is unknown is treated as held up to that end date, as for record retention.
- **An aircraft owner changes after its current management contract is ended.** Ending the contract preserves the signed record and lets the organisation record the change of owner separately. The recorded end day remains inclusive for historical lookup. A replacement signed on that day takes precedence when work is linked.
- **Programme reviews** keep the latest review date. A future date or an older submitted date is refused with the reason. A completed annual review retains its point-by-point evidence on the programme page.
- **Configuration baselines** keep a dependency open while its linked modification or repair is in progress. A transfer checklist's tick is checked against the live evidence when the aircraft moves to the Fleet.

[AMC1 CAO.A.025](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/05d%20CAO/3%20AMC/AMC1%20CAO%20A%20025%20Combined%20airworthiness.htm) identifies the Part-CAO exposition sections for subcontracting. A supplier decision records the organisation's assessment. Receiving inspection and installation eligibility use the item and its documents.

- **Maintenance subcontractor evidence** (REQ-348 and REQ-349, 28 November 2026). Use the supplier identity for separately dated assessments of a maintenance subcontractor working under the organisation's own approval. Keep the scope, approval reference, exposition reference, qualifications and controls. Part-145 also keeps the pre-audit reference when controls are recorded. A suspension can be added when earlier evidence is missing. Work-package use, release decisions and the exposition itself each retain their own controls. [CAO.A.095(a)(2)](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/05d%20CAO/2%20Regs/06100_CAOA095_Privileges_of_the_organisation.htm), [145.A.70(a)(14)](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/02%20145/2%20Regs/01656%20145.A.70%20Maintenance%20organisation%20exposition.htm), [AMC1 145.A.75(b)](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/02%20145/3%20AMC/AMC1%20145%20A%2075%20b%20Privileges%20of%20the%20organisation.htm).

- **Goods In** (REQ-353 to REQ-357). One shared receipt register records the information the organisation had on arrival. A receipt's class and document are arrival evidence. Acceptance into stores is an incoming inspection decision. Fitting remains a separate decision under the applicable maintenance data. [CAO.A.050(c)](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/05d%20CAO/2%20Regs/05830_CAOA050_Components_equipment_and_tools.htm), [145.A.42(a) to (c)](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/02%20145/2%20Regs/01648%20145.A.42%20Components.htm), [ML.A.501](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/05b%20ML/2%20Regs/03910_ML.A.501.htm), read 28 November 2026.
- **Receiving procedure** (D). The organisation records six observations and the final disposition in separate entries, matching the seven steps in the Receiving Inspection board. CAP 3037 and GM1 inform the checks, and the organisation's procedure determines the checks and release-evidence exceptions applicable to the item. Evidence may be a traceable document reference. An observation may be followed by a later dated observation before disposition. For Stores the six latest observations must pass, with an evidenced Not Applicable allowed for shelf life where no date was recorded. The recorded shelf life is inclusive at movement. A failed suspect-part check, an unserviceable item or an unsalvageable item leads to secure quarantine. Stores covers later transfers and controlled disposal. The movement date may be later than the inspection decision and a referenced authority report remains separate evidence. [CAP 3037 v2](https://www.caa.co.uk/data-and-publications/publications/documents/content/cap3037/), [GM1 145.A.42(b)(i)](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/02%20145/4%20GM/GM1%20145%20A%2042%20b%20i%20Components.htm), [ML.A.504](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/05b%20ML/2%20Regs/03940_ML.A.504.htm), read 28 November 2026.
- **Document assessment and Stores handoff** (D). The inspector records the checked evidence separately from what accompanied the delivery. A serviceable component needs a CAA Form 1, an accepted equivalent with its basis, or evidence for a permitted exception with its basis. A standard part needs conformity evidence traceable to its standard. Material needs a statement traceable to its specification and batch with both the manufacturing and supplier source. A Stores acceptance creates one linked entry in the same transaction. Returns and quarantine retain the original identity, evidence and movement on the receipt. [M.A.501](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/01%20M/2%20Regs/00360_M.A.501.htm), [M.A.504](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/01%20M/2%20Regs/00390_M.A.504.htm), [ML.A.501](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/1321-2014/Content/Document%20Structure/05b%20ML/2%20Regs/03910_ML.A.501.htm), and [21.A.307](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/748-2012/Content/Regs/03930_21A307_Release_of_parts_and_appliances_for_installation.htm), read 28 November 2026.
- **Recording and personnel** (D). The Continuing Airworthiness Manager and Planning and Records can enter Goods In evidence. The final record names the receiving inspector and the reference to their authorisation, alongside the actual account in the audit log. The reference records evidence from the organisation's procedure. It is free text. PostgreSQL validates inspection dates and tenant membership, and retains the dated approval reference and inspector name. The shipment disposition applies to the recorded line as a whole.


## Navigation

- The sidebar follows 1321/2014. See Sections in the README.
- Items show only for the approvals that need them: Owner, Part-CAO, Part-CAMO, Part-145. The canvas shows All.
- Documentation is its own section: Manuals and Procedures (the Combined Airworthiness Exposition opens from it), Revisions, Required Reading, Notices. Organisation keeps Approval and Scope, Changes and Oversight. Changes holds changes to the approval; document revisions live in Revisions.
- Maintenance Data is its own module in Documentation, shown to every approval and to owners, because it is its own requirement in every Part: M.A.401 and ML.A.401 (what counts as applicable data), 145.A.45, CAO.A.055 and CAO.A.080, CAMO.A.325. It holds five things:
  - Data Held: each set of data with author, the types or ratings it applies to, revision and when the amendment status was last checked. Applicable data is the Civil Aviation Authority's requirements, airworthiness directives, the type and supplementary type certificate holders' instructions for continued airworthiness, component manufacturers' instructions and the organisation's own modified instructions (M.A.401(b))
  - Amendment status: a subscription to each amendment service, a check that every amendment has arrived, and monitoring of status, with special attention to mandatory instructions and airworthiness limitations (AMC M.A.401(c) 5, AMC1 145.A.45(g))
  - Data Queries: inaccurate, incomplete or ambiguous data is recorded as a safety report and notified to the author, and the record kept until the author clarifies it (145.A.45(c), AMC1 145.A.45(c))
  - Customer Data: held only while the work is in progress or for the contract, with its amendment status shown by written confirmation, the work order or the customer's amendment list (145.A.45(a)(g), CAO.A.055(a), CAO.A.080, CAMO.A.325)
  - Modified maintenance instructions, Part 145 only: demonstrated to compliance monitoring, approved, the author informed, never for Critical Design Configuration Control Limitations (145.A.45(d), AMC1 145.A.45(d))
- Every work card names the maintenance data and its revision, transcribed accurately or precisely referenced (M.A.401(c), 145.A.45(e)). A Part-CAO need not hold all data all the time, but the data for a job must be held before the work starts (AMC1 CAO.A.055), so a work package checks its data is held and current before it is issued
- Data must be close to the aircraft for the people doing the work, with enough terminals (AMC M.A.401(c) 1 and 2, AMC 145.A.45(f))
- Required Reading, not Read and Sign, and the button is Acknowledge, never Sign, so it is not mistaken for a certifying signature. Regulators name the outcome, not the feature: ORO.MLR.100(e) (all personnel made aware of changes relevant to their duties), SRG1773 Revision Acknowledge form, GM1 145.A.200(a)(4) safety communication. Comparable systems (Centrik, Comply365, Q-Pulse, Web Manuals, EFOS by Evoke) keep acknowledgement inside their documents area.
- Every new starter gets the exposition as their first Required Reading item (CAP 2375 model text, MOE 3.6.2).
- Required Reading is assigned by exposition part to the people whose tasks it touches (AMC2 CAO.A.025(a)); a full revision goes to all staff.
- The Exposition record lists every procedure the guide requires (59 for Part-CAO, AMC1 CAO.A.025) with the module that does it, or Not Yet in Airworthiness+. See Procedures.md for every approval.
- Training is its own section: Recurrent Training, Aircraft Maintenance Licence, Competency Assessments.
- The Aircraft Maintenance Licence tabs run in order: Basic Examinations, Experience Logbook (CAP 741), Type Training (Theory and Practical), On the Job Training (first type rating in the first licence category only).
- Training pages show the Part-147 course route only; type evaluation for Group 3 aircraft (66.A.45(d)) is not shown yet.
- Actions, not Findings: the Actions page holds actions from hazards and from findings. Findings live in Compliance Monitoring.
- Work Packages, not Work Orders: a work order is the customer's written request and sits under Contracts. A work package is how the work is planned.
- Internal findings live in Compliance Monitoring; findings raised by the Civil Aviation Authority live in Organisation, Oversight. Both use Level 1 and Level 2.
- Form pages (Report a Safety Issue, Competency Assessment Form) link back to their list in the breadcrumb.
- References: N- notices, E- enquiries, Q- quotes, INV- invoices, SR- safety reports, OR- occurrence reports, WP- work packages, F- findings.
- Commercial covers the customer side and trade: Marketplace (find organisations), Enquiries and Quotes (an accepted quote is the written work order shown under Contracts), Customers (one record per owner or operator), Invoices (raised on release to service). Suppliers stay in Logistics because supplier evaluation is a quality requirement; the Marketplace links to it.
- Frameworks behind Commercial: two-sided platform (liquidity before network effects; supply seeded from the CAA lists), CRM (operational, collaborative, analytical), Kraljic matrix for suppliers, SCOR (Plan, Source, Transform, Deliver, Return, Enable), ATA Spec 2000 (Chapter 9 traceability, Chapter 16 release certificates), org-id.guide identifiers for the open data standard.
- Part 145 ratings are standardised by product: Aircraft Maintenance (A1 to A4, limited to SRG1782 type names, or a standard group description such as Single Piston Engine Aeroplanes, Metal Structure, 5,700 Kilograms or Less), Engine Maintenance (B1 to B3, limited to SRG1782 bracket engine terms such as CFM56, IAE V2500, Lycoming), Component Maintenance (C1 to C22, Appendix II class names) and Non-Destructive Testing (D1, by method: Eddy Current, Liquid Penetrant, Magnetic Particle, Ultrasonic, Radiography, Thermography). The Civil Aviation Authority list does not publish base or line per aircraft rating, so it is marked Not Published until the organisation claims its listing.
- The Marketplace is organised by legal entity, with each approval (such as UK.145.01384) as a child, because one company often holds several approvals at several locations. Entities are grouped by Companies House number (org-id GB-COH), matched on 18 November 2026: 951 of 1,013 approvals carry a number (549 companies, 13 matches of medium or low confidence). Approvals without one (foreign organisations, Crown Dependency companies, colleges, the Ministry of Defence, sole traders) fall back to normalised legal name.
- No locations in the Marketplace. The address on the Civil Aviation Authority list is the principal place of business (CAP1539), not where work is done; real sites are in each exposition and not published. No Location column, address, map or location search. An organisation may add its own sites when it claims its listing.
- Marketplace rows carry only what is needed to choose: organisation with company number, approvals, and for each approval its reference and ratings. No status unless suspended, no facts grid, no repeated reference, no design notes. Opening an approval shows its ratings, one line per class with its limitations, then the actions.
- Marketplace limitations are cleansed: exposition-level detail is removed entirely (capability list and exposition references, task-only scopes such as borescope inspection or blade blending); acronyms spelled out (Auxiliary Power Unit, Non-Destructive Testing); shouting capitals recased except SRG1782 names, which stay verbatim; engine makers follow SRG1782 forms (PW, PWC, GE, RR, CFM, Honeywell for Garrett and AlliedSignal). Organisation names stay exactly as the Civil Aviation Authority lists them (Ltd, Plc and t/a kept).
- Marketplace results are a list, not a table: one line per organisation (name, company number, what it is approved for). Opening it shows every approval at once, one level only: reference and type, then ratings grouped by product, one class per line, one aircraft or engine type per line. No nested disclosure, no truncated detail, no tooltips.
- SRG1769 is the Details of Nominated Personnel form, not a type list.
- Money uses £ with pence, excluding Value Added Tax unless stated.
- Logistics, not Parts, Tools and Equipment. Goods In (receiving inspection, CAP 3037), Stores (stock, quarantine and scrap), Tools and Equipment, Suppliers.
- Management System, not Safety Management, so it fits Part-CAO's quality system as well as safety management.
- No Pilot Phone or Log a Flight screen for now.

## Tools and Equipment

- Use one register across maintenance approvals. Select the governing approval to display its controls. Keep physical condition visible beside the next date. A condition hold or withdrawal takes precedence over use, while its control dates remain visible.
- The label uses calendar months from the accepted completion. If the target is a shorter month, use its last day. A fresh completion establishes its own anniversary. The due day is inclusive in Europe/London. The calendar lead time is the existing organisation setting. A before-use control has no calendar deadline. A passing interim check leaves the calibration anchor unchanged.

The person performing a technical action checks any narrower authority or competency conditions in the cited procedure. An actual organisation confirms its CAE or MOE delegation and control convention before relying on these labels. See ADR-0009.

- PR 2 records the open affected-work case and restricts the tool when it records an out-of-limits result. PR 4 records the investigation, dispositions and closure.

- Maintenance data, decided 18 November 2026 (REQ-080 to REQ-089; hazard H-005). A set of data is one of the five kinds of ML.A.401(b). It is Held, Not Held or No Longer Held. Held and Current means held, with an amendment check that is not overdue. Data needed but not held is recorded as Not Held so that it shows first, with overdue checks; among those, data carrying mandatory instructions or airworthiness limitations comes first. The date of the next amendment check is entered at each check; the regulation sets no period. A new revision supersedes the current one, and the database refuses to make a superseded revision current again. Data applies to an aircraft when the aircraft's type rating is among those the data names, matched exactly. Ours, except where a source is given.
- Airworthiness directives, decided 18 November 2026 (REQ-060 to REQ-068; hazard H-002). Every directive an organisation records is assessed against every one of its aircraft: until a decision is recorded for an aircraft the directive shows as Applicability to Assess, and no decision is offered in advance. A decision is Applicable or Not Applicable, with its basis, the person and the date, made for one aircraft at a time; the database refuses a statement that would mark more than one aircraft Not Applicable. An action with a limit, done once or repeating, is a task of the aircraft's programme, as ML.A.302(c)(4) requires, calculated by the same rules with no tolerance (ML.A.303). A terminating action ends the repetition and leaves every earlier record. The task of a directive ends only there, by a terminating action or a decision of Not Applicable: the programme's way of retiring a task refuses it, so what a directive makes due never leaves the Fleet without a record of why (corrected 20 November 2026 after the independent review of that day, finding 1). Which directives to record is the organisation's; the system holds no list of directives of its own. Ours, except where a source is given.
- Hours and cycles entries, decided 18 November 2026 (REQ-024, REQ-025; hazard H-011). An entry is a total on a date. It is refused if it is dated after today, below any entry on or before its date, above any entry after it, or more than 24 hours a day above another. More than 10.0 hours or 100 cycles a day needs the person entering it to confirm, and the entry is marked. The days counted between two entries are the days between their dates plus one. Every pair of entries is measured, not only neighbours. A wrong entry is superseded by a correction and stays in the record; an entry is never changed. The current total is the latest entry. These numbers are ours, not the regulation's.
- Limits on what a form takes, decided 20 November 2026 after the independent review of that day, finding 7. A whole number on any form is at most 2,147,483,647, the most the database holds. A calendar interval that runs past 31 December 9999, the last date the system holds, is refused; a task already held with one is shown as one that cannot be calculated, which the Fleet puts first. A correction names an entry of the same counter. A value typed against an imported value cannot hold a NUL character. Ours, not the regulation's: each was a server error before
- Tolerance, decided 18 November 2026: a tolerance never moves a due point. Each limit shows its due point and, where a tolerance applies, the last point the tolerance allows. A limit that has been passed while its tolerance has not is Within Tolerance, shown in amber with the words and what remains of the tolerance; it is Overdue, in red, only once the tolerance is passed. The task counts as due from the limit itself, and the task is shown by its worst limit: one limit Overdue makes the task Overdue. A tolerance is accepted only for an inspection of the Minimum Inspection Programme (ML.A.302(d)(1)), and only while the programme is on that basis. A programme cannot be changed under its inspections: what is refused when an inspection is added is refused when the basis or the category is changed, and an inspection is retired before the programme moves to the design approval holder's instructions (corrected 20 November 2026 after the independent review of that day, finding 4) and is refused for an airworthiness directive, an airworthiness limitation or a life limit (ML.A.303, GM1 ML.A.302(c)(4)). When the calendar tolerance ends after the airworthiness review certificate, the system says so (AMC1 ML.A.302(d)). The states, words and colours are ours, not the regulation's.
- Whichever comes first, decided 18 November 2026: every limit of a task (hours, cycles, calendar) is always calculated exactly and shown. To say which comes first, limits in hours or cycles are projected to a date using the aircraft's planning utilisation, rounded down to whole days so the projection is never late. Limits that fall on the same day are shown together. With no planning utilisation recorded, no limit is ranked above another and all are shown. The projection is for planning only; the task is due when any limit is reached.
- A calendar interval in months lands on the same day of the month, or on the last day of a shorter month (31 January plus 1 month is 28 or 29 February). Our rule, not the regulation's.
- Due Soon defaults, decided 18 November 2026 and adjustable for each organisation: 30 days, 10.0 hours or 10 cycles remaining, whichever limit governs. An airworthiness review certificate is Due Soon at 30 days; the 90-day early review window is shown in words, not colour. A regulatory deadline of 30 days or fewer (release to service in the records, copy of the review certificate to the Civil Aviation Authority) is Due Soon from the day it starts. These numbers are ours, not the regulation's.
- The Fleet lists aircraft under management. An aircraft being transferred in appears on the Transfers tab until its records are complete.

## Roles and Permissions

Decided 18 November 2026 (REQ-006, REQ-007). Write access by role is a condition of CAP 562 C-90; which role may do what is ours, derived from the people in `SampleData.md`, except where a source is given below. `airworthiness_plus/accounts/roles.py` holds the same matrix, and a test fails the build if the two differ.

- A user may hold more than one role, as Lucy Grant is both Airworthiness Review Staff and in continuing airworthiness. An action is permitted if any role held permits it.
- A user with no role, and an inactive user, may do nothing.
- What each action may read and set, value by value, is in `architecture/fields-by-action.md`, written 19 November 2026 to meet OWASP ASVS 5.0 8.1.2. Its table is made from the forms and addresses by `python manage.py field_rules`, and a test fails if an address or a form is left out of it
- The Administrator manages users and the organisation record and does not change airworthiness records. An Administrator can give themselves another role; the change is in the history.
- Read Only can view and nothing else.
- Only Airworthiness Review Staff carry out a review and issue a certificate (ML.A.903, CAO.A.045). Issue also needs a current authorisation, which is REQ-109 and separate from the role.
- An extension is open to the Continuing Airworthiness Manager and to Airworthiness Review Staff, because the regulation gives the privilege to the organisation under both continuing airworthiness management and airworthiness review (CAO.A.095(b)(4), (c)(1)(ii)). Who within the organisation signs an extension is for its exposition; this reading is to be confirmed by Alex King.
- Only the Continuing Airworthiness Manager changes the maintenance programme and decides whether a directive applies (hazard H-002). The programme review may also be recorded by Airworthiness Review Staff, who may carry it out with the airworthiness review (ML.A.302(c)(9)).
- Second factor at sign-in, decided 18 November 2026: codes from an authenticator application, the time-based one-time password of RFC 6238. It needs no new dependency. A code can be phished where a passkey cannot, so passkeys are considered again when the verification level is set in Step 7 (REQ-008).
- Sign-in, decided 18 November 2026 (REQ-008, REQ-009; threats TH-001, TH-002). These numbers are ours. They were set against OWASP ASVS 5.0 Level 2 and NIST SP 800-63B on 19 November 2026, below:
  - a password of at least 12 characters, then a code; the code must follow the password within 5 minutes; a code is accepted for its own 30 seconds, changed on 19 November 2026 from the 30 seconds either side as well to meet OWASP ASVS 5.0 6.5.5, and only once
  - the first time, the user adds the account to an authenticator application from a key shown once, and confirms a code
  - 5 failed attempts for a user name within 15 minutes, counted since the last sign-in, lock that name until 15 minutes after the fifth; wrong codes count as wrong passwords do. Corrected 20 November 2026 after the independent review of that day, finding 6: the lock ran 15 minutes from the first of the five, not the fifth
  - a session ends when the browser closes, 30 minutes after its last request, 12 hours after sign-in whatever happens, when the user signs out, when the user is made inactive, and when the password changes
  - the page gives the same answer for an unknown name, a wrong password, an inactive user and a locked name; the reason is in the access log
  - the 30 minutes and the 12 hours were set against NIST SP 800-63B on 19 November 2026 (OWASP ASVS 5.0 7.1.1). For a sign-in with two factors, revision 3 asks for a fresh sign-in after 30 minutes without use and at least every 12 hours; revision 4 says the two should be no more than 1 hour and 24 hours. Ours meet both, so there is nothing to justify
- Users, decided 18 November 2026 (REQ-005 to REQ-010). Ours:
  - the Administrator adds a user with a full name, a user name that is an email address and roles. The first password reaches the person by a route other than the system, and is changed by them before any other page answers
  - a first password is made by the system, changed on 19 November 2026 from one chosen by the Administrator to meet OWASP ASVS 5.0 6.4.1. It is random, of 16 characters, passes the password rules, is shown once to the Administrator on the page that follows, and is stored only as a hash. It works up to the seventh day after the day it is given, by the date in the United Kingdom, and not after; the Administrator then gives another
  - the first Administrator's password, given when an organisation is set up or the test site's sample is made, is a first password like any other: changed at the first sign-in and good for 7 days. Corrected 20 November 2026 after the independent review of that day, finding 8: it never had to be changed, so whoever typed it, or could read it in Render, knew it for good
  - a user name is used once across every organisation. A name that is taken, here or elsewhere, gets the same answer
  - a user may hold no role, and can then do nothing. An organisation always has an active Administrator: the last one cannot lose the role or be made inactive, and nobody makes themselves inactive
  - a user who leaves is made inactive, never deleted. Their sessions end at once. Corrected 20 November 2026 after the independent review of that day, finding 2: the sessions were held, not ended, and a browser that kept asking kept its session for up to 12 hours, to resume if the user was made active again. Now the session is removed at its next request, and one made before the user was made inactive never resumes
  - a lost password is replaced by the Administrator with a new first password; a lost authenticator is reset by the Administrator, and the user sets a second factor up again at the next sign-in. Both are entered in the access log with who did it. A user changes their own password by giving the current one; their other sessions end
  - a change to how someone signs in needs a fresh sign-in, decided 19 November 2026 to meet OWASP ASVS 5.0 7.5.1 (threat TH-002). Within 5 minutes of signing in nothing more is asked. After that, a user changing their own password gives a code as well as the current password, and an Administrator giving a new first password or resetting a second factor gives a code from their own authenticator application. The code is used up as one at sign-in is, and a wrong one counts towards the lock
  - corrected 19 November 2026 after the independent review of that day's code: every change on the Users page asks for the code, not only a new first password and a reset of a second factor. Adding an Administrator, or giving the role, needed no code, and the new Administrator, freshly signed in, could then reset anyone. What was sent is kept on the page that asks, and sent again with the code. Five wrong codes lock the name for 15 minutes here as at sign-in, and while it is locked the right code is refused too
- Security settings, decided 19 November 2026 against OWASP ASVS 5.0 Level 2 (`assurance/asvs.md`). Ours:
  - every response carries a Content Security Policy that allows the application's own stylesheet, script, fonts and images and nothing else, with no inline script or style; no response is kept by the browser except the static files; the application answers GET, HEAD and POST only
  - the session and forgery cookies are named with the `__Host-` prefix, so a browser accepts them only over HTTPS and for this host alone. Strict Transport Security covers subdomains of the service's own host name; it says nothing about the domain above it
  - a password is refused if it contains the name of the product, the regulator, the approval or the user's organisation, as well as what Django already refuses. Any character is allowed, and at least 64 characters
  - a user can end every other session of theirs, and the Administrator can end every session of a user. Both are entered in the access log. The number of sessions a user may have at once is not limited. Resetting a second factor ends the user's sessions
  - a user sees their own live sessions on the Change Password page, built 19 November 2026 to meet OWASP ASVS 5.0 7.5.2: when each began, when it was last used, the address and the browser it gave at sign-in. One can be ended, or every other, after a sign-in within the last 5 minutes or a code. A session is named on the page by a one-way hash of its key, never by the key. The list is read from every session the server holds; that is fine for private beta and is given its own table before many organisations use the service
  - breached passwords, decided 19 November 2026 after Alex King asked us to decide (OWASP ASVS 5.0 6.2.12): a new password is checked against the 20,000 most common passwords and no further, for private beta. A full check needs either a file of many gigabytes kept up to date on a small server, or a request from the service to another site, which the design does not allow. With 12 characters and a second factor the gap is accepted, and looked at again before public beta
  - second-factor keys are not encrypted in the database in private beta, decided 19 November 2026 after Alex King asked us to decide (OWASP ASVS 5.0 14.2.4). With the encryption key on the same server it would protect only a backup, which is already encrypted. It is decided again with the vault for secrets and with passkeys (`operations/keys-and-secrets.md`)
  - passkeys, decided 19 November 2026 after Alex King asked us to decide (OWASP ASVS 5.0 11.4.1): codes from an authenticator application stay for private beta. They use HMAC-SHA-1, which the standard lists as legacy and allows where compatibility needs it. Passkeys are planned before public beta; they need a new dependency and remove the legacy algorithm
  - there is no rule on which characters a password is made of, so a password of digits alone is accepted if it is long enough and not a common one
  - the address logged for a request is the one the reverse proxy saw, and only when the service is told it runs behind one
  - unexpected errors go to standard error, one line each with the time in Coordinated Universal Time. No request body, password or session identifier is logged
  - the export of an organisation is limited to 6 in any hour for each organisation, decided 19 November 2026 to meet OWASP ASVS 5.0 15.2.2. They are made one at a time for each organisation, so that requests sent together cannot all pass the limit. It reads every record and its history, and is the one heavy thing a signed-in user can ask for again and again. The seventh is refused with the reason. The export of one aircraft is not limited
  - every entry of the history names how its hash was made, decided 19 November 2026 to meet OWASP ASVS 5.0 11.2.2. The name today is `sha256-fields-1`: SHA-256 over the fields of the entry in the order the export's README gives. If the way of hashing is ever changed, new entries carry a new name and the entries written before are still checked by the old one. The check in the system and the check that comes with an export both report an entry whose name they do not know
- Certificates from before these records, decided 19 November 2026 (REQ-110, REQ-113; dogfooding D-3). Ours:
  - a certificate the organisation's own review staff issued before these records is recorded against that person, with the reference it was issued with and no serial of the system. It is entered on the aircraft's Airworthiness Review page or loaded by an import, by whoever may add or change aircraft
  - it is dated no later than the person's last review before these records, which the Administrator states on the review staff record. Recording one therefore cannot change whether the person has recent experience, and cannot stand in for a review carried out in these records. The database holds to the same rule
  - corrected 19 November 2026 after the independent review of that day's code: the last review before these records is never after the day the person was recorded as review staff, and that day is fixed. A restoration of a lapsed authorisation was being written into the same date, which moved the limit into these records; it now has its own date and still counts as the person's last review. Without this, a certificate dated inside these records could be recorded as the organisation's own with no review and no serial
  - the Administrator cannot move that date to before a certificate of the person already recorded
  - no copy to the Civil Aviation Authority is tracked for it: that copy was owed under the records it came from
  - the copy of an extension is tracked whoever issued the certificate, because the extension is the organisation's own (ML.A.903(f)). Before 19 November 2026 it was tracked only for certificates issued in the system; dogfooding D-6
- The Access Log page, built 19 November 2026 (REQ-009): the Administrator reads the organisation's own entries, newest first, 100 to a page, with who, what, the detail, when and the address. It sits beside Users. A failed sign-in under a name that belongs to nobody has no organisation and is shown to no organisation
- Access log, decided 18 November 2026 (REQ-009): every sign-in, failed sign-in, sign-out, second factor set-up and refused action, with the user name, the time from the database clock and the network address. It is added to and never changed. An organisation sees its own entries. A change of role is in the history of the user record instead.

### Readings Kept as Built

- A tolerance stated in the design approval holder's instructions is refused: ML.A.302(d)(1) gives a tolerance to the Minimum Inspection Programme only.
- The hours tolerance and the calendar tolerance each apply to their own limit.
- The last day of validity of a certificate that does not keep the pattern is the anniversary (REQ-103, Issue 38).
- The 90 days and the 30 days include their first day and the day of expiry.
- A calendar limit with 0 days remaining is Due, not Overdue.

- The programme is reviewed within 12 months of its last review.
- A certificate the organisation's own review staff issued before these records is dated no later than the person's last review before these records, which is never after the day the person was recorded as review staff.

## National Regime

Source: CAP 562 Issue 4 Amendment 3 (January 2020), read in full. Its wording predates the UK leaving the European Union Aviation Safety Agency: "EASA aircraft" reads as Part 21 aircraft and "non-EASA" or "Annex II" as Non-Part 21 aircraft. That mapping is ours, not the text's. Leaflets B-40 and 70-80 now sit in separate Supplementary Amendments and are not covered here.

- Every aircraft record carries a regime: Part 21 or Non-Part 21. The regime decides the release wording, licence checks, schedule type and which approvals can sign
- Non-Part 21 aircraft hold a National Certificate of Airworthiness or a Permit to Fly; a Permit to Fly does not expire but needs a 12-month Certificate of Validity
- An orphan United Kingdom type loses Certificate of Airworthiness eligibility and moves to a Permit to Fly
- Marketplace shows each British Civil Airworthiness Requirements approval by the chapter the Civil Aviation Authority lists (A8-1, A8-9, A8-15, A8-21, A8-23, A8-24, A8-25, A8-26, A8-6), never as a bare "BCAR"

### Maintenance history

Maintenance History is available from the aircraft tabs and Work Packages. The aircraft entry pins the aircraft. The planning entry can search the fleet in the current organisation or a selected aircraft. A chapter matches its recorded sections and a section matches the exact code. Earlier chapter-only records remain searchable. A defect search distinguishes the submitted report and note from the recorded rectification. Order and package results match these facets through their linked defect. The date refers to defect discovery, order agreement or package planned start, respectively. The state filter groups open and closed records while the results retain each source's own status. The written work order continues to represent the agreed work scope under CAO.A.055(b).
