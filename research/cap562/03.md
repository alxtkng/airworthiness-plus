## 2. BCAR and the National Regime

### 2.1 Two parallel regimes in the text
- **"EASA aircraft"** (now read as UK Part 21 / Part-ML / Part-M aircraft). These get an EASA C of A (non-expiring) plus an ARC, an EASA Permit to Fly (Part 21 Subpart P), EASA STCs, and continuing airworthiness under Part M.
- **"Non-EASA" / "Annex II" aircraft** (now "non-Part 21" aircraft, the old Annex II to Reg 216/2008). The CAA remains responsible for certification. These get a UK national C of A or a National Permit to Fly, CAA STCs or AANs, BCAR Sections A and B, ANO requirements (for example the CMR), CAA Specifications, and BCAR Section R radio approvals.
- C-60 excludes "aircraft engaged in military, police, customs or similar services" (State aircraft) and Annex II aircraft from the EASA Permit to Fly regime. It also excludes aircraft "only eligible for a UK National or EASA enduring Permit to Fly".
- C-50 Note 2: an aircraft must be UK registered for a Permit to Fly (EASA aircraft) or for flight under **"A" Conditions** (national aircraft).
- C-80 applies only to aeroplanes "for which the certification and approval remains the responsibility of the CAA"; for all others, consult EASA.
- C-100 applies only to non-EASA aircraft. The CAA may continue to approve radios "installed predominantly in non-EASA aircraft".
- C-110 and C-150 apply only to Annex II aircraft. C-150 notes that "Mutual recognition is not automatically achieved within EASA Member states for STCs to Annex II products".

### 2.2 BCAR approval types and chapters named in this chunk
- **BCAR A8-20, ex-military aircraft** (aircraft above 2730 kg). An A8-20 organisation may, within its scope, submit for initial approval of an ex-military aircraft. It may do maintenance, repair, defect rectification, inspection and modification of the aircraft and components, including complete rebuild or restoration. It may recommend issue or renewal of a Permit to Fly. There are two sub-approvals:
  - **E4 approval** (A8-20 para 1.2.1), design aspects. Needed to recommend the **initial** issue of a Permit to Fly for an ex-military aircraft. It does **not** allow designing or seeking approval of Major Modifications; for that, apply under A8-8 (E1) and/or A8-2 (A2).
  - **M5 approval** (A8-20 para 1.2.2), maintenance aspects. Allows maintaining the aircraft and recommending **renewal** of the Permit to Fly.
  - Aircraft complexity grouping under A8-20 para 1.2.1: **Simple, Intermediate, Complex** (para 1.2.1(c) defines Complex). The capability list may give blanket cover for Simple or Intermediate aircraft but must be type-by-type for Complex aircraft. An M5 capability list may give blanket cover for one or more Groups (para 1.2.2).
  - Nominated senior person under A8-20 para 3.2(b). Nominated signatory under para 3.2(c), who establishes the aircraft's design standard and signs statements.
  - The M5 Exposition states the level of approval as one or more of: (a) Maintenance of airframes and engines; (b) Overhaul of engines; (c) Restoration of airframes and associated systems; (d) Component Overhaul.
  - "A8-20 Supplement No. 1", paras 3.2 and 3.4, covers maintenance and overhaul arrangements for specialised equipment.
  - Approval reference format in the sample statement: "Civil Aviation Authority Approval AI/....../....". The example company name is "HEAPWARBIRD LTD".
- **BCAR A8-8 (Group E3)**, approved design organisation. C-50 says that for non-EASA aircraft above 15,000 kg it certifies conformity of records. A "report from an approved BCARs E3 design organisation" may stand in for an Export C of A for a British-built non-EASA aircraft without a valid C of A. **A8-8 (E1)** is the route for major modifications (C-120).
- **BCAR A8-2 (Group A2)**, supplier or manufacturing approval. Major structural items for ex-military aircraft should be manufactured under A8-2 control. A8-20 does not replace an A8-2 Group A2 Supplier Approval.
- **BCAR A8-9**, flight testing provisions ("Permit to fly for test purposes" approval). C-150 says any specific flight testing provisions granted by the CAA should be noted in the compliance plan.
- **BCAR A2-5 / B2-5**, the modification approval procedure (normal CAA route). Also used for other UK repairs by approved design organisations that are not the TDO. A/B2-5 para 2.1.4: all modifications shall be approved, except those the CAA agrees do not affect airworthiness.
- **BCAR A3-7 / B3-7**, Permits to Fly. Para 3.1(d) gives the basis on which ex-military aircraft may qualify. Appendix 1 lists the evidence: para 2.1 safety record, para 2.2 conformity to type design standard. A3-7 Appendix 2 covers the Flight Release Certificate.
- **BCAR A/B3-2**: para 1.3, conformity to type design (restoration). A3-2 para 4: construction under supervision of a CAA-approved organisation. C-50 also cites A/B2-2 and A/B3-2 for C of A matters.
- **BCAR A/B5-3 para 2.1**: manuals such as the SRM may be certified and published under the authority of the approved UK TDO, or under the local process for foreign TDOs. SRM repairs and SRM amendments therefore need no direct CAA approval.
- **BCAR A/B6-2 para 8**: conditions for a CRS (non-EASA).
- **BCAR A/B6-7 para 2 / 2.1.1**: repairs to follow "the approved Manuals, drawings and schedules related thereto, and any other documents required or recognised by the CAA".
- **BCAR A4-8 (Accessory Procedure) and A4-10; B4-8 and B4-10**: equipment approval, applied to radio. **A2, A4 (Component Procedure) and A8**: approval of radio within an aircraft-level modification.
- **BCAR Section R** (radio) and CAP 208 Vol 2 (obsolescent): radio approval number prefixes **WR, VC, LAI, LAIII**. Other BCAR approval categories used for radio: **Q, MISC, SA, G**.
- **BCAR Section K**: possible design code for justifying ex-military modifications, alongside JAR-23.
- BCAR Section A (D-10): every drawing must carry a descriptive title, drawing number, issue number and date of issue. Alterations go through a drawing amendment system.
- **UK TDO** = Approved UK Type Design Organisation. "Foreign" means non-UK.

### 2.3 Certificates and permits under the national regime
- **UK national C of A**: for non-EASA aircraft. C-50 5.2(c): the accepted maintenance organisation, the LAE or, above 15,000 kg, the A8-8 (E3) design organisation assesses the previous records and certifies conformity to a CAA-approved standard. CAA Specifications must be complied with.
- **National Permit to Fly for ex-military aircraft** (C-120 App 2 s11): "a non-expiring document that requires an associated **Certificate of Validity**", both kept in a wallet. The Certificate of Validity lasts 12 months. Ex-military aircraft are never accepted as "series" to one another; each gets its own **Airworthiness Approval Note (AAN)**, though cross-reference to earlier AANs for identical standards is allowed. Restored Permit aircraft must also comply with their AAN; they are "not accepted by the CAA as series aircraft" (C-140).
- **Permit to Test** / "Permit to Fly for test purposes": issued before the flight test of an ex-military aircraft once the draft AAN is accepted. It is also needed when a Certificate of Validity has expired and a test flight is required.
- **Mandatory Permit Directives** (MPDs): CAA directives for Permit types; compliance is required for ex-military aircraft.
- **Flight Release Certificate** (BCAR A3-7 Appendix 2): the release after scheduled maintenance or defect rectification on Permit aircraft. For Complex ex-military aircraft the signatory must have practical experience gained with the manufacturer or an acceptable Military Authority.
- **Certificate of Maintenance Review (CMR)**: Non-EASA aircraft only. Required by the ANO for aircraft with a C of A in force used for Public Transport and Aerial Work not operated on an EU-OPS 1 or JAR-OPS AOC.
- **CAA STC (non-EASA)**: issued "Pursuant to the National Regulations"; an alternative to, not a replacement for, the AAN procedure.
- **Airworthiness Approval Note (AAN)**: the CAA design approval document for national aircraft or modifications. It is produced and signed by the CAA, and the applicant (E4 organisation) drafts it.
- UK national **Noise Certificate**: applied for on CA3 (C-50).

### 2.4 Ex-military aircraft (C-120) summary
- The type's safety record must be acceptable: total loss and fatal accidents per million flying hours. Combat losses and peculiarly military causes (for example low-level training) may be discounted by qualified personnel. The CAA must accept the record before the main investigation starts. Limitations may make a type acceptable. For Complex aircraft the CAA usually needs manufacturer support.
- Conformity to type design standard: get English-language lists of modifications essential for airworthiness (manufacturer, Military Engineering Authority, Military Operators), plus RAF Special Technical Instructions (STIs) and Service Instructions (SIs). Compile an embodiment statement for each.
- Fatigue state: identify fatigue-critical components, their lives and accounting procedures. Prove each is within limits, with documentary evidence for the aircraft's full life. Agree civil-operation procedures with the CAA (role factors).
- Limitations: normal operating limits, plus more restrictive ones for unsafe envelope areas, missing equipment (for example no oxygen means a maximum altitude limit), or engine conservation. Ejector seats and drop tanks need particular investigation.
- Published information: Aircrew Manual or Pilots Notes; airframe, engine and propeller overhaul, maintenance and repair manuals (English).
- Complex aircraft need permanent support and specialist cover for powered flying controls, variable geometry intakes and nozzles, reaction controls and digital systems. Some types may be supportable only by the armed forces or the manufacturer.

### 2.5 Sailplanes (C-70, C-130)
- Before 28 Sep 2008 UK sailplanes were under **British Gliding Association (BGA)** governance. The CAA accepts modifications and repairs made before 28 Sep 2008 (following the EASA Airworthiness Review Team visit, January 2007).
- "Duplicate Sailplane" means two of the same type with identical serial numbers, usually one "created" around a repaired fuselage. A "non-standard aircraft manufacturer's serial number" is one not in the manufacturer's format.
- These are not eligible for a standard EASA C of A. They may get an **EASA Restricted C of A** once provenance is established in an EASA **SAS (Specific Airworthiness Specification)**. Apply to EASA (Certification Manager General Aviation, Cologne), and EASA fees apply.
- A new fuselage and new serial number under manufacturer control means a new registration and eligibility for a standard EASA C of A.

### 2.6 Steep approach (C-80)
- 3° is normal. Up to 3.5° is routine. Over 3.5° and under 4.5° means consulting the manufacturer. **4.5° or more** is a steep approach needing specific airworthiness and operational approval.
- London City: glideslope originally 7.5°, reduced to 5.5°; runway 1199 m (3934 ft).
- Approval is shown in the Flight Manual as a maximum approach angle. No Flight Manual approval means not approved. Type clearance does not automatically clear each individual aircraft (TAWS, autopilot and flight director may need modification). The operator determines each aircraft's eligibility. UK operators also need operational approval from the CAA Flight Operations Inspectorate.

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### C-50 (C of A issue)
- Form **CA1** (registration) goes to CAA Aircraft Registration. Issue needs evidence of de-registration or never-registered status from the previous State. **The Certificate of Registration must be issued before the C of A.**
- Form **CA3**: application for C of A, Noise Certificate and ferry Permit to Fly. It also records the FM/POH/OM reference.
- An Export C of A must be **not more than 60 days** from issue when received by the CAA. Alternatives: (a) a valid domestic C of A issued within the last 60 days; (b) a valid C of A plus a signed statement from the exporting authority within 60 days confirming TCDS conformity and airworthiness; (c) an **EASA Form 52**; (d) an EASA C of A from another member state plus a valid ARC; (e) for a British-built aircraft without a valid C of A, a report from a BCAR E3 design organisation (non-EASA) or a Part M Subpart G organisation with C of A/ARC recommendation privileges (EASA), with TC holder support in either case. Derogations or waivers by the exporting country must be agreed with the CAA in advance. The original export document must be available at survey.
- For used EASA aircraft (except **ELA1**), a CAMO performs the airworthiness review and recommends the C of A and ARC. The CAMO must notify its CAA Regional Office **at least 10 days in advance** of the time and place of the review.
- An aircraft moving from another EU state with a valid C of A and ARC gets a new C of A, and its ARC is amended to the new registration and stays valid to expiry. A national ARC cannot be accepted by another Member State; a new ARC is needed under M.A.901.
- For imports from third countries, a CAMO recommends after an M.A.710 review. Below **2730 kg MTOM** and for all balloons, the review may be done by a CAMO or directly by the CAA.
- A check flight is needed (with a pilot accepted by the CAA, who signs the Check Flight Schedule), except for imports from within the EU.
- Weight and Balance Report/Manual is required **above 5700 kg**. The aircraft is normally re-weighed before C of A issue; the previous weighing may be accepted with technical justification, by agreement with the Surveyor.
- AD compliance is needed for: State of Design ADs, EASA ADs, and CAA Additional ADs (CAP 747). There must be a compliance statement for the aircraft, engines (including APU), propellers and equipment.
- UK Additional Requirements for Import (ARIs), CAA Generic Requirements and JAA/EASA Additional Certification Requirements apply (CAP 747).
- An alignment (bridging) check may be needed when moving to a maintenance programme; its content is agreed with the Surveyor. For aircraft below **2730 kg** going on LAMS/LAMP, an **Annual Inspection** is normally required.
- A **Modification Record Book** is required above **2730 kg MTOM**. Engine, propeller and APU modifications are recorded in their logbooks.
- Repairs previously applied must comply with EASA-recognised data: TC/STC holder, a Part 21 Subpart J DOA, an EU NAA approval **before 28 September 2003**, or a bilateral partner authority.
- A detailed electrical load analysis is **not** required for single-piston-engine aircraft or helicopters of **2730 kg MTOM and below** unless approved for night or IMC.
- Records kept by means other than logbooks need written CAA approval.
- If an FDR readout is delayed at C of A issue, agree a timescale with the Regional Office. The FDR must meet UK operational rules whatever the aircraft's origin.
- If the type is new to the operator, the maintenance programme is based on the MRBR or MPD; departures from it need in-service reliability data.

### C-60 (EASA Permit to Fly)
- Two elements: approval of flight conditions, then issue of the Permit.
- **Design-related** flight conditions are approved only by EASA (EASA Form 37 with Form 18b) or a Part 21 Subpart J DOA with the privilege (Form 18a or equivalent). AD non-compliance is normally design-related.
- **Non-design-related** flight conditions are approved by the CAA, on form **SRG 1728a (CAA Form 37)** plus **SRG 1728b (CAA Form 18b)**. Examples: positioning for maintenance with an invalid C of A or an overdue check; flight to storage, maintenance or painting; flights for C of A or ARC issue; delivery or export of a new aircraft.
- To apply to the CAA for the Permit, use **SRG 1733 (CAA Form 21)** plus the fee.
- CAMO issue (**M.A.711(c)**), where the CAMO holds ARC privileges for the type:
  - CAT aircraft and aircraft **above 2730 kg MTOM** (except balloons): only when in a **controlled environment**, and only by the managing CAMO.
  - Non-CAT aircraft of **2730 kg and below**, and balloons: any appropriately approved CAMO.
  - CAT aircraft or aircraft above 2730 kg not in a controlled environment: the CAMO **cannot** issue; apply to the CAA.
  - The CAMO may issue only when it is eligible, the flight conditions are approved, it has verified conformity to them, and any maintenance required by them is done. A CAMO acting for the owner may apply for flight conditions approval.
- A **CRS** (M.A.801 or Part 145) must be issued before flight, confirming conformity to the Permit conditions (and the Form 18b or 18a) and that the aircraft is fit for the intended flight.
- Validity: the Permit is valid in the UK and all EU states (subject to national airspace restrictions); outside the EU it depends on local rules.
- Urgent operational need (mainly recovery of a damaged aircraft for repair): the CAA may grant the issuing organisation an exemption from EASA flight-conditions approval, using the same data (normally from the TC holder). The applicant pays the extra CAA costs.

### C-70 / C-130 (sailplanes)
- Dates that matter: Part M continuing airworthiness for sailplanes from **28 September 2008**; repairs before that date are accepted.
- Major damage (fuselage, wing or tailplane) must be repaired by a Part 145 or M.A. Subpart F organisation, with TC holder or Part 21 Subpart M design support.
- After fuselage replacement, the identity and serial number stay with the sailplane and its records. The data plate moves to the replacement fuselage. Record the transfer in the repair scheme (approved scheme) or in the maintenance records (existing data).
- A repaired fuselage re-used on another sailplane must have an approved repair scheme, a Part 145 or Subpart F repairer and an EASA Form 1 (content in section 4). It must be the same type (TC and part number) and modification status. Its life used and ultimate fatigue life go into the receiving aircraft's records.
- For major damage, tell the local CAA Regional Office before work starts; a Special Survey may be needed (C-140).

### C-80 (steep approach)
- Visual steep approaches need a cloud base of **500 ft** (propeller) or **1000 ft** (turbojet) and **2 nm** visibility.
- Flight tests at limiting weight and CG, with visual guidance at the nominal angle and at least **2° steeper**. An ILS calibrated at the nominal angle is needed if flight director or autopilot approval is sought.
- A steep approach never automatically reduces the scheduled landing distance; short-field performance is a separate certification.

### C-90 (computer records)
- Acceptance does not exempt compliance with ANO 2009 Articles **150, 151, 156, 157, 158 and 159**.
- Safety-critical data (CMRs, AD compliance) may need **up to 100% sampling** at data migration.
- Extensions of CAA-related functions must be discussed with the CAA before they go in.
- Normal testing period is **six months** of parallel running with hard copy, with a bound (not loose-leaf) **Problem Log**.
- For approved organisations, the Company Manual content is incorporated into the Exposition.

### C-100 (radios)
- **ANO 2009 Article 39(8)**: all radio communication and navigation equipment installed or carried must be of a type approved by EASA or the CAA for its purpose (the note mentions Article 20(5)(a)).
- Apply on **Form AD70**. Operation needs a Wireless Telegraphy Act Aeronautical Radio Licence (DAP is Ofcom's agent; form **DAP 1902 (ACT1 1001)**) for aeronautical bands. Non-aeronautical bands are licensed by Ofcom. Emergency services and taxi radios are usually covered by the ground station licence.
- For radio licences (C-50), all airborne transmitters must be listed (VHF, HF, DME, transponder, TCAS, radio altimeter, weather radar). ELTs need not be listed.

### C-110 (repairs)
- An aircraft may be released while a damage tolerance evaluation is pending, for **up to 12 months** for completion and incorporation into the AMS. There must be a procedure to put the resulting inspections into the AMS.
- Normally no release without approval documentation (for example 8110-3 or a Repair Assessment Sheet). A **"No Technical Objection" (NTO)** is not an approval.
- The CAA may investigate any TDO repair if a survey raises doubts, the repair is novel or very extensive, or it conflicts with CAA requirements.

### C-120 (A8-20)
- Ex-military aircraft **above 2730 kg**.
- Submit **AD458** forms (qualifications and experience, "Biographical Details") for nominated persons: E4 to the Airworthiness Division Organisations Section at Gatwick, M5 to the Regional Office.
- M5 needs at least one nominated senior engineer holding CAA licences without type ratings appropriate to the aircraft. An unlicensed person with long CAA association may be accepted for established organisations.
- Agree the maintenance schedule with the Regional Office **before** the Permit to Fly is issued.
- Engines, propellers and gearboxes are overhauled before initial fitment unless their history and status are documented.
- Manufacture to pattern is normally prohibited.
- **Permit validity renewal**: after an annual inspection, submit **AD200** (application), the statutory fee, the flight test report if applicable, and **AD202P** (recommendation). The CAA returns a **Certificate of Validity valid 12 months**.
  - **Anticipation**: renewal up to **31 days** early keeps the consecutive 12-month period. More than 31 days early limits the certificate to one year and 31 days after the recommendation date. (Text as written; the likely meaning is that validity runs from the recommendation date.)
  - If expired: dated from the date the CAA receives it, and expiring one year from the recommendation date.
  - If expired and a flight test is needed: submit AD200 and the fee first for a test Permit, then the flight test report and AD202P.
  - **Flight test period: three years**. A flight test is mandatory for the first issue of the non-expiring Permit. The next flight test due date is stated on the Certificate of Validity.

### C-140 (restoration)
- For a project **expected to exceed fifteen months**, tell the local Regional Office at the earliest opportunity, for a preliminary assessment and acceptance of parts sourcing.
- A Special Survey (owner applies to Applications and Approvals, Gatwick) or a major modification may be needed. After a Special Survey is accepted, no C of A or Permit application is needed until near completion, unless an unapproved modification is to be embodied. If certification fails, the C of A or Permit fee is refunded, but the Special Survey cost is not.
- Failure to prove major components are original (made by or for the first C of A or TC holder) or an approved alternative means refusal of the C of A or Permit.

### C-150 (CAA STC)
- Applicants: UK applicants, or non-UK applicants seeking validation, conditional on their Authority having or being willing to enter an Arrangement with the UK. Apply on **Form STC01** plus the fee.
- The applicant must hold a CAA-recognised Design Organisation Approval. The terms may need updating to cover the STC.
- Certification basis: the TCDS for Non-Significant STCs; agreed with the Team for Significant STCs.
- **ANO 2009 Article 16** (when an aircraft may fly) and **Article 19**: a modification invalidates the C of A or Permit until approved.
- Amendments only by the STC holder, classified Major or Minor. A Significant change needs a new STC.
- The STC remains valid until surrendered, withdrawn or otherwise terminated. On transfer it is re-issued. The STC is published in publicly accessible databases.

### C-160 (owner responsibilities)
- "Large aircraft" means an aeroplane with MTOM **exceeding 5700 kg**, or a multi-engined helicopter.
- Controlled environment: continuously managed by the same CAMO for the preceding **12 months** and maintained by Part 145 or Subpart F (except permitted pilot maintenance by the owner or an independent Part 66 engineer). The ARC can then be extended twice, giving a full review **every three years** (the CAMO extends for years 2 and 3).
- Not controlled: a full review and ARC **annually**. At **2730 kg or below** the CAMO may issue. **Above 2730 kg** the CAMO reviews and recommends to the CAA.
- If the contract becomes null, the owner informs the competent authority **within two full weeks**.

### C-170 (expired national C of A, EASA type)
- All EASA aircraft were to have a non-expiring EASA C of A plus ARC by **28 September 2008**.
- Apply on CA3, including events affecting airworthiness and modifications or structural repairs since the last renewal. The review is by a CAMO with ARC privileges, or for **ELA1** by a licensed engineer holding a CAA ELA1 approval. They decide whether a check flight is needed; it needs an EASA Permit to Fly, applied for at the same time as the CA3.
- C of A expired **before 28 Sep 2003**: a Regional Office Surveyor surveys before issue. **After 28 Sep 2003**: normally no survey.

### C-180 (POA supplier control)
- Overseas POA facilities are listed on **EASA Form 55** (Terms of Approval) and visited by the CAA **at least once in every two-year renewal cycle**, more often for critical or hazardous-or-worse parts.
- Significant subcontractor changes are notified before implementation on **EASA Form 51** and approved under 21A.147.
- Renewal recommendation on **EASA Form 56** every two years. Ratings are None, UK or International. The Manpower Plan (manager responsible, resources, visit plan covering QMS assessment and Product Audit) is updated every **2 years** and on significant change.
- Witness frequency per 2-year cycle: **LOW**, a desktop assessment only; **MEDIUM**, a desktop assessment plus at least **1** witnessed audit; **HIGH**, a desktop assessment plus at least **2**. Priority goes to critical parts and process subcontractors and those not visited before. Overseas costs are recoverable.
- Local NAA surveillance on the CAA's behalf is reported on **EASA Form 58A** (GM No. 4 to 21B.220(c)).
- The CAA's decision on how far to investigate a subcontractor change goes to the POA in writing, with a copy retained.
- Vendor materials that directly affect airworthiness need periodic independent testing by the POA, or by a traceable laboratory.

### D-20 (calibration)
- Traceability to the manufacturer's or design organisation's standard, or to a national or international standard. If none is specified, use **BS EN 30012-1**. UKAS-accredited providers may be accepted.
- Intervals follow the supplier's instructions unless continuous analysis of results justifies a variation.

## 5. Practice Guidance

### C-50 App 2: survey sampling items
Structures and systems (condition, conformity, repainting, repairs); Flight Manual vs DFMS; cabin (approved drawing, seated passenger space, exit access, IFE, galleys, lavatories, and for C of A, seat pitch, aisle width, Type III/IV overwing exits); data plates; registration marks (fireproof plate); compass cards valid; exit and break-in markings; placards; mandatory requirements (FM, ANO, modifications, ADs, TCDS/AAN, CAA Specifications, CMRs); records (logbooks, work packages, tech log, life item records and component log cards); emergency equipment against the drawing; CAT operational requirements (JAR-OPS, EU OPS, ANO, JAR-26). Operational approvals: RVSM, ETOPS/LROPS, AWOPS/autoland, MNPS, RNP, B-RNAV/P-RNAV.

### C-50 maintenance status at C of A
All maintenance certified with a CRS: scheduled inspections, CPCP, SSID tasks, CDCCL. Known defects are corrected or carried forward under control. Life limits from EASA, the CAA, the State of Design and the TC holder are in the AMP, and none has been exceeded (engines, propellers, appliances).

### C-90: computer record controls (assessment checklist)
- Know the system's scope (CAA and non-CAA functions). Watch for "scope creep" into areas not assessed.
- Power interruption and memory preservation, backup ("standby tape"), dumping and consolidation procedures in the manual.
- Data migration method defined; verification by representative sampling (up to 100% for CMR and AD data); ongoing accuracy oversight.
- Access control: only authorised persons may alter or delete (PIN or ID card). The assessor should try to "beat the system".
- Amendment history: does the system keep the full input history (for example a component call-off at 10,000 h changed after a defect at 8,000 h; an SB terminating action deleting repetitive inspection history)? Preferably it should keep history.
- If it generates release certificates (for example a distributor's Approved Certificate), it must control consecutive serial numbers.
- Software compatibility and interchangeability tested.
- Bring-on-line: operations manuals available, Company Manual, 6-month parallel run, Problem Log.

### C-100: radio installation
EMC tests with engines running and all ground-operable systems on, across the operating band. Susceptible systems include FADEC, engine computers, displays, required comms and nav, and AP/FD. Flight test if ground testing is impractical. Electrical load analysis. Flight Manual Supplement where crew action matters. COTS controls (see Leaflet B-110). The equipment approval gives no credit for system performance; it is a no-hazard, no-interference finding.

### C-110: repair data acceptance logic
- Foreign TDO repair approved under the local process (for example FAA DER 8110-3): a recognised document.
- Foreign non-TDO repair (including STC holders): CAA investigation with credit for the foreign approval.
- UK TDO repair: normal BCAR Section A route, or associated with the SRM under TDO authority (A5-3 para 2) if the Terms of Approval allow repair design and the Exposition has procedures. These repairs take the same controls as the SRM.
- Other UK approved design organisation (not TDO): BCAR A2-5.
- For EASA aircraft, see C-20 Appendix 2 (acceptable data for Part-145).

### C-120 App 2: ex-military maintenance practice
- Maintenance programme based on the original type documentation plus CAA additions, NDI and supplementary inspections, adjusted to utilisation. Consider converting hours or cycles to calendar periods (agreed with the CAA). Include life limitation listings (component overhaul, fatigue). Watch for major structural inspections missing from military service notes. Add LAMS-type general requirements (compass swings, radio checks).
- Spares: original sources or reputable distributors. Military-sourced items are inspected for condition, life, records, modification status and compatibility, then accepted and recorded by a certifying engineer. Consider internal examination or strip (boroscope, NDT) for age and corrosion. Visually inspect and NDT structural forgings and castings.
- Alternative parts only with manufacturer or approved DO agreement, a modification, or Regional Office agreement.
- Non-critical parts may be made in house for supported aircraft only. Major structure is made under A8-2 to original drawings. Deviations in material, heat treatment or process need a technical case to the Regional Office. Material substitution or repairs not to the manufacturer's manuals count as modifications.
- Secondary sites: defined in the Exposition, environmentally suitable. Line support is not a secondary site.
- Authorisations: limited full authorisations for flight release, plus task-specific ones (taxiing, ground runs, pre-flight).

### C-130: sailplane repair practice
See section 3. Key model point: the data plate and serial number follow the airframe identity, not the damaged fuselage.

### C-140: restoration
Replacement parts must match the manufacturer's part number for the type, model and serial, or be approved by modification. They must be serviceable and documented. Repairs not in the repair manual must be approved (A/B6-7 or Part 21 Subpart M). Use calibrated tooling. Keep in regular contact with the Regional Office to avoid dismantling for later stage inspections.

### C-180: production supplier control
Covered in section 3. Product Audit means physical conformity, process and acceptance test review (FAIR, raw material certificates, routing documents, NDT and treatment records). Vendor testing examples: extinguisher gases (composition), structural raw materials and fasteners (composition and hardness), structural adhesives (composition and cure), weld consumables (composition), cabin interior materials (heat and smoke release). Adhesives and weld consumables may be validated with production test pieces. A POA cannot skip a supplier just because the supplier holds other-party approval, unless the POA is part of that scheme.

### D-10: engineering drawings (useful for parts and data features)
- Drawing practice follows BS 8888 and ISO; older drawings may be imperial or follow first-angle or older standards. Every calculation on which airworthiness depends is independently checked. Any deviation from approved drawings during manufacture needs EASA or CAA approval. During maintenance, replacement parts and repairs must follow approved drawings.
- Drawing number = project identity + group + register number (for example A2-R-21-29, where R means repair). The drawing number is usually also the part number, except for repair drawings. Handed parts: left, upper, inner or forward part takes the odd number and the opposite hand the next even number ("AS DRAWN" / "OPP HAND"). Multi-sheet drawings: "SHEET 1 of X SHEETS".
- Any change except clerical gives a new issue number and date. **If interchangeability is affected, a new drawing number and part number are allocated.** Some organisations use letter issues for prototypes and number issues for production.
- ND = no separate drawing (for example "A1 31 101 ND"). A/R = As Required (locking wire, shim). BS and SBAC standard parts are listed but not drawn.
- Drawing queries: provisional answer on the query form; temporary approved answer by Drawing Office Instruction (same authority as the drawing); permanent answer by re-issue. Limit the number outstanding.
- Never scale a drawing for a missing dimension; refer to the Design Office. Chain dimensioning is avoided because tolerances build up.
- Surface texture equivalents run N1 (0.025 µm / 1 µin) to N12 (50 µm / 2000 µin); see BS 1134 and BS 2634.
- Process symbols (Table 4): N solution treated, no precipitation (also normalised steel); W solution treated, requiring precipitation; P precipitation; WP solution treated and precipitated; A annealed; HT hardened and tempered; M mechanical test; PFD dye penetrant; UFD ultrasonic; AFD anodic flaw detection; AC cleaned pipes; R repaired/reconditioned; PL proof load; SR stress relieved; PT pressure test; XR X-ray; S salvaged; MFD magnetic flaw detection; W/S welding; E etch inspection of steel.
- Geometric tolerances: form (straightness, flatness, roundness, cylindricity, line and surface profile), attitude (parallelism, squareness, angularity), location (position, concentricity, symmetry), composite (run-out). (M) means maximum material condition.

### D-20: calibration (model in tooling module)
- An appliance needs calibration if it measures or tests an aircraft, system or component against limits in the TC holder's data.
- Requirements: a register; a programme of periodic inspection, service or calibration within time limits, staggered for availability (loan arrangements are acceptable where there is no redundancy); intervals per the supplier unless data supports a change; labels showing the next due date and serviceability; doubtful items removed and labelled, not returned until fixed and re-validated. If pre-adjustment results show previous measurements may have been wrong, take corrective action (a look-back). Records allow drift and wear analysis. Calibrate, transport and store in suitable conditions (temperature, humidity, vibration, dust, EMI, lighting), or apply compensation. Check standards used by the user are themselves calibrated. The company quality system reviews the arrangements periodically.
- Subcontracted calibration does not remove the user's duty to keep controls and records.

## 6. Software Implications

### 6.1 Aircraft and certificate model
- `Aircraft.regime`: EASA/Part-21 (UK Part 21), Non-Part-21 (Annex II / national), ex-military (national Permit, AAN-based), sailplane (BGA legacy), and State aircraft (excluded).
- `Aircraft.weightClass` thresholds used throughout: **2730 kg** (CAMO ARC issue, Permit by CAMO, Mod Record Book, LAMS/LAMP annual inspection, electrical load analysis exemption, A8-20 scope), **5700 kg** ("large aircraft", W&B manual), **15,000 kg** (E3 DO certifies records for a national C of A). Also flags for ELA1, balloon, multi-engined helicopter, CAT use, night/IMC approval.
- `AirworthinessCertificate` types: EASA/UK Part 21 C of A (non-expiring) + ARC; Restricted C of A (SAS basis); UK National C of A; National Permit to Fly (non-expiring) + Certificate of Validity (12 months, next flight test date, 3-year flight test cycle); EASA/UK Part 21 Permit to Fly (flight conditions reference, issuer CAA or CAMO); Permit to Test; Noise Certificate; Certificate of Registration (prerequisite).
- Status logic: C of A invalidated by unapproved modification (ANO Art 19), damage, overdue maintenance or AD, or expiry. Use the C-60 flowchart as a "do I need a Permit to Fly?" wizard: damage outside MEL/CDL/SRM; modified or repaired without approval or CRS; overdue maintenance or AD; flight for ARC renewal; flight outside certification limits; other. Route to the DOA/EASA, the CAA, or a CAMO.
- `ControlledEnvironment` flag: same CAMO for the preceding 12 months plus Part-145/Subpart F maintenance. It drives ARC extension eligibility (2 extensions, full review every 3 years) and CAMO Permit issue eligibility.
- Certificate of Validity renewal calculator: 31-day anticipation window, lapsed handling (from the CAA receipt date), next flight test due (3 years). Alerts at 60/31 days.

### 6.2 Workflows
- **C of A application checklist (C-50)**: CA1 and registration issued, then CA3; export document age at most 60 days (alert); CAMO 10-day notice to the Regional Office; check flight (not needed for EU imports); weighing; AD statement (State of Design, EASA/UK, CAA additional per CAP 747); ARIs; CMRs; life limits; logbooks; Mod Record Book; repairs data source check; cabin; CAA Specifications; avionics/ELA; FDR/CVR statement; bridging check; tech log; CMR (national).
- **Permit to Fly flight conditions workflow (C-60)**: classify as design or non-design related, then the form set (EASA 37/18b, DOA 18a, CAA SRG 1728a/1728b), approval, Permit application (SRG 1733 or CAMO form), issue, CRS with conformity statement, then flight. Store the flight conditions document against the Permit.
- **A8-20 ex-military project**: CA3, complexity grouping agreed, safety record accepted, conformity statements, fatigue, limitations, manuals, draft AAN, CAA survey, Permit to Test, flight test, AAN and Permit, then annual renewal (AD200 + AD202P + fee + flight test every 3 years).
- **Restoration project (C-140)**: if the expected duration exceeds 15 months, prompt to notify the Regional Office. Special Survey tracking; parts provenance register (original or approved alternative); stage inspection log.
- **Repair approval tracking (C-110)**: repair data source type (SRM, TDO-associated, foreign TDO 8110-3, foreign non-TDO/STC, UK non-TDO A2-5, EASA Part 21 Subpart M, RAS). NTO is not acceptable as approval (validation rule). Pending damage tolerance evaluation with a 12-month deadline to incorporate inspections into the AMS.
- **CAA STC project (C-150)**: PNxxxxx project number distinct from STC number; classification; DOA reference; certification plan items with status tracking; compliance sheets open/closed; report index with CAA approval status; witnessed tests; meetings and minutes; manuals affected; Declaration of Compliance; Final Statement; STC record (valid until surrendered/withdrawn/terminated; re-issued on transfer).
- **CAMO contract module (C-160)**: required fields (type, registration, MSN, owner/lessee name and address, CAMO name and address) and mandated clause text. Termination triggers a 2-week notification alert to the authority.
- **POA supplier control (C-180)** (if POA users are supported): supplier register with category (partner, supplier with Form 1/8130-3, subcontractor, significant subcontractor, vendor, COTS); significance flag (critical part, hazardous or worse); Form 51 change notifications; risk rating None/UK/International; LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH witness counters per 2-year cycle; transition plan checklist (13 elements a to m); vendor material test schedule; second/third-party scheme membership.

### 6.3 Records-system compliance (C-90): design requirements for Airworthiness+ itself
- Immutable audit trail of every input, amendment and deletion (keep the full history, not just the latest value). SB terminating actions must not erase earlier repetitive inspection records.
- Role-based access; only authorised users can alter or delete; per-user credentials.
- Data migration tooling with a verification report and sampling; 100% verification mode for AD and CMR data.
- Backup, restore and power-loss resilience; archive/export ("dumping") procedures documented.
- Serial number control for any generated release certificates (consecutive, no gaps).
- Support a 6-month parallel-run mode and an in-app Problem Log (bound, non-deletable entries).
- Generate Company Manual / Exposition text describing the system functions, query procedures, authorised persons, security, a flow chart and training.
- Written CAA approval is needed to keep records other than logbooks (C-50 5.11). Store the approval reference and date per customer.

### 6.4 Reference lists to seed

### 6.5 Parts and components
- Component record fields for a Form 1 on a repaired major component (C-130): hours, cycles, last maintenance date and organisation, ADs/repairs/modifications list, life used per limit type (fatigue, overhaul, ultimate), attached history record, repair scheme reference.

- Ex-military spares acceptance inspection record (condition, life, records, modification status, compatibility to serial; certifying engineer sign-off before fitment).
- Data plate / identity transfer event (sailplane fuselage replacement).

### 6.6 Tooling and calibration (D-20)
`Tool/Appliance` entity: ID, calibration standard, results, uncertainty, interval (supplier default with override justification), permissible error limits, release authority/provider (UKAS flag), use limitations, last calibration date, next due date, status (serviceable / quarantined / unserviceable label), loan arrangement. Alerts before due date; block use of an overdue tool in a task card; look-back workflow when an out-of-tolerance result is found; drift analysis.

## 8. Possibly Outdated

Marked as superseded by the text itself:
- C-100 supersedes and cancels AIL/0167 (29 October 2004).
- CAP 208 Volume 2 is described as "now obsolescent".
- C-180 says it "updates the CAA process" for supplier witness assessment (March 2016 version).

Probably outdated (my assessment):
- **All EASA references** (EASA C of A, EASA Permit to Fly, EASA Forms 1/18a/18b/37/52/55/56/58A, EASA approval of flight conditions, EASA SAS, "valid in all EU Member States", Part M M.A. references, EASA ADs, EU imports without a check flight). Since 1 January 2021 the UK CAA is the competent authority under UK Reg 748/2012 and 1321/2014. Forms are now CAA Forms (for example CAA Form 1, CAA Form 18a/18b), and design-related flight conditions would go to the CAA or a UK Part 21J DOA. EU-to-UK transfers are now imports from a third country. Leaflet C-60 needs reinterpreting in UK terms.
- **C-160 / Part M**: Light non-CAT aircraft are now under **Part-ML** (with Part-CAO), not Part M Subpart G. The "controlled environment" and ARC extension concepts and the 2730 kg CAMO split differ under Part-ML (ML.A.901 to 903: CAO/CAMO or independent certifying staff for ARC). The 2-week contract-nullity notice comes from Part M Appendix I.
- **C-170**: the 2008 transition is historic; still relevant only for aircraft with long-lapsed national C of As.
- **C-70 / C-130**: Regulations 216/2008 and 1702/2003 have been replaced (216/2008 by 2018/1139; 1702/2003 by 748/2012). In the UK, the sailplane deregulation and the BGA's role have changed again since 2021.
- **C-50**: the 2003/2008 dates, JAR-OPS 3 and EU-OPS (now UK Air Ops Reg 965/2012), JAR-26 (now Part-26), the CAA addresses (Kingsway, Aviation House at Gatwick; the CAA moved to Aviation House, Crawley, with new contact details), Safety Regulation Group (now Safety and Airspace Regulation Group), ORS 5 references, the Light Aircraft Maintenance Schedule CAP 411/412 (superseded by LAMP CAP 766/767, and for Part-ML aircraft by the Minimum Inspection Programme). "Annex II" is now "non-Part 21" aircraft under the UK Basic Regulation (Annex I to the retained 2018/1139).
- **C-60**: the reference to Commission Regulation 2042/2003 for the CRS (replaced by 1321/2014).
- **C-80**: London City runway and glideslope data; the CAA department names.
- **C-90**: references to tape backup and "computer type" are dated. ANO 2009 article numbers are now ANO 2016 articles (different numbering).
- **C-100**: DTI references; DAP form numbers; Ofcom arrangements; ANO 2009 Art 39(8)/20(5)(a).
- **C-110**: "Annex II to EU Regulation 216/2008".
- **C-120**: the AD200/AD202P renewal process and "pads supplied by the CAA" may since have changed (the CAA has moved to online forms; current ex-military policy is in CAP 632 and later revisions of BCAR A8-20). The 3-year flight test period should be verified against current BCAR A3-7.
- **C-150**: the "Arrangement with the UK" basis and the 1702/2003 reference; the UK now issues its own STCs for Part 21 products too.
- **C-180**: written while the UK CAA was the "Competent Authority for production" under EASA Part 21. EASA Form 51/55/56/58A are now CAA equivalents, and EASA ED 2010/016/R and the EASA FAQ link are pre-Brexit.
- **D-10**: refers to approval "by EASA or the Civil Aviation Authority in accordance with ... 1702/2003 or BCAR".
- **D-20**: BS EN 30012-1 has been withdrawn and replaced by ISO 10012 (my recollection; verify). JAR references are dated.
