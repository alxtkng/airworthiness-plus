## 2. BCAR and National Regime

Most of this chunk is technical. The regime content is in H-10 and H-20.

### Annex II / State aircraft and BCAR Section L (H-10)
- H-10 2.3 NOTE: "Annex II and State aircraft are aircraft that are not included under EC Regulation 216/2008 and therefore Part-145 and Part-M do not apply." (The UK term is now "non-Part 21" aircraft. The text still uses "Annex II".)
- Maintenance certification for "some aircraft categories e.g. Annex II aircraft and airships" continues "for the time being" to be regulated by the UK CAA under the national requirements of **BCAR Section L**.
- H-10 3.1: applicants may still **renew BCAR Section L licences** "where a need can be shown", for example for Annex II type aircraft ratings or airships.
- H-10 1.2 refers to the "cessation of BCAR Section L" for EASA aircraft. Most Section L licences had to be converted to Part-66 by 28 September 2008 for EASA-certified aircraft.

### BCAR Section L licence types and categories (H-20)
- **Aircraft Maintenance Engineer's Licence (AMEL)** issued under BCAR Section L, compared with the **Aircraft Maintenance Licence (AML)** issued under Part-66.
- **Type Rated Licence**: a Section L licence with type ratings. It confers certification privileges in its own right.
- **LWTR (Licence Without Type Rating)**: under Section L this confers no certification privileges on its own. It must be used with a certification authorisation. A basic Part-66 AML is the same, except for "that allowed under Part M.A.801(b)2", which is the owner/independent-certifier route.
- Section L categories named in this chunk:
  - 'A' (Aeroplanes): airframe and systems, not engines
  - 'B'
  - 'C': engines
  - 'D'
  - 'X': specialist category; 'X' Compasses is named
  - 'R': radio
- H-20 2.1: a Category 'A' holder may issue CRSs for maintenance of **non-commercial air transport aeroplanes that fall under EC Regulation 216/2008 Annex II** (not including engines) for which the licence is type rated.
- A UK national licence gives no certification privileges on aircraft not registered in the UK, unless the licence is validated by that state's authority and/or the holder is authorised by an organisation that authority has approved.

### Certification authorisations (H-20)
- An "authorisation" is one issued by an EASA or CAA Approved Maintenance Organisation (under BCARs, Part-145 or Part-M) that lets the holder certify within the limits it states.
- H-20 1.10: licences that can support an authorisation within a **CAA-approved maintenance organisation** (national/BCAR):
  - BCAR Section L LWTR, provided the authorisation is within the scope of the licence held at **1 June 2001**. Aircraft below 5700 kg MTOM only.
  - BCAR Section L LWTR extended with the appropriate type rating. Aircraft below 5700 kg MTOM only.
  - Part-66 Category A, provided the authorisation complies with AMC 145.A.30(g).
  - Part-66 Category B, only if extended with the type rating. No authorisation against a basic licence.
  - Part-66 Category C, only if extended with the type rating. No authorisation against a basic licence.
- A **Part-66 licence is required** for every Part-M Subpart F or Part-145 authorisation.
- When certifying under an authorisation, the engineer is **not** certifying under the privileges of the licence, and company procedures apply.

### Certificates named and their legal bases (H-20 1.1)
- **Certificate of Maintenance Review (CMR)**: Air Navigation Order 2009 articles 25 and 26.
- **Certificate of Release to Service (CRS)**, national: ANO 2009 articles 28, 29, 30 and 31, for aircraft or components **not** required to be maintained under Part-145 or Part-M.
- CRS under **Part-145, 145.A.50**.
- CRS under **Part-M, M.A.801**.
- **Certificate of Fitness for Flight**: under BCAR Section A/B Chapter A/B3-8.
- **Flight Release certificate**: for aircraft not required to be maintained under Part-145. It can be issued by a type-rated Section L AMEL holder, or a Part-66 holder endorsed for the type and "rendered valid in the United Kingdom", under the terms of an **EASA Permit to Fly**.

### BCAR chapter references used for national certification
- **BCAR Section A, Chapter A5-3**: definition of "vital points".
- **BCAR Section A/B, Chapter A/B6-2**: duplicate inspection, and the conditions for CRS and CMR.
- **BCAR Section A/B, Chapter A/B6-7**: CRS and CMR requirements, cited together with A/B6-2.
- **BCAR Section A/B, Chapter A/B3-8**: Certificates of Fitness for Flight.
- **BCAR Section A sub-section A8**: approval of welders, for aircraft maintained under national requirements.
- **Maintenance Schedule Approval Document**: governs CMR issue and may require specific 'X' or 'R' category certification of scheduled inspections.

### Other national items
- CAP 747 Generic Requirement (GR) No. 23: NDT personnel qualification (see section 3).
- "Mandatory Requirements listed in CAP 747" must be used, together with EASA Airworthiness Directives.
- G-20 3.4: furnishing materials in passenger smoking compartments must be flame-resisting and "approved by the CAA".
- Process Specifications **DTD 929** (penetrants), **DTD 901** (pickling of aluminium alloy forgings) and **DTD 911** (magnesium alloy surface protection) are UK national material and process standards.
- F-70 defines a **Class 1 part**: a part whose failure in flight or ground manoeuvres "would be likely to cause catastrophic structural collapse, loss of control, power unit failure, injury to occupants, unintentional operation of, or inability to operate, essential services or equipment". Magnetic flaw detection is normally applied to all Class 1 parts made of ferromagnetic materials.

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### Licensing and certification (H-10, H-20)
- Most BCAR Section L licences had to be converted to Part-66 by **28 September 2008** for all EASA-certified aircraft.
- Where protected rights did not convert to a full Part-66 category or sub-category, **limitations** were applied. They can be removed by passing conversion examinations.
- No further applications to extend a Part-66 licence through "protected rights" were accepted after **28 September 2012**.
- The UK CAA issued its first Part-66 licence for aircraft above 5700 kg MTOM on **1 June 2004**, and its first covering aircraft both above and below 5700 kg on **29 November 2004**.
- A proposed **Part-66 ELA licence** was required to be in place by **28 September 2015**. It was proposed for certifying ELA1 aeroplanes not used in commercial air transport, and possibly gliders, sailplanes, airships and balloons.
- From **28 September 2008**, certifying maintenance on non-commercial-transport aircraft with a Part-66 licence as the authority is limited to items **not** on the list in **Part-M Appendix VII**.
- **Recency (H-20 1.8)**: to certify under a type-rated licence or an authorisation, the licence or authorisation must be valid **and** the holder must have been engaged for periods totalling **at least 6 months in the 24 months** before the certification date, on work giving experience comparable with that needed for the grant.
- **CMR signatory**: the holder of an appropriately type-rated licence in **at least two Categories (other than Category 'X' Compasses)**, subject to A/B6-2, A/B6-7 and the Maintenance Schedule Approval Document.
- **Work records**: record work as it progresses or before starting an unrelated task, and in any case **no later than the end of the work period or shift**. Records include **open entries** for the actions still needed before release.
- **Duplicate inspection**: any assembly or disturbance of a vital point (A5-3) or control system needs a duplicate inspection, completed and certified under A/B6-2, **before** the CRS is issued.
- Part-M M.A.402(a): an **independent inspection** is required after any flight-safety-sensitive maintenance task, unless Part-145 specifies otherwise or the competent authority agrees.
- **Minor-adjustment duplicate inspections** (H-20 1.6c): holders of type-rated Category 'A' and/or 'C', or Part-66 B1, may certify duplicate inspections of minor adjustments to control systems on **other types within the Category** for which the licence is type rated, on aircraft (or engines) **below 5700 kg MTOM**. A minor adjustment is "a single point adjustment or reassembly of a control".
- **NDT certification (H-20 1.7)**: type-rated Section L holders in 'A', 'B', 'C' or 'D', or holders of a type authorisation equivalent to Section L 'A' and/or 'C' or Part-66 B1, may issue a CRS covering NDI, provided that:
  - a) Field Kit **Colour Contrast Dye Penetrant** inspection is done by a person type rated or authorised as above, or one qualified under **CAP 747 GR No. 23**;
  - b) **every other NDT technique, including Fluorescent Penetrant Dye**, is carried out and signed for by a person qualified under **GR No. 23** before a licensed or authorised engineer issues the CRS.
- **Welding**: where welding a metallic part is essential to airworthiness, the certifying engineer must establish that the welder is approved under **BCAR A8** (national aircraft). For Part-M Subpart F and Part-145 aircraft, the organisation controls competence.
- **No deviation**: licensed engineers certifying on their licence must not depart from the manufacturer's maintenance data or use alternative parts unless the departure is supported by the manufacturer's written agreement, approved as a change, or agreed by the CAA. In difficulty, consult the local CAA Regional Office.
- Certifying for work done by others: the certifier takes responsibility. They must inspect a sufficiently representative sample of the work and records, and be satisfied that the staff are competent. Complex tasks may need progressive inspections.
- Where the work crosses trade categories, each category holder issues a CRS for their own aspects. The person primarily responsible must involve the other trades.
- **Category 'A' exclusions (H-20 2.3)**: no CRS for overhaul, or for work involving:
  - a) bolted joints requiring special techniques;
  - b) complete riveted joints in primary structure;
  - c) complete glued joints in primary structure;
  - d) bonded assemblies in primary structure;
  - e) composite primary structure;
  - f) welded and brazed joints, other than minor weld repairs by an Approved Welder (and excluding replacement of structural members or repairs to flying control components);
  - g) cotton, linen, polyester or glass-fibre fabric covering of a complete fuselage or aerofoil;
  - h) disturbing individual parts of bench-tested units, except items normally replaceable or adjustable in service.
- **Category 'A' instrument systems** (excluding engine and APU systems):
  - systems in H-30 para 8.1: CRS may be issued;
  - systems in H-30 paras 8.2 to 8.4: replacements only, and only if functioning checks need no test apparatus.
- Category 'A' avionics: where no limitation is shown, CRSs may cover repair, replacement, modification, mandatory inspection or scheduled maintenance inspection. If the Maintenance Schedule Approval Document does not require 'X' or 'R' certification, the 'A' holder takes responsibility for schedule compliance on those systems.

### NDT numbers (F-20 to F-90)
- **F-20 dye penetrant**:
  - warm a cold or humid part to **32 to 38°C (90 to 100°F)**, never above **60°C (140°F)**;
  - hold an aerosol about **12 in** from the surface;
  - penetration time is usually **5 minutes to 1 hour**, extended proportionately below **15°C (60°F)**, and testing near freezing should be avoided;
  - oven drying at about **54°C (130°F)**, never above **79°C (175°F)**;
  - wet developer spray at no more than **1.0554 kg/sq cm (15 lb/sq in)**;
  - allow additional development time roughly equal to the penetration time, and compare indications at **30 seconds** and about **10 minutes**;
  - leak testing through walls of **1.5 to 3 mm**: at least **3 times** the one-side penetration time, plus one extra dye application for each 1.5 to 3 mm of wall thickness.
- **F-30 fluorescent penetrant**:
  - warm the part to **21 to 32°C (70 to 90°F)**;
  - aerosol at **30 cm (12 in)**;
  - penetration up to **10 minutes** for larger defects;
  - Table 1 penetration times (minutes):

    | Material | Defect | Minutes |
    |---|---|---|
    | Sheets and extrusions | heat-treatment, grinding and fatigue cracks | 15 |
    | Forgings | laps, cracks | 30 |
    | Castings | shrinkage, cracks, porosity | 3 to 10 |
    | Castings | cold shuts | 20 |
    | Welds | cracks, porosity | 20 |
    | Welds | included flux | 1 |
    | Plastics | cracks, crazing | 1 to 5 |

  - rinse with water at about **2.11 kg/sq cm (30 lb/sq in)**;
  - developer spray at no more than **1.05 kg/sq cm (15 lb/sq in)**;
  - after developing, stand for at least **15 minutes** before UV examination; if an indication is in doubt, re-examine after at least **2 hours**; use goggles if viewing exceeds **30 minutes**;
  - post-emulsification drain-off: no less than **15 minutes** and no more than **2 hours**; once an optimum is found, hold it within **±20%**;
  - emulsification about **2 minutes** (range **30 seconds to 5 minutes**);
  - developer may be removed with a **2% chromic acid** aqueous solution.
- **F-40 penetrant materials testing**:
  - keep a **one-pint sample** of every new batch of penetrant and emulsifier, in an airtight glass container, identified to its batch;
  - test at the manufacturer's intervals and whenever contamination is suspected;
  - test piece: **2024** rolled aluminium plate, heated to **525°C (977°F)** in not less than **4 minutes**, then water quenched;
  - drain for at least **20 minutes** and develop for **15 minutes**;
  - store the test piece in **50% trichloroethylene / 50% toluene**;
  - dye and fluorescent test pieces must never be interchanged.
- **F-50 ultrasonic**:
  - frequencies **500 kHz to 10 MHz**;
  - transverse wave speed about half that of longitudinal; surface waves about **90%** of transverse;
  - an example sensitivity setting is a peak of **2.54 cm (1 in)** from the reference flat-bottomed hole;
  - pulse-echo lamination checks only for plate over **1.27 cm (½ in)**;
  - after testing with a water couplant, dry the part before storage or service.
- **F-60 radiography**:
  - portable sets **10 to 250 kV**, typically 10 to 100 kV or 100 to 250 kV;
  - Cs-137 at **0.66 MeV** is equivalent to about 660 kV;
  - commonly used isotopes are Co-60, Ir-192, Cs-137 and Tm-170;
  - lead screens only intensify above **120 kV**;
  - image blur must not exceed **0.025 cm (0.01 in)**;
  - copper filter **0.1 mm** for 100 to 200 kV;
  - exposures of **2 minutes** or less usually eliminate internal scatter in light alloy;
  - viewing periods no longer than **90 minutes**, followed by at least **30 minutes** of other work;
  - pitting below **10 to 15%** of total thickness (including sealant) is doubtfully detectable;
  - **fuel tanks must be completely drained** before exposure through them (lead in avgas);
  - operations must comply with the **Radioactive Substances Act 1993** and the **Ionising Radiations Regulations 1999 (SI No. 3232)**.
- **F-70 magnetic particle**:
  - sensitivity falls sharply below **45°** between defect and flux, so normally **two tests at 90°** to each other are needed;
  - ink solids **0.8 to 3.2% by volume** (fluorescent inks about one tenth of that), to BS EN ISO 9934-2:2002;
  - current guide **500 A rms per inch of diameter**, or **150 A rms per inch of periphery** for irregular shapes;
  - equivalents of 500 A rms a.c.: d.c. **710 A**, half-wave rectified a.c. **225 A**, full-wave rectified a.c. **450 A**, peak-reading a.c. ammeter **710 A**;
  - threading bar **100 to 200 A per inch** of periphery;
  - coils: a.c. **2½ to 4 turns**, d.c. **6 to 10 turns**;
  - impulse method uses impulses under **1 second**;
  - flux time at least **3 seconds** on a.c.; d.c. time switches give **½ to 1 second**;
  - test with coatings in place only if paint or chrome is no thicker than **0.003 in** and electroplated nickel no thicker than **0.001 in**;
  - hand lens about **5x** to tell tool marks from cracks;
  - demagnetise by withdrawing the part at least **1½ m (5 ft)** along the coil axis;
  - **compass test**: deflection over **1°** means demagnetise and retest. The test distance should equal the distance from the aircraft compass to the installed part;
  - prods are often prohibited on finished parts, especially high tensile steel, because of burning risk;
  - do not use trichloroethylene in situ.
- **F-80 eddy current**:
  - **58 m/Ω·mm² = 100% IACS**;
  - standard depth of penetration is where current falls to **1/e (about 37%)**, P ≈ 500/√(σµf);
  - aircraft frequencies **5 kHz to 4 MHz**, and as low as **50 Hz** for ferromagnetic material properties;
  - aluminium conductivity rises with exposure up to about **500°C**;
  - unclad **7075-T6** is about **31 to 35% IACS**;
  - corrosion set-up uses sound material of about **90%** of the test thickness;
  - use dedicated coating-thickness equipment for coatings under **0.12 mm (0.005 in)**.
- **F-90 endoscope**:
  - initial torques for blanking plugs must be those in the Maintenance Manual;
  - never mix anti-seize types (graphite dry film and metal paste), because they form a hard-setting compound;
  - the inner third of a compressor blade, and the areas each side of mid-span shrouds, are usually "no damage allowable".

### Ground handling and fire (G-10, G-20)
- Jacking: keep the ram locking nuts within **50 mm (2 in)** of the jack heads while lowering. A maximum safe wind speed for jacking is set in the Maintenance Manual.
- Engine cleaning solvent is left for **5 to 10 minutes**.
- Turn a cold piston engine at least **two revolutions** before starting.
- Fuelling zone extends at least **6 m (20 ft)** radially from filling and venting points.
- "No Smoking" signs at least **15 m (50 ft)** from fuelling equipment and tank vents.
- No fuelling within **30 m (100 ft)** of radar equipment under test or in use.
- No photographic flash within **6 m (20 ft)** of filling or venting points.
- A major spillage is over **5 m² (55 ft²)**: stop the flow, evacuate, and notify the Aerodrome Fire Service.
- Methyl bromide left in an engine (liquid below **4°C**) means the engine must be stripped and checked for corrosion. Chemical foam deposits also need stripping. CO2 and BCF are harmless if the engine is run or turned within a few hours.
- Highly toxic extinguishants (methyl bromide, carbon tetrachloride) are prohibited in crew or passenger compartments.
- After a gas turbine "wet start", do a "dry run" before trying again. Drain the vent tank as the manufacturer advises.
- After a tyre failure, inspect the associated wheel (Leaflet 32-20). Other wheels may also need inspection if the aircraft landed or was towed on a deflated tyre.
- Towing on deflated tyres: if an axle still has one sound tyre, the aircraft may be towed to maintenance. If not, move it only clear of the active runway, then change the wheels.

## 4. Records, Forms and Documents

- **Radiographic Technique Sheet** (F-60 Figure 5, as recommended in BS M34). It is numbered for identification, with sheet x of y.
  - Header fields: company name and address; set used; type of radiation; source size; film processing; description; part no.; purpose of inspection; area to be inspected; acceptance standard; material and specification; preparation; associated documents (BS M34).
  - Signatures: **Prepared by / Date** and **Approved by / Date**.
  - Exposure table columns: aspect or position; angle of beam to film; s.f.d.; kV; mA; time; filters on tube and on film; screens; Ug; film; size and pattern; radiograph no.; figure reference.
  - Notes: items to remove (fuel, radiation-sensitive items, sealant or paint), any jacking or trestling, and positioning measurements for film, set or isotope. Isometric drawings and photographs may be added.
  - A record must be kept of the conditions under which the technique was established.
- **Magnetic particle Technique Sheet** (F-70 para 5). It details each magnetising operation:
  - type of equipment;
  - current strength and form;
  - acceptance standard;
  - contact areas;
  - flux detector positions;
  - coil type;
  - threading bar size;
  - test pattern;
  - illustration, cleaning and demagnetising method.

  Use BS 6072:1981 symbols. Techniques must state whether currents are rms or peak.
- **MPI defect records**: defects are marked with grease pencil or paint. Permanent records can be photographs, transparent tape lifted onto contrasting card, strippable coating, or thermosetting plastic powder. A part accepted after MPI is identified per its drawing to show that the test was done satisfactorily.
- **Penetrant test piece reference record** (F-40): a photograph of the initial indications after **15 minutes** of development. Each later test compares against it for vividness, definition, very fine defects, retention in wide cracks, and background.
- **Retained penetrant batch samples**, identified by batch.
- **Ultrasonic**: automated wheel inspection lets an electronic record of each inspection be kept. Reference standards can be identical parts with known or drilled or spark-eroded defects. For a known casting defect, oscilloscope pattern sketches of sound and faulty parts are useful.
- **Eddy current reference pieces**: marked with the depth beside each cut and with the material specification. The acceptance level is the signal from a specified cut.
- **Endoscope inspection report** (F-90 3.5, 3.6). For static parts, identify damage by:
  - a) access port used;
  - b) direction of view;
  - c) area or component (by name and/or number);
  - d) dimensions and type of damage.

  For rotating parts, give:
  - compressor port;
  - direction of view;
  - damage details;
  - turbine port;
  - the turbine blade number at the centre of the field of view (blades numbered on the convex surface at overhaul).
- **Engine records** (F-90 5.5):
  - All damage found must be recorded in the engine records, whether the inspection was routine or special.
  - Engine-type inspection sheets or charts with blade and flame tube drawings, marked with defects, become part of the engine records. Photo and video may be added.
- **Log books / technical logs** (F-90 5.5.2):
  - Where the Maintenance Manual accepts a defect for a finite number of hours or cycles, entries must limit engine operation to that period.
  - Where related defects each have an allowance, consider certifying for **less than the most restrictive** allowance.
- **Scheduled endoscope inspections** are recorded on a chart specific to the engine type. It becomes the record of deterioration and supports decisions to continue to the next inspection or to monitor more often.
- **Maintenance work records** (H-20 1.4b): progressive recording, open entries, and handover at shift change. **Pre-planned stage sheets** for frequent complex tasks must reflect current manufacturer requirements and record all key stages.
- **Certificates**: CRS (ANO national, Part-145 145.A.50, Part-M M.A.801), CMR, Certificate of Fitness for Flight, Flight Release certificate, and duplicate inspection certification (see section 2 for who signs).
- **Certification authorisation document**: issued by the approved organisation, stating limits.
- **Technique sheets** generally (MPI, radiography, ultrasonic) are written into Maintenance Manuals, Maintenance Schedules or a separate **NDT Manual**.
- Lubrication schedule approved for the aircraft. Intervals are usually in flying hours, with extra lubrication after de-icing (CAP 512) and after cleaning.
- Ground equipment maintained to the manufacturer's schedule. Bonding and earthing leads regularly continuity tested. Fire extinguishers regularly inspected.

## 5. Practice Guidance

### Penetrant (F-20, F-30, F-40)
- **Process**:
  1. Clean: inadequate cleaning is the main cause of failure.
  2. Dry.
  3. Apply penetrant by dip, flood, aerosol or brush.
  4. Allow the penetration time.
  5. Remove the excess (water-washable, or post-emulsifiable with solvent or emulsifier).
  6. Dry.
  7. Apply the developer evenly and thinly; brushing is not recommended.
  8. Inspect: white light for dye, UV in a darkened room for fluorescent.
  9. Mark defects; no crayons on highly stressed heat-treated parts.
  10. Remove the developer and reprotect promptly.
- **Materials**: penetrants must comply with **DTD 929** where penetrant inspection is mandatory. The manufacturer's storage life must not be exceeded. Flash points are low, so take fire precautions.
- **Surface preparation**:
  - Sandblasting can peen over defects; aluminium forgings are preferably pickled (DTD 901).
  - Magnesium castings are tested after chromating (DTD 911).
  - Not all trichloroethylene suits titanium, and methanol is not to be used on titanium.
  - Remove paint before testing.
  - Dye penetrant is not recommended on transparent acrylics (crazing). Fluorescent penetrant is unsuitable for absorbent materials.
- **Choice of method**:
  - Post-emulsification is the most sensitive and suits machined parts and tight defects.
  - On rough surfaces, use a penetrant containing an emulsifier.
  - Dye suits large parts and well-lit in-situ work; fluorescent suits dark areas.
  - For steel castings, both penetrant and MPI may be specified. If MPI comes first, degrease thoroughly.
- **Interpretation**: the rate of staining shows width and depth; the extent shows volume.
  - scattered dots: porosity or pitting;
  - dotted lines: tight cracks or laps;
  - continuous lines: wide cracks or lack of fusion.
- **Materials performance (F-40)**: compare in-use against unused material on a split test piece. Possible outcomes:
  - both halves good: satisfactory;
  - used material poor: discard it;
  - both halves poor against the initial record: process fault (temperature, washing, development) or test piece contamination.

### Ultrasonic (F-50)
- Ultrasonic testing complements other methods and is not foolproof. Operators must be trained and qualified on the equipment.
- It is the only satisfactory method when a defect lies parallel to the only accessible surface.
- Uses: in-situ checks of spar booms, forgings, castings and extrusions where visual or penetrant access is impossible; wheels, engine bearers and axles by immersion at overhaul; bonded-structure delamination (resonance, when dry); skin thickness of hollow blades.
- **Sensitivity**: calibrate against flat-bottomed-hole blocks (longitudinal) or a hole or saw cut in the same material and thickness (shear). The IIW test block checks the equipment. An attenuator gives dB relative to the reference.
- Coarse grain needs lower frequency; fine grain allows higher frequency.
- Dead zone below the surface. Couplant is required (oil or grease in situ, water for removed parts).
- Welds: thick-section welds by angled shear-wave probes; thin sheet welds by X-ray. Correlate with radiography when in doubt.

### Radiography (F-60)
- Radiography uses an approved technique, proven initially (if necessary by dismantling the structure). It is an extension to inspection and not foolproof.
- Training is needed for both safety and interpretation.
- **IQIs** (step-wedge or wire) go on the source side. The last visible step or wire is expressed as a % of thickness. A simulated-defect IQI can be used for fatigue cracks. For thin aircraft structure, the structure itself acts as an adequate IQI.
- Cracks normal to the beam may be missed, so the beam angle matters.
- Use the lowest usable kV for best contrast. A double-film technique handles varying thickness.
- Interpretation needs knowledge of the structure and reference radiographs of sound structure:
  - corrosion has fuzzy outlines, sealant sharp ones;
  - stress corrosion shows as lines of spots;
  - rivet-line cracks show as arcs;
  - cracked overlapping stringers show as light lines;
  - sealant cracks can mimic structural cracks.
- Trend radiographs taken by an identical technique can reveal corrosion over time. Wing tension cracks can be opened by jacking.
- Drain fuel tanks first.
- Gamma sources are used where X-ray access is impossible, for example internally via guide tubes or engine rotor shafts.
- Fluoroscopy is for debris and assembly checks only.

### Magnetic particle (F-70)
- Complementary to **BS EN ISO 9934-1:2001**. It does not override the component manufacturer's techniques.
- Applied to all ferromagnetic Class 1 parts.
- **Procedure**:
  1. Degrease.
  2. Demagnetise.
  3. Plug oilways.
  4. Check the equipment (known-defect specimen, "portable crack", flux indicators).
  5. Magnetise in two directions.
  6. Ink during magnetisation.
  7. Interpret with a low-magnification lens.
  8. Demagnetise between flux-direction changes and at the end.
  9. Do the compass test for parts that could affect the aircraft compass.
  10. Clean, apply a temporary rust protective, and identify the part.
- **Method selection** (Table 1):
  - bar: current flow for longitudinal defects, magnetic flow for transverse;
  - tube: coil or magnetic flow for transverse, threading bar or current flow for longitudinal;
  - ring: threading bar for defects in line with the axis and radial defects, current flow or induced current for circumferential;
  - plate: current flow or prods;
  - disc: current flow or prods, rotated 90° between tests;
  - sphere: current flow or prods, rotated; magnetic flow or coil with extension pieces.
- Spurious indications come from brazed joints, the weld heat-affected zone, section changes, magnetic writing and over-magnetisation (furring). Welds with permeability changes may need radiography instead.
- Lighting: good daylight for normal inks; black light for fluorescent, checked periodically to **BS EN ISO 3059:2001**.
- Discard inks that are diluted or contaminated. Agitate inks.
- Keep magnetised parts apart from each other and from steel benches.

### Eddy current (F-80)
- Comparative method against reference pieces. It does not override manufacturer data.
- Advantages: little preparation, no removal, portable, and high sensitivity to surface defects.
- Limitations: penetration depth, coatings, hidden geometry. Signal size does not equal defect size.
- **Fastener hole procedure** (8.2.2):
  - a) clean;
  - b) calibrate and set lift-off;
  - c) set the depth stop and sensitivity on the reference notch;
  - d) rotate the probe in each hole and mark any reading above the reference; re-check on the reference frequently;
  - e) repeat at depth increments;
  - f) ream marked holes per the manufacturer and retest with the right probe size.

  Ovality gives a slower-changing signal than a crack.
- **Heat damage**: map conductivity around the area with an IACS meter and draw a demarcation line. Remove material up to that line.
- **Hidden corrosion**: use phase-sensitive equipment with penetration at least equal to the sheet thickness. Beware of stringers, frames, chem-milled areas and debris.
- **Material sorting**: phase-sensing equipment is needed for close alloys.
- **Coating thickness**: by lift-off, calibrated with non-conducting shims such as mica.

### Endoscope (F-90)
- Read with the Maintenance Manual and approved Maintenance Schedule, which give the damage and time limits.
- Inhibit start, ignition and thrust reverser systems first. Allow for residual heat, windmilling, contamination, electrical potential difference between probe and structure, and fuel or oil leaks.
- Remove plugs with care, since inserts may come out. Refit all plugs and panels securely: a missing plug risks gas leak, fire warning, blade flutter or loss of cooling air.
- Count all blades in a stage, making several passes on long blades. Withdraw the probe before rotating the engine. Turn the HP shaft by air motor or by hand; LP and IP shafts by hand or with a local tool.
- **Inspection types**:
  - **Scheduled**: approved schedule, driven by cycles or flight time, mainly combustion and turbine.
  - **Special**: monitors a known developing defect.
  - **Non-scheduled**: after FOD, bird strike, surge, or over-temperature or over-power events. It may support a flight back to base.
- **Compressor**: FOD (nicks, dents, scratches, cracks); surge (root trailing-edge cracks, rub marks, tip curl, rotor-stator "clanging"); oil and grime deposits (indicating front bearing leak); mid-span shroud wear (metallic streaking, wavy join, hammering).
- **Combustion**: concentric burners and flares; carbon deposits; swirler security; flame tube cracks (circumferential worse than axial; around dilution chutes serious; Manual limits such as "beyond the third row of cooling holes"); wigglestrip distortion; burning versus discolouration; holes (inspect the turbine unless the hole was caused by burn-through; check whether it is carbon); nicks and dents (extend to the NGVs).
- **NGVs**: discolouration; cracks (not acceptable if the surface lifts; converging cracks or cracks on convex surfaces may mean rejection); burning and erosion; dents and nicks; trailing-edge tearing.
- **Turbine blades**: stricter limits. Trailing-edge cracks are not normally allowable. Dents on hollow blades can start internal cracks. Baked-on sand shortens engine life.
- Engines without hard-time limits can stay in service on condition monitoring (performance, oil analysis, endoscope).

### Ground handling (G-10)
- **Light aircraft**:
  - steering arm on the nose wheel;
  - push only on designed structure, never trailing edges, control surfaces or streamlined wires, and never by the propeller (treat engines as live);
  - prefer pushing backwards;
  - observe the turning limits marked on the nose leg;
  - do not lock rudder controls when towing a pedal-linked steerable nose wheel;
  - tail-skid aircraft: lift at the tailplane strut roots;
  - tow-bar with a brake person in the cockpit;
  - bridles on the main gear for soft ground.
- **Large aircraft**:
  - tow-bar shear pin;
  - check C of G for nose-wheel load (maximum aft limit and maximum towing weight, ballast if needed);
  - fit ground locks;
  - disconnect steering;
  - check shock absorber extension;
  - build brake pressure;
  - remove the torque link apex pin for large turns;
  - two people in the cabin with telephones, wing and tail walkers, one person in overall control;
  - wide turns, and a straight tow before stopping;
  - reconnect and lock the steering afterwards.
- **Parking and picketing**:
  - between flights: brakes, control locks, chocks; light aircraft into wind;
  - overnight: ground locks, blanks and covers with streamers, and a pre-flight check that covers and locks are removed;
  - tie down light aircraft (allow slack in natural-fibre rope);
  - large aircraft only in very strong winds, using tension-meter cables;
  - helicopters: tether blades, collective in fine pitch, rotor brake on; observe the maximum blade pull-down from the droop position; fold blades or hangar in high winds.
- **Jacking**:
  - C of G and ballast;
  - jacking point load limits and load cells (Leaflet 8-10);
  - isolate squat-switch circuits;
  - light aircraft inside a hangar;
  - one person per jack plus a coordinator;
  - procedure: check weight, fuel and C of G; head into wind; check clearance; earth; ground locks; pads and adapters; vertical jacks; raise level until the wheels are a few inches clear; ram nuts close to the jack shoulders; wing and fuselage steadying supports at ribs or frames;
  - lowering: clear equipment, check brakes free, lower in unison, then reinstate the circuits;
  - watch for sticking shock absorbers.
- **Levelling**: spirit level on pegs or screws; plumb bob and levelling plate; engineer's transit (most accurate, also used for dihedral and incidence checks). Re-check longitudinal level after lateral adjustment. Do not over- or under-inflate tyres to level.
- **Servicing**:
  - ATA 100 Chapter 12;
  - wing walkways, mats and soft footwear; no buttons or pocket tools (loose-article hazard);
  - ground equipment clean, maintained, and blanked;
  - **refuelling**: correct grade, earthing and bonding before opening, no radio or radar, essential circuits only, watch pressure-refuel shut-offs, move chocks clear of the tyres, ladders for high wings, clean up spills, and after refuelling sample drains for water, sediment and microbiological growth (wait as long as possible with turbine fuel; check SG);
  - **external power**: check voltage and polarity, or frequency and phase rotation, and use the correct sequence;
  - **compressed gas**: charge pressure by temperature graph, placard, or weight and C of G; charge slowly; never unscrew a valve body under pressure; remove the valve core before working on a depressurised unit (ice can block a tyre valve); fit blanking caps; oxygen needs no oil and fire cover;
  - **liquids**: approved fluids only; dedicated clean utensils; do not overfill; release pressure first; observe toxicity warnings; clean up spills (they damage paint and sealant);
  - **lubrication** per the approved schedule;
  - **cleaning**: blank apertures; manufacturer's agent; no chlorinated solvents; dry, then lubricate.
- **Cold weather**: clear snow and ice; watch for grit ingestion; fit covers promptly; antifreeze under covers but not on windows; check turbine intakes for ice; drain fuel and pitot-static systems; drain or treat water systems; surfaces must be free of frost, snow and ice before take-off (CAP 512); watch for refreeze after leaving a heated hangar.
- **Engine running**: minimise ground runs; head into wind; FOD check; observe the jet blast and intake danger areas from the Manual; intake guards; a look-out; manned fire cover.
- **Hand swinging**: only after demonstration and check-out. Always someone at the controls and chocks in. Use the standard calls: "Switches off, petrol on, throttle closed, suck in", then "Contact", then "Switches off, petrol off, throttle open, blow out".

### Fire precautions (G-20)
- No steel-studded footwear in hangars or fuelling areas. Do not carry matches.
- Fuel only at sites approved by the aerodrome authority. See CAP 748 (fuelling and defuelling fire prevention).
- **APU and GPU in the fuelling zone**: start the APU before caps are removed, and do not restart it during fuelling. Do not connect or disconnect GPUs during fuelling.
- **Bonding**: bond nozzles, funnels and chamois filters. Bonding stays on until caps are refitted.
- **Work during fuelling**: equipment clear (the aircraft settles); no main engines; essential circuits only (some operators allow unit replacement during kerosene fuelling, and none with higher-hazard fuels); no strobes; flameproof or intrinsically safe lamps; no combustion heaters; minimum personnel. Call the Fire Service if the gear is overheated, and suspend fuelling in electrical storms.
- **Hangars**: drain tanks in the open before work with ignition sources; purge vapour; have extra cover for in-hangar fuel tests (aircraft on jacks cannot be towed); maintain electrical equipment; use flameproof torches in tanks; non-flammable cleaners; isolate electrics and remove batteries when using flammable solvents; spray painting in a spray shop; no open containers of dirty fluids; remove magnesium and titanium swarf (special dry powder, never water).
- **Before closing panels**: bays clean, drains clear, sealants dry, electrics checked and systems functioned. **Duplicate inspection of flying controls** before covers are fitted where the bay contains flying controls.
- **Powerplant**:
  - inspect for cracks and leaks;
  - igniter leads;
  - pipe routing and clearances restored, colour coding, clipping, bonding (Leaflets 20-50 and 20-60);
  - trace leaks;
  - flame traps;
  - firewall grommets (no gaps permitted);
  - bonding;
  - exhaust and combustion chamber cracks;
  - vibration;
  - APU fire precautions;
  - saturated pipe lagging (soggy or blistered) must be renewed.
- **Airframe**:
  - fluid leaks (integral tank leaks can show far from the source);
  - oil-soaked lagging must be renewed;
  - oxygen kept free of oil, with cylinders clearly marked (confusion with air, CO2 or nitrogen has been fatal);
  - combustion heater NO-HEAT and OVER-HEAT switches checked at the schedule interval;
  - hot air ducting;
  - pyrotechnics.
- **Electrical**: design fuse and cable ratings; ventilation gauzes; commutation and terminal torque; insulation resistance and chafe tests at the intervals in the Manual; bearing failures.
- **Engine running fire drill**: CO2 trolley extinguisher manned; communication between cockpit and ground; avoid over-priming; deal with intake fires; after a wet start, do a dry run; watch jet pipe temperature; shut down other engines during a fire. Nacelle fire extinguisher doors (BS C.6) must operate freely inward.

## 6. Software Implications

### Licensing and certification (most relevant)
- **Licence object** with:
  - regime: BCAR Section L AMEL / Part-66 AML (UK);
  - type: Type Rated / LWTR / basic;
  - categories: Section L A, B, C, D, X (with subjects such as Compasses), R; Part-66 A, B1, B2, B3, C, L in UK practice;
  - type ratings list;
  - limitations, for example conversion limitations removable by examination;
  - validity / expiry;
  - validations by foreign authorities;
  - an "applies to aircraft register" flag (UK-registered only unless validated).
- **Authorisation object**: issuing organisation and its approval type (BCAR / Part-145 / Part-CAMO / Part-CAO / M Subpart F); scope; limits; underlying licence; MTOM limit (LWTR-based authorisations only for aircraft below 5700 kg). Rules:
  - an LWTR-based scope cannot exceed the scope of the licence held at 1 June 2001;
  - Part-66 B or C needs a type rating for an authorisation;
  - Part-145 or Subpart F authorisations require Part-66.
- **Recency check**: at least 6 months in the preceding 24 months of relevant work before any certification. Prompt for the experience log and warn before signature.
- **Certificate types enum**:
  - CRS (ANO art. 28 to 31, national);
  - CRS (145.A.50);
  - CRS (M.A.801 / ML.A.801);
  - CMR (ANO art. 25 and 26);
  - Certificate of Fitness for Flight (A/B3-8);
  - Flight Release certificate (Permit to Fly);
  - Duplicate Inspection;
  - Independent Inspection.

  Each certificate type stores its legal basis, its required signatory qualifications, and preconditions.
- **CMR signatory rule**: a type-rated licence in at least 2 categories, excluding 'X' Compasses, plus reference to the Maintenance Schedule Approval Document.
- **Category 'A' privilege engine**: block CRS for the eight excluded work types (section 3), and for overhaul and engines. Instrument systems use the H-30 8.1 to 8.4 lookup (replacement-only flag). The Maintenance Schedule Approval Document may require an 'X' or 'R' signatory for specific tasks. Model this as a task attribute "required certifying category".
- **Vital point / control system flag** on task cards. If set, the CRS is blocked until the duplicate inspection (national, A/B6-2) or independent inspection (M.A.402 / ML.A.402) is signed. Store both inspector identities.
- **Minor-adjustment duplicate inspection exception**: a type-rated A or C, or B1, holder can do the second signature on other types in the same category, for aircraft below 5700 kg, where the work is a single-point adjustment.
- **NDT certification rule**: an NDT task record has a technique (colour contrast dye penetrant field kit / fluorescent penetrant / MPI / ET / UT / RT / endoscope). For anything other than dye penetrant field kit, require a signatory with **CAP 747 GR No. 23** qualification (store level and method) before the CRS. The CRS signer is a separate person.
- **Welder approval**: welding tasks on national aircraft require the welder's BCAR A8 approval reference. Part-145 and Subpart F rely on organisation competence records.
- **Work-in-progress recording**: task step entries with timestamps; an end-of-shift check forcing open items to be recorded; open entries listing remaining actions; handover records; stage sheet templates with a version tied to the manufacturer data revision.
- **Maintenance data currency**: link the CRS to the revisions of manuals, ADs and CAP 747 mandatory requirements used; tool calibration status (block uncalibrated tools); a "working environment" attestation.
- **Deviation control**: any deviation from manufacturer data or any alternative part needs an attached justification of type: manufacturer written agreement / approved change / CAA agreement.
- **Multi-trade CRS**: allow multiple CRS signatures per work package, each scoped to a trade category.
- **Applicability flag** on aircraft: regime (Part-ML / Part-M / non-Part 21 (Annex II) / State). This drives which certificate types and licences are valid. H-10 confirms that Part-M and Part-145 do not apply to Annex II and State aircraft.

### NDT and inspection data objects
- **NDT technique sheet**: id/number, method, part no., material spec, area, purpose, acceptance standard, equipment, parameters, preparation notes, prepared by/date, approved by/date, revision. Method-specific fields:
  - RT: kV, mA, time, s.f.d., filters, screens, Ug, film;
  - MPI: current type rms/peak, value, contacts, coil, threading bar, flux indicators;
  - UT: probe, frequency, reference block, sensitivity;
  - ET: frequency, probe, reference piece.
- **NDT inspection result**: technique sheet reference, operator and qualification, date, equipment serial and calibration, reference standard used, indications (location, type, size), accept/reject, permanent record attachments (photos, tape lifts, radiographs, electronic scans).
- **Consumable batch tracking** for penetrants, emulsifiers, developers and magnetic inks:
  - batch no.;
  - manufacturer storage life expiry, with alert on expiry;
  - retained sample flag;
  - performance test log with outcome (satisfactory / discard material / investigate process) and due date at the manufacturer's interval;
  - ink solids % check;
  - UV/black-light check log (BS EN ISO 3059).
- **Equipment calibration**: UV lamps, IACS conductivity meters with calibration samples, UT sets (IIW block), X-ray sets, demagnetisers, flux indicators, compass test.
- **Post-MPI compass test record**: distance used, deflection in degrees; pass if ≤1°, otherwise demagnetise and retest. Required for steel parts near the aircraft compass.
- **Radiography preconditions checklist**: fuel tanks drained, items removed, jacking. Radiation safety compliance per the Ionising Radiations Regulations.
- **Class 1 part flag** on part records. This drives MPI requirements for ferromagnetic parts.

### Engine condition monitoring (F-90)
- **Borescope inspection record**: engine S/N, trigger (scheduled / special / non-scheduled with event type: FOD, bird strike, surge, over-temperature, over-power), access ports, direction of view, stage, blade no. (referenced to the numbered turbine blade), component, damage type (enum: nick, dent, crack axial/radial/circumferential, burning, erosion, discolouration, distortion, hole, tearing, deposits, shroud wear), dimensions, Manual limit reference, disposition, images or video, annotated diagram.
- **Damage trend tracking**: crack length over successive inspections, with growth alerts.
- **Time-limited defect acceptance**: an allowable-until value in hours or cycles, creating a repeat-inspection or limit item in the tech log and logbook. Where several related defects each carry an allowance, the system should propose the minimum or lower and flag it to the certifier.
- Plug refit / access panel closure check item on completion. Torque values come from the Manual.
- Borescope inspection intervals by cycles or flight hours, which "need not be concurrent" with airframe checks. The engine program needs its own interval basis.

### Ground handling and fire
- Task-card safety prerequisites, as reference checklists: jacking (weight, fuel, C of G, wind limit, earthing, ground locks, squat-switch circuits isolated and reinstated); towing (C of G, steering disconnect and reconnect, brake pressure); fuelling (bonding, zone).
- **Defect triggers**: tyre failure, then a wheel inspection task (Leaflet 32-20) for the failed wheel and possibly the others. Fire extinguishant ingestion (methyl bromide or chemical foam), then an engine strip task. Saturated lagging, then a renew task. Overheated brakes, then hold fuelling.
- **Duplicate inspection of flying controls** before closing panels on bays that contain flying controls. Link it to the panel-closure step.
- Lubrication tasks triggered by de-icing and by washing events, as well as by flight-hour intervals.
- Fuel sampling after refuelling (water, sediment, microbial). This could be a pre-flight or daily check item.
- Ground equipment maintenance schedules; bonding lead continuity tests; fire extinguisher inspections. These are organisation asset tracking items.
- Hand-swinging authorisation: a personnel qualification record (demonstrated and checked out).
- Reference lists:
  - NDT methods;
  - DTD process specs (929, 901, 911);
  - British Standards (BS M34, BS EN ISO 9934-1/-2, BS EN ISO 3059, BS 6072, BS EN 1330-3, BS 3683 Pt 5, BS 3889 Pt 2A/2B, BS C.6);
  - isotopes;
  - IACS values (7075-T6: 31 to 35%).

## 8. Possibly Outdated

- **H-10 and H-20 are written in EASA-era terms** (2012). Each item below is given as the text states it, then the likely current position (a guess):
  - "EASA Part-66 licence", "EASA Commission Regulation (EC) No. 2042/2003", "EASA Approved Maintenance Organisation" and "EASA Permit to Fly": UK Regulation 1321/2014 now applies; Part-66 licences are UK-issued; permits to fly are issued by the UK CAA.
  - "EASA Airworthiness Directives" and "an inspection made Mandatory by EASA": the UK CAA now issues or adopts ADs.
  - The "Annex II" terminology is replaced in UK law by "non-Part 21" aircraft (Article 2(3)(d) / Annex I to the UK Basic Regulation).
  - "Part-M" rules for non-commercial light aircraft have largely moved to **Part-ML** (ML.A.801 CRS, ML.A.402).
  - The Part-M Appendix VII limit for Part-66 certification is no longer framed as stated.
  - The **Part-66 ELA licence** proposed for 2015 became the B3 and L licences.
- **Dates already past**: conversion by 28 September 2008; protected-rights extensions closed 28 September 2012; ELA licence by 28 September 2015.
- **ANO 2009** article references (25, 26, 28 to 31): replaced by ANO 2016 (a guess).
- **"BCAR Section A/B" chapter numbering** (A/B6-2, A/B6-7, A/B3-8) and Section A Chapter A5-3: BCAR Section A has since been reissued (Issue 8 and later) with A8-x approvals. The numbers may have changed (a guess).
- **Section L LWTR** "within the scope of the licence held at 1st June 2001": a historic grandfathering date. Section L is largely closed apart from renewals where there is a need.
- **CAP 747 GR No. 23** (NDT qualification) may have been revised or replaced by EN 4179 / Part-145 based requirements (a guess).
- **Toxic or obsolete materials**: carbon tetrachloride as a cleaning solvent (F-20, F-30); trichloroethylene (F-20, F-30, F-40, F-70); methyl bromide and BCF (halon) extinguishants (G-20). These are now banned or restricted under health, environmental and halon rules. Treat as outdated practice.
- **Old standards**:
  - BS M34, BS 6072:1981 and BS 3683/3889: largely superseded by EN/ISO standards;
  - BS EN ISO 9934-1:2001, -2:2002 and BS EN ISO 3059:2001: have newer editions;
  - BS EN 1330-3:1997;
  - DTD process specifications: many are withdrawn.
- **Old technology**: cathode ray oscilloscopes, valve oscillators, film radiography (digital/CR is now common) and 1960s reference textbooks (F-80). Factories Act references are replaced by later health and safety legislation (a guess).
- **Internal inconsistencies in the NDT cross-references**:
  - F-40 calls F-60 the fluorescent penetrant leaflet, but fluorescent penetrant is F-30 and F-60 is radiography.
  - F-30 points to F-50 for magnetic flaw detection; MFD is F-70 and F-50 is ultrasonics. F-20 correctly cites F-70.
  - F-50 cites F-10, F-20 and F-30 as surface methods.
  - F-20 gives developer spray pressure as 1.0554 kg/sq cm and F-30 as 1.05 kg/sq cm (both 15 psi).
- **Passenger smoking compartments** (G-20 3.4): smoking on aircraft is now prohibited. This is largely historic.
- The F-90 reference to a "Maintenance Schedule based on a 'Hard Time Life' philosophy" reflects older maintenance programme concepts.
