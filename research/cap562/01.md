## 2. BCAR and National Regime

### EASA vs non-EASA (now Part 21 vs non-Part 21) terminology
- B-30 defines "EASA Aircraft" as aircraft required to comply with Regulation (EC) No. 216/2008, and "Non-EASA Aircraft" as aircraft not required to comply. Lists of each are in CAP 747.
- B-80 3.3 defines non-EASA aircraft as aircraft within **Annex II** of the Basic EASA Regulation, plus aircraft engaged in military, customs or police services. CAA-classified Annex II aircraft are listed in CAP 747 Section 1 Part 2. (In today's UK terms, Annex II became Annex I to Regulation 2018/1139 and the UK now says "non-Part 21 aircraft". That mapping is my inference, not stated in this chunk.)
- B-110 2.1 describes aircraft "subject to national legislation as defined in Articles 1(2) and 4(4) of Regulation (EC) No. 216/2008", i.e. Annex II aircraft and aircraft engaged in military, customs, police or similar services.
- B-80 4.5: national legislation (ANO, implemented through BCAR Sub-Sections A/B-3) continues to apply to non-EASA aircraft, which keep a **national Airworthiness Certificate**.

### BCAR sections and chapters mentioned in this chunk
- **BCAR Section A** (CAP 553) and **Section B** (CAP 554). Section A covers approved organisations and general procedures; Section B is the parallel set for B-category subjects (e.g. B2-2, B3-2, B3-3). (B-80 reference list: "British Civil Airworthiness Requirements Sections A and B (CAP 553/554)".)
- **A2-5 / B2-2 paragraph 7 / "A/B2-5"**: approval of changes (modifications and repairs) and Flight Manual changes for national aircraft. B-120 requires critical standard-part substitutions to be embodied by modification under BCAR A/B2-5.
- **A3-1 and A3-3**: issue of C of A and National Airworthiness Review Certificate (National ARC); a check flight is required before first issue for non-EASA types (B-60 6.2.1).
- **A3-2 / B3-2**: requirements for issue of Certificates of Airworthiness; for MTOW above 15 000 kg require a design organisation report (B-100).
- **A3-8 "A Conditions"** and **A3-9 "B Conditions"**: flight testing of non-EASA aircraft without a C of A (B-30 5.6 note 2; B-60 6.1.2 mentions "A" or "B" conditions when no C of A is in force).
- **B3-3**: C of A for series aircraft (B-140 reference).
- **A4-8**: design approval of aircraft equipment and accessories; paragraph 3 says A4-8 need not be followed for Aircraft General Spares (AGS) and standard parts to National/International specifications recognised by the CAA (B-120).
- **A5-1**: continued airworthiness obligations of UK Type Design Organisations (publication of continued airworthiness information); paragraph 4 provides for **Type Responsibility Agreements**; paragraph 1.2 applies whether or not a UK-manufactured type has a UK TC (B-90).
- **A6-1 paragraph 4** (and its AMC): decision on when a check flight is needed for continuing airworthiness of a national aircraft rests with the pilot-owner, maintainer or CAMO (B-60 6.2.1).
- **Sub-Section A8** (organisation approvals):
  - **A8-1, A8-2**: production approvals; before 28 Sept 2005 their holders released parts on JAA Form 1 with a Block 13 national-rules statement (B-110 4.2).
  - **A8-8 Group E3**: design organisation approval allowing reports certifying an aircraft conforms to, or differs in a defined way from, a CAA-approved standard for C of A issue; now also covers inspection of the aircraft against all C of A airworthiness and operational standards. A8-8 has an appendix on the format and content of the E3 report (B-100).
  - **A8-9**: organisation approval for flight testing of non-EASA aircraft under A/B Conditions (B-30).
  - **A8-15**: national maintenance approval; holders could continue on EASA aircraft only from 28 Sept 2008 to 5 Jan 2009, limited to "non-complex" tasks, until they got Part M Subpart F or Part-145 (B-80 6.2).
  - **A8-20**: approval for restoration, airworthiness control and maintenance of ex-military aircraft (C-120 title, in Contents).
  - **A8-21**: CAA's set of common requirements based on Part-21, replacing older BCAR A8 design/production approvals for organisations working only on non-EASA aircraft (B-30 5.5).
  - **A8-25**: national continuing airworthiness management organisation approval (CAMO equivalent); used for State aircraft imported with a National C of A first required (B-60 6.1.3).
- **BCAR Section L, B or D licence**: national licences; CAA could consider an Exemption letting such a holder oversee and certify an EASA "complex task" where an EASA-approved organisation could not be contracted (B-80 6.3).

### Certificates and approvals under the national regime (this chunk)
- **National Certificate of Airworthiness (C of A)** issued under the ANO by the CAA. Needed for non-EASA aircraft and for aircraft used for State purposes (except under ANO Art 17(3)). Aircraft with CAA-approved State-role modifications must have a CAA national C of A.
- **National Airworthiness Review Certificate (National ARC)**: issued with the national C of A after a check flight (first issue only); renewal no longer needs a check flight.
- **Special Category** national C of A: B-230 (title only in this chunk) covers aircraft not over 2730 kg with a national C of A in the Special Category.
- **Permit to Fly**: B-90 says orphan foreign types without a Type Responsibility Agreement can no longer hold a C of A and must move to a Permit to Fly. (The LAA, BMAA and CAA Permits are not detailed in this chunk.) EASA Permit to Fly (Form 20) is covered in C-60 (later chunk).
- **Type Design Organisation** (UK) vs **Type Responsibility Agreement (TRA) holder** (BCAR A5-1 para 4) vs CAA direct responsibility: three tiers of continued airworthiness support for UK-design non-EASA types (B-90).
- **CAA Supplement to a Part-21 DOA/POA**: lets an EASA-approved Part-21 organisation work on national design or national production (B-30 5.2, 5.4).
- **UK CAA Approved Certificate**: national equivalent of EASA Form 1 for parts of non-EASA aircraft (B-30 App 1).
- **CAA Airworthiness Specifications** Nos 1 to 22 (B-20).

### State aircraft (B-60)
- Basic Regulation Art 1(2) (as amended by Reg 1108/2009) excludes products, parts, appliances, personnel and organisations engaged in "military, customs, police, search and rescue, fire-fighting, coastguard or similar activities or services".
- CAA interpretation: a State aircraft is any aircraft (a) in the service of the UK military; (b) in the service of a Chief Officer of Police; (c) in the service of HM Revenue and Customs; or (d) engaged (Government service or not) in search and rescue, fire-fighting, coastguard duties, fisheries patrol, border/immigration control, or safeguarding national security.
- Nationally regulated activities: Police, SAR, Fire-Fighting, Fisheries Patrol, Revenue and Customs, Border/Immigration Patrol, Military activities including training and target towing/simulation for the military, national security; possibly others "under the control or responsibility of a government or public authority" (ask CAA). Government-sponsored environmental monitoring is also national, and such aircraft are likely Annex II as "specifically modified for scientific purposes".
- Not excluded (stay EASA): HEMS, calibration of navigation/landing aids, recreational gliding or parachuting even with military participants.
- Certificates: EASA aircraft get EASA certificates; State-use aircraft normally hold national certificates, so change of use means change of certificate. Exception: **ANO 2009 Article 17** lets an aircraft with an EASA certificate be used for State purposes if it complies with the Basic Regulation and implementing rules as if EASA, does not fly outside the UK without permission of the relevant competent authority, and meets UK national requirements in CAP 747 (intended e.g. for a temporary replacement aircraft).
- Chicago Convention: only "military, customs and police services" aircraft are State aircraft for international purposes; they have no automatic overflight rights. Other State-use categories (SAR, fire-fighting etc.) are civil for Chicago purposes.
- Continuing airworthiness: the ANO was amended (Part 3A) with a simplified, EASA-style continuing airworthiness regime for non-EASA civil aircraft (non-military State aircraft and Annex II aircraft); three-year transition started August 2012.
- State aircraft of an EASA type with a **National** C of A must: (a) be of a type design approved by EASA or the CAA for a national C of A; (b) have continuing airworthiness managed under **ANO 2009 Part 3A**; (c) be maintained by an organisation approved for the type by the CAA; (d) follow a CAA-approved maintenance schedule; (e) operate to a Flight Manual approved under the TC, STC or CAA modification procedure, with changes approved by CAA (or EASA-approved and CAA-accepted) under Part-21 or BCAR A2-5 / B2-2 para 7; (f) have mods and repairs approved the same way (special-role equipment without civil approval is assessed only as "no hazard" to the aircraft); (g) have parts and appliances maintained by an approved maintenance organisation, except special-role equipment without civil approval, which follows the manufacturer's recommendations; (h) comply with ADs, mandatory modifications and inspections per CAP 747.
- State aircraft with an EASA C of A follow the EASA continuing airworthiness rules exactly as EASA aircraft.

### Design and production (B-30)
- EASA aircraft: all design changes, repair designs and initial design to Part-21, approved by EASA or a Part-21 Subpart J DOA. A State aircraft's design change can still go through EASA if it is a series modification under the TC (not State-specific); if aircraft-specific, it can be approved nationally. Advice: prefer Part-21 approval, since any non-Part-21 change or repair prevents an EASA C of A; if the aircraft leaves State use, non-EASA changes must be removed or approved under EASA first.
- Non-EASA aircraft: design approved by the CAA, a BCAR-approved design organisation, or a Part-21 DOA with a CAA supplement. Examples: Annex II design changes or repairs; State-use changes not certifiable as EASA changes.
- Production, EASA: Part-21, released on EASA Form 1 by a Subpart G POA or under Subpart F. JAA Form One not appropriate in the UK since 28 September 2005.
- Production, non-EASA: where design data is nationally approved, EASA Form 1 is not appropriate; a BCAR-approved organisation or Part-21 POA with CAA supplement releases on the **UK CAA Approved Certificate**.
- Organisations doing only non-EASA work may keep BCAR Section A8 approvals; CAA has replaced them with BCAR A8-21.
- Flight testing: for EASA aircraft the CAA made Permit to Fly findings until 28 March 2007 (Reg 1702/2003 Art 2 para 11); Reg 375/2007 (4 April 2007) put flight testing into Part-21 (see C-60). For non-EASA aircraft, BCAR A3-8 (A Conditions) and A3-9 (B Conditions) apply, with an A8-9 approval where needed.

### Light aircraft / GA (B-80)
- "Light Aircraft": MTOM 5700 kg or less, excluding multi-engined helicopters.
- EASA airworthiness certificates: Certificate of Airworthiness (**Form 25**), Restricted C of A (**Form 24**), Permit to Fly (**Form 20**).
- Non-CAT EASA certificate renewal was through BCAR Sections A/B until Part-M applied in full.
- Public transport (continuing airworthiness sense): valuable consideration for hiring an aircraft (e.g. club hire); otherwise the flight is private. Aerial work: valuable consideration for the flight (e.g. instruction). If the only payment is to the pilot/instructor, the flight is private for airworthiness purposes (owners can pay an instructor in their own aircraft).
- Owners/operators must declare the purpose of operation so maintainers know what maintenance to do.

### Continued airworthiness support for UK-design types (B-90)
- Covers aeroplanes and rotorcraft only; not balloons, airships or gliders.
- BCAR A5-1 requires Type Design Organisations to publish continued airworthiness information; ICAO Annex 8 Part II requires the CAA to send it to States with the type on their registers.
- If the TDO stops existing or fails: CAA looks for another approved organisation to take type design responsibility, or for simple types a capable organisation to enter a TRA with the CAA; CAA may take responsibility temporarily.
- If a TDO or TRA holder ceases: CAA may (per ICAO Doc 9760 Airworthiness Manual Part III Ch 4) take Annex 8 responsibility itself and/or limit TC validity until an unsafe condition appears with no one able to propose a fix. CAA support is for a limited period; otherwise expect the TC to be withdrawn and C of A cancelled.
- UK-design types not listed in the appendices may no longer be eligible for a C of A. ANO 2009 Article 18 and BCAR A5-1 para 1.2 apply whether or not a UK TC exists.
- Withdrawal of support must be notified by the TDO via an **Alert Service Bulletin** or similar CAA-approved document; CAA tells other authorities.
- Foreign "orphan" types (no TDO or State-of-Design support): UK-registered examples cannot hold a C of A unless owners conclude a TRA with the CAA; otherwise transfer to a Permit to Fly.

### Imported aircraft for national C of A (B-100)
- MTOW above 15 000 kg: a report from an appropriate design organisation (BCAR A8-8 Group E3, or the TC holder if acceptable) certifying the aircraft's airworthiness standard conforms to, or differs in a defined manner from, a CAA-approved standard for the type. Outside the UK, where no specifically approved organisation exists, the overseas organisation must be acceptable to the CAA.
- MTOW below 15 000 kg: a suitably approved maintenance organisation, or (subject to CAA agreement) appropriately licensed engineers for types not listed in Leaflet H-30 paragraph 17. E3 organisation recommended where compliance work is significant.
- For EASA types, the non-expiring C of A (Part 21 Subpart H and Part M Subpart I) from 28 September 2007 ended the E3 report requirement; imports instead need an Airworthiness Review under M.A.710 (may look like an E3 report but must meet M.A.710 categories).

Certification by an appropriately licensed engineer or specifically approved organisation.
  - Pilot maintenance is **prohibited for aircraft operated in the parachuting role**.

## 4. Records, Forms and Documents

### UK CAA Approved Certificate (B-30 Appendix 1)
National release certificate for new parts (and maintenance on items) for non-EASA aircraft, issued only by CAA-approved organisations within scope. Not for releasing whole aircraft. Not for EASA aircraft parts. "This certificate has been issued under national rule provisions" pre-printed. Not a delivery or shipping note.

Block layout (portrait):
1. "United Kingdom Civil Aviation Authority" (pre-printed)
2. "UK CAA Approved Certificate" (pre-printed)
3. Form Tracking Number: unique, pre-printed (or computer-generated)
4. Approved Organisation Name and Address: exactly as on the approval certificate; second address if place of manufacture/release is not listed on the approval; logo allowed
5. Work Order / Contract / Invoice: mandatory for traceability if no serial/batch number in Block 11; else "N/A"
6. Item: line numbers; required if more than one line item; separate list permitted with cross-reference
7. Description: prefer IPC designation
8. Part No.: prefer IPC number
9. Eligibility*: (a) at least one specific aircraft/engine/propeller model (for engines/propellers state aircraft applications or "type-certificated engine/propeller"); (b) "None" (no type-approved application yet: pending TC, test only, pending approved data; explanation in Block 13; new items only for conformity); (c) "Various" per a CAA-approved procedure. Cross-reference to an attached list allowed. Not authority to install; installer must cross-check IPC, SBs etc. Standard parts need no Approved Certificate, but a POA releasing one must show control of its manufacture.
10. Qty
11. Serial No / Batch No, or "N/A"
12. Status/Work: standard words for new parts: "MANUFACTURED" (new production to design data; or re-certification by original manufacturer after rectification of an unused item, e.g. defective, inspection/test needed, shelf life expired; or conformity-to-airworthiness re-certification at design approval); "INSPECTED"/"TESTED" (examination of a previously released new item for conformity, to a customer standard, or for serviceability before re-release as a spare when obtained on EASA Form 1 or Approved Certificate); "MODIFIED" (alteration by the original manufacturer before entry into service). Must reference approved data/manual/specification in Block 12 or 13.
13. Remarks: all data/limitations needed by the installer, per item; "None" if nothing. Required statements: "ONLY FOR CONFORMITY, NOT ELIGIBLE FOR INSTALLATION ON IN-SERVICE TYPE-CERTIFICATED AIRCRAFT / ENGINE / PROPELLER" (at start of Block 13 itself) for conformity use; "DESIGN DATA APPROVED BY <third-country authority and approval reference>" where the CAA did not approve the design data; re-certification wording "RE-CERTIFICATION OF NEW PARTS FROM CONFORMITY TO AIRWORTHINESS: ..." on a second certificate from the same organisation that raised the conformity certificate. Typical other content: TC reference for complete engines/propellers; engine emissions compliance at date of manufacture; usage restrictions for repaired items; modification standard; alternative approved items; concessions; non-compliance with certification specifications; repair details; AD/SB compliance or non-compliance; life-limited item information; condition; manufacturing or cure date; shelf life; shortages; TSN, TSO; re-certification of previously released new items.
14. Manufacturing certification (new items only): conformity to "approved design data and are in a condition for safe operation" or "non-approved design data specified in block 13". Valid in the UK.
15. Authorised Signature: hand-written normal signature of a person with written authority from the approved production organisation; no stamp instead of a signature (stamp may be added); computer-generated signatures if CAA agrees and equivalent control is shown
16. Approval No.: full CAA authorisation reference
17. Name (printed)
18. Date dd/mmm/yyyy, month in letters
19. Maintenance release statement (work in Block 12 per UK airworthiness requirements, ready for release to service); 20 Authorised Signature; 21 Approval No.; 22 Name; 23 Date. Blocks 19 to 23 struck out for new items.
User/installer notes: the document alone is not authority to install; installers under another authority must check it accepts UK CAA parts; Blocks 14 and 19 are not installation certification: the aircraft maintenance record must carry an installation certification under national regulations before flight.

## 5. Practice Guidance

### Component acceptance (B-110)
- Definitions: Aircraft Component = engine, propeller, part or appliance. Product = aircraft, engine or propeller. Standard Part = designated so by the design approval holder, with all design, manufacturing, inspection and marking data public in a national/international specification; TSO/JTSO/ETSO and grandfathered National Equipment Approval parts are not standard parts; if the DAH adds selection criteria (extra inspection, burn-in, environmental tests) it must use its own part number and the part is not standard. Critical Part = failure analysis shows hazardous or worse effects not more often than extremely remote, or parts with replacement times/inspection intervals in the Airworthiness Limitations section/ICA. Non-required Equipment, Permanent equipment (not a PED, needs maintenance to fit/remove), Specification (drawing and/or DDP).
- End user must also confirm eligibility for the specific aircraft, considering CAP 747 and other technical publications.
- COTS permanently installed "no hazard" equipment (not used to operate/control the aircraft) need not have an EASA Form 1; a Certificate of Conformity supports authenticity. "No hazard" criteria: no unsafe operating modes, no unsafe failure modes (structural, electrical, system interface), no effect on aerodynamics/flight characteristics, not used to meet any airworthiness or operational requirement. Approved "no hazard, no credit"; DOA substantiates and issues ICA.
- COTS equipment not excluded can be approved if shown compliant; needs a release certificate; DOA specification (inspection of base item, mitigation of unsafe modes, new part marking) lets the POA release it on Form 1 as "manufactured"; repairs via approved maintenance organisation with sub-contract oversight per 145.A.75.
- Portable attached/docked equipment: DOA test-based no-hazard assessment (EMI, battery safety); guidance EFB: JAA TGL No 36; PEDs: FAA AC-91.21-1B; medical equipment: Leaflet 25-20. Unmodified COTS portable equipment is not Form 1 eligible. Attachment/docking design goes through normal change approval.
- PEDs: not Form 1 eligible; ANO Art 38(5) applies; AIC 1/2004 (Pink 62); EU OPS 1.110 / JAR OPS 3.110 for commercial operators.

### Standard parts in restorations (B-120)
- Critical nature joints (examples: spar or wing attachments, fin/tailplane attachments, engine/strut mounting structure, flying control systems or surface attachments): substitutes even of equal or better specification only by BCAR A/B2-5 modification or per the TC/type design holder's product support publications (see B-90).
- Non-critical (fairings, cabin furnishing, panel attachment): equal or better parts acceptable without CAA involvement where the manufacturer's data permits; otherwise A/B2-5.
- AGS, SBAC, BSI standard items covered by BCAR A4-8 para 3 exemption.

### Bogus parts (B-130)
- Unapproved part: not made to approved procedures, not conforming to approved type design, or not conforming to declared specs/industry standards. Includes falsely documented IPC parts, direct-ship production overruns from suppliers without production approval, and parts not maintained per approved data or by unauthorised persons.
- Examples: scrapped main rotor blade with release documents; engine mount "new in 1979" actually installed 1966.
- Verification: contact manufacturers (or UK agents) to confirm serial numbers are in their archives.
- User (approved organisation, authorised person, or CAA type-rated licensed engineer issuing the CRS) must ensure parts are serviceable and conform; purchase orders must define parts and quality/certification requirements; verify supplier compliance (possibly by visits). Private owners: exercise extreme caution.

### Safety critical tasks (B-150)
- Arises from an incident where drive cover plates were not refitted on both engines of a twin. Apply ETOPS-style philosophy to multi-system aircraft: stagger, independent checks, separate teams, task cards identifying critical tasks and their stages, maintenance programme rules identifying critical scheduled tasks, training on consequences.

### MEMS (B-160)
- Maintenance error defined as the maintenance system (including the human element) failing to perform as expected. System layers: engineer, immediate environment, supervision, organisation, regulation, wider environment (Boeing/Baines 2001 model).
- Required elements: policy cascade from Safety Policy/SMS; management commitment and roles; aims; encouragement of reporting; just culture with published disciplinary boundaries; published trigger events; root-cause investigation process; trained investigators (e.g. MEDA, Maintenance Error Decision Aid); staff education; corrective action with effectiveness monitoring; workforce feedback incl. continuation training; feedback into Quality system; trend analysis of contributing factors.
- Small organisations can use simple investigation but should use independent staff; large ones formal process and tools.
- No punitive action for unpremeditated/inadvertent lapses unless: (a) intended harm; (b) no constructive attitude to safe procedures; (c) knowing violation of available, workable, intelligible, correct procedures; (d) previous similar lapses; (e) concealment; (f) substantial disregard for safety (defined separately for certification authorisation holders and others); (g) knowingly reporting for work unfit. Sliding scale from retraining to dismissal.
- Also investigate near misses with no operational significance (e.g. oil filler cap caught before flight). CHIRP is not an alternative to MOR or MEMS. A statement that an incident is under MEMS investigation without explanation is not enough to close an MOR. CAA confidentiality: won't disclose reporter names or pass MEMS reports to third parties unless legally required or authorised; no criminal proceedings for inadvertent breaches revealed only via MEMS except gross negligence or recklessness. ECCAIRS for European data. Directive 2003/42/EC (occurrence reporting) enacted through the ANO 2009.

- Restoration projects: standard-part substitution workflow requiring classification critical/non-critical; critical requires a modification (BCAR A/B2-5) reference.

- Purchasing policy: purchase orders with part definition and quality/certification requirements; supplier verification records; quality manager approval for deviations.

**Organisations and approvals reference list**
- BCAR approval types: A8-1, A8-2 (production), A8-8 Group E3 (design, import reports), A8-9 (flight test, A/B Conditions), A8-15 (maintenance), A8-20 (ex-military restoration and maintenance), A8-21 (Part-21-like design/production), A8-25 (national CAMO); plus CAA supplement to Part-21 DOA/POA; Type Design Organisation; TRA holder.
- Licence types: BCAR Section L, B, D (national), Part-66.

**Maintenance planning**
- Task attribute `safety_critical` and `system_group`; scheduler rule to prevent identical critical tasks on redundant systems (e.g. both engines) in the same flight cycle, or require independent inspection / different teams (B-150). Task card stages for critical tasks.
- Check flight requirement flags: after prolonged storage, after reassembly of a shipped aircraft, after role modification, before first national C of A / National ARC for non-Part-21 types; decision for renewal by owner/maintainer/CAMO per BCAR A6-1 para 4; record Check Flight Schedule (CAP 1038).

**Occurrence / MEMS module**
- Internal occurrence report, with MOR-reportable flag, CHIRP option, investigation (root cause, contributing factors taxonomy, MEDA-style), corrective action with effectiveness check, risk assessment, workforce feedback, trend dashboards, just-culture classification (inadvertent lapse vs the seven punitive-trigger criteria), confidentiality controls (restrict reporter identity). MOR cannot be closed only by "under MEMS investigation".
- Audit evidence pack for surveyors (policy, procedures, trigger list, investigations, training, actions, feedback).

**Import / C of A issue**
- Import workflow: if MTOW above 15 000 kg require E3 (or TC holder) conformity report; below, approved maintenance organisation or licensed engineer (subject to CAA agreement). For Part 21 types, Airworthiness Review (M.A.710 / now ML.A.903 or M.A.903, guess) instead.
- New-aircraft acceptance checklist from B-140 (FAA Export C of A, compliance letters, log books, AFM, W&B, ELA, compass certificate, radio list, software criticality list, FDR conversion data, de-registration confirmation).

**Insurance**
- Owner checklist item: insurance endorsed to cover CAA test pilot flights.
