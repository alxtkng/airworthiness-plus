# CAP 562 Notes, Chunk 02 (PDF pages 114 to 223)

Covers the end of Chapter B (Leaflets B-170 to B-250, including the large B-180 "Experience from Incidents" with about 60 appendices) and the start of Chapter C (Leaflets C-20, C-30, C-40). Most pages are dated 15 April 2011, with some revised 30 Nov 2011, 31 Oct 2012, 28 Feb 2017 and 23 Jan 2020 (B-180 App 1-2). Much of the text describes the pre-Brexit EASA arrangement ("EASA aircraft" / "non-EASA aircraft" / "Annex II") and needs mapping to today's UK terms (Part 21 / non-Part 21 aircraft, UK Reg 1321/2014).

## 2. BCAR and National Regime

### Non-EASA / Annex II / national aircraft
- **B-220 para 1.2** defines the national regime: aircraft "designated as falling under Annex II to Regulation EC 216/2008 ... known as non-EASA aircraft", under CAA regulatory control and "issued with **National Certificates of Airworthiness and Permits to Fly**". The leaflet does not apply to EASA type-certificated aircraft, which are covered by Part 145 and Part M. Today's equivalent is "non-Part 21 aircraft" under the UK Basic Regulation.
- **C-20 App 1 note**: non-EASA aircraft are those (a) listed in Annex II of EC 216/2008, (b) excluded by Article 1(2) of EC 216/2008 (State aircraft: military, customs, police and similar), or (c) excluded at the time by Article 2(3)(c) of EC 1702/2003.
- **C-20 2.8.2.1**: non-EASA aircraft are defined in EC 216/2008 Articles 1(2) and 4(4). The current list is in **CAP 747 Mandatory Requirements for Airworthiness, Section 1, Part 2**.
- **C-20 2.4.6**: licensing for non-EASA (Annex II) aircraft is under **BCAR Section L**.
- **B-240 1.4**: the national power to mandate Flight Manual statements and placards no longer applies to EASA aircraft. It remains for non-EASA Annex II aircraft. This is an example of a requirement that applies only to national aircraft.
- **B-180 App 4-5 para 5 and table**: for non-EASA ("Annex II") aircraft, record keeping follows the ANO, not Part-M.

### Special Category C of A (B-230)
- Concerns aircraft of **2730 kg MTOM or less with a National C of A in the Special Category**.
- **ANO 2009 Art 19(a)**: a C of A "shall cease to be in force" if the aircraft, or equipment needed for its airworthiness, is overhauled, repaired or modified, or a part is removed or replaced, "otherwise than in a manner and with material of a type approved by the Authority either generally or in relation to a class of aircraft or to the particular aircraft".
- **ANO Art 29** exempts some Special Category aircraft from needing a CRS, but **Art 19 applies to all aircraft without exception**. The person doing the work needs adequate knowledge, facilities and manuals, and must use parts and materials known to meet CAA requirements. If in doubt, ask a CAA approved organisation or a LAME.
- Flying an aircraft whose C of A has lapsed under Art 19 can lead to prosecution under **ANO Art 16**.

### Permit to Fly aircraft
- **B-180 App 5-1 Aged Components – Permit To Fly Aircraft**: aircraft not maintained to an approved Maintenance Schedule and used little (examples: Hercules and Gipsy Queen engines) need periodic inspection of components that degrade with age. Use the original servicing schedules where available, and add calendar-based inspections.
- **C-20 2.6.1(c)**: a CAA approval can issue a **Flight Release Certificate for an EASA Permit to Fly**, and **Certificates of Fitness for Flight** under **BCAR A/B 3-8 ('A' Conditions)**.
- **B-180 App 25-5**: lists the **Popular Flying Association** (now the LAA) and the **British Microlight Aircraft Association** alongside owners, LAMEs and approved organisations as bodies that do routine inspection.

### Ex-military aircraft
- **B-250**: many de Havilland Chipmunks came onto the civil register from the RAF. Civil clearance for aerobatics depends on de Havilland **Mod H 231** (anti-spin strakes) and placard **Mod H324**. Aircraft without the strakes need placard **Mod H323** ("AEROBATICS AND SPINS PROHIBITED"). The long chord rudder is **Mod H 104**. This shows type-specific conditions applied to ex-military aircraft.
- **B-180 App 32-2**: a Yak was found with a US tyre of a different size and ply rating from the parts catalogue. Any change to the original specification "must be supported by an approved modification".

### BCAR approvals, chapters and references (exact)
- **BCAR Sub-section A8**, national maintenance approvals (C-20 2.5):
  - 2.5.1: organisations maintaining only aircraft of **2730 kg MTOM or less, not used for CAT**, may keep, gain or vary approval under BCAR A8. This is a CAA "**National**" approval.
  - 2.5.2: organisations maintaining aircraft **over 2730 kg, not used for CAT**, fall under the "appropriate **BCAR Maintenance Approval Group Requirements** under BCAR Sub-section A8".
  - 2.5.3: component maintenance with no intended CAT use can also be approved under A8.
  - 2.5.4: the CAA still accepts variations to existing A8 approvals where Part-145 is not needed.
- **A8-1**: "Group A1 Company" under BCAR Chapter A8-1. Named as an acceptable supplier of lithium batteries as Controlled Items (B-180 App 24-6 2.2). The alternative is EASA Part 21 Subpart G production approval.
- **A8-3**: an approval route for organisations reporting on **C of A renewal** assessments and recommendations (C-20 2.6.1(e)).
- **A8-15**: approval under which the Maintenance Organisation Exposition defines the aircraft for which the organisation can make **C of A renewal** reports (C-20 2.6.1(e)).
- **A8-18**: maintenance of **Airships** and its supplements (C-20 2.4.2). Certification authorisations are based on **BCAR Section L Category 'A and C' airship LWTRs** until Part-66 covers airships. Type training normally comes from a Part-147 organisation.
- **Cancelled from 28 Sep 2005** (C-20 App 1 para 1.1): **A8-1, A8-2, A8-4, A8-5, A8-6, A8-7, A8-9, A8-16 and A8-19**, unless the organisation designs or manufactures non-EASA aircraft. The text says **A8-5 and A8-6** "have a considerable impact on aircraft maintenance and continuing airworthiness". This chunk does not say what they cover; from general knowledge they are probably specialist processes and NDT/test houses, which is a guess to check against the BCAR A8 list in another chunk. Their work now goes through subcontracting (App 1 paras 2 and 3).
- **BCAR Chapter A3-3**: approval of a **Controlled Item**, also called "**Accessory Approval**". A single cell fitted as an LRU counts as a battery for this purpose. Hard-wired button cells are approved as part of the equipment (B-180 App 24-6, paras 2.2 and 3).
- **BCAR Chapter A/B4-8**: approval procedures for cable manufacturers, who can provide a **Declaration of Design and Performance (DDP)** (B-180 App 24-3 3.1(e)).
- **BCAR A/B 3-4**: **Star Inspections** (C-20 2.6.1(d)).
- **BCAR A/B 3-8**: **Certificates of Fitness for Flight** ('A' Conditions) (C-20 2.6.1(c)).
- **BCAR A/B 5-3**: amending Maintenance, Overhaul and Repair Manuals and Wiring Diagrams (C-20 2.6.1(f)).
- **BCAR Section A6-2**: releasing components to approved data (B-220 2.6, together with Leaflet H-20).
- **BCAR Section L**: licensing for non-EASA aircraft, and airship LWTRs.
- **BCAR Equipment Approval Number AR 515** (L-3 F-800 DFDR): declared **obsolescent**. It may not be newly installed to meet a mandatory carriage requirement. Existing installations may continue to be used, serviced and repaired (B-180 App 4-4).
- **BCAR CO limit** (B-190 1.2): CO in occupied compartments must not exceed **50 parts per million by volume for any period over five minutes**. Other limits exist for fuel, oil, de-icing and hydraulic fluids, extinguishants and fumes.
- **BCAR 4.4 g Semi-Aerobatic Load Factor** (B-240 1.1).

### Functions only a CAA (national) approval covered (C-20 2.6.1)
These are the functions "not as yet addressed by EASA Requirements":
a) issue **Certificates of Maintenance Review** under **ANO 2009 Art 26**;
b) issue **Certificates of Release to Service** under **ANO Art 28** (for work on aircraft and components NOT used for CAT);
c) issue **Certificates of Fitness for Flight** (BCAR A/B 3-8, 'A' Conditions) and a **Flight Release Certificate for an EASA Permit to Fly**;
d) perform **Star Inspections** (BCAR A/B 3-4);
e) provide reports to the CAA under **ANO Art 244**, namely assessments and recommendations for **C of A renewal**, as defined in the MOE or approved under **BCAR A8-15** or **A8-3**;
f) amend Maintenance, Overhaul and Repair Manuals and Wiring Diagrams (BCAR A/B 5-3);
g) **control of Welders Approvals**.

### How the national regime differs from the Part 21 / Part-M track (as described in 2011)
- Part-145 is required for CAT aircraft and for all large aircraft (aeroplane over 5700 kg MTOM, or any multi-engine helicopter) regardless of use. Part-145 applied to CAT from **29 Nov 2003** and to all large aircraft from **28 Sep 2008**.
- Part-M Subpart F covers aircraft of 5700 kg MTOM or less and single-engine helicopters not used for CAT.
- Non-EASA aircraft components are released by **BCAR A8** organisations holding the right rating (C-20 2.8.2.1). EASA aircraft components are released by Part-145 or Part-M Subpart F (2.8.1.1).
- Licensing: Part-66 for EASA aircraft, BCAR Section L for non-EASA aircraft. Sailplanes and balloons follow Leaflet H-40 until Part-66 provides for them.

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### Dangerous goods (B-180 App 1-1 and 1-2)
- Hold-floor screw protrusion of **0.25 mm to 0.5 mm** is enough to damage DG drums. Drums can be up to **60 litres** on passenger aircraft. Screws must not stand above the floor surface.
- DG training: recurrent training "within **24 months** of previous training in addition to the remainder of the month of completion". If recurrent training is completed in the **final three months** of validity, the new validity runs from the month of completion to 24 months from the **expiry month** of the previous training.
- Training must be followed by a test, and completion must be confirmed. The employer keeps training records for at least **36 months from the most recent training completion month**, available to the employee or the authority on request.
- Persons preparing DG consignments: detailed training from a **CAA Approved Dangerous Goods Training Organisation**, or in-house training meeting **CAP 483** at minimum.
- Procurement staff, stores staff (other than shippers) and line engineers: basic DG training and testing (instructor, CBT or self-study).
- Unserviceable tyres inflated to **200 kPa or more at 20°C (2 bar / 29 psi)** are DG.
- Lithium batteries being shipped need evidence of **UN Manual of Tests and Criteria Part III subsection 38.3**.
- Chemical oxygen generators (**UN3356**) are forbidden on passenger aircraft. The cargo-aircraft package must stop other generators actuating, must not ignite, and its outside surface must stay at **100°C or below** when one generator actuates.
- The carrier's acceptance check is required even for AOG shipments.
- Engineers' toolboxes must not carry DG spares or consumables by air unless the DG are properly prepared, declared and accepted.
- Explosives must have a classification from an ADR Competent Authority (in the UK, the **HSE**) and be packed exactly as the classification document specifies.
- DG handling must be within the scope of the compliance monitoring programme.
- SDS Section 14 gives the UN Number, Proper Shipping Name, Class/Division and Packing Group.

### Maintenance intervals and limits (B-180)
- **Electrical power checks, light aircraft (App 24-1 3.5)**: about every **50 flying hours or six months, whichever is sooner**. Indicators and warnings checked **daily or at pre-flight**.
- **Circuit breakers (App 24-4)**: CBs not regularly operated get **two manual switching cycles at periods of not more than two years**. CBs not supplied from an approved source, or **stored for two years or more**, must be checked before fitting. Any CB trip in operation needs a Tech Log entry and an investigation. In flight, reset only for essential services with no smoke or fumes, and **never a second reset**. Cabin crew must not reset domestic CBs.
- **Escape slides (App 25-6)**: every slide deployed when due for overhaul at the manufacturer's interval, **not exceeding 36 months**. Alternatively, a CAA-agreed sampling programme of **at least 10 or 10% (whichever is greater)** of fleet exits within **not more than two years**, spread evenly across exits. Inadvertent deployments do not count but must be investigated if the slide fails. Failure criteria include inflation taking more than **10 seconds** (from first door actuation to a usable slide). Videos of failed deployments kept at least **one year** or until the MOR or investigation closes. Failures go to the MOR scheme and to the TC holder.
- **Mode S address (App 34-2)**: confirmed and recorded with a field test set at intervals **not exceeding 2 years**, as an AMP/schedule task. Periodic Mode S EHS/ELS testing is also expected (no more than 2 years).
- **Strobe light bonding (App 33-1)**: no more than **0.05 ohms** between housing and airframe, achieved **no later than the next scheduled airframe maintenance inspection**.
- **Tyres (App 32-2)**: pressures checked accurately **at least daily**. A visual check is "totally inadequate". High-speed tyres are those marked above **160 mph**.
- **Wire (App 24-7)**: temperature rise from electrical heating ideally no more than **40°C above ambient**.
- **H-11 steel (App 51-2)**: heat-treatable to above **1400 MPa (200,000 lbf/in²)**. Avoid it in tension, single load paths, phosphate-ester fluid above **120°C (250°F)**, exhaust exposure and weathering.
- **Chloride materials (App 70-3)**: break down above **150°C**.
- **Ice falls (App 12-4)**: more than **200** UK reports in 1997 to 2002. An engine separated at **35,000 ft**.
- **LAMS (App 11-1, 56-1)**: placards inspected in **Section 7** at Check A, 50 h, 150 h and Annual. Windscreens at Check A. All windows at 50 h, 150 h and Annual.
- **Seat fittings (App 25-1)**: claw-and-mushroom fittings kept in service must have at least **four groups of holding claws plus two interconnected locking devices**.

### CO and heaters (B-190)
- CO no more than **50 ppm by volume for more than 5 minutes** (BCAR).
- Combustion heater overhaul normally at intervals **not exceeding two years**. Some combustion chambers need a **pressure test at half the overhaul life**.
- Heater jacket pressure test must not exceed the Maintenance Manual's maximum test pressure.
- CO tests are done with the engine(s) running and the heater on, **on the ground and in flight**.

### Chipmunk (B-250)
- Spin entry at **50 kt**. In a spin, airspeed holds steady at **30 to 50 kt IAS**. A spiral dive goes from 40 to 80-90 kt.
- Recovery normally takes **1 to 2 turns, about 1,000 ft**, and exceptionally **up to 4.5 turns, about 2,000 ft**.
- Recovery must start **at least 3,500 ft AGL** so the aircraft is level by **1,500 ft**.
- Anti-spin strakes are about **3 feet** long.

### Aerobatics (B-240)
- Semi-aerobatic means a limit load of less than **6 g**, usually the BCAR **4.4 g**.

### Organisation approvals (C-20, C-30, C-40)
- Large aircraft: aeroplane over **5700 kg MTOM**, or a multi-engine helicopter.
- National approval threshold: **2730 kg MTOM**.
- Alignment check when moving an aircraft from non-CAT to CAT: for aircraft of **2730 kg or less**, at minimum a **100 h check (helicopters) or 150 h check (aeroplanes)** to LAMP or an approved alternative schedule. Over 2730 kg, the check content must be agreed with the CAA. The check ends with a **145.A.50 CRS**.
- A person placing work with another organisation must confirm that organisation's approval is valid (www.caa.co.uk/UKAO).
- Approval changes (C-30): tell the CAA **before** a name change or business transfer happens. On a sale or transfer of business, company A's approvals become "**null and void at that time**". No further design or release certifications may be made. Company B must apply for a new approval with a new number.
- Charges: **Official Record Series 5** (ORS5) and the CAA Scheme of Charges.

### Accident-recovered parts (B-220)
- Without clear evidence that an item is unaffected by the accident, it **may not be returned to service**.
- Distortion that cannot be assessed because the original dimensions are unknown means the item is rejected. Any sign of overheating needs a laboratory investigation.
- UK licensed engineers and CAA approved organisations cannot assess impact damage or fatigue without the manufacturer or a CAA Approved Design Organisation, unless approved data gives specific inspections.

## 5. Practice Guidance (for planning and maintenance features)

### Human factors and work control
- Prepare non-scheduled task documentation before work starts. Break it into steps and certify each step or group of steps. Hand over fully at shift change (App 5-2).
- Use open-item tracking for anything that makes the aircraft unairworthy while fitted: pitot/static covers, rigging pins, gear pins, intake blanks, control locks (App 10-1, 27-3). Flags alone are not enough, especially at night or in bad weather.
- Rigging pins: tool-store control (shadow boards), serviceability check including the flag, only flagged pins used, full-and-free check after rigging.
- Loose-article and FOD control after every job. A duplicate inspection does not catch loose articles (App 51-7, 1-3).
- Precision cutting tools need issue control (App 51-5).
- Report ambiguous data to the author (App 4-1).
- A licence does not allow departure from instructions or procedures (App 4-3).

### Dormant-fault and functional checks to schedule
- Brakes and anti-skid: each sensor controls its own wheel, and the whole system works. Check periodically and after any disturbance (App 32-1).
- Circuit breakers: exercise every 2 years or less, and gather reliability data, not only pass/fail (App 24-4).
- Oxygen drop-out systems: functional integrity, removal of inhibit devices, portable bottles and masks (App 35-2). Leak check after any disturbance (App 35-1).
- Escape slide deployment programme (App 25-6). Duplicate or independent inspection of critical slide tasks: slide installation, firing mechanism connections, girt bar, door assist deactivation and safety pin removal.
- Autopilot servos maintained as part of the flying controls. Pilot checks the slip clutch pre-flight (App 22-1).
- Flap limit microswitches. If one is faulty, check for static overload (App 27-2).
- Mode S address verification at 2 years or less (App 34-2).
- Strobe bonding no more than 0.05 ohm (App 33-1).

### Light aircraft / GA items
- Electrical system checks every 50 h or 6 months (App 24-1). An emergency battery modification is suggested.
- Battery installation checklist: correct model, terminal and stud not loose or deformed, clean lug, lug fits, wing nut tight enough that the lug cannot be moved by hand but not over-tightened (App 24-5).
- Air intake filters: the downstream face must be inspected, which usually means removal. Applies to 50 h checks on Private Category LAMS aircraft (App 72-1).
- Seat belt stitched joints should face away from the body (App 25-5).
- Altimeter baro knob pre-flight check (App 31-1). VSI limit stops on imported aircraft (App 31-2).
- Control cable end fittings: stress corrosion pitting on aircraft 20 years old or more. Inspect when access allows (App 27-4).
- Aged components on low-use Permit aircraft (App 5-1).

### Materials, processes and parts
- Soft metal shims are not allowed where preload matters (App 20-1).
- Crowded ball races need shaped washers (App 20-2).
- No unauthorised alteration. Do not defeat wrong-assembly prevention features (App 20-3).
- Pipes and looms: routing, chafing, attachment, corrosion under P-clips and B-nuts, especially in wheel bays, wing trailing edges and undercarriage (App 20-4).
- Arc burns: blue oxide, pitting or remelting. Without specific instructions, ask the manufacturer or reject the part. Inspect anything near faulty electrical equipment (App 20-5).
- Hydraulic fluid: avoid chlorinated solvents. Test acidity and water content periodically (App 20-6).
- Cable marking: HV test to **BS 3G 230:2000 Test 16** after hot-stamp printing (sample whenever a machine setting changes). Wet arc tracking evaluated by **Test 42**. Clean and dry looms after fluid contamination (App 24-3).
- Lithium batteries: **BS 2G 239** (formerly G239), TSO-C97 / ETSO-C97. Do not install near occupants if venting is foreseeable. Dispose of per BS 2G 239. HSE Guidance Note **GS43** (App 24-6).
- Single-wall ETFE wire (SAE-AS/MIL-W-22759/16-19): check heat sources, compare with the existing wiring, protect from abrasion, check bundle compatibility, meet fire tests (CS 25.869(a)(4), CS 23.1359(c), CS 27.1365(c), CS 29.1359(c)), and set inspection intervals (App 24-7). Abrasion reference **EN3475-503**.
- H-11 fasteners: replace by fleet campaign after failures. Correct torque (App 51-2).
- Corrosion-inhibiting compounds: justify non-OEM products and check viscosity. Reprotect at the next opportunity if non-conforming material was used (App 51-3).
- Lock-bolts: check NAS 1468 collar security when the opportunity arises (App 51-4).
- Self-locking nuts: re-use only if the friction element still works, following the manufacturer (App 51-6).
- Chloride wrappings (Neoprene, PVC, glass-fibre tape) are not to be used on stainless or titanium (App 70-3).
- Fluids: marked filling points and containers, re-labelled containers, trusted sources, on-board stowage checks (App 12-1).
- Toilet and water servicing points: clean the staining, include in audits, investigate loss of flush charge, check drain mast heaters (App 12-4).
- Tyres: daily pressure checks, follow removal criteria, a specification change needs an approved modification. See Leaflet 32-10 (App 32-2).
- Radio nav: course and alarm current settings adjusted only in a workshop. Label units and mountings with the load they are set for (App 34-1).
- TCAS/transponder ground test precautions: OFF or standby when not needed, Mode A/C with altitude reporting ON, test in a hangar, absorption covers, radiate into the test set through an attenuator for altitude tests, standby between tests. Radiating simulated TCAS from a workshop antenna is not permitted (App 34-3).

### Carbon monoxide (B-190)
- Inspect the exhaust for security, warping, dents, cracks and leak traces at clips, slip joints, clamps, expansion joints and heater jackets.
- Heater jacket: remove it, or pressure-test with the outlet blanked and a non-return valve. No leakage is allowed.
- Inspect bulkheads, access-panel gaskets, lap-joint sealant, window and door seals, and wheel well and flap shroud sealing on twins.
- CO test: bellows and sampling tube (iodine pentoxide, selenium dioxide, fuming sulphuric acid). Green-brown staining, read in ppm. Test with the engine running and the heater on, on the ground and in flight.
- Repairs: prefer renewal. Fit new gaskets. Weld to the Maintenance Manual and keep the original contour. Use compatible material. Pre-heat or heat-treat where specified. Pressure test after welding. Heater ducting is generally not repairable.

### Electronic data (B-200)
- **Primary Source** means data issued by the TC holder, design organisation or airworthiness authority, in any format. **Secondary Source** means data from a third party.
- The CAA cares about the source, not the format.
- Secondary source data must be as accurate as the primary source and revised as often. The supplier must be legally entitled to supply it. Check for UK-specific limitations. Go back to the primary source when in doubt.
- Controls: only current versions accessible, with an archive if superseded data is needed. Data available close to the work. Printouts automatically annotated with the source and edition date. Enough terminals, and a fallback if the system fails. No unauthorised alteration. Support contract for hardware and software. User training and user instructions at every terminal. Revisions frequent enough to keep mandatory requirements current. Confirmation of a contract between the publisher and the manufacturer.
- References: BSI PD0008, FAA AC 120-78, ATA Spec 2100.

### Scrap and suspect parts (B-210, B-220)
- Scrap categories: (a) non-repairable defects; (b) outside the approved design and cannot be brought back; (c) cannot be made eligible for certification; (d) irreversible unacceptable modification or rework; (e) life-limited parts at or past their limit, or with missing or incomplete records; (f) exposed to extreme forces or heat; (g) PSEs from high-cycle aircraft that cannot meet ageing-aircraft mandatory requirements.
- Effective mutilation: grinding, burning, removing a major lug, permanent distortion, cutting a hole by torch or saw, melting, sawing into many pieces. Less effective: stamping an 'R', paint, hammer marks, tags, small holes, sawing in two.
- Non-flight use (training, R&D): permanently mark "NOT SERVICEABLE" (ink stamp is not acceptable), remove P/N and data plate, track by serial number, include written disposition instructions in the contract.
- Quarantine store for scrap and expired life-limited parts.
- Manufacturers should consider recording serial numbers of retired life-limited parts. Owners are encouraged to return the data plate or serial number and the disposition to the manufacturer.
- Receiving-inspection warning signs: list (a) to (k), for example reworked "new" parts, rework near the data plate, missing or photocopied Form 1, suspicious TSO/PMA marks, prices too good to be true, intact scrap sold by weight. Report suspect parts per Leaflet B-130 para 6.
- Salvage: competent assessment covering accident circumstances, storage and transport, and history from valid records. Check for cracks, distortion and overheating. Consult the manufacturer if manuals are not enough. Refer to the CAA Airworthiness Evaluation and Surveillance Department with full accident details if unclear.

### Organisation subcontracting (C-20 App 1, C-40)
- A non-approved subcontractor (plating, heat treatment, plasma spray, fire testing and similar) works under the approved organisation's quality system (145.A.75(b)) or as a specialised service (M.A.615(b)).
- Procedure: pre-audit for valid national accreditation (UKAS). List the subcontractor and its accreditation status in the MOE or MOM. Describe the process controls and release format. Keep the accreditation valid. Audit the subcontract control and sample the subcontractors. Release always under the approved organisation's reference, by authorised staff (who may be subcontractor staff meeting the certifying standard). The contract gives the competent authority right of access.
- UKAS test houses: no CAA application. The test house sends a Chief Executive statement and its current UKAS schedule. Recognition continues while UKAS accreditation for aerospace requirements continues. The CAA may visit and may witness tests.

## 6. Software Implications

### Aircraft and regulatory classification
- `Aircraft.regulatory_basis`: Part 21 / non-Part 21 (was EASA / non-EASA / Annex II), with the reason (Annex I listing, State aircraft exclusion, etc.). Link to the CAP 747 Section 1 Part 2 list.
- `Aircraft.airworthiness_document`: National C of A (with category, e.g. **Special Category**), CAA Permit to Fly, LAA/BMAA Permit, Part 21 C of A, EASA-era Permit to Fly. Include MTOM thresholds of **2730 kg** and **5700 kg** and multi-engine helicopter.
- `Aircraft.use`: CAT or non-CAT. Moving from non-CAT to CAT starts an **alignment check workflow** (100 h helicopter or 150 h aeroplane to LAMP for 2730 kg or less; CAA-agreed content above that) ending in a 145.A.50 CRS. Components released under BCAR A8 or Subpart F must be assessed by a Part-145 organisation.
- **Art 19 warning** for Special Category and national C of A aircraft: if a repair, mod or replacement is recorded without approved data or approved parts, flag "C of A may have ceased to be in force" and block release.
- Ex-military and type-specific condition flags, for example Chipmunk Mod H 231 fitted, which controls whether aerobatics are permitted and which placard (H323 or H324) is required. Semi-aerobatic placard required for non-Part 21 aircraft.

### Organisation approvals
- `Approval` object: type (Part-145 with ratings A/B/C/D, Part-M Subpart F (now Part-CAO), BCAR A8-xx national, A8-15, A8-3, A8-18 airships, A8-1 Group A1), unique reference number, **legal entity / company registration number**, status (active / varied / void).
- Approval is not transferable (C-30). A business transfer sets status to void immediately and blocks certification under that reference. A name change with the same company number keeps the approval but triggers a reissued Certificate of Approval. Store the Certificate of Incorporation.
- **Privileges list** for national approvals: CMR (Art 26), CRS (Art 28), Certificate of Fitness for Flight (A/B 3-8), Flight Release Certificate, Star Inspection (A/B 3-4), C of A renewal reports (Art 244), manual amendment (A/B 5-3), welder approvals control.
- Supplier approval validity check before placing work (UKAO list). Recognised foreign approvals.
- Subcontractor register: process type, accreditation body (UKAS), accreditation reference and schedule, expiry or validity, listed in the MOE/MOM (yes/no), last audit date. Alert when accreditation lapses.
- Cancelled approval types reference list (A8-1, -2, -4, -5, -6, -7, -9, -16, -19 cancelled 28 Sep 2005 except for non-EASA design and manufacture).

### Certifying staff and licensing
- Licence types: Part-66 (with restricted licences and NAA-specific limitations) and BCAR Section L (non-Part 21 aircraft, airship LWTR Categories A and C). Sailplanes and balloons per H-40.
- Component certifying staff authorisations kept separate from aircraft authorisations.
- Part-66 independent certifier scope for ELA1: may maintain components to the CMM, but may not overhaul components. May overhaul engines and propellers on CS-VLA, CS-22 and LSA aircraft.

### Parts and components
- `Part.release_document`: UK CAA Form 1 (EASA Form 1), FAA 8130-3, TCCA 24-0078, dual release, manufacturer's new-part certificate. Rule: a Subpart F/CAO organisation may convert a used-part 8130-3 or 24-0078 to a Form 1 after checks.
- Receiving inspection checklist driven by the B-210 warning signs, with a "suspect unapproved part" status and a report to the CAA (B-130).
- `Part.status`: serviceable / unserviceable / quarantine / scrap / mutilated / non-flight use (marked NOT SERVICEABLE). Scrap reason codes (a)-(g). Mutilation method. Serial-number tracking of transfers. Optional notice to the OEM of retired LLP serials.
- Salvaged / accident-recovered flag: requires evidence of no damage, history from records, manufacturer or design organisation assessment, and links to the accident details. Default is "may not be returned to service".
- **Dangerous goods flag** on part and consumable master records (App 1-2 5.1.2), with UN number, Proper Shipping Name, Class, Packing Group (from SDS Section 14), and UN38.3 evidence for lithium batteries. Tyre DG rule (unserviceable and 200 kPa or more).
- Equipment approval status, for example BCAR AR 515 **obsolescent**: block new installation, allow continued use and repair.
- Circuit breaker shelf-life rule: from an unapproved source or stored 2 years or more means test before fitting.
- Lithium battery approval: BCAR A3-3 Controlled Item plus a DDP to BS 2G 239.

### Records retention engine
- Retention rules by entity and record type (table in section 3). ANO aircraft: log books and tech log kept until 2 years after the aircraft is destroyed or permanently withdrawn. Part-145 work records: 2 years from release. Subpart F: 3 years. Tech Log under M.A.306: 3 years from last entry.
- Cross-referenced work packs inherit log book retention.
- Component log cards travel with the component.
- DG training records: 36 months.
- Failed slide deployment videos: 1 year or until the MOR closes.

### Tech Log and open items
- "Open entry" item types: blanking cover (which probe), rigging pin, gear pin, intake blank, control lock. These keep the aircraft **not airworthy** until certified closed. Block release-to-service and flight while any are open.
- Required Tech Log entry for any CB trip, followed by an investigation task.
- INOP placard plus MEL reference for toilets.

### Maintenance programme / task library (recurring tasks)
- Electrical system check: 50 FH / 6 months, whichever first (light aircraft).
- CB exercise: 2 cycles every 2 years or less.
- Mode S address verification: 2 years or less, recorded per transponder.
- Escape slide deployment: at overhaul (36 months or less), or fleet sampling (10 or 10%, whichever greater, in 2 years), with records of position, P/N, date of manufacture, result, failure mode and cause, and fleet summary reports.
- Combustion heater overhaul: 2 years or less, with chamber pressure test at half life.
- Strobe bond check: 0.05 ohm or less, by the next scheduled inspection.
- Tyre pressure: daily.
- Hydraulic fluid acidity and water: periodic.
- LAMS Section 7 placard and windscreen and window items at Check A / 50 h / 150 h / Annual.
- Calendar-based aged-component inspections for Permit aircraft with low utilisation.
- Pre-flight items (owner or pilot): altimeter knob, autopilot clutch slip, electrical warnings.

### Defects and occurrences
- MOR triggers: disruptive cable failure, broken or detached battery terminals, slide deployment failures. Keep a physical evidence flag.
- Data ambiguity report log, open until the author resolves it.

### Data library (B-200)
- Each document records its source type (Primary / Secondary), publisher, legal entitlement or contract with the OEM, revision and edition date, and status (current / superseded / archived).
- Printouts are stamped with the source and edition date. Edits by users are prevented. Revision checks against mandatory requirements.

### Repair data acceptance (C-20 App 2)
- `RepairData`: state of design (US / Canada / Brazil / other), classification (minor / major), source type (DOA, DAS, SFAR 36, DER, TC holder, US operator, Part 21 DOA), approval form (8110-3, 8100-9, 337, Canadian Repair Design Certificate), basis of acceptance (pre-28 Sep 2003 acceptance, Part-21 Subpart M, Decision). Critical-part flag.

### Dangerous goods training
- Employee DG training record with expiry calculation: 24 months plus the rest of the month, with the early-renewal rule (completed in the final 3 months keeps the expiry-month anchor). Role level (detailed for shippers, basic for stores, procurement and line). Test passed (yes/no).

- BCAR A8-1, -2, -4, -5, -6, -7, -9, -16 and -19 approvals were cancelled from 28 Sep 2005, except for non-EASA design and manufacture.
- LTO 461 was revised to LTO 461/A on 18 Dec 1981.
- C-20 2.8.1.4 / 2.4.x notes say EASA intended to add component certifying staff standards and airship, sailplane and balloon provisions to Part-66 in future. These may since have happened (Part-66 L licence, B2L and so on).

Likely changed since UK exit from EASA on 1 Jan 2021 (my guesses, to verify):
- "EASA aircraft / non-EASA aircraft / Annex II to EC 216/2008" is now **Part 21 / non-Part 21 aircraft** under the UK Basic Regulation (Annex I). The CAP 747 list reference may have moved.
- "EASA Form 1" is now **UK CAA Form 1**. "EASA Permit to Fly" is now the UK equivalent.
- "Part-145 approvals by EASA or other EU Member State competent authorities are recognised in the UK without further evaluation" (C-20 2.2.2) and "applications from outside the EU go directly to EASA" (2.11.1) almost certainly no longer apply. The UK now has its own foreign Part-145 approvals, and EU approvals are not automatically recognised.
- Part-M Subpart F (C-20, B-180 App 4-5) is replaced for most GA by **Part-CAO**. Part-ML replaced Part-M for light aircraft. ELA1 and Part-66 independent certifier rules now sit under Part-ML / M.A.801.
- EASA repair-data Decisions (2004/02/CF to 2004/04/CF, ED 2007/001/C) and the FAA BASA arrangements are EU-side. The UK CAA has its own acceptance arrangements and UK-US / UK-Canada bilaterals. This needs checking.
- "EASA standardisation team" right of access to subcontractors (C-20 App 1 2.7) no longer applies to the UK.
- References to EC 2042/2003 are now UK Reg 1321/2014. EC 1702/2003 is now UK Reg 748/2012. EU-OPS and JAR-OPS 3 are now UK Air Ops.
- ANO 2009 article numbers (16, 19, 26, 27, 28, 29, 34, 244) are now **ANO 2016** articles with different numbers, for example C of A validity, CRS and log book articles. The ANO 2016 equivalents should be mapped.
- The Part-M Subpart F record retention of 3 years is now under Part-CAO. M.A.305/306 periods may differ under the current Part-M / Part-ML.
- Organisation names and contacts (Safety Regulation Group, Aviation House Gatwick, aanda@caa.co.uk, www.caa.co.uk/UKAO, ORS5, DfT VCA URL, Directorate of Airspace Policy) are probably out of date.
- The PFA is now the LAA. The DTI is now Ofcom for radio licensing.
- REACH and CLP are described as EU regulations. The UK now has UK REACH and GB CLP.
- The description of BCAR A8 national approvals for 2730 kg or less non-CAT (C-20 2.5) predates the 2019-2021 changes. Some A8 approvals (for example A8-15 / A8-20 / A8-23 / A8-25) may have been restructured. Check against BCAR Section A Issue 8 elsewhere in the full CAP 562 text.
