## 2. BCAR and National Regime

### Terminology for aircraft categories used in this chunk
- **"Annex II aircraft"** (51-180, 61-10): aircraft outside EASA (Annex II to Regulation (EC) 216/2008), i.e. today's "non-Part 21" aircraft. Modifications to manufacturer approval data on Annex II aircraft are assessed by the CAA; on EASA aircraft by EASA (51-180).
- **"non-EASA aircraft" as defined in CAP 747 Mandatory Requirements for Airworthiness** (70-20 para 1.5). 61-10 also refers to "aircraft designated as EASA aircraft in CAP 747". So CAP 747 is the reference list for which regime an aircraft type falls under.
- **"EASA aircraft types issued with a UK national Certificate of Airworthiness and operating for State purposes, such as the Police"** (70-90 para 3.2): State-use aircraft of EASA types can hold a national C of A. Important for the app: aircraft type may be EASA/Part 21 but the individual aircraft be in the national regime because of State use.
- **Ex-military / ex-UK Government** engines and propellers (70-30, 70-40, 70-20 para 6.2(b)): military powerplants similar to a civil equivalent must be modified to civil requirements with the manufacturer unless CAA agrees otherwise. 70-30/70-40 notes: the leaflets apply to UK Government engines "whether obtained directly from the UK Government or from another source".
- **Special Category** (61-10 Note): some propellers listed are approved only for aircraft certificated in the Special Category; this does not qualify them for other Categories.
- **Public Transport Category** (70-40 Note): if the ex-Government engines have been operated since being obtained and are to be registered for use in Public Transport Category, 70-40 does not apply.
- Certificates of Airworthiness issued by the CAA for UK-manufactured aircraft (61-10 para 1); CAA Type Certificate Data Sheet (TCDS) to be consulted where current; EASA TCDS for EASA aircraft.

### BCAR references in this chunk
- **BCAR Chapter A4-4** (61-10 para 6): procedure to apply for approval of an unapproved propeller for Annex II aircraft (non-Annex II aircraft apply to EASA). A4 is the BCAR Section A chapter on design approvals / type approval of engines and propellers (my interpretation from context).
- **BCAR Chapter D3-7** and **ACJ 25.775(d)**: CAA requirements for tests on pressure panels (windscreens) (56-10 para 1).
- **BCAR Chapter D4-2 Appendix 2** (or ACJ 25.775(d)): electrically heated windscreens and impact resistance at low temperature (56-10 para 3.1.2). Section D = aeroplanes (older large-aircraft code).
- "BCAR/JAR/FAR/CS Design and Construction Codes" referenced by 51-180.
- 70-60 para 2.1 refers to "the Requirements" recognising two basic methods of testing piston engines after overhaul (fan and dynamometer); 70-70 para 1.1 says other parts of "the Requirements" relevant to testing piston engines after overhaul are repeated in 70-50 and 70-60 (BCAR engine overhaul requirements, likely Section A/C chapters - guess).
- **Engine Technical Certificate**: the type certificate-style document defining engine performance (power/rpm curves), against which test schedules are based (70-60 para 2, 70-70).
- **Approved Engine Test Schedule**, **Test Fan Calibration Schedule**: approved manufacturer documents required before testing.

### Legal basis quoted (70-20)
- **Article 19 of the Air Navigation Order 2009**: a C of A "shall cease to be in force" if the aircraft is overhauled, repaired or modified otherwise than in a manner and with material of a type approved by the CAA.
- **Article 16(10)(a)** (non-Commercial Air Transport): CRS required when aircraft overhauled, repaired, modified or maintained in a manner and with material approved by the CAA.
- **Articles 28, 29, 30 and 31 of the ANO 2009 (as amended)**: alternative requirements met by following 70-20 (these are the CRS / maintenance articles for public transport aircraft - guess).
- For EASA aircraft: Part M Subpart E, **M.A.501** and AMC/GM govern acceptance of components from non-EASA sources; 70-20 does not apply.

### Approvals, certificates and release documents named
- CAA Type Certificate Data Sheet (TCDS); EASA TCDS.
- Authorised Release Documents acceptable under 70-20 para 4.1: **EASA Form 1**; **JAA Form 1** issued by a JAR-145 Maintenance Organisation listed in "JAR-145 Listed Organisations"; **FAA Form 8130-3** from an EASA-approved FAR-145 Repair Station (listed in EASA Foreign Part-145 organisations located in the US); **Transport Canada Form 24-0078** from an EASA-approved Transport Canada Approved AM573 Maintenance Organisation (listed in EASA Foreign Part-145 organisations located in Canada).
- Certificate of Release to Service (CRS).
- "Part 145 maintenance organisation" or "appropriately type rated CAA Licensed Aircraft Engineer" (70-40); "approved maintenance organisation or appropriately type rated Licensed Aircraft Engineer" (70-20); "appropriately licensed engineer or Approved Inspector" (70-10) – Approved Inspector is a BCAR-era national certifying role.
- CAA approved overhaul organisation (70-30/70-40).
- CAA Surveyor / Regional Office Surveyor (51-180, 70-20 para 6.3).
- Maintenance Management Exposition (MME) (70-20 pool conditions; 100-10 procedures).
- For FLS/DFLD: EASA Form 1, FAA 8130-3, JAA Form One, Certificate of Conformity (not equivalent to Form 1/8130-3), EASA Letter of Acceptance (LOA) Type 1 and Type 2 for navigation databases.
- AOC or EASA Form 14 (CAMO approval certificate) listing significant subcontracted responsibilities (70-90 App 1).
- CAP 747 Generic Requirement (GR) No. 24 (engine TBO extension).

### How the national regime differs (from this chunk)
- Powerplant acceptance for non-EASA aircraft is a CAA-defined procedure (70-20) with prescribed log book statements, in contrast to M.A.501 for EASA aircraft.
- TBO for ex-military engines on national aircraft set or waived by CAA leaflet (70-30, 70-40) with extension via CAP 747 GR No. 24.
- Modifications on Annex II aircraft are CAA-assessed; Regional Office Surveyors are involved.
- Approved propeller combinations for UK-built national aircraft listed in 61-10 (as of 28 Sept 2003 cut-over).

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### 51-180 Aerials
- Installers must ensure adequate structural strength. Where airframe manufacturer's modification schemes are not used, structural substantiation extent to be discussed with the Regional Office Surveyor. Changes to manufacturer's approval data = modification (CAA for Annex II, EASA for EASA aircraft).

### 56-10 Windscreens
- Glass begins to flow under own weight at about 600°C.
- Stress-relieving edge separation (deliberate) penetrates 6 mm to 25 mm (0.25 in to 1 in), yellowish/brownish appearance – not a defect.
- Scratch classes: hairline (visible, hard to feel with fingernail); light (< 0.254 mm / 0.010 in deep, can be felt); heavy (0.254 mm / 0.010 in or more, readily felt, extensive edge chipping).
- Scratches permissible within Maintenance Manual limits if integrity not suspected and visibility not seriously affected; polishing not recommended – return panel to manufacturer and replace.
- Delamination limits and associated flight limits (e.g. pressurised flight) from Maintenance Manual and Flight Manual.
- Shattered toughened glass: remove and replace. Vinyl rupture: panel change. Vinyl bubbling: not cause for replacement unless vision seriously impaired, but heat control system should be rendered inoperative pending rectification. Black or brown discolouration viewed normal to surface: possible defect needing replacement.
- Wipers only operated when windscreen wet.
- Standby compass between panels: non-magnetic bolts nearby; check compass swing with windscreen heat ON and OFF after installation.
- Electrically heated panel: note etched resistance code before installation.
- Sealing compound must cure before final bolt tightening; bolts torqued evenly in staggered sequence.
- Cabin pressurisation and leak rate test after installation if prescribed by Maintenance Manual.
- Storage: both faces covered with adhesive polythene, acid-free paper, cellulose wadding, reinforced cartons; racks away from light at 10°C to 21°C (50°F to 70°F), well ventilated; thicker ply uppermost; polythene not removed until fitted.

### 61-10 Propellers
- Each propeller approved only for the specific engine-airframe combination shown.
- Issue number for minor mod with same pitch and diameter: may be assumed approved. Issue number indicating changed pitch or diameter: not approved unless it appears in list.
- Propellers made by other appropriately approved organisations accepted if made to relevant drawing requirements.
- Before fitting variable-pitch propeller, basic pitch-range setting must conform to latest approved setting for engine-airframe combination.
- Pitch measured at manufacturer's defined radius: usually 70% or 71%, sometimes 75%.
- 28 September 2003: responsibility for approval of new engine/propeller combinations for non-Annex II aircraft passed to EASA.
- Imported foreign-origin aircraft: consult foreign authority / manufacturer / CAA Safety Regulation Group.

### 70-10 Engine storage
- Installed piston engine out of service up to one month: ground run every seven days, at 1000 to 1200 rev/min until normal temperatures, exercising engine and propeller controls. If inactivity extended, go to long-term storage (continued runs cause excessive wear).
- Out of service more than one month: inhibit internally and externally. Treatment good for six months, extendable to twelve months in ideal conditions; then prepare for service, thorough ground run and re-protect, or remove and store per para 4.
- British method: turn engine at least six revolutions by hand after first spray, then spray half quantity with engine stationary.
- Installed stored engine must not be turned; warning notices on propeller and in cockpit.
- Installed turbine engines out of use up to seven days: covers/blanks only. Should not be ground run solely for preservation (temperature cycles limit life). Short-term storage: up to one month. Long-term: up to six months; re-preserve at end of each successive six months or remove and put in MVP envelope. Fuel cocks off after inhibiting.
- Uninstalled engines: MVP envelope; essential for overseas transport. Humidity indicator checked after 24 hours (must not have turned pink). After three years in an envelope: inspect for corrosion and re-preserve.
- Inspection: engines not in sealed envelope approximately two-weekly; sealed envelopes approximately monthly (if indicator pink, unseal, renew desiccant, reseal).
- Adhesive tape never used as a blank by itself.
- Times quoted apply to covered storage in temperate climates; manufacturer data overrides.

### 70-20 Powerplant acceptance (non-EASA aircraft)
- Para 2 general requirements (established by owner's/operator's approved maintenance organisation or type-rated LAE): type approved by CAA; civil identification plates fitted; log books or equivalent issued; original or certified true copies of relevant documents (mod standard, test results) from construction or previous HSI/Refurbishment/Overhaul/Performance Restoration provided with CRS; compliance with all applicable ADs of State of Design and CAA (pool: State of Design ADs only as minimum); storage per manufacturer, no unserviceability through operational abuse, damage or removal of components; hours and cycles of life-limited parts clearly defined; all mods and repairs approved by the Type Certificating Authority of the Powerplant or the CAA.
- Para 3: where UK programme has fixed HSI/Refurbishment/Overhaul/Performance Restoration intervals and previous operator's programme differs, time remaining must be agreed by the CAA.
- Para 4: not operated since last shop visit + Authorised Release Document (EASA Form 1, JAA Form 1, FAA 8130-3, TC Form 24-0078 as defined) = acceptable.
- Para 5: operated since last shop visit under EASA/JAA/FAA/TC control: needs serviceability statement from previous operator declaring hour/cycle restrictions for inspection, lubrication, replacement or overhaul; all defects rectified or recorded; maintained to an EASA/JAA/FAA/TC approved programme.
- Para 6 triggers: origin/traceability/serviceability not confirmable; no Authorised Release Document; not under EASA/JAA/FAA/TC operator control since last shop visit; fails any para 2 requirement. Owners advised to review 6.2/6.4 before buying.
- Para 6.2: demonstrate acceptability to the CAA considering unapproved mods/repairs; civilianisation of military powerplants with manufacturer; last shop visit to a specification and by organisation/person acceptable to CAA; serviceability statement from authorised organisation or national authority.
- Para 6.4: if serviceability cannot be established, dismantle and inspect (approved organisation or type-rated LAME) using manufacturer's recommendations as workscope basis; rectify. If records not accurate and complete, all life-limited parts must be scrapped, and CAA decides whether other parts must also be scrapped.
- Para 7: long-term lease/loan/PBH from original manufacturer or approved organisation: confirm para 2 compliance, supplier serviceability statement with restrictions, maintained to manufacturer's or approved programme, defects rectified or recorded.
- Para 8: Pool Powerplant interchanged on temporary basis "limited to a maximum of 200 hours"; conditions laid down in advance, agreed by CAA and lodged permanently in operator's MME; compliance with State of Design ADs; signed airworthiness statement from previous operator with restrictions during loan.

### 70-30 Gipsy Major 8 (Chipmunk)
- Waiver of 70-20 if supplied before acceptance: military serviceability statement; build standard statement (military body or CAA approved overhaul organisation) verifying all mods CAA approved and all CAA mandatory mods embodied; repairs CAA approved statement; engine log book verifying mandatory inspections complied with.
- Rolls-Royce TNS GE No. 27 (24 October 1974): civilian TBO for Gipsy Major 8 Mk 2 is 1500 hours. Exceedable via CAP 747 GR No. 24. GR 24 para 3.1.1 c) requirement (200 hours operation in UK-registered or other EASA Member State-registered aircraft immediately before completing manufacturer's overhaul period) waived, so engines with TSO up to 2250 hours (MOD overhaul period limit) may be accepted, subject to other GR 24 conditions including calendar life limits.
- All Gipsy Major 8 modifications up to and including 4037 have CAA approval; later mods require application to CAA, preferably via TC holder; applicant may be charged.
- Chipmunk propellers per 61-10.

### 70-40 Lycoming IO 360-A1B6 (Bulldog)
- Waiver (established by owner's/operator's Part 145 organisation or type-rated CAA LAE): serviceability statement; build standard (all CAA mandatory mods and inspections done); repairs CAA approved; engine and propeller log books issued; civil identification plates fitted.
- Lycoming SI 1009AU: recommended TBO 2000 h; inverted oil system mod would imply AEIO treatment (1400 h), but CAA set TBO at 1800 hours (consistent with military service). Extension under CAP 747 GR No. 24 with 200-hour precondition waived; TSO up to 1800 hours accepted for TBO extension.
- Propeller TBO per manufacturer for propellers fitted to aerobatic aircraft.
- Next TBO for engine and propeller timed from last overhaul in military service.

### 70-50 Dynamometer testing
- Approved Engine Test Schedule must be available; overhaul per manufacturer's Overhaul Manual.
- Instruments calibrated periodically and checked by approved organisation; speed indicators cross-checked with Hasler instrument.
- Oil filter elements cleaned or renewed before test; test oil tanks drained and flushed approximately every 100 hours running.
- Oil consumption sight glass readings every 15 minutes in prescribed stages, or weigh-drain method for wet sump (after running-in and after Endurance Test).
- Brake power = torque (Nm) x 2π x rpm / 60 000 kW, or torque (lb ft) x 2π x rpm / 33 000 BHP; simplified Brake Power = W x N / K (K dynamometer constant on nameplate).
- Hydraulic dyno water temperature about 60°C satisfactory; eddy-current water outlet not above 60°C, preferably 50°C.
- Eddy-current dynamometers typically used above 150 kW (200 BHP).
- Helicopter engines tested in installed attitude; correct for gearbox losses.
- Single ignition check: record power drop per magneto. Final Test power/rpm curve at Maximum Weak-mixture Power manifold pressure should be smooth. If components adjusted or replaced, repeat test or portions unless manufacturer agrees otherwise.
- Pass-off requires the specific performance standards in the Test Schedule.

### 70-60 Fan testing
- Fixed-pitch fans calibrated to absorb Maximum Continuous Power at Max Continuous rpm at standard sea level, without overspeed at Max Take-off manifold pressure.
- Fan type determination: fan cube law curve through MCP calibration point must intersect full-throttle (or Take-off MAP) curve between 90% and 97% of maximum rpm, with ±2% rpm adjustment allowed; otherwise use variable-pitch fan. VP fan power check stop set to give 97% of Maximum Take-off rpm at Max Take-off MAP.
- Metal flight propeller once used as a test fan must not again be used for flight.
- Calibration on a new or recently reconditioned "calibrated engine" whose power was dynamometer-determined; in the test cell where used; separate calibration per bench unless shown no effect; wind corrections agreed with engine manufacturer.
- Tolerance not more than ±20 rpm; corrected rpm of overhauled engines with same fan on same bed should not be less than 98% of acceptance rpm.
- Two or three runs, mean of three readings at 1-minute intervals in stabilised conditions.
- Record acceptance rpm with wind direction and speed; compile calibration report. Periodic weighing, static balance, blade angle measurements, condition check; re-calibrate at longer agreed periods, on environment change or suspected blade distortion.
- Fan used only for engine type and cell calibrated for; recalibrate if cell environment changes.
- Lubrication primed with warm oil under pressure; reprime if not run within three hours.
- Fan air thermometer 2.5 to 4 metres (8 to 12 ft) forward of engine, outside fan arc.
- Running-in is Stage 1 of Endurance Test.

### 70-70 Corrections
- Standard sea level: 101.325 kN/m2 (29.92 inHg, 1013.2 mbar, 760 mmHg, 14.7 lbf/in2) and 15°C. 1 kN/m2 = 1 kPa = 10 mbar; 1 lbf/in2 = 6.895 kN/m2; 1 inHg = 3.386 kN/m2.
- Correction to power uses (400 + t) temperature terms, manifold pressure minus exhaust back pressure / compression ratio; friction power Pf = 27 x 10^-16 x N^2 x d^2 x l^2 x n (SI) or FHP = 15 x 10^-10 x N^2 x d^2 x l^2 x n.
- Cylinder temperature corrections must be approved by the CAA before use.
- Worked examples: 196 kW observed -> 199.54 kW (acceptance 196 to 204 kW); supercharged 348.5 -> 352.329 kW; supercharger ratio 1.386 -> 1.392 with k = 0.001.
- Acceptance expression: sum of ratios (corrected power / ETC value + corrected compression ratio / ETC value) greater than 1.96; for fan tests (r2/r1) + (N2/N1)^2 not less than 1.96 (example 2.005).
- Humidity factor x table (air/fuel 9 to 16: 2.15, 1.80, 1.54, 1.32, 1.18, 1.07, 1.02, 1.00); C table (0.00167 to 0.00228); C = 0.0022 for take-off with water-methanol. Free water effect within ±1%, no correction.
- Charts valid for test cells sea level to 1000 ft; above 1000 ft request charts from manufacturer. Example rpm factor at +22°C = 0.9962.
- Supercharged engine run at full throttle during power check: correct as normally aspirated.

### 70-90 PBH
- Applicability: large transport aeroplanes in CAT, business jets, commercially operated rotorcraft; typically EASA types with EASA C of A under Part-M Subpart G CAMO, or EASA types with UK national C of A for State purposes (e.g. Police).
- Owner remains responsible (M.A.201(a)); operator ultimately accountable (AMC M.A.201(e)(2)3).
- PBH subcontracted CAW activities must be referred to in the CAME and included in CAMO quality monitoring; PBH workshops audited by CAMO.
- Contract must reference maintenance provider, location, Part-145 approval reference (M.A.708(c)); scope, tasks, communications, review meetings with participants and frequency.
- Competent authority (e.g. CAA) expects to attend a sample of CAMO/PBH co-ordination meetings.
- CAMO must engage in workscoping before shop visits; control configuration/mod standard of pooled components received.
- Safety-significant teardown findings to CAMO so an MOR can be raised; OEM design organisation to be included in feedback.

### 100-10 FLS/DFLD
- After any loading of FLS or DFLD a CRS must be issued by an appropriately authorised/licensed person.
- All loading recorded in the ACL; copy on board and copy in operator's maintenance records.
- Copies made only using the type design organisation's approved replication process; recorded in an Aircraft FLS/DFLD Replication Register traceable to source; copy of original release documentation accompanies copied media.
- Virus check on receipt (or immediately before loading if store protection doubtful).
- Electronic distribution release must provide equivalent formal controlled release, agreed by the CAA.
- UMS changes that alter performance information presented to flight crew: seek CAA advice; may need approval and lose UMS classification.
- Does not apply to EFB software (JAA TGL 36).

## 5. Practice Guidance

### 51-180 Aerials
- Aerial designs and locations vary; mounting technique must suit each; use manufacturer modification schemes where possible; doublers and reinforcement needed.

### 56-10 Glass windscreens
- Strength factors: heat treatment, duration of loading, rate of loading, surface/edge condition, installation method. Toughened (DTD 5576A Type 1) vs strengthened (Type 2). Tempered glass cannot be filed, drilled or trimmed; adjust frame instead.
- Specifications: DTD 218, DTD 869, DTD 761, DTD 5576A.
- Interlayer: polyvinyl butyral (vinyl); impact resistance depends on vinyl temperature (heated screens keep it plastic). Large aircraft screens: up to seven layers (glass, vinyl, stretched acrylic, polyester); two control sensors (one spare) and an overheat sensor. Heating films: tin oxide (0.000002 in, sprayed at 1000°C) or gold film (DTD 5576A).
- Mounting: friction mounting (simple) vs bolts through extended interlayer with aluminium reinforcing strip and inserts (high-impact/heated).
- Installation checklist: inspect for damage; clean frames; check flatness; correct cleaning agents only (wrong ones cause delamination); clearances; seals; fluid sealing compound uniform thickness, cure before final tightening; silicone release agent; note resistance code; non-magnetic bolts near compass and compass swing; sealing compound on bolts; support panel during bolting; even staggered torque; connect and test heating; pressurisation/leak test.
- Inspection: delamination types (clear/cloudy, rough-edge, smooth-edge; reflection test with light-beam flaw detector), scratches (three classes), chips (conchoidal, V-shaped; inner-surface chips critical), cracks (pattern by glass type), vinyl rupture, bubbling, discolouration; sealant erosion/adhesion/holes – remove with plastic tool only.
- Cleaning: warm water, mild detergent, soft cloth; only specified detergents/proportions; clear grit from recesses.

### 70-10 Storage
- American method vs British method of internal inhibiting (storage oil, cylinder protective sprays, dehydrator plugs, desiccant, blanking). Special requirements: coolant, fuel components, auxiliary gearboxes, propeller shaft. External: clean (white spirit), treat corrosion, grease control rods, cover magneto vents, plug lead transport blanks, mask connectors, cover rubber, blank fire extinguisher spray holes, lanolin/varnish film.
- Turbine: inhibit fuel system per MM, lubrication per MM, desiccant or VPI, covers; long-term grease controls, greaseproof paper over rubber, external protective forward of exhaust.
- Uninstalled: drain oil, inhibit, pad sharp projections, MVP envelope, desiccant/VPI quantities per MM, humidity indicator visible.
- Materials: DEF 2181 storage oil, DTD 791 cylinder protective, D.Eng.R.D. 2490 turbine fuel inhibiting oil, DTD 900-series varnish; blanks AGS 3802 to 3807 (screw caps), AGS 2108 (plugs), coloured.

### 70-50/70-60/70-70 Overhaul testing
- These are test-house practices for BCAR-approved engine overhaul organisations (relevant to piston engine overhaul shops, e.g. Gipsy, Cirrus, Lycoming engines on national aircraft). Key modelable items: instrument calibration intervals, test fan calibration records and acceptance rpm, test schedules, pass/fail thresholds (98% of acceptance rpm, ±20 rpm, ≥1.96 combined ratio), oil tank flush every ~100 h of test running, repriming if not run within 3 h.
- Test bench instruments list (17 items: speed, MAP, oil pressures, fuel pressure, oil temps, cooling air temp/speed, stop watch, CHT, fuel flowmeter, intake temp, exhaust back pressure, barometer, intake pressure, fan air temp).

### 70-90 PBH
- Distinguish time-and-material (operator/CAMO controls workscope and SB embodiment) vs PBH (fixed rate per flight hour/cycle; provider may own spares and drive SB/rework policy). PBH may require routine tasks at regular intervals (compressor washes, borescope inspections, fuel nozzle changes).
- AMP should cover component on/off-wing maintenance; reference TCH MPD, engine planning documents, ADs, SBs, SLs; and reflect operator policy on module soft lives, rework specs, PMA parts and third-party repairs, operational profile selection for LLP control.
- Trade-name examples: Power by the Hour, Eagle Service Plan, Goldcare, Totalcare.

### 100-10 FLS/DFLD
- Loading per MM or approved data; verify part number electronically; if unit has only one combined part number, nameplate changed per aircraft manufacturer's SB; vendor SB does not authorise embodying a different part number on the aircraft (needs aircraft manufacturer's SB or STC).
- Don't upgrade for its own sake; check support agreements; record changes between versions.
- CRC/corruption checking; virus checks; controlled storage (access, environment: water, fire, heat, electrical/magnetic fields); isolated electronic store with configuration control per ED-12B/DO-178B objectives; backups; avoid connecting store directly to the internet.
- Tooling: OEM, COTS, in-house; selection questions (functions, control, supplier experience/support/training, confirmation of what was loaded, CAA-accepted existing tools, human factors); laptop/PED virus protection; tool access controlled and recorded.
- Reference standards: RTCA DO-178B / EUROCAE ED-12B; RTCA DO-200A / EUROCAE ED-76; JAA TGL 36 (EFBs).
