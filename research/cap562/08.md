# CAP 562 Notes, Chunk 08 (pages 795 to 914)

Chunk covers the end of Chapter 20 (Leaflet 20-80), all of Chapter 24 (Electrical Power, Leaflets 24-10 to 24-50) and all of Chapter 25 (Equipment/Furnishings, Leaflets 25-10 to 25-60). Most leaflets are dated 15 April 2011. This is mainly maintenance-practice and design-approval guidance; there is little on BCAR approval types, but there are several important hooks: duplicate inspection under BCAR A6-2 and A5-3, the BCAR A6-2 para 8 release statement, the "UK MF Approval" reference on battery release records, CAP 747 Generic Requirements GR No. 4 and GR No. 6 (former Airworthiness Notices 82 and 88), "frozen" CAA E Number type approvals, BCAR Section J (historical), BCAR Safety Acceptance (SA) registration certificates, and the statement that GR No. 6 does not apply to Permit to Fly aircraft.

## 2. BCAR and National Regime

This chunk contains no description of BCAR approval types (A8-x, B, E etc.) or national C of A / Permit categories in their own right, but it contains these regime-relevant points:

- **BCAR Section A, Chapter A6-2** (Leaflet 20-80): source of the definition of a "control system" ("a system by which the flight attitude or the propulsive force of an aircraft is changed") and of the duplicate inspection requirement for control systems. The recommended certification wording cites "BCAR Section A Chapter A6–2".
- **BCAR Section A, Chapter A5-3** (Leaflet 20-80): source of the "Vital Point" definition: "Any point on an aircraft at which single malassembly could lead to catastrophe, i.e. result in loss of aircraft and/or in fatalities".
- **BCAR A6-2, paragraph 8**: named (Leaflet 24-20, Fig. 1) as one of the alternative release to service statements for a battery service record, alongside "Part M, AMC M.A.801" and "Part 145, AMC 145.A.50(b)". So BCAR A6-2 para 8 holds the national (non-Part 145) certificate of release wording.
- **"UK MF Approval Ref."**: the battery record has a field "EASA/UK 145 Approval Ref. or UK MF Approval Ref." (UK MF = UK Maintenance Facility approval, i.e. the national BCAR maintenance approval, interpreted; Note: name as given in text only, "MF" is not expanded in this chunk).
- **Persons who may certify duplicate inspections** (Leaflet 20-80 para 5.1, per BCAR A6-2):
  a) aircraft engineers "appropriately licensed in Categories A, B, C, D and X" (these are the old BCAR Section L licence categories; guess that they map to modern Part-66/national licences);
  b) persons employed by approved organisations who are appropriately authorised under company procedures; for commercial air transport the organisation must hold Part-145 approval;
  c) away from base, for minor adjustments, the second part may be done by a pilot or flight engineer licensed for the type, with authorisation from the responsible Part-145 organisation if CAT.
- **CAP 747 Generic Requirements**: CAA Airworthiness Notices (ANs) notified to EASA under Article 14.1 of Regulation (EC) 216/2008 were transferred to CAP 747 "Mandatory Requirements for Airworthiness", Appendix 1, as Generic Requirements (GRs). CAP 747 is described as "a single point of reference for all mandatory information for continuing airworthiness, including Airworthiness Directives, as applicable to civil aircraft registered in the UK".
  - **GR No. 4** = former AN No. 82 (Issue 2, 29 October 2001); CAP 747 GR No. 4 dated 30 November 2009. Applies to all multi-engine aircraft not exceeding 5700 kg MAW: generation failure warning, minimum 30 min battery, crew drills.
  - **GR No. 6** = former AN No. 88 (Issue 3, 29 October 2001); GR No. 6 dated 31 July 2008. Single-engined aircraft with engine-driven generation must have a low bus voltage warning. Title of Leaflet 24-50 restricts it to aircraft "With A UK Certificate of Airworthiness". Para 9.2: "GR No. 6 is not applicable to aircraft operating on a Permit to Fly."
  - **GR No. 2** "Minimum Space for Seated Passengers" and **GR No. 3** "Access to and Opening of Type III and Type IV Emergency Exits" are listed as applicable requirements for seats (Leaflet 25-30).
- **CAA "frozen" E Number Type Approvals** (Leaflet 24-50 para 4.1): low-volts warning units are quoted with "the original CAA frozen E Number Type Approval" (E13746, E13823, E13887, E13889, E13845). This is the legacy national equipment approval system, now frozen.
- **UK CAA BCAR Safety Acceptance (SA) registration certificate** (Leaflet 25-20 para 7): electrically powered medical equipment could have been issued one by the UK CAA "before 28 September 2003"; for changes to such equipment, seek advice from UK CAA or EASA. (28 Sept 2003 is the EASA start date.)
- **BCAR Section J (Electrical) (CAP 466)**: listed as a "Historical Reference" for ELAs; Appendix 3 of Leaflet 24-40 provides tables derived from it.
- **CAA Specification No. 1 – Safety Belts** (Leaflet 25-40 App 1): a national CAA equipment specification. Materials: British Standards and D.T.D. Specifications are approved; other specs approved by CAA. FAA TSO-C22(f) acceptable if paras 3.8, 4.2, 5.3, 5.4, 5.5, 5.6 also complied with; FAA TSO-C22g or JTSO-C22g acceptable with no further consideration. A 44 mm approved military belt is acceptable for belts not needing TSO approval (relevant to ex-military / national aircraft).
- **Minor modification procedure**: GR No. 6 low-volts installations "can normally be approved in accordance with MINOR changes procedures (see EASA Part 21A.95)", application via the CAA to EASA. A "one-off" self-installed unit: the documentation must be retained with the aircraft records.
- Air Navigation Order (ANO) referenced as the source of lifejacket carriage and ELT carriage requirements for UK operators (alongside JAR OPS 1/3).
- Rotor blade attachment and re-securing after unfolding must be inspected in duplicate (20-80 para 9.9).

No content here about LAA/BMAA, Annex I / non-Part 21 aircraft, ex-military or State aircraft as categories.

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### Leaflet 20-80 Control Systems
- Duplicate inspection: first made and certified by one qualified person, then made and certified by a second qualified person. No disturbance/readjustment between parts; second part to follow as nearly as possible immediately after the first. Both parts may sometimes have to be simultaneous.
- Components with parts concealed during bench assembly: inspected in duplicate on assembly during manufacture, overhaul or repair; both parts and test results certified on the Inspection Record for the part.
- Duplicate inspection of the control system in the aircraft required: (a) before first flight after initial assembly; (b) before first flight after overhaul, replacement, repair, adjustment or modification of the system. Must be the final operations. If disturbed afterwards before flight, disturbed part re-inspected in duplicate before flight.
- Sections to be "boxed in"/sealed must be accepted before sealing and the Inspection Record endorsed.
- Sub-contracted bench assembly: instructions for duplicate inspection stated on the order, and incoming release documentation endorsed accordingly.
- Static friction limits (Table 1, CAA recommended): aeroplane 5700 kg (12 500 lb) or less: elevator 17.79 N (4 lbf), aileron 8.89 N (2 lbf), rudder 26.68 N (6 lbf). 22 680 kg (50 000 lb) or more: elevator 44.48 N (10 lbf), aileron 35.59 N (8 lbf), rudder 44.48 N (10 lbf). Linear interpolation between. Measured with cables rigged at stipulated tensions, using spring balance.
- Cable identification tags other than those per BS SP53, SP54, SP105, SP106: remove before installing; particulars entered in the Aircraft Log Book.
- Locking wire must not be used more than once. Turnbuckle end fittings must be "in safety" (checked with a hardened pin probe through inspection hole).
- Large cables 45 to 120 cwt: tube with three bolts over the turnbuckle often specified to prevent unlocking.
- Rigging pins must have a red warning flag permanently attached; must be removed immediately after rigging and before operating power systems; ground locks never to be used in lieu of rigging pins.
- Rotorcraft tracking: in still air if possible, otherwise into wind not exceeding 10 knots, free from gusts.
- Blade rigging adjusted for tracking: re-check after test flying.
- Functioning check list drawn up in duplicate; two competent persons, exchange positions for the second part and repeat in identical sequence.
- Periodic inspections per Maintenance Schedule or manufacturer's Manual.

### Leaflet 24-10 Charging Rooms
- Never use same facility for Ni-Cd and lead-acid charging; no cross-contamination via ventilation.
- Max electrolyte temperature during charging normally 50 deg C (122 F), lower for some special batteries.
- Room environmental temperature: above 27 C (81 F) for lead-acid and 21 C (70 F) for Ni-Cd imposes time penalties and may harm batteries.
- Ni-Cd produces hydrogen at overcharge point and for 24 hours thereafter.
- Bench height about 0.5 m (20 in).
- Charging boards needing 240 V mains supplied from a ring main.
- No parallel connection of batteries to one output pair.
- Records of battery servicing should be maintained (para 7.1).
- Two sets of tools/equipment marked "acid only" / "alkaline only".

### Leaflet 24-20 Ni-Cd
- Typical constant-potential twenty-cell battery: needs 28.5 V to regain charge; thermal runaway could start at 29.0 V. Loss of 0.1 V per cell from top-of-charge potential in several cells can cause instability.
- Temperature monitoring for constant potential bus batteries: crew indication typically at 50 C; further warning at 70 C, at which electrical disconnection is required.
- Capacity assumptions: one-hour rate = 85% of five-hour rate; assume 80% of nameplate at one-hour rate and 90% state of charge = 72% of nominal rated one-hour capacity at +20 C. Higher 80% may be recognised if the CMM limits state of charge after shop maintenance (e.g. min 110%).
- Workshop removal intervals: manufacturers indicate as low as 50 hours, seldom exceeding 500 flying hours or 3 months installed life for constant potential systems; up to typically 1,000 flying hours if a charge controller limits overcharge accurately.
- Consumable electrolyte water typical 25 cm3 per cell; guideline if no data: not more than 1 cc per ampere-hour capacity added to any cell.
- No more than 5 new cells in a 20 cell battery (strong recommendation). Only manufacturer-rebuilt (identified) cells may be treated as new; cells rebuilt by other agencies must not be used as spares.
- Every battery through the shop must have each cell's top-of-charge voltage measured; any procedure not measuring every cell voltage is not acceptable.
- Battery shop instructions for charging equipment must be officially authorised by a competent engineer; history cards cross-reference instructions used.
- Battery room staff need a defect reporting route to air safety procedures (burnt cell boxes, arcing at links).
- Electrolyte: KOH solution relative density 1.24 to 1.30; new cells stand minimum 21 days after formation.
- Capacity test example: 23 Ah battery stands 15 to 24 hours, discharged at 23 A to 20 V; must give at least 80% of nameplate or minimum authorised design capacity, whichever greater. Record true capacity (full discharge). Not below 1 V per cell; no cell reversal.
- Recycling: discharge at or below one-hour rate; short each cell below 0.5 V; leave shorted minimum 16 h, preferably 24 h; charge 24 h; after ~5 min add water if any cell > 1.50 V (max approx 1 cc per rated Ah); after ~10 min reject any cell < 1.20 V or > 1.55 V; record at 20 h; at 24 h reject cell whose voltage is more than 0.04 V below its 20 h reading.
- Cell balancing (if <80% capacity): 23 Ah example discharge 23 A to 20 V; then 2.3 A, short cells below 1 V (some makers 0.5 V); shorted with main terminals shorted not less than 16 hours; repeat after charge.
- Voltage recovery: 1 ohm shorting strips, stand 16 to 17 hours, typical min 0.20 V; then 24 h open, typical reject below 1.08 V.
- Leakage: example permissible 0.020 A.
- Installation check: typical 30 A for 30 s load with steady voltmeter.
- Rejected batteries/cells permanently marked for "general ground use" only.
- Storage: long-term about 20 C; stored discharged, filled, shorting strips fitted; transport by air per IATA rules for alkaline electrolyte; returned battery accompanied by its service record.
- Electrolyte level not adjusted when installed in aircraft; adjustments only fully charged, after removal.

### Leaflet 24-30 (GR No. 4)
- Multi-engine aircraft not exceeding 5700 kg MAW: clear visual warning of generation failure; at least 30 minutes battery duration; precise crew drills.
- Warning lights red, undimmable, near pilot's scan on left or centre panel; exceptions by agreement with CAA Airworthiness Evaluation and Surveillance Department; flashing must be cancellable.
- Only 72% of nameplate considered available. Add 20% to pre-load-shed consumption if average discharge current numerically exceeds twice the one-hour rating.
- Pre-load shed period 10 minutes (or 5 minutes) from warning; landing period assumed 5 minutes; normal night cruise loads (de-icing may be excluded).
- CAA may accept up to 3 minutes short of the calculated 30 min.
- Owners/operators responsible for preparing and submitting documentation where Flight Manual/Pilot's Notes lack the drills and durations.
- If duration depends on equipment being switched off, minimum equipment and any "one-shot" limits must be in the Flight Manual or equivalent.
- Aircraft with only electric attitude instruments (no vacuum) need a detailed design investigation (GR No. 4 paras 2.4, 2.4.1); outside the leaflet.
- Example: 20 Ah battery, 864 amp-min available, result 47.6 min (example FM supplement states 52 min; VHF transmission limited to 3 minutes total).

### Leaflet 24-40 ELA
- Typical phase durations (commercial): ground 30 min, engine start 5, taxi 10, take-off/climb 30, landing 30. Helicopter: start/warm-up 10, take-off 10, cruise night 30, cruise day 30, landing 10, emergency landing 5, one-generator cruise 10.
- Time bands: 5-second analysis (loads > 0.3 s), 5-minute analysis (> 5 s), continuous (> 5 min).
- ED-14D 28 VDC terminal voltages: maximum 30.3 V, nominal 27.5 V, minimum 22.0 V, emergency 18.0 V.
- Manual load shedding assumed after 5 min if warning clear and attention-getting (AMC 25.1351(d)); 10 min if no flashing warning; automatic shedding immediate.
- Time-limited emergency source (AMC 25.1351(d)): at least 60 minutes, at least 30 minutes of which available under IMC, unless lesser time shown adequate; endurance stated in Flight Manual.
- Charging current I = A x C.
- Worked example: 12 Ah, 518.4 amp-min, total 44 min.
- ELA should include MMEL permissible unserviceabilities.

### Leaflet 24-50 (GR No. 6)
- Single-engined aircraft with engine-driven generation: clear warning when battery is supplying loads. Waived where aircraft equipped only for day VMC and loss of power could not prejudice safe flight and landing. Not applicable to Permit to Fly.
- Not acceptable: warnings sensed in generator/alternator windings (used on many Piper singles); wide-differential warnings. Such aircraft "should be modified".
- Spec: red light steady or flashing; flash 50 to 100 cycles per minute; dimming not acceptable unless auto-reset; trigger 25.0 to 25.5 V (24 V systems), 12.5 to 13.0 V (12 V systems); reset at 0.5 V above; voltage ranges 33.0 to 18 V or 16.5 to 9 V; operating temp -10 C to 40 C; endurance test not less than 2 hours unlit and 1 hour lit; identification of manufacturer, part number, serial number; compass safe distance specified; submit to CAA for acceptance before fitting.
- Detector connected via its own fuse/CB or one for a non-essential service.
- Minimum battery endurance after warning should be stated, not less than 30 minutes; amend only after refit with extra loads.

### Leaflet 25-10 Lifejackets
- Inspection periods per manufacturer: e.g. every 6 months with more comprehensive every 18 months, or yearly. Max service/storage life in years must not be exceeded (jacket and CO2 cylinder).
- Demonstration lifejackets returned for inspection as if time expired; kept out of normal stowage with warning label.
- Dropped/loaded/sea-water immersed jackets often to be rejected.
- Scales accurate to 0.1 gramme for CO2 weighing; cylinder outside weight limits replaced; CO2 cylinders lifed and returned to manufacturer at expiry; date code on base checked.
- Light unit battery electrical test e.g. 1 megohm minimum insulation resistance; milli-voltmeter should register no voltage.
- Patches overlap damage at least 25 mm (1 in); reinforcing strip 25 mm; exterior tape 50 mm (2 in). Vulcanising period 2 to 4 days. Welded jackets: minor patch repairs only.
- Tie-on label/card with serial number and next inspection due date.

### Leaflet 25-20 Medical Equipment
- "No hazard, no credit"; CAA/EASA do not endorse functionality.
- Voltages above 50 V r.m.s.: mark/placard.
- Gaseous equipment pipes/vessels tested to 2.5 proof and 3.0 ultimate times normal working pressure (CS-25 App K).
- Mechanical retention: emergency alighting load factors (para 561 of CS 23/25/27/29), noting CS 23.561 severe; ultimate flight inertia factors.
- Batteries allowed: sealed lead acid and sealed alkaline (not liquid electrolyte), dry cell, lithium if CAAIP Leaflet B-180 Appendix 24-6 and BS 2G 239 complied with. No charging on board.
- Acceptance per equipment type, P/N and S/N (or controlled standard) for the aircraft type tested.
- Maintenance tasks to be included in aircraft maintenance schedule.

### Leaflet 25-30 Seats
- Seat approval: Part 21 Subpart K with Subparts B, D or E, or ETSO via Subpart O. ETSO-C39b, TSO-C39c, ETSO-C127a contain no electrical requirements.
- DDP compliance against ED-14 Sections 4 to 25; software criticality per ED-12.
- Installing Design Organisation responsible for compliance with declared limitations.
- CMM required on application. FHA required. Seat manufacturer must declare in release documentation that in-seat equipment does not invalidate seat approval.
- Minor mods by original DO via Service Bulletin; major mods need Part 21 Subpart J DOA. Seats modified by other organisations lose ETSO status.

### Leaflet 25-40 Seat belts
- On-condition (in absence of manufacturer's recommendations): detailed visual inspection including seat attachments for security, integrity, distress, wear or biochemical staining at intervals not exceeding 6 months.
- After repair: design ultimate load applied to 1% of batch or at least one assembly, whichever greater; then buckle release load must not exceed 20 lbf.
- Specification values: restraining parts not less than 50 mm (2 in) wide (44 mm / 1.75 in military acceptable when no TSO needed); belt at about 45 deg; certified strength basis 670 N/g (150 lbf/g) times forward g; attachment fittings additional factor 1.33; strap angle limit 15 deg; adjustment load not exceeding 134 N (30 lbf); release assessed under residual 1112 N (250 lbf); lever release not exceeding 90 N (20 lbf), recommended min 22.5 N (5 lbf); lever release angle 70 to 95 deg; spring fatigue 100,000 cycles = 10 years; prototype test load x 1.2; series webbing members loaded to half certified strength; compass safe distance not greater than 305 mm (12 in), max 1 deg deviation.
- Marking of each detachable member: manufacturer's approved inspection mark; part/model number; serial or batch number.
- Reconditioned belts: tests like 7.3.2.1 and 7.3.2.2 or sample destruction tests.

### Leaflet 25-50 ULB N15F210B
- Beacons over 12 years old show significant output reduction.
- Serial Nos 1 through 14500: remove from service at next battery change.
- Serials with one or two letters before the number: manufactured after 1978, not immediately affected.
- Serial Nos 14500 through C5500 (all 5-digit serials and one letter plus 4 digits): one more battery change, then removed at the following battery change.
- Refurbished serials listed in two appendices (pre-1979 and 1979 to 1983) are excluded. Removed beacons can be refurbished/recertified by Dukane; replacement DK100.

### Leaflet 25-60 ELT
- Frequencies: 121.5 MHz and assigned channel in 406.0 to 406.1 MHz; some 243 MHz.
- Operational test typically once per month; performance test on installation and every one or two years per approved maintenance programme and manufacturer.
- LIVE TESTING IS NOT PERMITTED. Self-test only; self-test transmission max 5 seconds (typically < 0.5 s); 121.5 MHz test transmissions within five minutes of the hour by accepted practice; up to three audio sweeps.
- Performance and any live testing only in RF-shielded environment or at approved repair facility.
- Excess testing depletes battery life; consider cumulative effect.
- 121.5 MHz output approximately 0.1 W; 406 MHz approx 5 W.

Sheet 2 data/calculation.
- **Flight Manual Supplement (GR No. 4)** (24-30 App 2): aircraft type, registration, serial no, supplement number; para 2.3 battery duration (night); para 2.4 standby attitude instrument; para 2.5 crew drills (pre-flight checks, emergency procedure, retained services); notes; Prepared by; CAA Surveyor; Date.

- **Lifejacket Inspection Record** at maintenance base: all details incl. modifications, inspections, Inspector's stamp or signature; serial number, date of manufacture, mods must match the jacket markings. **Tie-on label or valise card**: serial number and next inspection due date.
- **Medical equipment**: Test Schedules available for review; design change (TC change or STC); operating procedures; maintenance tasks in maintenance schedule.
- **Seats**: Declaration of Design and Performance (DDP); Electrical Interface Control Document (Electrical ICD); Component Maintenance Manual (CMM); Functional Hazard Analysis (FHA); seat release documentation declaration; Service Bulletin for minor mods; Flight Manual / Operations Manual / cabin crew procedures; passenger placards/cards.
- **Seat belt marking** (manufacturer's approved inspection mark, P/N or model, serial/batch no).
- **ELT**: approved maintenance programme defines test intervals; FAA Action Notice A8150.3 referenced.

## 5. Practice Guidance

### Control systems (20-80)
- Duplicate inspection scope: main surfaces, lift/drag devices, trim and feel, flight control locks, operating mechanisms; rotorcraft collective, cyclic, yaw; engine controls (throttle, fuel cock, oil-cooler). Autopilot parts that cannot be instantly overridden or that are integral to the control system count as control system.
- Installation checks: pulleys/sprockets alignment, bearings, guards and covers locked against rotation, pressure-bulkhead seals and retaining rings, levers/fairleads, chains (Leaflet 20-40), cables (Leaflet D-20; no washing out lubricant; no broken wires/kinks/bird-caging; tension with correct meter, temperature allowance; equal turnbuckle adjustment), control rods straight with self-aligning bearing orientation, gearboxes/torque tubes, surfaces and tabs (clearance, no backlash), rotor blades, locking (Leaflet 51-100), lubrication, placards, bonding, corrosion protection.
- Settings: full range, stops, sequence of stops, backlash, dual controls synchronised, friction devices, tabs and screw jacks in safety, spring devices; powered controls per manufacturer: no bottoming of jacks, rigging pins, tension compensators, independent hydraulic system isolation, filters, bleed, ground engine run.
- Functioning: after cleaning and all work complete; two-person sequence; flaps/slats synchronisation and asymmetry; spoiler interconnect; feel simulator with pitot test rig; operational time limits for flaps/spoilers. Rotorcraft: directions, interconnects, ranges, pitch angles, tracking, tabs, tail rotor, dual controls, static loads.
- Control locks engagement and warnings; final FOD inspection; panel screw length.
- After overhaul: complete checks of disturbed independent system, or all systems if more than one affected.

### Batteries (24-10, 24-20)
- Charging room design (impervious floors, outward doors, sparkproof fittings, extraction of light and heavy gases), segregation of lead-acid and Ni-Cd everywhere (rooms, store, tools, PPE).
- Neutralising agents: sulphuric acid with bicarbonate of soda solution, ammonia powder, borax; KOH with boric acid solution or crystals; sawdust soak-up and burial; CO2 extinguishers and sand.
- Ni-Cd: separator failure is the key hidden failure; capacity alone insufficient, so measure every cell's voltage. Causes: excess discharge duty, high temperature, high DC voltage, inadequate maintenance. Thermal runaway factors: regulator mis-set, frequent/lengthy starts, loose links, leakage currents, poor GSE charging, high charge on hot battery, unbalanced cells (centre cells hotter). State of charge only measurable by discharge test.
- Workshop sequence: identify/raise record card, external inspection, cover, arcing, leakage, receptacle, links torque, vents, temperature sensors calibration; electrolyte level only at end of charge; cleaning (no wire brushes or solvents; flushing not recommended); constant current charging; leakage check; capacity test; recycling; cell balancing; voltage recovery; insulation resistance; cell replacement (same manufacturer, P/N, matched capacity; torque sequence from positive terminal and complete it); re-charge, IR test, capacity test if new cells fitted.
- Installed battery inspections per approved Maintenance Schedule: mounting, case, latches, connectors, leakage, level (adjust off-aircraft), ventilation and non-return valves. Compartment previously used for lead-acid: neutralise and alkali-resistant paint.

### Electrical generation and load (24-30, 24-40, 24-50)
- Three failure detection methods: generator output detection near bus (needs reverse current blocking), alternator auxiliary output, low bus voltage detection.
- Battery endurance method: available capacity (72%) minus pre-load-shed (with 20% factor if heavy) minus landing, divided by post-shed cruise load, plus pre-shed and landing time.
- ELA: account for transient, intermittent (RMS), cyclic and motor inrush loads; constant power devices draw more current at battery voltage; voltage drop in cables; AC kVA vector addition; growth capacity (% of source capacity); normal/abnormal/emergency source conditions; ETOPS section if relevant; review ELA for specialised helicopter roles.

### Lifejackets (25-10)
- Facility: clean, stable temperature, smooth tables, test rack away from sunlight, leak tracing by immersion tank or acid-free soap.
- Fabric deterioration signs: discoloured, sticky, hard/stiff, shiny areas. Two-stage pressure tests; mouth valve leak and opening pressure test; light unit activation check; CO2 weighing; mechanism travel, cocked lever, break thread/clip; sea-water immersion requires strip and clean. Repairs per Overhaul Manual; remove deflation key before folding.

### Medical equipment and seats (25-20, 25-30)
- Evaluation topics: flammability, smoke, explosion, EMC, magnetic interference, shock, burns, CRTs, depressurisation arcing, fluids, bonding/ESD, gaseous equipment, toxicity, batteries, mechanical integrity, location relative to evacuation, sharp edges. Aircraft-level tests at every likely location; crew procedures for limiting use, isolation, flight deck/cabin coordination, interference reporting.
- Seats: fault isolation per seat/row/all; mechanical override operable by one person; TTL position indication visible from aisle; FHA incl. uncommanded movement; inspect harnesses vulnerable to passenger damage and reconfiguration.

### Seat belts (25-40)
- On-condition philosophy with 6-month detailed visual; load-test sampling after repair; specification design/test rules for new/series/reconditioned belts.

### ULB and ELT (25-50, 25-60)
- ULB life/battery-change linked removal by serial number. ELT operational self-test vs performance test; no live 406 MHz transmission; G-switch verification; battery replacement; corrosion check; digital message verification (identification, position, 24-bit address/tail number).

## 6. Software Implications

**Data objects and fields**
- `DuplicateInspection` (independent inspection): task/work order link, system (flight control, engine control, rotor blade attachment, vital point), first inspector (name, signature, authority/licence category or company authorisation), second inspector, dates/times, statement text "BCAR Section A Chapter A6-2", flag "performed simultaneously", flag "away from base pilot/flight engineer second part" with authorising Part-145 org. Validation: two different persons; no intervening disturbance; must be the last operation before closing the package; re-trigger if system disturbed after completion.
- `VitalPoint` / `ControlSystem` reference list per aircraft type.
- `InspectionRecord` for components with concealed parts, with sub-contract endorsement.
- `RiggingRecord`: control surface movements measured vs rigging diagram, cable tensions with ambient temperature and tension meter ID, static friction values vs Table 1 (auto-compute limit by MTOW with linear interpolation between 5700 and 22 680 kg), component serial numbers, rigging pin removal confirmation (checklist item).
- `CableTagTransfer`: tag particulars entered into Aircraft Log Book.
- `Battery` asset: manufacturer, P/N, S/N, type (Ni-Cd semi-open/semi-sealed/sealed, lead-acid, lithium), rating V and Ah (one-hour rate; convert 5 hour rate x 0.85), mod state, function (main, standby, APU start, INS), installed aircraft/registration, date installed, hours flown; status (serviceable, unserviceable, awaiting initial charge, awaiting routine servicing, quarantined, rejected-ground use only, returned to manufacturer).
- `BatteryCell`: position, serial number, manufacturer, P/N, origin (new, manufacturer-rebuilt), replacement history. Rule: warn if more than 5 replacement cells in a 20-cell battery; block cells rebuilt by other agencies.
- `BatteryServiceEvent` mirroring Fig. 1: removal date/reason, date serviced, instructions used (document ref and revision), ambient temp, operations with mech and inspector initials, per-cell water added, voltage, temperature, final voltage, capacity; main terminal voltage; release statement selector (Part M AMC M.A.801 / Part 145 AMC 145.A.50(b) / BCAR A6-2 para 8); approval ref (UK 145 or UK MF). Auto-checks: capacity >= 80% of max(nameplate, min authorised); water added per cell vs consumable limit (default 1 cc/Ah or 25 cm3 typical); recycling thresholds (1.50, 1.20, 1.55, 0.04 V); voltage recovery (0.20, 1.08 V); leakage limit (e.g. 0.020 A).
- Battery installed-life tracking: interval in flying hours and calendar (e.g. 50 to 500 FH or 3 months; up to 1,000 FH with charge controller) configurable per operation type; trend of water consumption to adjust interval.
- `ChargingFacility`: chemistry segregation (acid only/alkaline only) for rooms, tools, PPE; calibrated equipment list (IR tester, multimeter, DVM, hydrometers, thermometers) with calibration due dates.
- `ElectricalLoadAnalysis` per aircraft (living document): baseline (TC) version and revisions linked to modifications; buses, circuits, CB codes, loads per phase, operating time category, power sources with ratings (continuous, 5 s, 2 min), normal/abnormal/emergency cases, MMEL items, battery endurance results, growth capacity. Calculator for battery duration (72% rule, 20% heavy discharge factor, 5 or 10 min pre-shed, 5 min landing) and AC kVA vector sums. Alert when a modification adds electrical load without ELA update.
- `MandatoryRequirement` references: CAP 747 GR No. 2, 3, 4, 6 with applicability rules (GR 4: multi-engine <= 5700 kg MAW; GR 6: single-engine with engine-driven generation, UK C of A, not Permit to Fly, waiver for day-VMC-only); compliance status per aircraft and evidence (FM supplement, minor mod number).
- `FlightManualSupplement` record (aircraft, supplement no, issue, battery endurance minutes, approval ref).
- `EquipmentApproval` types list: CAA E Number (frozen), BCAR Safety Acceptance (SA) registration (pre 28 Sept 2003), ETSO/JTSO/TSO, STC, minor change, CAA Specification No. 1.
- `Lifejacket` asset: S/N, type, manufacture date, max service/storage life, mods, inspection intervals (6/18 month or annual), next due date (label), CO2 cylinder S/N, charged/empty weight, cylinder life expiry, light battery activation status, demonstration flag (forces inspection on return), immersion/drop event (reject flag), aircraft/stowage location.
- `SeatBeltHarness` asset: P/N/model, S/N or batch, approval spec (TSO-C22g etc.), last detailed visual (max 6 months interval for on-condition), repair/recondition history with sample load test results (1% of batch or min 1; buckle release <= 20 lbf).
- `ULB` asset: model, S/N, battery change history, age; rule engine for N15F210B serial ranges with refurbished exclusion list (age > 12 years output degradation).
- `ELT` asset: type (121.5/243/406), ETSO ref, 406 hex ID / encoded data (24-bit address, tail number), battery expiry, last operational test (monthly typical), last performance test (install + 1 to 2 years), G-switch check, test log noting self-test only. Prompt with "no live testing" warning; optional "within 5 minutes past the hour" guidance for 121.5 MHz test.
- `MedicalEquipmentAcceptance` / `CabinEquipmentApproval`: equipment type, P/N, S/N, aircraft type/variant, design change ref (TC/STC/minor), prohibited locations, operating restrictions, maintenance tasks linked into maintenance programme.
- Seats: DDP, ICD, CMM, FHA document links; ETSO status flag that becomes invalid if modified by a non-original DO.
