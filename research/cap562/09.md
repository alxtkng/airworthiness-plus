## 2. BCAR and National Regime

There is little direct BCAR or regime material in this chunk. The relevant points are below.

- **Annex II (non-EASA) aircraft, now "non-Part 21"** (28-20): The Mogas Generic Concessions in CAP 747 cover "certain Annex II (non-EASA)" aircraft and, under Grandfather Rights, some EASA aircraft. Regulation (EC) 216/2008 decided which aircraft were EASA and which were not. GC 7 (UL 91) applies to Annex II aircraft only. EASA aircraft rely on EASA SIB 2011-01R2. GCs 2 and 5 are "frozen": EASA adopted many of the permissions under Grandfather Rights and the CAA cannot change them. Any new Mogas or UL 91 approval for an Annex II aircraft is granted one aircraft at a time. Ask the engine manufacturer first. If the manufacturer no longer exists or no longer supports the engine, apply to CAA Applications and Approvals for a modification approval.
- **LAA**: Owners of aircraft under LAA airworthiness administration may use "LAA Airworthiness Approval Notice LAA-999-413 Supplement 5 (or later)" as approval to use UL 91, under the conditions it states. (The text also calls it "Approval Note".)
- **Microlights**: Mogas containing alcohol is generally prohibited in aircraft, except microlights. GC 3 and GC 4 cover forecourt fuel for microlights and other light aircraft.
- **BCAR major modification procedures** (25-90 para 5.7, 25-100 para 5.9): Seat or structure changes must use Part 21 Subpart E, "or, for non-EASA aircraft, the appropriate BCAR Section A or Section B major modification procedure, Chapters A2-5/B2-5".
- **Controlled item approval** (25-90 para 2.1): Seats are approved under CS 25.785 or BCAR Section D Chapter D4-4 para 2.1.2. The approval procedure for such controlled items is Part 21 Subparts E and K, "or in BCAR Sections A or B, Chapter A4-8 or B4-8 where applicable". Seat limits, including minimum pitch, are recorded on the General Arrangement drawing, the **Declaration of Design and Performance (DDP)** or a letter of approval.
- **BCAR sections cited**: Section D (large aeroplanes), with D3-8 para 2 (emergency accelerations, used for megaphone mounting), D4-3 (compartment safety: 4.2.5(d) exits, 6.1 flammability, 6.4.1 waste receptacles) and D4-4 (seats). K4-3 and G4-3 cover compartment design for Section K (light aeroplanes) and Section G (rotorcraft). G6-7 (rotorcraft lighting) is cited in 33-20. 32-30 says BCAR requires the tread depth below which wet braking friction is impaired to be stated at certification of new types, and that it must be possible to tell in service when a tyre has worn below that depth. 34-10 says BCAR sets requirements for overall compass accuracy.
- **Generic Requirements (GRs) inherited from Airworthiness Notices** (25-90 to 25-130): The GRs came from Airworthiness Notices and were later in CAP 747. They were removed from CAP 747 in November 2014 when Regulation (EU) 965/2012 and Part 26 (Additional Airworthiness Specifications for Operations) came in. They are "not part of an EASA approved certification basis" unless a UK certification basis from before 28 September 2003 was grandfathered by EASA. They now apply only to the "relatively few aircraft" whose certification basis includes them. The software should treat them as applicable only if a flag on the aircraft's type or certification basis says so.
- **HISL** (33-20): Fitting a HISL is a major design change under 21A.97. Use the Part 21 Subpart E STC process, or Subpart D. The applicant must be the TC holder or a Subpart J DOA holder. The leaflet cites Reg 1702/2003, which has since been replaced.
- **CAA exemption**: The CAA issued a general exemption to Rules of the Air Rule 9(1)(b) so that dual red/white HISLs can show white in daylight.

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### 25-70 Megaphones
- Required by the ANO on UK-registered public transport aircraft with seating for 60 or more passengers. The FAA equivalent is FAR 121.309(f).
- Intelligibility: at least 90% of cabin occupants understand messages. Open-air test: intelligible at 100 m; ambient noise no more than 40 dBA; at least 3 observers at 100 m; 90% correct.
- Mounting must withstand BCAR D3-8 para 2 emergency accelerations. Compass deviation from the unit no more than 1 to 2 degrees (CS 25.1327).
- Two preset volume levels, one for inside the aircraft and one for outside. Instructions printed on the outside. Carrying harness.
- The battery check must be in the maintenance schedule. A label on the outside shows the battery replacement time.
- Environmental proof: function checks every 3 months (for example). A year of three-monthly checks with correct function counts as proof of environmental resistance.
- Only use cells date-coded by the manufacturer. Load test every 3 months. Replacement time of 6 months or 1 year may be reasonable with good cabin climate control; it may need to be shorter. Zinc-carbon cells have a recommended 3-month limit.
- Terminal voltage test under load (shout into the megaphone or apply about 300 mA). Reject cells below 1.1 V.
- The airline maintenance organisation should record cell manufacture, installation and test dates.
- Spring-contact unit cells (AA/HP7) are unsatisfactory. Use a proper battery pack with screw-on connectors.

### 25-80 PLB (appendices)
- Test to EUROCAE ED-14F categories: Sect 4 Temp/Altitude B2 (unpressurised, up to 25,000 ft / 7,620 m); 5 Temp Variation B; 6 Humidity C; 7 Shock/Crash Safety B; 8 Vibration U (helicopter, unknown rotor frequencies); 9 Explosive Atmosphere A Env II; 10 Waterproofness S; 11 Fluid Susceptibility F (Table 11-1 fluids); 12 Sand and Dust D; 14 Salt Fog T; 15 Magnetic Effect Z (compass-safe distance >0.3 m); 20 RF Susceptibility T; 21 RF Emissions M; 24 Icing A; 25 ESD A; 26 Fire/Flammability C (non-metallic, largest dimension >50 mm).
- The PLB is tested while not transmitting. It passes these "no-hazard, no-credit" tests if it does not transmit. For tests 15, 20 and 26 it must also have no effect beyond the test limits.
- Sections that do not apply: §13 Fungus, §16 Power Input, §17 Voltage Spike, §18 AF Conducted Susceptibility, §19 Induced Signal, §22 Lightning Induced Transients, §23 Lightning Direct Effects.
- Alternative or equivalent test regimes may be used if justified. Removing or disabling the power supply may be accepted as an alternative way to prevent accidental transmission.

### 25-90 Seat space (GR 2)
- Applies to UK-registered aeroplanes over 5,700 kg MTWA, in CAT, configured for 20 or more passengers.
- From 1 Apr 1989: new or amended seating configurations must comply. From 1 Jan 1992: all such aeroplanes.
- Dimension A, back cushion to the seat or structure in front: at least 26 in. Dimension B, seat to the seat or structure in front: at least 7 in. Dimension C, vertically projected distance between rows: at least 3 in.
- Measured from a datum 3 in above the uncompressed squab, up to 25 in above the carpet; centre half of seat width / central 12 in region. Literature in the magazine rack, tables stowed, seats upright, armrests down. No credit for deformable soft furnishings.

### 25-100 Type III/IV exits (GR 3)
- Applies to the same aeroplane population as 25-90, fitted with Type III or IV exits. Exits smaller than Type III, including elliptical exits, count as Type IV.
- Compliance dates: 1 Jul 1986 for Type III; 1 Dec 1987 for Type IV; 1 Apr 1989 for new or amended configurations; 1 May 1989 for face-to-face seating.
- Single access route to a Type III exit: access width at least half the hatch width (with trim) or 10 in, whichever is greater. No part of a seat beneath the exit may extend past the exit centre line.
- Seats beyond the centre line (Type III), or in line with the aperture (Type IV), are allowed only with a space at least one seat wide next to the exit and two access routes.
- Dual route to Type III, or any route to Type IV: at least 6 in vertically projected access.
- Face-to-face seating: at least 16 in between rows, vertically projected, and at least 52 in between the planes of the seat backs, measured 3 in above the cushions.
- Boundary seat backs must stay upright under 150 lbf, within 20° rearward and 10° forward. Seats meeting GR 3 dated 30 Nov 2009 are still acceptable.
- Opening and disposal placards at about seated eye level on seat backs forward of the access route (or beside the exit for face-to-face seating). Placards and the Cabin Safety Leaflet go to the CAA Operations Inspectorate before the mod goes to SARG.
- No significant projections on the hatch; no stub armrests. Any gap over 1 in that a hand or foot can enter is unacceptable. Unused meal tables at the access route must be removed or inhibited.

### 25-110 Flame-resistant furnishings (GR 13)
- Use inherently flame-resistant materials where possible. Materials treated with flame retardant must pass before, and after, three representative cleaning processes.
- Operators and maintenance organisations must control cleaning. They need to know the material type, approved cleaning and finishing methods, the effect of time in service, the retardant used and how to reapply it. Unsubstantiated claims are not acceptable; re-test to the BCAR/JAR methods.
- Repair or modification materials must be at least as flame resistant as the original, or meet current requirements.

### 25-120 Heat release (GR 14)
- Applies to UK public transport aeroplanes over 5,700 kg certificated for 20 or more passengers, of types whose TC was issued on or after 1 Jan 1958.
- From 20 Aug 1988 to before 20 Aug 1990: newly built aircraft or substantially complete interior renewals must meet JAR-25 App F Part IV, limited to 100 kW·min/m² total heat release in 2 minutes and 100 kW/m² peak. From 20 Aug 1990: Parts IV and V (smoke).
- The CAA could grant dispensations for aircraft built or renewed before 20 Aug 1991. A request needs the compliance steps, reasons and a component-by-component analysis.
- "Substantially complete cabin interior renewal" means renewing all sidewall panels, ceiling panels and/or overhead stowages, at once or progressively in a planned programme. Replacing an individual unserviceable panel does not count.
- Aircraft in service may continue until the interior is substantially renewed.
- Excluded: galley and stowage internal structure, floors, lenses, small items. Large PSU panels are included.

### 25-130 Toilets (GR 20)
- Applies to all aircraft over 5,700 kg in public transport.
- History: AN 83 first issued 22 Aug 1974 required inspection within 1 month and then every 1,000 hours. Letters to Operators 554 and 554A (July 1983) revised the periods.
- **Inspect at intervals not exceeding 72 hours elapsed time**, or another interval agreed with the CAA from data. Check that flaps and doors operate, fit, seal and latch; ashtrays fitted; notices present; compartment clean. Correct any defects. The inspection must be in the Maintenance Schedule.
- Re-assessment: operators propose mods to the CAA, with an agreed date, to meet the design rules (BCAR D4-3 6.4.1, JAR/CS 25.853(h)). Receptacles must be fire-resistant, keep their strength in a fire, and be fully enclosed with a rigid self-closing flap. An open-topped receptacle is acceptable inside a compliant cabinet. A towel dispenser must not be next to or above a waste flap or ashtray.

### 28-10 Fuel fungus
- Risk zones: between 30°N and 30°S, high temperature and humidity, and long periods in heated hangars (one aircraft was found contaminated after 2 months in a heated hangar).
- If fuel tank temperatures often rise above 25°C, a fungicide approved by both the airframe and engine manufacturers is strongly recommended, at their stated frequency and dilution. An unapproved biocide may make the aircraft unsafe.
- Add a visual internal tank check to the Maintenance Schedule at the manufacturer's interval. Keep drainage passages clear. Check fuel filters for slime. Investigate erratic contents gauge readings. Drain water before and after refuelling, and again before flight after standing. Clean the system by an approved method as soon as possible once fungus is found.

### 28-20 Fuel concessions
- GC 2: leaded BS 4040 four-star Mogas from a managed aerodrome installation, for combinations in GC 2 Schedule 1. Limited now that the fuel is scarce.
- GC 3: BS 4040 leaded Mogas from a filling station forecourt for microlights, gyroplanes, powered sailplanes and single-engine combinations under 2,730 kg listed in GC 2 Schedule 1.
- GC 4: forecourt unleaded EN228 for microlights already approved for EN228 or BS 7070.
- GC 5: forecourt EN228 with no alcohol for aircraft in Schedule 2. Group 1: TC holder approval in the Flight Manual. Group 2: FAA STC mods to airframe and engine, validated by the CAA. Group 3: FAA STC mods to the engine only.
- GC 7: UL 91 in Annex II aircraft if (a) the aircraft is approved for 100LL and the engine manufacturer accepts UL 91 in service information; or (b) it is approved for RON 95 EN228 (may need GC 5 mods); or (c) it is approved for 80/87 and TEL is not needed for lubrication, with no later mods affecting fuel specification. The CAA accepts no liability for warranty or long-term effects.
- UL 91 has no altitude or fuel temperature restrictions, unlike unleaded Mogas. CAA Safety Sense Leaflet 4 "Use of Mogas".

### 31-10 Fairchild A100 CVR SMP
- Intervals are maximum elapsed calendar time. Flight-hour schedules are allowed if fleet statistics show calendar limits are unlikely to be exceeded.
- Item 1: daily operational check by flight crew (TEST function), before or after flight.
- Item 2: installation check and functional test every 6 months or less. Covers every channel (area mic, receiver audio, sidetone, interphone, PA, boom mic including "hot mike") and the bulk erase inhibit logic.
- Item 3: helicopters only, data recording functional test every 12 months or less.
- Item 4: remove the CVR every 24 months or less. Clean heads and transport, replace worn parts, weigh the Thermopack insulation, test to CMM 23-70-01. Evaluate the recording before cleaning. Some items are replaced at 7,000 to 9,000 equipment operating hours.
- Item 5: ditching sensor (helicopters) every 24 months or less. Items 6 and 7: crash sensor and ULB at the vendor's interval.
- Item 8: sample check, removing the CVR straight after flight to replay it, at an interval agreed between the operator and the CAA. The operator proposes the sampling programme to the CAA, allowing for routes, fleet size and utilisation.
- Annual review of sampling results with a report to the CAA. Shorten intervals if serviceability falls.

### 32-10 Tyres
- Tubeless tyres are about 7½% lighter and run about 10°C cooler. High-pressure tyres run at 1400 kN/m² (200 psi) or more. Awl vents are about 1.5 mm, marked with green or grey spots. A red spot or triangle marks the light point.
- Size format "26 x 10·00–18" means outside diameter x width – bead diameter, in inches. The part number is the only positive identification. Speed rating is marked for tyres used above 160 mph.
- Use nitrogen (preferred) or air with moisture below 0.02 g/m³ (CAP 747 GR No. 16). Above 700 kN/m² (100 psi), use a screw-on connector and a safety cage.
- Tubed tyre inflation: at least 2 minutes to reach 140 kN/m² (20 psi), then at least 4 more minutes to test pressure.
- Duration test: record pressure, wait 12 hours. Reject if loss is over 10%; otherwise re-inflate. Wait another 12 hours. Reject if loss is over 2½%. 3°C of temperature change is about 1% of pressure.
- Immersion test (tubeless, when short of time) after 20 minutes (end of first-stage venting). A continuous stream of bubbles means rejection.
- Wait at least 1 hour after inflation before testing the inner O-ring.
- Storage and transit pressure is 20% of unloaded pressure (para 8.5). Para 15.4 gives 140 to 210 kN/m² (20 to 30 psi) for assembled wheels in storage.
- Creep marks are 25 mm apart (tyres up to 600 mm OD) or 38 mm (larger). For tubed tyres, misalignment greater than the mark width means remove and refit. For tubeless tyres, creep is acceptable if the beads and pressure are OK.
- Loaded pressure = rated (cold, unloaded) pressure + 4%. A tolerance of 5 to 10% above that is allowed. Expect up to a 10% rise after landing; do not bleed it off.
- Cold check: more than 10% below loaded pressure means reject the tyre and its axle companion. 5 to 10% below means re-inflate and check at the next daily check; reject if more than 5% low again.
- Hot check: 10% or more below the highest tyre on the same leg means re-inflate to that pressure. Reject if the loss recurs at the next check.
- Visual check before each flight (rotate the tyre).
- Wear limits: patterned tread to pattern depth; ribbed tyres to the top of the tie bars; ribbed tyres without tie bars to within 2 mm (0.080 in) of the groove bottom; twin contact until the crown centre touches the ground; plain tread until cushion rubber or cords show.
- Deflated under load: remove. If taxied deflated, scrap that tyre and its axle companion. If more than one tyre on a multi-wheel gear ran deflated, scrap all tyres on that gear.
- Signs of overheating (tacky bead) mean reject. After a high-energy rejected take-off, follow the Maintenance Manual. Fit replacement tyres on twin or multi-wheel gear selectively so they share load.
- Repair limits: minor damage (no cords) up to 38 mm (1.5 in) diameter, any number of repairs. Cut cords repairable in the tread only if no more than 20% of the cord layers, or 4 in total, are damaged. Tube repairs up to 50 x 50 mm. Inflate to 140 to 210 kPa for crown or shoulder repairs and 70 to 140 kPa for sidewall repairs. Vulcanise at about 150°C; if the probe test fails, add 15 minutes.
- Only the original manufacturer or an approved specialist may remould. Casing strength is about 1% lower after 10 remoulds. New tyre types are approved for one remould life first, then extended.
- Storage in the dark at 10 to 27°C, upright on two-point racks, turned every 2 to 3 months. Tubed tyres may be stacked at most 4 high; never stack tubeless tyres. **Shelf life up to 7 years from manufacture; keep stock to no more than 4 years; return to the manufacturer after 7 years.**
- Records: keep a count of landings for each tyre when the Maintenance Schedule requires it.

### 32-20 Wheels and brakes
- Fusible plugs melt at about 150°C. If a plug has blown, scrap the tyre, renew all plug seals and check the wheel.
- If any tie bolt is defective, remove the wheel and renew the whole set. The manufacturer may require stiffnuts to be discarded after disassembly. Fit new seals at reassembly. Statically balance the wheel and tyre assembly.
- Some wheels need overhaul after a set number of landings. Do a detailed inspection after operation on a deflated tyre (or with the companion tyre deflated), and at schedule intervals. Use ultrasonic or eddy current for bolt holes and the bead seat radius. Hardness check after overheating if specified.
- Drum brakes: contaminated linings are unserviceable and must not be cleaned. Fit new separators at reassembly. Confirm zero pressure from the brake gauge, not the general system gauge.
- Disc brakes: wear is measured by wear pin protrusion. If any pad is beyond limits or contaminated, change the whole set. Part-worn pads go back in their original positions. New seals, gaskets and self-locking nuts.
- Low-pressure system test: hold the brake fully on for 30 seconds with no pedal creep and no leaks. Do not reuse bled fluid.
- High-pressure system: discharge the accumulator before disconnecting; counting full applications also checks internal leakage. Check that the main system, not the emergency system, runs through the anti-skid. Charging cylinders must have relief valves.
- A spring-loaded anti-skid unit locked out with a pin must carry a red streamer.
- Brake fire: dry chemical is preferred. Approach in line with the tyre's rolling path, never in line with the axle. Apply CO2 or foam lightly from at least 6 m (20 ft). Magnesium wheels burn fiercely.

### 32-30 Tread depth
- Applies to public transport aeroplanes over 5,700 kg on the UK register with no specified limit. Scheduled distances are not enough for tyres more than 80% worn on wet runways.
- Withdraw the tyre if any groove is under 2 mm deep over more than a quarter of the circumference, or if tread is under 2 mm across the full contact width at any point. Equivalence is possible, for example one groove under 2 mm while all others are 3 mm or more.

### 33-10 Floor path lighting (ANO Sch 4 scale Z(3))
- Exit identifiers visible from the aisle centre line at 4 ft or below; recommended mounting 18 in to 4 ft above the floor; visible with the adjacent seat occupied.
- Point or EL markers no more than 20 in apart (40 in under typical MEL relief). Seat-side incandescent lights no more than 40 in apart. Red or orange markers at the aisle ends. Overwing cues: 3 red/orange markers recommended, minimum 2. Flood-light spacing agreed with the CAA. Strobes are not effective cues.
- Only "designated" exits (the minimum for certificated capacity) and "excess" exits (extra exits meeting the same rules and readily accessible) are identified.
- 25% rule, CS/FAR/JAR 25.812(l)(1): no more than 25% of lights inoperative after any single transverse vertical separation.
- CAA evaluation is required. New concepts need a demonstration in darkness, with lighting above 4 ft off, with the MEL configuration simulated, and with adult subjects including men and women over 60. Each exit type must be identified both open and closed.

### 33-20 HISL
- The ANO Rules of the Air (1996, Rules 9 and 10) require an anti-collision light on helicopters over 5,700 kg MTWA, and on those of 5,700 kg or less whose type was first certificated on or after 1 Apr 1988. JAR-OPS 3 requires it for day VFR.
- HISLs required on AOC helicopters doing pipe and powerline survey since 30 Jun 1992 (CAA letter 2 Jul 1991).
- Minimum 2000 candela white. Effective flash rate 40 to 100 per minute, up to 180 in overlaps. As an anti-collision light: visible ±30° above and below the horizon in all directions.
- Separate switch from the red anti-collision light. No glare. Cockpit warning placard unless shown not to distract. Amend the electrical load analysis. Raise a Flight Manual Supplement.

### 34-10 Compass bases
- The CAA does not survey or approve compass bases. QinetiQ standards (formerly DERA) are recognised.
- Siting: datum circle at least 46 m (50 yd) from hangars and steel buildings, and at least 91 m (100 yd) from power plant and power cables. No ferromagnetic material. Re-survey fully after any construction.
- Class 1: ±0.1° maximum deviation (refined swings, for example remote compasses feeding Doppler). Class 2: ±0.25° (standard swings). Larger deviation is acceptable where a direct-reading compass is the primary reference.
- **Periodic re-survey: Class 1 every 5 years; Class 2 every 2 years, plus a QinetiQ survey every 6 years where possible. Annual check of all bases** for markings, new works and changes in magnetic variation.
- Survey grid every 6 m (20 ft) at tripod height of about 1.5 m. Reciprocal bearing method: deviation = difference minus 180°; mobile reading higher than master means negative. If the mean deviation is more than half the class limit, suspect a buried object at the centre.
- Distant bearing method: object at least 2 nm away; only for initial surveys and Class 2 gross-error checks.
- Pole method: poles 9.1 m (30 ft) from centre; reciprocal within ±1°; deviation within 1° along the line; chords agree within ±76 mm (3 in).
- Area survey for "electrical" swing: monitor 3 to 4.6 m from the console. Index error difference between tripod heights no more than about 6 minutes (typical). ±6 minutes over a 1.5 m radius circle. Monitor 23 to 30 m from the aircraft. Reference target at least 800 m away. Readings at the two locations within 30 minutes of each other.

## 4. Records, Forms and Documents

- **Megaphone battery records** (25-70): the airline maintenance organisation records the date of manufacture, installation and testing of the cells. An external label shows the battery replacement time. The battery check is a Maintenance Schedule item.
- **Environmental Qualification Checklist** (25-80 App 2): table of Section, Test, Category, Pass/Fail and Test Report Reference for the 16 ED-14F tests, with Signature, Company and Position lines. Signed by the applicant or installer.
- **Declaration of Design and Performance (DDP)**, General Arrangement drawing or letter of approval (25-90): record seat limitations, including minimum seat pitch.
- **Instruction placards and Cabin Safety Leaflet** (25-100): agreed with the CAA Operations Inspectorate before the mod is submitted.
- **Dispensation request** (25-120): compliance steps, reasons and a component analysis.
- **Maintenance Schedule entries**: toilet receptacle inspection every 72 hours (25-130); internal tank check (28-10); megaphone battery check (25-70); CVR SMP tasks (31-10); tyre removal periods and landing records (32-10).
- **Modification proposals to the CAA** (25-130): operator proposals with a compliance date agreed by the CAA.
- **CVR sampling programme proposal and annual report to the CAA** (31-10).
- **Tyre landings record** (32-10 para 16), where the schedule requires it. Tyre sidewall markings give the data for a tyre record: size, part number, serial number, date of manufacture (for example "2283 Nov 72"), ply rating ("10PR"), speed rating, tubed or tubeless, retread count and date, and retreader.
- **Tyre defect marking**: mark with wax crayon before removal. Label scrapped tyres.
- **Flight Manual Supplement** (33-20 Annex A, specimen): company, supplement number, aircraft type, MSN, registration; introduction (mod number, position, intensity, switch location); limitations (placard text); emergency procedures (no change); normal procedures (HISL ON after take-off when clear of persons; OFF before landing); performance (no change). Inserted at the back of the Flight Manual, with the CAA revision record sheet amended. Marked "CAA Approved" and dated.
- **Compass base observation log** (34-10): a scaled diagram of the base showing deviations, objects (drains, ducts, lights, picket points) and prohibited areas. Instrument test certificates are checked for corrections. Permanent base markings: centre, central area, datum compass circle, anomalies, nose wheel turning circles, N-S line, and monitor and turntable positions with the reference target bearing.
- **Electrical load analysis**: amended for a HISL fit.
- **Letters to Operators 554 and 554A** (1983): historical.

## 5. Practice Guidance

- **Cabin GRs (25-90 to 25-130)**: These are configuration and design-compliance checks, not routine maintenance. The exception is 25-130, which sets a recurring 72-hour inspection. A cabin reconfiguration feature could check against seat dimensions A, B and C (26, 7 and 3 in) and the exit access rules, but only for aircraft whose certification basis includes the GRs. Furnishing cleaning control is a process and records duty for operators and maintenance organisations: keep the material type, cleaning method and retardant reapplication history.
- **Fuel contamination (28-10)**: Water drain checks before and after refuelling and before flight after standing. Check filters for slime. Investigate erratic fuel gauges. Biocide only with OEM approval and at the OEM's dilution. Look for fungus whenever a tank is opened. Fungus can lead to corrosion of the bottom skin, the chordal member at the wing root and fuel pipes.
- **Fuel eligibility (28-20)**: Fuel eligibility depends on the airframe/engine combination, mods embodied (STCs), the alcohol content and the source (aerodrome installation or forecourt).
- **CVR (31-10)**: This is a model SMP table with item, equipment, task, maximum interval and interpretation.
- **Tyres (32-10, 32-30)**: Pre-flight visual checks. Daily pressure checks with cold and hot rules. Creep mark checks. Wear limits by tread type. Rejection triggers: bulge with broken fabric, cords exposed, deflated under load, overheating, contamination beyond slight. Repair and remould are done by specialists. Storage environment and shelf life. In doubt, change the tyre and consult the manufacturer's representative.
- **Wheels and brakes (32-20)**: Wheel NDT (eddy current or ultrasonic) when the wheel is off. Bearing inspection and repacking. Tie bolt policy. Brake wear pin measurement. Whole-set pad replacement. Bleeding and functional test after work. Anti-skid self-test. Brake temperature monitor test switch. Fire safety rules for overheated wheels.
- **Lighting (33-10, 33-20)**: Mostly design and installation guidance. The maintenance link is the MEL relief for floor path markers, assessed at 40 in spacing. For a HISL: the warning placard, a separate switch and an FMS.
- **Compass base (34-10)**: Base classification and its validity drive whether a swing done on that base is acceptable. This links to the compass swing tasks in 34-20 (next chunk).

## 6. Software Implications

- **Applicability flags on the aircraft or type record**: `public_transport` / `CAT`, `MTWA_kg` (5,700 kg threshold), `passenger_seats_configured` (20 and 60 thresholds), `TC_issue_date` (1 Jan 1958 for GR 14; 1 Apr 1988 for helicopter anti-collision lights), `exit_types[]` (Type III/IV, elliptical counted as IV), `uk_cert_basis_includes_GR[]` (GR 2, 3, 13, 14, 20), `regime` (Part 21 / grandfathered / Annex II / non-Part 21 / LAA / BMAA / microlight), `rotorcraft_offshore`, `AOC_pipeline_survey`.
- **Rules engine for equipment carriage**: megaphones (60 or more seats, public transport); anti-collision light or HISL (helicopter weight and date rules; pipeline and powerline survey); PLB compliance pack (CAT.GEN.MPA.140 / ANO Art 119(4A)); floor path lighting (ANO Sch 4 scale Z(3)).
- **Recurring task templates**:
  - Toilet receptacle inspection, 72 hours elapsed (or a CAA-agreed interval).
  - Megaphone battery load test, 3 months; replacement 6 to 12 months (configurable); cell rejection below 1.1 V at 300 mA.
  - CVR A100: daily; 6 months; 12 months (helicopter); 24 months removal; 24 months ditching sensor (helicopter); vendor interval for crash sensor and ULB; sampling at the CAA-agreed interval. Calendar-based with an optional flight-hour equivalent. Component hours trigger for parts at 7,000 to 9,000 hours.
  - Internal fuel tank fungus check (OEM interval, conditional on operating environment); biocide treatment frequency.
  - Tyre pressure daily; tyre visual pre-flight; wheel overhaul after N landings.
  - Compass base: annual check; re-survey at 5 years (Class 1) or 2 years (Class 2); QinetiQ survey at 6 years (Class 2).
- **Tyre component object**: part number (primary identifier), serial number, size string, ply rating, speed rating, tubed/tubeless, date of manufacture, retread count, last retread date, retreader, approved remould limit, landings count, position, fitted date, axle companion link, storage start date. Shelf-life alerts at 4 years (stock policy) and 7 years (return to manufacturer). Statuses: serviceable, in storage, quarantined, scrap, at repairer or remoulder.
- **Tyre pressure check workflow**: inputs are measured pressure, cold or hot, and the loaded pressure (from a table or a weight graph). Logic: more than 10% low when cold means reject the tyre and its companion; 5 to 10% low means re-inflate and flag a re-check at the next daily check; hot tyre 10% or more below the leg maximum means re-inflate and flag; a repeat means reject. Allow 1% per 3°C.
- **Duration test record**: P0, P12 and P24, with 10% and 2.5% thresholds giving pass or fail automatically.
- **Event-driven triggers**: tyre run deflated (scrap rules for the companion and all tyres on the gear); high-energy RTO (inspect per the MM); fusible plug blown (scrap the tyre, renew seals, check the wheel); defective tie bolt (renew the set); anti-skid unit locked out (red streamer reminder and open item); erratic fuel gauge (fungus check).
- **Brake wear**: wear pin measurement against MM limits, with trend forecasting; whole-set pad replacement flag.
- **Fuel eligibility reference list**: GC 2, 3, 4, 5 (Schedules 1 and 2, Groups 1 to 3) and GC 7 conditions; LAA-999-413 Supp 5; EASA SIB 2011-01R2. Record the permitted fuels per aircraft (100LL, UL 91, EN228 alcohol-free, BS 4040, BS 7070, 80/87), mods embodied (STC references) and the source restriction (aerodrome or forecourt).
- **Modification records**: seat layout mods with dimensions A, B and C and exit access measurements; HISL mod with FMS attached, ELA amended and placard fitted; approval route field (Part 21 Subpart D/E/J, BCAR A2-5/B2-5, A4-8/B4-8 controlled items).
- **Qualification checklist object** (25-80 App 2): rows by ED-14F section with category, pass/fail and test report reference, plus signature, company and position.
- **Compass base register**: location, class, survey type and date, surveyor (QinetiQ or in-house), observation log attachment, prohibited areas, next due dates. Link to compass swing records so a swing on an expired or out-of-class base raises a warning.
- **MEL link**: floor path lighting relief at 40 in marker spacing.
- **Document library**: tag these GRs as "reference only – applies if in certification basis".
