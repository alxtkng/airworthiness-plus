## 2. BCAR and the National Regime

This chunk has very little regime content. These are the only direct references:

- **BCAR Section A, Chapter A1-4** (quoted in E-20 para 1.2). Materials used in "parts affected by airworthiness requirements" must comply with one of four kinds of specification:
  - a) British Standard Aerospace Series Specifications;
  - b) DTD Specifications;
  - c) "Specifications approved by the CAA";
  - d) a specification prepared for the material under BCAR Chapter **D4-1** (large aeroplanes) by an organisation approved for design, where the material is used in a part designed within that design approval. A footnote gives the equivalents in other BCAR sections: **K4-1** (light aeroplanes), **G4-1** (rotorcraft) and **C2-2** (engines). So this route is tied to a BCAR design-approved organisation, the pre-Part 21 national equivalent of a DOA.
- **BCAR Section A, Chapter A4-8**, "uncontrolled items" or "general supplies" (E-20 para 9). Parts for general supplies may be made from material with no specified identification-marking requirement. The material manufacturer should still mark the material so it can be matched to its "accompanying release documentation", and the material must be identifiable after delivery so the purchaser cannot confuse it with other stock. This is the BCAR concept of uncontrolled or standard items that do not need full airworthiness release.
- **"Approved Certificate"** (E-20 paras 3.4, 5.6.1 and 8). This is the release certificate that goes with material supplied to approved specifications. It is the older BCAR term for a material release document; the CAA Form 1 is not named in this chunk.
  - The test report number marked on the material links it to the Approved Certificate and the test report.
  - Material supplied in a heat-treatment condition other than the one the specification calls for must have its Approved Certificate "clearly annotated 'annealed', 'not aged', etc."
  - For leaded steels, the Approved Certificate must be "appropriately endorsed".
- **Air Navigation Order, Schedule 3** (E-10 para 1.2). Every registered aircraft must carry a metal nameplate fixed near the main entrance. It must be stamped or engraved with the nationality and registration marks and the registered owner's name and address. The plate must be fireproof so the aircraft can still be identified after a fire. The CAA recommends stainless steel. (This applies to all UK-registered aircraft, Part 21 and non-Part 21 alike.)
- **"Approved Maintenance Schedule"** (D-40 para 3.13.8 note; E-10 para 5.10.3). The maximum service life of seals is "usually to be found in the approval Maintenance Schedule". Overhauls and inspections of rescue equipment are done to the "time/life requirements of an approved Maintenance Schedule".
- **"Relevant Overhaul Manual"** (D-40 para 3.8.1). Instruments that reach the end of their storage limiting period must be "re-certified in accordance with the relevant Overhaul Manual".

What this chunk does not cover: national Certificates of Airworthiness, Permits to Fly, LAA or BMAA arrangements, Annex I / non-Part 21 aircraft, ex-military or historic aircraft, State aircraft, and the BCAR A8-x approval types. These are not discussed anywhere in pages 343 to 454.

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### D-30 Clean Rooms

- **Particle size controlled:** filtration is normally designed to control particles of 0.5 µm and larger. The unit of measurement is the micrometre.
- **Table 1: classes.** "Periodicity" is the recommended interval for air sampling and particle counting. Particle limits are per m³.

| Class | Air flow | Periodicity | 0.5 µm | 1 µm | 5 µm | 10 µm | 25 µm | Final filter efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class 1 | Unidirectional | Daily, or continuous by automatic equipment | 3,000 (footnote: subject to a maximum particle size of 5 µm) | n/a | Nil | Nil | Nil | 99.995% |
| Class 2 | Unidirectional | Weekly | 300,000 | n/a | 2,000 | 30 | Nil | 99.95% |
| Class 3 | Unidirectional or conventional | Monthly | — | 1,000,000 | 20,000 | 4,000 | 300 | 95.00% |
| Class 4 | Conventional | 3-monthly | — | — | 200,000 | 40,000 | 4,000 | 70.00% |

  - Contained work station: unidirectional flow, 99.997% filter.
  - Portable clean boxes: specified "to suit required class".
  - The text extraction of the Class 3 and 4 columns is ambiguous; treat those values as indicative.
  - The leaflet notes that "Class 1 is outside the scope of this Leaflet and would not normally be used".
- **Table 2: typical products by class.**
  - Class 2 (0.5 to 10 µm): air bearings, miniature bearings and contacts, floated gyros, hydraulic and pneumatic systems, optics, semiconductors, miniature timing devices.
  - Class 3 (1 to 25 µm): hydraulic and pneumatic systems, precision timing devices, stable platforms, gyros.
  - Class 4 (5 to 25 µm): ball bearings, electronic components, engine pumps, aerospace instruments, printed circuits, valves, hydraulic and pneumatic systems, precision measuring equipment.
- **Environment:**
  - Working temperature normally 20 ± 2 °C.
  - Relative humidity 35 to 50% for all classes. A later paragraph (15.2.2) says a humidity of "not less than 40%" is generally desired.
- **Air velocity, air changes and pressure:**
  - Unidirectional horizontal-flow rooms: 0.45 ± 0.1 m/s. Vertical-flow rooms: 0.30 ± 0.05 m/s.
  - Conventional rooms: not less than 20 air changes per hour, or not less than 10 per hour for Class 4.
  - Pressure step between successive areas: not less than 15 Pa (1.5 mm water gauge). 25 Pa (2.5 mm) is "normally regarded as adequate".
- **Noise and lighting:**
  - Maximum noise, operational but unmanned: 65 dB.
  - Lighting at bench level: not less than 3,000 lux (fluorescent).
- **Garments:** three sets per person (one in use, one being cleaned, one in reserve).
- **Table 3: monitoring intervals.**

| Environment | Particle sampling | Temperature | Humidity | Pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class 1 | Daily or continuous | Continuous | Continuous | Continuous |
| Class 2 | Weekly | Continuous | Continuous | Continuous |
| Class 3 | Monthly | 4-hourly | 4-hourly | Continuous |
| Class 4 | 3-monthly | 12-hourly | 12-hourly | Continuous |
| Contained work station | Daily, to suit the product, or as Class 2 | Dependent on use | Dependent on use | Not applicable |
| Controlled area | Dependent on use | To meet personnel and product needs | To meet personnel and product needs | Not applicable |

- **When monitoring is also required:** at initial commissioning, at regular periods, after filters are changed, and whenever the room appears to be downgrading.
- **Air handling maintenance:** inspect, maintain and test air handling components to their maintenance instructions, at periods set by the type of operation, and when contamination levels start to degrade.

### D-40 Storage (defaults when the manufacturer gives no figure)

**Desiccants**
- Inspect or renew at specified periods, or whenever an airtight container has been opened.
- Reactivation: silica-gel at 135 °C for at least 2 hours; activated alumina at 250 °C for 4 hours. Cool in a sealed container.
- Tell-tale silica-gel colour against surrounding RH:

| Colour | Surrounding RH | Moisture content of gel |
|---|---|---|
| Deep blue | 0.5% | 0.2% |
| Blue | 10% | 5.5% |
| Pale blue | 20% | 7.5% |
| Pinkish blue | 30% | 12% |
| Bluish pink | 40% | 20.2% |
| Pink | 50% | 27% |

**General store rules**
- **Rotation of issue:** issue oldest stock first ("strict rotation"). This matters most for perishables, instruments and items with storage limiting periods.
- **Storage limiting period:** when it expires, withdraw the part for checking or overhaul as the manufacturer recommends. Manufacturer periods only apply if the prescribed storage conditions are met, and users should develop their own limits from experience.
- **Temporary protectives:** parts stored for long periods should be inspected at intervals to check the coating.

**Lead-acid batteries** (store fully charged)
- Every 4 to 6 weeks: remove from storage and fully recharge.
- Before going into service: full charge and capacity check.
- Capacity test every 6 months for new batteries, or every 3 months if the battery has been in service.
- Insulation resistance test every 12 months, or earlier if a leak is suspected.
- Maximum storage 18 months if all of the above is observed.
- Electrolyte must not exceed 48.8 °C. Do not leave discharged. Trickle charging is not recommended.

**Silver-zinc and silver-cadmium batteries**
- Storage of more than 2 years: ask the manufacturer for special instructions.
- Charged batteries: discharge and recharge every 4 to 6 weeks.
- Long-term storage: discharge at the 40-hour rate until below 0.8 V per cell, and top up with KOH at 1.300 sg.
- No sulphuric acid or acid-contaminated utensils nearby.

**Nickel-cadmium (pocket plate)**
- Charge, top up, then discharge for 1 hour at normal rate before storage.
- Inspect every 6 to 9 months.
- Before service: double charge and capacity check.
- Sintered-plate cells: follow the manufacturer's instructions.

**Braided rubber cord**
- Store at no more than 18 °C (preferred), about 65% RH.
- Storage limiting period: 4 years from the date of manufacture. Cord issued from stores within that 4 years may stay in service until 5 years from the date of manufacture.
- Year colour code: French Blue 1986, Night Black 1987, Nato Green 1988, Service Brown 1989, Canary Yellow 1990, then repeating on a 5-year cycle.
- Quarter code: the number of coloured threads (1 = Jan to Mar, 2 = Apr to Jun, and so on).
- References: BS 2F 70 and 2F 71.

**Compressed gas cylinders**
- Oxygen cylinders stacked horizontally: no more than four high, with wedges.
- Acetylene cylinders stored upright, and never with oxygen.
- Full and empty cylinders in separate rooms.
- Breathing oxygen and welding oxygen segregated and labelled. Welding oxygen must not be used in aircraft oxygen systems.
- If a cylinder may have been damaged or overheated, tell the supplier before it is refilled.

**Fabric:** about 21 °C, away from sunlight. Discolouration such as iron mould is grounds for rejection.

**Instruments**
- Default storage limiting period: no more than 3 years, after which the instrument is re-certified to its Overhaul Manual.
- Gyro equipment: exercise and run the gyros for 24 hours at the end of each period of no more than 12 months in storage.
- Silica-gel that has turned from blue to pink should be dried or renewed.

**Paints and dopes**
- Store at 7 to 23 °C. Mark containers with the date received. Invert containers about monthly.
- Storage limiting period in original sealed containers: 12 months in the UK, or 6 months in tropical conditions.
- A Petroleum Act licence may be needed.

**Pipes**
- Flexible pipes: about 15 °C, in the dark, unstressed, hung vertically where possible.
- Blank all ends with blanks that cannot be left in place when the pipe is fitted. Rags and paper are prohibited.
- Do not wrap stainless steel or titanium pipes in chloride-based materials (they break down above 150 °C).

**Pyrotechnics:** examine at manufacturer-specified periods. Proof-test a proportion at regular intervals. A maximum serviceable life applies whatever the proof-test results.

**Rubber parts and components containing rubber**
- Temperature 10 to 21 °C, with heat sources at least 3 ft away. Some materials (such as neoprene) tolerate -12 to 26 °C, but check with the manufacturer first.
- RH about 75%.
- Keep dark, with no ozone sources.
- Seal check: stretch the seal to 20% of its internal diameter. Reject if cracks are visible at x10 magnification.

**Hydraulic and pneumatic components**
- "Nominal 7 year shelf life", which can usually be extended in 2-year periods by inspection.
- In some cases, operate the component several times at three-monthly intervals.
- Components filled with a storage-only fluid (for example DTD 5540B) must be labelled so the fluid is fully removed before installation.

**Tyres and inner tubes**
- Tyres: store vertically on two support tubes, with two-thirds of the tyre above them. Turn every 2 or 3 months.
- Inner tubes: keep in their cartons, or lightly inflated inside covers.

**Rubber hoses**
- Inspect and test every 2 years, and immediately before installation (unless the manufacturer says otherwise).
- Store uncoiled.

**Survival equipment (liferafts and life-jackets)**
- Store at 15 to 21 °C.
- Storage limiting period: normally 6 months. At the end of that period, open up and inspect before further storage; inspect, test and overhaul before operational packing.
- Re-test before installation if not operationally packed and stored for more than 10 days after the last test.
- Stacking: no more than 3 liferafts, or up to 10 life-jackets, with shock-absorbing separators.

**Tanks**
- Flexible tanks: some must be coated if left empty for more than 2 or 3 days. Store at no more than 25 °C, preferably below 15 °C.
- The leaflet defines "short term" and "long term" storage for flexible tanks.

**Acrylic sheets:** stack no more than 12 sheets.

**Electrically heated windscreens:** 10 to 21 °C.

**Wire rope**
- Reel diameter: 40 to 50 times the rope diameter.
- Hand coils: at least 50 times the rope diameter, and at least 152 mm (6 in).
- Do not make reels from oak, chestnut or western red cedar.

### E-10 Marking

- **Electro-chemical etch:**
  - Test-piece check at regular intervals. Depth should not normally exceed 0.025 mm (0.001 in).
  - Stencil life: paper about 200 impressions, plastics about 2,000.
- **Electrical (pencil) etching:** must not be used on aircraft parts.
- **Steel stamping:**
  - Not on aluminium alloy sheet thinner than 20 s.w.g., on high strength aluminium alloy parts, or on steel with UTS above 850 MN/m² (55 tonf/in²).
  - Type size 1.58 mm or 2.38 mm, with a "U" rather than "V" impression.
  - Do not use worn stamps.
- **Grit blasting:** not normally permissible on magnesium alloys. Not suitable for bearings or hollow parts.
- **Parts too small to mark** (springs, bolts under 6.35 mm diameter, nuts under 9.52 mm, split pins, taper pins): pack them in a sealed container marked with identification and inspection approval.
- **Nuts of 9.52 mm and over:** mark on the hexagon flats only.
- **Bolts:** mark on the head only, never the shank. Cold-headed bolts may omit the inspection stamp if they carry the maker's symbol and are in sealed, inspection-approved packs.
- **Serial numbers:**
  - Must not be duplicated between main contractor and sub-contractor for identical components.
  - Repair or overhaul organisations must not add extra serial numbers.

### E-20 Materials marking thresholds

- Individual stamping is required for:
  - billets and bars over 19 mm (0.75 in);
  - sheet and strip over 19 mm wide;
  - sections over 19 mm;
  - tubes over 25 mm (1 in), or over 19 mm for light alloy and steel tubes.
- Smaller items from the same cast or batch are bundled, with a stamped metal label.
- Small ingots, castings, forgings, stampings and bars: up to 6.5 mm (0.25 in) need not be individually marked.
- **Aluminium all-over marking:** green ink, 13 mm high characters, line pitch 100 mm, for widths of 152 mm and over.
- **Stretched material:** marked "CS" in a circle.
- **Off-condition material:** red marking "As Rolled", "Annealed" or "Not Aged", 13 mm high at about 101 mm intervals. Lengths over 5 m are marked at both ends.

### F-10 Oil and chalk

- **Hot fluid process:** about 28% lard oil in paraffin by volume, at about 80 °C. Chalk applied at about 0.70 kg/cm² (10 lbf/in²).
- **Bristol Modified Method:**
  - 50% paraffin and 50% spindle oil at 70 °C, for about one hour.
  - Degrease in 5% Teepol, 5% cresylic acid (BS 524 Grade A or B) and 90% water at 70 to 80 °C, for 3 to 5 minutes with agitation.
  - Hot water swill for 3 to 5 minutes.
  - Chalk applied at 4.22 to 5.63 kg/cm² (60 to 80 lbf/in²). Surplus removed at 1.75 to 2.11 kg/cm² (25 to 30 lbf/in²).
  - A crack shows as a thin white line of chalk.

## 5. Practice Guidance (for maintenance, stores and planning features)

### D-30 Clean rooms (relevant to Part-145 and BCAR component shops overhauling instruments, gyros, hydraulics and bearings)

- **Two room types.** Conventional rooms use turbulent mixing and are simpler to run. Unidirectional (laminar) rooms come as vertical down-flow, horizontal cross-flow or twin cross-flow, and need stricter entry control.
- **Personnel and parts flow** goes from "dirty" to clean. The route is: lockers, air shower (interlocked doors), wash-up area, change area with a tacky mat, a second air shower, then the clean room. Parts enter through interlocked pass-through boxes.
- **Construction.** Vinyl floors with radiused junctions to the walls. Flush, non-flaking walls with no structural timber. Gasketed ceilings. Fluorescent lighting serviceable from outside. Non-flaking ducting (stainless steel or aluminium). Pre-filters, then HEPA ("absolute") final filters.
- **Garments.** Smocks with no pockets and double-stitched seams; overalls; caps and hoods (masks where beards are allowed); nylon shoe covers; gloves. Supervisors can be identified by coloured neckbands. Garments are laundered in a dedicated three-zone laundry.
- **Discipline.** Selection screens for allergies, skin shedding and acid hands. Staff with colds or sunburn are kept out. No food, smoking, cosmetics, nail polish, jewellery, pencils, erasers or ordinary paper; use ball-point pens only. Tools are cleaned on a schedule. Visitors must be authorised.
- **Cleaning.** Cleaning takes place when no work is in progress. Benches are cleaned by the staff assigned to them.

### D-40 Storage: general principles

- Stores should be clean, ventilated and dry, at an even temperature. Use a wet/dry bulb hygrometer and check at regular intervals where conditions are specified.
- **Protective methods:**
  - Vapour phase inhibitor (VPI), used only where the part's manufacturer approves it.
  - Protective oils, with the method approved by the manufacturer.
  - Desiccants.
- **Racks and bins:** open racks are preferred. Painted metal bins are better than wood.
- **Segregation:**
  - Acid away from raw materials.
  - Phenolic plastics away from cadmium-plated steel.
  - Magnesium away from flammables, and not stored in sacks.
  - Oxygen away from acetylene and from oil or grease.
  - Acrylics away from the listed solvents and fluids (acetone, ammonia, AVGAS, AVTUR, benzene, Skydrol and similar phosphate esters, dopes, thinners, trichloroethylene and others).
  - Rubber away from copper, brass, manganese, PVC and ozone sources.
- **Long material:** store long lengths vertically.
- **Sheet:** store sheet on edge and lift it off rather than sliding it. Keep it off composition floors, and keep chloride floor cleaners away from metals, particularly austenitic steel.
- **Flammables** go in an isolated store that meets local fire authority rules.
- **Bearings:**
  - Clean in white spirit and do not spin them dry.
  - Re-protect with mineral oil and lanolin.
  - Discard brinelled or contaminated miniature bearings.
  - If special grease was specified on the order, re-apply grease of the correct specification.
- **Spark plugs:** inhibit, cap both ends, and keep in a heated cupboard.
- **Rigid tanks:** dry, blank, and fit a silica-gel cartridge.
- **Timber:** check moisture content periodically (see Leaflet 51-30).
- **Windscreens:** sandwich types are stored vertically with desiccant cartridges and must be able to "breathe" in transit. Heated windscreens are kept thick laminate uppermost, with the polythene film left on until fitting.

### E-10 / E-20 Part and material marking

- Choose a marking process that suits the material and how highly stressed the part is.
  - Avoid marking highly stressed areas, edges, holes, bends, changes of section, bearing surfaces, sealing surfaces and hardened surfaces.
  - In stressed regions, use etching or electro-chemical methods.
  - Electro-engraving is prohibited, and metallic stamps and vibro-percussion are not used at highly stressed locations.
- Mark before NDE (radiography) and before protective treatment such as anodising, unless the marking medium will not damage the coating.
- **Pipes:**
  - Do not use wrap-round or tie-on metal tags. They have worn pipes through and caused pin-hole leaks.
  - Use a soldered brass plate, electro-chemical etching, a non-corrosive dye, a slip-on sleeve or an adhesive label.
  - Pipe system identification follows BS M23.
- **Marking inks:**
  - No phenol inks on nylon, and no copper inks on rubber fabric (rescue equipment).
  - Do not use aluminium or zinc labels on nickel-base alloys unless the labels are cadmium plated.
- **Plastics:**
  - Glass-fibre reinforced plastic under stress: do not vibro-etch; use paint and ink instead.
  - Thermoplastics: heated dies.
  - Thermosets: vibro-etch, or paint and ink.
  - Transparent plastics: avoid marking. If unavoidable, mild sandblast on the inner face.
- **Wooden propellers:** steel stamps on the rounded part of the boss, with stage inspection stamps for timber and cementing, "in the white" and final inspection.
- **Metal propellers:** controlled acid etching.
- **Timber parts:** rubber stamp and ink, plus a date stamp so the age of the part can be found.
- **Fabric-covered components:** the stencilled serial number on the fabric must always match the permanent plate (fabric is periodically re-covered).
- **Serial numbers:** must be visible when the component is installed; a window or rip-off patch may be needed.

### E-30 / E-40 Fasteners (parts identification and IPC look-up)

- **The key safety warnings:**
  - Fasteners that look identical may not be interchangeable.
  - A shear bolt must never replace a tension bolt.
  - If in doubt, consult the Parts Catalogue.
  - American fasteners come in both UNC and UNF. Check threads with gauges; visual comparison of small threads is not recommended.
  - There is no marking symbol that distinguishes Unified from UNJ thread form.
  - AN machine-screw nuts and engine nuts (AN 355 and 360, which are thicker) should not be used in airframe locations.
- **British A series, BA/BSF threads:**
  - Code = standard number + plain-shank length in tenths of an inch + diameter letter (Table 2). Example: A57 31J is 3.1 in long, 3/8 in diameter.
  - Screws are coded in thirty-seconds of an inch (A41 C16). Grub screws are coded in sixteenths (A55 E8).
  - Bolts of 1/4 in and over carry the BS number on the head. Bolts of 7/16 in and over also carry the part number.
- **British A series, Unified threads:**
  - A102 to A212: length in tenths, then diameter (A102-10D).
  - Screws A204 to A221: diameter, then length in thirty-seconds (A217-A32).
  - A226 to A232: diameter, then length in sixteenths (A229-E16).
  - Unified symbols are contiguous circles, a recessed head or a dog point.
  - Finish shows the material: green dye = aluminium alloy, cadmium = high tensile steel, natural = corrosion-resistant steel or brass.
  - Obsolescent or replaced: for example A61 replaces A28, A175 replaces A172. The AGS series is entirely superseded.
- **SBAC AS series:**
  - Tables 5 to 8 list the AS numbers.
  - Round-head Unified bolts (AS6760 to 7308) use a unique number for each diameter and length.
  - Heat-resistant double-hexagon bolts (AS13000 to 23799, in DTD 5066, 5026 and 5077 materials) are marked with the AS number. The UNS-thread versions need AS20620 to 20639 nuts.
  - AS1 and AS2 titanium bolts are obsolescent.
- **AN early series:**
  - The last figure(s) of the number give the diameter in sixteenths (3 = No.10, 4 = 1/4 in). The dash number gives length in eighths.
  - Suffixes: A = undrilled; H = drilled head; C = corrosion-resistant steel; DD = aluminium alloy; "A" in place of the dash = UNC.
  - Screws, nuts and washers have their own codes (for example AN 500A6-32, AN 507C832R8, AN 960C-616L).
- **AN late series:** six-figure numbers (101001 to 150450), always material-marked. Look the size up in the specification tables.
- **MS series:**
  - Marked with the material or MS number.
  - Nut dash codes: -04 = No.4 up to -19 = 1 in.
  - Washers: MS 35338 replaces AN 935; MS 35333 and 35335 replace AN 936.
- **NAS series:**
  - NAS 1347 marking: Type I = material code; Type II = basic part number; Type III = part number plus material letter; Type IV = full part number.
  - Tri-Wing recess size is shown by an encircled number (NAS 4000).
  - Oversize repair bolts are marked "X" (0.0156 in oversize) or "Y" (0.0312 in oversize). Several ranges are "for repair work only".
  - Plating codes: W, B, H, P, U, A.
  - Locking codes: D, H, L, N, LK, NK, K.
  - Recess codes: T = Torq-Set, H = Hi-Torque, P or R = Phillips.
  - Material codes: CR, C or E = corrosion-resistant steel at 125, 140 or 160 ksi; V = titanium.
  - Colour codes: green dye on the shank end = cadmium-plated corrosion-resistant steel; red = cadmium-plated titanium.

### F-10 Oil and chalk NDT

- Suitable only for fairly large surface cracks, for example in rough non-ferrous castings. It is not suitable for minute or tightly closed cracks, and contrast is poor on dark (chromated magnesium) surfaces.
- Thorough pre-cleaning and drying are essential to avoid false indications.
- The cold process is only for parts that cannot be immersed (assembled structure, very large parts).
- Inspect when the part is cool, under strong light.
- The leaflet says these processes are largely superseded by penetrant and fluorescent methods (F-20). Ultrasonic (F-30), radiography (F-40) and magnetic (F-50) methods are covered in their own leaflets.

**StockItem / Batch fields:**
- part number, serial number, batch number, cast number
- specification reference and test report number

- shelf-life expiry date and extension count (for example hydraulic: 7 years, plus 2-year extensions after inspection)

- leaded steel flag
- "for repair only / oversize" flag
- release document reference (Approved Certificate, CAA Form 1, etc.)
- storage location
- segregation group

**Default shelf-life rules to seed.** These are guidance defaults that the manufacturer's figure overrides:
- Instruments: 3 years, then re-certify to the Overhaul Manual. Gyro run of 24 hours every 12 months or less.
- Braided rubber cord: 4 years in store; 5 years from date of manufacture in service.
- Paints and dopes: 12 months in the UK, 6 months in the tropics.
- Hydraulic and pneumatic components: 7 years, extendable in 2-year steps by inspection. Operate every 3 months where recommended.
- Rubber hoses: inspect and test every 2 years, and before installation.
- Survival equipment: 6 months. Re-test if not operationally packed and stored for more than 10 days since the last test.
- Lead-acid batteries: recharge every 4 to 6 weeks; capacity test every 6 months (new) or 3 months (ex-service); insulation test every 12 months; maximum storage 18 months; charge and capacity check before service.
- Silver-zinc and silver-cadmium batteries: cycle every 4 to 6 weeks when charged; more than 2 years needs manufacturer instructions.
- NiCd batteries: inspect every 6 to 9 months; double charge and capacity check before service.
- Tyres: rotate every 2 or 3 months.
- Paint containers: invert monthly.
- Desiccants: renew at specified periods and whenever a container is opened.
- Pyrotechnics: proof-test a sample periodically; hard maximum life.

**Alerts:**
- Shelf life expiring or expired, with the item blocked from issue until it is re-certified or inspected.
- Periodic storage task due: battery recharge, gyro run, component exercise, tyre rotation.
- Desiccant colour check.
- "Re-test before install" when survival equipment has been stored for more than 10 days since its last test.

**Issue logic:**
- FIFO / first-expiry-first-out ("strict rotation").
- Block issue of expired or quarantined items.
- Warn about a shear-for-tension fastener substitution and about UNC/UNF mismatches.

**Quarantine statuses:** Serviceable-in-store; Shelf-life expired; Awaiting re-certification; Rejected (for example, a seal cracked at x10 under a 20% stretch, or fabric with iron mould); Condemned (destroy the identification plate).

### Component record and traceability

- **Component** fields:
  - identification plate data (serial number, drawing number and issue, inspection date)

  - modification record list (mod numbers incorporated), to show the modification state

### Facilities and approval support (organisation module)

- Store environment log (hygrometer readings).
- Flammable store and gas cylinder store compliance checklist.

## 8. Possibly Outdated

Items marked "guess" are my own assessment, not stated in the text.

- **Leaflet dates:** all leaflets in this chunk are dated 15 April 2011. The CAP 562 amendments of 2020 and 2022 did not appear to revise these pages.
- **F-10 oil and chalk:** the text itself says these processes have "largely" been superseded by penetrant dye and fluorescent ink processes (F-20).
- **E-30, British fasteners:**
  - The text says the AGS series is "entirely superseded by other standards".
  - Many A-series and AS bolts are marked "obsolescent".
  - The "future trends" paragraphs (metric threads, titanium bolt specifications "being prepared", UNJF replacements whose AS numbers are "not, as yet, available", Unified marking to be discontinued "at some future date") are clearly decades old.
  - The leaflet also says the AN series has largely been replaced by MS and NAS.
- **D-30 clean room classes (guess):** the four-class Table 1 (Class 1 to 4 by particles per m³) follows the old BS 5295 scheme. Current BS EN ISO 14644-1:2015 uses ISO Classes 1 to 9. The cited 14644 editions (2000, 2001, 2004) have since been revised. The text also contains typos ("14664-4", "14664-5").
- **Hazardous substances (guess):** the guidance mentions asbestos as a filter medium; benzene, carbon tetrachloride and trichloroethylene as cleaning solvents; cresylic acid; and widespread cadmium plating. These are now heavily restricted or prohibited under UK health and safety law (COSHH, the asbestos regulations) and UK REACH. A software reference list should not present them as current recommendations.
- **Regulatory bodies (guess):**
  - "HM Inspector of Factories" has been replaced by the HSE.
  - The "Petroleum Act" licence for paint stores is now dealt with under the Petroleum (Consolidation) Regulations 2014 and DSEAR.
- **Air Navigation Order, Schedule 3 (guess):** the leaflet refers to the older ANO. The fireproof identification plate requirement now sits in the ANO 2016 (Part 2 and its Schedule on registration and marking), so the schedule number may differ.
- **Rubber cord colour codes:** the published colours cover 1986 to 1990, then "repeated in the same sequence for a further 5 years and subsequently until further notice". Any current code should be checked against the current BS 2F 70 and 2F 71 (guess).
- **BCAR Sections D, K and G (guess):** the material specification chapters (D4-1, K4-1, G4-1, C2-2) belong to BCAR design sections that have largely been withdrawn or replaced by CS/BCAR equivalents for new designs. They may survive only for legacy national-type aircraft.
- **"Approved Certificate":** a legacy BCAR release document. Under UK Part 21/145 practice, material and parts release now generally uses the CAA Form 1 or a supplier's certificate of conformity (guess).
- **EASA:** this chunk contains no EASA references.
