# Brief: Reading CAP 562 (CAAIP)

CAP 562, Civil Aircraft Airworthiness Information and Procedures (CAAIP), UK Civil Aviation Authority, Issue 4 Amendment 3 (2020, file revised 2022), 1,369 pages. It gives guidance supporting British Civil Airworthiness Requirements (BCAR), especially BCAR Section A (Issue 8 Amendment 1), and covers national (non-Part 21 / non-EASA-type) aircraft, organisation approvals, licensing, materials, processes, non-destructive testing and maintenance practice by ATA chapter. Leaflets are numbered by Part (A to H, general) and by ATA chapter (for example 20-10, 51-50, 70-90). Note: leaflets B-40 and 70-80 were superseded by separate Supplementary Amendments and their content removed from the full edition.

The reader is building Airworthiness+, a UK continuing airworthiness web app for owners, operators, Part-CAO, Part-CAMO, Part-145 and BCAR-approved organisations and licensed engineers. It already follows UK Regulation 1321/2014 (Part-ML, Part-M, Part-145, Part-CAMO, Part-CAO, Part-66, Part-147) and 748/2012. The aim here is to understand BCARs and the national (non-Part 21) regime: which aircraft and organisations it applies to, what approvals and certificates exist under it, and how CAP 562 guidance affects continuing airworthiness, maintenance, records, parts, licensing and the software.
