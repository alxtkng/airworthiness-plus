## 2. BCAR and National Regime

Little in this chunk explains the regime directly, but the following references matter:

- **"non-EASA aircraft" vs "EASA aircraft"** (34-30, 34-40). 34-40 states it "is only applicable to non-EASA aircraft". 34-30 gives parallel approval routes: BCAR route for non-EASA aircraft, EASA Part 21 for EASA aircraft. Today read as non-Part 21 (national) vs Part 21 aircraft.
- **Equipment approval routes (34-30 para 5)**:
  - Radio altimeters: "CAA or EASA approved in accordance with BCAR Section A Chapter A4-10", conforming to EUROCAE ED-30, EASA ETSO-2C87, FAA TSO C87 or RTCA DO 155.
  - AVADs: "CAA or EASA approved" under the "Accessory Procedure or Component Procedure of BCAR Section A, Chapter A4-8, Section B, Chapter B4-8 (non-EASA aircraft) or EASA Part 21 (EASA aircraft)".
- **Installation (modification) approval (34-30 para 6.1)**: "modification procedures of BCAR Section A, Chapter A2-5, BCAR Section B, Chapter B2-5 (non-EASA aircraft) or EASA Part 21 (EASA aircraft)".
- **Modification classification (34-40 para 3)**: design changes normally classified per **BCAR A8-21**; a TCAS 1 installation may be classified **MINOR** if all of: (a) equipment of a type approved by CAA/ETSO authorisation and previous installations approved; (b) AFM/POH supplement needs no limitations beyond the template; (c) no effect on existing flight deck instruments (displacing an instrument, mounting outboard on a bracket or on the glare shield may need a Flight Department assessment); (d) interfacing with existing sensors introduces no failure modes to existing systems (e.g. grounded altitude input affecting barometric display or Mode C); (e) TCAS 1 aural alerts do not inhibit other aural alerts.
- 34-40 references **BCAR Section A (CAP 553) sections A8-1, A8-2, A8-8, A8-9 and A8-21**, plus **BCAR Section K** and **BCAR Section G** (no content given on what each covers in this chunk; A8-21 is treated as the design-change classification chapter, others are organisation approval chapters in BCAR A).
- **Radio equipment approval**: ANO 2009 Article 39(8) (as amended): all radio communication and navigation equipment installed in, or carried for use with, a UK-registered aircraft must be "of a type approved by EASA or the CAA" for its purpose.
- **BCAR chapters in 39-10**:
  - **A4-8**: requirements for electrical aircraft components; definitions of terms; para 5 requirements for Controlled Items; para 6.1 quality control of components from overseas suppliers; para 7 DDP format.
  - **A4-2**: control of identification, reliability and modification standard of component parts within equipment.
  - **BCAR Section D6-1**: installation of electrical items; para 2 requires designers to consider "Unclassified parts and Equipment", which "shall be controlled ..... to a standard to ensure compliance with the relevant requirements of ....... Section D".
  - CS 25.1351 to 25.1363 for electrical aircraft components.
- **Component approval concepts (39-10)**:
  - "Approval under Component Procedures": authorisation of a DDP by an Approved Signatory in an Approved Organisation plus acceptance by the user.
  - "Accessory Approved": design authority additionally obtains CAA approval for the product. Does not absolve the user from checking suitability for the application.
  - Selection and use of Aircraft Components is normally the responsibility of an Approved Organisation with appropriate Terms of Approval under BCAR Section A.
  - "Approved Certificate" issued by a CAA Approved Organisation holding an Approval Number "DAI/ xxxx/ xx" or "AI/ xxxx/ xx" (old BCAR design-and-inspection / inspection approval numbering). It ensures the buyer gets what was ordered; it does not confer "CAA Approval" status; Design Approval by the CAA is not needed before release unless the order requires it. "CAA Release" should be requested only for items within BCAR scope.
- **51-20 para 4c**: purchasers of foreign-registered wooden aircraft should establish whether the type is "an EASA or Annex II type". EASA type → eligible for an EASA C of A. Annex II type → "eligible for either a UK C of A or a Permit to Fly". For wooden aircraft the CAA will require information on type of construction and glue used before issuing a C of A or Permit to Fly.
- **BCAR Section G**: 44-30 references **BCAR Chapter G4-3, para 5.2.7 as amended by Blue Paper G779** (offshore helicopter emergency exit illumination), plus "CAA Direction to Offshore Helicopter Operators dated 18 October 1985".
- Approval of persons for compass work (34-20): rectification of compass defects "may only be undertaken by an organisation approved by the CAA or JAA"; adjustment/tightening of compass mounting "only ... by an organisation approved by the CAA for such work or by an engineer licensed in the appropriate category".
- Wooden structure access holes (51-10 note): where no approved scheme exists, obtain one from the aircraft manufacturer or "an Organisation appropriately approved by the CAA/EASA".

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### Compasses (34-20)
- Compass/detector location set at design; not to be altered unless authorised by the Airworthiness Authority.
- Uni-directional current cables at least **2 feet** from compass where possible.
- **Compass safe distance**: minimum distance at which equipment produces max deviation of **1°** under all operating conditions, measured from magnet pivot to nearest equipment surface.
- Most adverse combination of electrical loads must not cause deviation over **2° (5° for light aircraft)**.
- Pivot friction test: deflect ~**10°**, hold **30 seconds**, both directions; difference within manual limits; no tapping.
- Damping test: deflect **90°**, hold **1 minute**; time to return through **85°** within manual limits.
- Do not store compasses on steel racks/shelves/cupboards.
- Datum compass distance typically **50 to 150 feet**; datum compass positioning within **5°** of cardinal/quadrantal is acceptable; conventional swing positioning limits commonly **±3° to ±5°**; plumb-bob malalignment usually max **1°** (example 76 ft 2 in separation, 4 in → 18 minutes).
- Fuselage-mounted engines: compensate with engines running, or at least re-check on four equally spaced headings with engines running.
- Electrical swing: final swing through **15°** increments; optical transfer reference target at least **half-mile** away; crosstalk measured at 90°, 180°, 270°.
- INS swing: position within **2°** of North; diurnal variation ~**0.1°** winter, **0.3°** summer, up to **0.5°** (known up to 1°) in high sunspot activity. Westerly variation added, Easterly subtracted.
- Residual deviations after correction must not exceed **3° (5° for light aircraft)** on any heading.
- Deviation card: headings at **45° intervals (30° for light aircraft)**; state if radio equipment on/off during swing.
- Full swing required whenever a direct-reading compass or separate compensator is installed; for remote systems after installing a complete system, changing standard or pre-indexed flux detector units, usually after changing a compensator. Otherwise a check swing (four headings 90° apart compared with card); any difference → full swing.
- Check-swing occasions (para 15.3): after check inspection if required by approved Maintenance Schedule; reported heading inaccuracies; modification/repair/major replacement involving magnetic material; shock (e.g. heavy landing); severe electrical storm or lightning strike; magnetic crack detection; change of operating area to different magnetic latitude; significant electrical/radio change near compass; magnetic freight load; after long-term storage.
- Compensators: set to neutral only on initial swing or new detector/compensator; otherwise leave at previous settings to trend coefficients.

### Radio altimeter / AVAD (34-30)
- ANO Schedule 6 since **1 February 1986**: audio warning below pre-set height and visual warning at pilot-selectable height when RA fitted. Police AOC alternative: both audio and visual at pilot-selectable height.
- Trigger height typically **100 to 160 ft**; must not coincide with common approach minima (**200 ft**); message within **0.5 seconds**; only on descent, inhibited on ascent; datum not alterable in flight (except Police AOC alternative).
- Voice warning required; tone alone not acceptable; compact information messages ("One fifty feet"); command messages not acceptable unless height monitoring ("Check height"); not inhibited by other audio warnings.
- Analogue display required; digital-only not acceptable; amber "DH" light acceptable as visual warning; single indicator acceptable in side-by-side cockpit if visible to both.
- Police AOC helicopters: RA must be powered in all flight phases including emergencies without crew action (recommended for others).
- RA/AVAD may also be operationally required for night and NVIS flights.

### TCAS 1 / TAS (34-40)
- Not to be connected to emergency or battery-supported busbar.
- Ground test must show correct bearing and range in each quadrant; EMC test required; flight evaluation at installer's discretion (targets of opportunity).
- Electrical Load Analysis update; continued compliance with **CAP 747 GR Nos. 4 and 6**; structural analysis of antenna installation required.
- AFM/POH supplement to Flight Manuals and MMEL Unit for approval; limitation "Navigation must not be predicated on the use of the TCAS 1 display"; no manoeuvre on TA without visual acquisition.
- Systems transmitting on **1030 MHz** require separate approval from the **National IFF/SSR Committee (NISC)**; some ACAS 1 equipment has generic NISC approval, others case-by-case (AIC 37/2006 Yellow 199, CAP 761).

### Electronic components (39-10)
- Overhaulers must obtain authorisation before substituting or deviating from spares listed in approved overhaul/maintenance manuals of a "controlled" item; alternatives must be authorised by an appropriate Design Organisation.
- Users of BS 9000 components expected to hold copies of the specifications and understand AQL sampling risk.

### ESD (39-20)
- Workstation RH ideally **30 to 50%**.
- Floor-to-bench bonding strap ~**2000 to 4000 ohms per linear foot**; wrist strap lead **200k ohms to 1M ohms**; work surface resistivity ~3000 ohms. Never connect work surface to building power ground.
- Ioniser: 8000V; range 13 cm (5 in), up to 61 cm (24 in) with 14 kN/m2 (2 lbf/in2) air boost; ozone below 0.05 ppm at 50 mm (limit for long-term exposure); discomfort above 1.0 ppm.
- Air ioniser blower run **2 to 3 minutes** before work.
- Static meters 30 to 50 000 V at 6.5 to 30 cm.
- Ground connecting strip: max **0.25 V** galvanic potential with joined material.
- Human body model 200 pF / 1000 ohms; table of charges (e.g. walking on carpet 35 000 V at 10-20% RH vs 1 500 V at 65-90%).
- No multimeter testing of ESD devices; no removal/insertion with power on; grounded soldering iron bits; periodic wrist-strap resistance checks.

### HIRF (39-30)
- HIRF requirements generally applied since early **1992** (JAR 25 in 1992, JAR 27/29 in 1997). Interim policies INT/POL/25/2 Issue 2, INT/POL/27, 29/1 Issue 3 Draft, INT/POL/23/1 Issue 1.
- Changes to electrical/electronic equipment treated as "HIRF significant" in the first instance; STCs and amended STCs assessed irrespective of original basis.
- New electronic systems replacing non-electronic ones with Major, Hazardous or Catastrophic failure classification must show HIRF compliance. Control system changes whose failure prevents safe flight and landing need aircraft test or similarity.
- Discuss with CAA early; acceptance depends on availability of original design data.

### Lightning (39-40)
- Copper strap / woven sheath at least equivalent to **1" x 26 SWG**; metallic foil tapes require routine erosion inspection; metal tip tank nose and tail not less than **0.080"** thick; bond navigation lamp, earth return and duct near tip.

### IFE (44-10)
- Existing IFE: applicable aspects to be incorporated into the AMP "within one year of publication of this Leaflet" to comply with Part M.A.302 (originally AN 60, September 2005; M.A.302 in force 28 September 2005).
- Named project manager in an approved organisation (contractually linked to the operator if external), identified to the CAA and liaising early with the assigned Surveyor.
- New AMP tasks (inspection, functional check, restoration, discard) require CAA approval before entry into service; crew procedures and training accepted by CAA before entry into service.
- Installer competence/scope described in the Part-145 MOE.

### Cabin monitoring (44-20)
- Must be on a bus not supplying systems needed for continued safe flight; flight crew manual power disconnect as close to the bus as possible; no cabin control; CBs not acceptable as the sole means; system deactivated during take-off, approach and landing.

### Helicopter escape (44-30)
- Exit lighting: two activation means (crew switch for all, automatic per exit when cabin more than half submerged); full brightness at least **10 minutes**; operational submerged to at least **50 feet**; identifiable from at least **20 feet** in clear water; survive single transverse fuselage separation; battery sized for routine testing.
- Baggage: ANO 2016 Art 106(4)(c) and CAT.OP.MPA.160: no loose items of significant size/weight in cabin unless secured even when inverted/flooded.

### Wooden structures (51-10, 51-20, 51-30, 51-40)
- All known/suspected trouble spots inspected regardless of log book history.
- Access holes cut only to approved drawings / repair manual; made good and protected after.
- Feeler gauge penetration of glue line = defective joint (wood must be dry, joint unloaded).
- Replacement screws usually identical length, one size larger; refit same number of shrinkage washers.
- 51-20: records must show dismantling/opening up to inspect an adequate sample of timber and glued joints; extend sample if defects found; applies whether or not on an approved maintenance schedule. Pre-1950 glues lose strength with age.
- Spruce: shipment stacking ~60 days; air drying 1 to 3 years; discard ~6 in from plank end before sampling; oven 100-105°C; moisture **15% ±2%**; Grade A density not less than **384.45 kg/m3 (24 lb/ft3)** at 15%; Izod not less than **6.78 J (5 ft-lb)**, tolerance 0.68 J; drop test 17.63 J (13 ft-lb), optional extra 8.81 J (6.5 ft-lb); reject fewer than **6 rings per inch**; grain slope limit **1 in 15 grade A, 1 in 12 grade B**; knots generally not over 1/4 in; reject pin-knot clusters, dead gum pockets, sapwood (blue stain); dote-infected wood burnt. **Test records related to plank; test pieces kept not less than 2 years.**
- Adhesives: BS EN 301:2006 WBP or MR; moisture 8 to 16% (resorcinol up to 20% for gluing but not for aircraft); clean utensils in warm water with 5% sodium carbonate; some adhesives not below 15°C; heat up to ~80°C; clamps checked ~10 minutes after assembly; full strength at least 2 days (repairs sufficient after 1 day); max moisture resistance 2 to 3 weeks at 21-24°C; test samples min 50 x 25 mm, 12-18 mm overhang, at least **75% wood fibre** on fracture; wet test 24 h cold water (15-25°C) after 2-3 weeks conditioning; storage not above **21°C**, never beyond shelf life, reject caked powder/gelled liquid; UF glue surfaces wiped with 10% acetic acid (not for RF); casein residues removed; close contact glue line max 0.125 mm; gap filling max 1.25 mm; cold setting 10-32°C.

### Metal structures (51-50, 51-60)
- Battery corrosion: clean with water and washing soda, rinse, dry, re-check after **24 hours**, then restore protection.
- Magnification over **8X** not to be used unless specified (except re-examining); mirrors 2X common.
- Flameproof lighting (e.g. BS 229) in fuel tanks / flammable areas.
- Magnetic flaw detection on aircraft: demagnetisation and residual magnetism test mandatory.
- 51-60: "high time" = age beyond half original design life aim; >50% thickness loss found shortly after major checks; corrosion repairs must be to an approved repair scheme; operators using non-OEM inhibiting compounds must justify fitness to the CAA and control procurement (B-180 App 51-3); corrosion control programmes "have mandatory status"; significant corrosion reported at once to manufacturer and CAA.

## 4. Records, Forms and Documents

- **Compass swing record form** (34-20 Table 1): columns (1) datum compass or base reading, (2) aircraft compass cardinal readings, (3) deviation, (4) deviations corrected for C or B, (5) aircraft compass quadrantal readings, (6) deviation, (7) all deviations corrected for A, (8) finally corrected readings for card. Coefficient formulas: C = (dev N - dev S)/2; B = (dev E - dev W)/2; A = sum of 8 deviations / 8.
- **Deviation / "Steer-by" card**: magnetic heading vs compass reading to steer, at 45° (30° light aircraft) intervals, positioned adjacent to compass, legible and secure; back of card shows aircraft type and registration, compass type and serial number, place and date of swing, signature and authority of the compiler; radio on/off condition stated.
- **Technical Log Book entry**: a record of the swing to be entered and certified.
- **AFM / POH Supplement** (34-40 App 1 template): aircraft type, AFM/POH reference, company name, supplement number and issue, registration mark, serial number, modification number; sections Limitations, Normal, Emergency, Abnormal, Performance; "CAA Approval" and date block; record sheet / log of supplements in manual amended (installer provides one if none). Supplement approved for a specific registration/serial applies only to that aircraft. Submitted by the Design Organisation to the Flight Manuals and MMEL Unit.
- **Electrical Load Analysis** and **structural analysis** (34-40, 44-20).
- **Declaration of Design and Performance (DDP)** (39-10, 44-10): format per BCAR A4-8 para 7, BS 3G100 Part 1 or ISO R224; authorised by an Approved Signatory of an Approved Organisation.
- **Approved Certificate** (39-10): issued by a CAA Approved Organisation (Approval No. DAI/xxxx/xx or AI/xxxx/xx); purchase order must reference the part/drawing number and require "CAA Release".
- **Overhaul Manuals** must contain information on special testing, selection or condition of component parts.
- **Certification Review Items (CRIs)** record HIRF field strengths used in the certification basis (39-30).
- **ICAW**, Maintenance Manual, Wiring Diagram Manual, Standard Practices Manual, Component Maintenance Manual, AMP and MMEL (44-10, 44-20).
- **Documented training needs analysis** (44-10 Box 8).
- **ESD packaging labelling**: unserviceable items repackaged in conductive packaging labelled as unserviceable but containing ESD devices; goods-inwards packages labelled if ESD devices but unmarked.
- **Aircraft records for wooden aircraft** (51-20): must evidence dismantling and sample inspection.
- **Spruce test records** related to plank; test pieces retained at least 2 years (51-30).
- Radiographs repeated under the same conditions for corrosion trending; endoscope photographs for comparison/record (51-50).

## 5. Practice Guidance

- **Compass (34-20)**: Pre-installation inspection (glass, bowl dents, liquid clear and free of bubbles/sediment, no leaks, compensator spindle smoothness, returned to magnetic neutral, lighting does not deflect); install with lubber line on/parallel to longitudinal axis (grid steering points forward, card type aft), non-magnetic brackets, fasteners and tools. Preparation: all equipment fitted, no magnetic tools, control locks stowed, tug effect checked, gear locks, struts inflated, flying controls neutral, flaps/throttles in flight positions, electrical loads per manual, supply voltage/frequency monitored. Routine inspection: mounting security, bubbles, discolouration, sediment, scale clarity, leakage, cracked glass, anti-vibration mounts, grid-ring lock, deviation card legibility.
- **Remote compass systems**: flux detector (26 V 400 Hz typical); pre-indexed vs master (pre-calibrated) detector units; changing a master unit usually needs only check swing unless mounting alignment altered.
- **ESD (39-20)**: conductive packaging, store ESD items in grounded metal racks separately from non-ESD items, never open packaging outside an ESD workstation.
- **HIRF (39-30)**: scale assessment to functional criticality from the system hazard assessment; check modifications have no adverse effect on existing systems (e.g. wiring changes).
- **Lightning (39-40)**: bond extremities and tip tanks; gliders - straight bonding runs avoiding loops at transport joints.
- **IFE (44-10 App 1)**: MSI identification questions (undetectable failure, safety effect, operational effect, economic effect); for each MSI define function, functional failure, failure consequence (ignition, heat, electric shock), failure cause (wiring damage, fluid spill, dust/debris overheat, overheating components, passenger damage, frequent removal, poor bonding, wear). Task types: 5a inspection (Detailed or General Visual, definitions given), 5b functional check, 5c restoration, 5d discard, 5e update maintenance practices/instructions, 5f training, 5g design/modification. 5a-5d go into the AMP (CAA approval); 5e documents amended and validated; 5f via training needs analysis; 5g via approved design process. Designer instructions must equal original production standards; "install in accordance with industry standard practices" is unacceptable.
- **Cabin monitoring (44-20)**: flammability CS 25.853/869, wiring per OEM standards and TGM/21/07, CB protection, EMI testing, restraint to ultimate inertia loads, no glare, no inadvertent control operation, no escape obstruction; integrated displays must not obscure required flight data.
- **Wood (51-10)**: external survey for gross deformation (may declare beyond economical repair), dry hangar, open all panels, smell test (musty = fungus/damp), no contour change acceptable for double-skin stressed structure, slight panting allowed for single skin if sound, check leading/trailing edges, trapped water at upper-surface panels, fabric splits (remove fabric), heavy dope and insertion patches as clues, glue-line scraping and feeler checks, discolouration and dry rot, screw corrosion comparison, bolt/bush removal at spar root and hinge fittings, clear drain holes, shrinkage, elongated bolt holes (steel bushes per repair manual), bruising, compression shakes (torch along grain), previous repairs, glue failure diagnosis (fibre adhesion good; grain imprint = case hardening; crazing/star patterns = pot life exceeded, so other joints suspect), heavy landing secondary damage remote from gear.
- **Metal (51-50)**: condensation and bilge drainage, thermal acoustic lining damage, boxed structure venting and drains, honeycomb sealing, faying surfaces/spot welds, top-hat bonded stringers, exhaust areas, stress corrosion (high-strength Al, titanium with saline), fretting (black/grey-brown powder; NDT), spillage (galley, toilets, animals with impervious sheeting, batteries, mercury), agricultural aircraft washing, fatigue at stress concentrations (periods by flight time, landings, load recorders or calendar), cleaning (approved fluids, vacuum, no air jets), loose articles (formed rivet = failure), protective treatment condition, chromate sachets. NDT: penetrant (F-20/F-40), radiography (F-60), ultrasonic (F-50), eddy current (F-80), magnetic (F-70), endoscope (F-90).

## 6. Software Implications

- **Compass swing record object**: aircraft, compass(es) (type, serial, direct/remote, detector type: standard / pre-indexed / master), swing type (full conventional / electrical / INS / check swing), place, date, compiler signature and authority, electrical/radio configuration, engines running flag, per-heading readings table with computed A, B, C coefficients and residual deviations; validation of residual limit 3° (5° light aircraft) and electrical-load deviation 2° (5°); auto-generate steer-by card (45° or 30° intervals). Link to Technical Log entry certification.
- **Check-swing trigger rules / alerts**: on events tagged in defect/work order modules: compass or compensator replacement (full swing), flux detector change (full unless master unit), heavy landing, lightning strike / electrical storm, magnetic particle inspection performed, modification involving magnetic material or electrical/radio near compass, change of operating region magnetic latitude, magnetic freight, return from long-term storage, scheduled check per AMP, reported heading errors.
- **Compass safe distance** as an attribute on installed equipment records.
- **Modification records**: approval route field (BCAR A2-5 / B2-5 / Part 21 / now UK Part 21), classification (minor/major per A8-21), with TCAS 1 minor-classification checklist (5 conditions). AFM/POH supplement register per aircraft (supplement number, issue, mod number, approval date, approving unit; registration-specific flag). Link ELA and structural analysis documents.
- **Equipment approval data**: TSO/ETSO/ED references, accessory vs component approval, DDP reference, NISC approval reference for 1030 MHz transmitters.
- **Parts receiving**: Approved Certificate / CAA Release document type with issuer approval number (DAI/AI legacy formats), controlled vs uncontrolled item flag, substitution requires design organisation authorisation; ESD-sensitive flag with packaging/labelling checks at goods-in and storage segregation.
- **Tooling/facility records**: ESD workstation and wrist strap periodic resistance checks; moisture meter periodic calibration (51-30, 51-40); static meter checks.
- **IFE / cabin STC tracking**: list of STCs on aircraft with holders, contact links, project manager, ICAW references; MSI analysis records with task type (5a-5g); tasks feed AMP revision requiring CAA approval; reliability feedback to STC holders; AMP review flag for IFE content.
- **Helicopter offshore equipment**: emergency exit lighting test tasks, battery capacity, escape windows inventory; applicability rule: guidance applies to helicopters not certificated to CS 29 Amdt 5 or later.
- **Wooden aircraft module**: aircraft construction material attribute (wood, glue type, torsion box) driving inspection programmes; sample-inspection records (areas opened, findings, extension of sample); storage condition (open / hangar / long dry storage) influencing inspection extent; adhesive batch records (shelf life expiry, storage temp max 21°C, pot life, mix time, batch test piece result with % wood fibre, wet test); timber batch/plank test records (moisture, density, Izod/drop, grain slope, rings per inch, grade A/B) with 2-year test piece retention reminder.
- **Corrosion programme**: CPCP tasks as mandatory; ageing-aircraft flag (beyond half design life); corrosion findings with level, reportable flag (report to manufacturer and CAA); approved compounds list with justification records for non-OEM compounds; agricultural operation flag triggering wash/inspection tasks; battery-area recheck at 24 h; animal carriage flag.
- **NDT method reference list**: penetrant (F-20/F-40), ultrasonic (F-50), radiography (F-60), magnetic (F-70, with demagnetisation and residual magnetism check plus compass check-swing trigger), eddy current (F-80), endoscope (F-90).
- **Inspection level definitions**: General Visual Inspection and Detailed Inspection (44-10) as a reference enum on task cards.
