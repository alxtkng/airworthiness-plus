## 2. BCAR and National Regime

### 2.1 BCAR Section L licences (national AME licence) — H-20 / H-30
- BCAR Section L = "Aircraft Maintenance Engineer's Licence" (national). Categories: 'A' (aeroplanes/rotorcraft, maintenance excluding overhaul), 'B' (aeroplanes/rotorcraft overhaul), 'C' (engines maintenance excluding overhaul), 'D' (engines overhaul), 'X' (Instruments, Electrical, Automatic Pilots, Combined Instrument/Automatic Pilots, Compasses), 'R' (Radio). Combined 'A and C' Rotorcraft licence.
- A licence can only be used to certify for non-commercial air transport. CAT aircraft must be maintained by a Part-145 organisation with certifying authorisations (cross-ref Leaflet C-20).
- "As of November 2006, new BCAR Section L Licences shall not be issued except where required to certify (EC) Regulation 216/2008 Annex II aircraft." (i.e. now "non-Part 21" aircraft).
- "As of 28 September 2006 a BCAR Section L licence cannot be used in support of certification of aircraft exceeding 5700 kg MTOM" (stated under Category X Instruments).
- As of 28 September 2008, certification of maintenance on aircraft using a licence as authority is limited to items not listed at Part-M Appendix VII.
- Regulation 2042/2003 has no provision for overhaul licences, so Section L categories B and D do not transfer to a Part-66 B1. CAA was "considering the continuance and issue of National overhaul licences" for Annex II types; new applicants for a national airframe overhaul licence (Section L B) would likely first need to meet Part-66 AML criteria.
- Annex II types (not adopted under 2042/2003) continue under National licensing; CAA continues to issue BCAR Section L licences for them "for the time being"; anticipated future endorsement of Annex II types in an annex to a Part-66 licence, with certification basis remaining National Requirements.
- H-30 header note: CAA proposed to replace BCAR Section L with a document aligned with EASA regulation once Airships are in Part-66 (amendment including ELA aircraft, Balloons and Airships expected "late 2014 to mid 2015").
- Applicability (Section L only): a Group Type Rating holder may certify for a type provided (a) holds valid UK CAA Type Rated Licence and (b) an aircraft of the type is UK registered and holds a UK-issued C of A. For types not covered by a group or by name, a type rating application will be considered if an aircraft of the type is UK registered with UK C of A and does not exceed 5700 kg MTOM.

### 2.2 Part-66 national privileges (ANO)
- Part-66 B1 (and B2, via an Annex endorsement) issued by UK CAA may allow certification "under the provisions of Articles 25, 26, 28, 29, 30 and 31 of the Air Navigation Order 2009" (B2 text lists 25, 28, 29, 30, 31) for work outside a CAA approved maintenance organisation on non-CAT aircraft; limited to UK registered aircraft; only for types endorsed individually or as Group Type Ratings (B2: "BCAR Section L Group Type Ratings"); limitations mirror relevant Section L categories.
- Part-66 licences issued on "protected rights" may contain technical restrictions equivalent to former Section L licence or company authorisation limits; conversion to full Part-66 standard is optional.
- Part-66 licence may not give full equivalence in another Member State for National legislation outside Part-145; additional language requirements may apply.

### 2.3 Certificates issued under a licence (H-60 para 2.1)
- Certificates of Maintenance Review, Certificates of Release to Service, and "Certificates of Fitness for Flight under the 'A' Conditions Flight Release Certificate", plus similar certificates for Annex II aircraft.

### 2.4 Maintenance schedule requirements by regime (5-10 intro)
- ANO 2009 Article 19: all aircraft with a C of A must be maintained to a schedule "Approved" by the CAA.
- EASA CAT: Part M.A.302 requires an "Approved" Maintenance Programme.
- Permit to Fly aircraft: BCAR A3-7 states "maintenance arrangements must be agreed with the CAA" (tacitly a schedule is required).
- Ex-military aircraft: BCAR A8-20 states a Maintenance Schedule is required.
- Aircraft below 2730 kg MTOM: CAA and EASA permit generic "Light Aircraft Maintenance Schedule" (LAMS), references CAA/LAMS/FW (fixed wing) and CAA/LAMS/H (helicopter); CAP 411 and 412; no application needed to use LAMS. LAMS users must also consider manufacturer recommendations.
- Aircraft above 2730 kg MTWA not CAT: from 1 April 1995 must be maintained to a CAA Approved Maintenance Schedule (5-50); SMP 19 / Leaflet 5-40 + manufacturer schedule; approval by Form AD271.
- C of A renewal recommendation may be made to CAA by an organisation approved under "BCAR A8-3 Supplement No.2" when the schedule is CAA approved (5-50 para 1.2).

### 2.5 BCAR chapters cited in this chunk
- BCAR Section A Chapter A6-2 / Section B Chapter B6-2 (A5-3): certification, duplicate inspection of vital points/control systems, CMR and CRS information; A6-2 Appendix No.1 sets minimum content of a Condition Monitored Maintenance Programme Document.
- BCAR A6-2 also cited (5-10 para 3.6.1) as guidance on "Engine Maintenance Programme" (EMP) content for turbine engines (text says A6-2; possibly a different numbering intended).
- BCAR A6-7 / B6-7: CRS cross-reference in log books.
- BCAR A3-8 / B3-8: Certificate of Fitness for Flight ('A' Conditions).
- BCAR A4-2: Engine Inspection and Test Certificate data.
- BCAR A2-5: modification procedure.
- BCAR A3-7: Permit to Fly maintenance arrangements.
- BCAR A8-20: ex-military aircraft (maintenance schedule required).
- BCAR A8-3 Supplement No.2: organisation recommending C of A renewal.
- BCAR Sections A and B: structural fatigue lives mandatory for UK register.

### 2.6 C of A categories and CRS exemptions (5-70 para 5.1.5)
- CRS required for all types of work except: aircraft in the Special Category not exceeding 2730 kg MTWA, and aircraft in the Special or Private categories of similar weight, when the work is within the Air Navigation Regulation list of work an owner/operator who is a licensed pilot may personally do. Such work recorded and signed in log book by the person doing it. CAA may direct a CRS be issued in exceptional circumstances; C of A then endorsed accordingly.
- Some aircraft do not necessarily require a CMR or CRS by virtue of C of A category and MTWA, but C of A validity still depends on compliance with Approved Maintenance Schedule and mandatory requirements.

### 2.7 Other named approvals / documents
- "Approved Welder" (Approval number to be recorded on weld repairs).
- "Approved Test Certificate" (organisation approved for propeller balancing inspection).
- "Airworthiness Approval Note (AAN)", "CAA Form AD 261" reference (modification authorisation), CAA Additional Airworthiness Directive, STC.
- Form AD271 (Schedule Approval Document); Forms AD981C, AD981D (LAMS-based), AD981M (application form for other programmes); AC968NR (CAA approved aircraft radio installation).
- CAP 747 Mandatory Requirements for Airworthiness; GR No. 24 (piston engine overhaul life extension for non-CAT).

## 3. Obligations and Numbers

### 3.1 H-20 licence privileges and limits (Section L)
- Category 'A' (from earlier part): electrical systems — 9.1 types: CRS may be issued; 9.2–9.4: replacements only, provided functioning checks need no test apparatus. Autopilots — 13.1: CRS; 13.2: replacements only (no test apparatus). Radio: no CRS except VHF comms replacement only if MTOM not exceeding 2730 kg. Compass compensation and adjustment: not unless licence endorsed.
- Category 'B' Aeroplanes: CRS for overhaul, repair, replacement, modification and mandatory inspection on non-CAT aeroplanes/rotorcraft (not engines) for which type rated; responsible for all parts except those of C or D holders; excludes overhaul of electrical, instrument or autopilot systems; no manufacture of parts. May also certify engine mounting structures and cowlings, engine controls, engine fuel/oil/coolant systems, engine fire extinguishing, engine fluid de-icing (except parts forming part of / attached to the engine).
- Category 'B' Rotorcraft: as above but excludes overhaul of electrical, instrument, autopilot systems, main and tail rotor blades; limited as sub-para 6.5.
- Category 'C' Engines: maintenance (excluding overhaul) of non-CAT engines and APUs type rated. Excluded work: dismantling piston engine beyond piston/cylinder access or rear accessory cover for oil pump (no internal gear removal); dismantling turbine main casings/rotating assemblies (except per symbols); removal/dismantling of reduction gears (except BS (DH) Gipsy Queen 70 after suspected shock loading); propeller balancing (except static balancing where MM requires, and dynamic balancing on installed propellers with electronic equipment where permitted; work may be by a trained LAE or approved organisation issuing an Approved Test Certificate); welded/brazed joints except minor weld repairs to exhaust units by an Approved Welder (excluding component replacement); disturbing bench-tested units except normal in-service replaceable/adjustable items.
- Turbine engine dismantling symbols (glyphs lost in extraction; two symbols): first symbol — removal/replacement of main casings excluding those whose removal also removes a rotating assembly; second symbol — includes those, provided solely by disconnecting casing flanges; no dismantling of main rotating assemblies in either case.
- Category C engine-associated instrument/electrical/autopilot systems: CRS by aircraft system class (8.1 / 9.1 / 13.1 full; 8.2–8.4 / 9.2–9.4 / 13.2 replacements only without test apparatus; paragraph 10 aircraft or 13.3 — none).
- Where no limitations shown, CRS may be issued for replacement, modification, repair, mandatory inspection or scheduled maintenance inspection; but the Maintenance Schedule Approval Document may require certification by an 'X' (or 'R') licensed engineer.
- Category 'D' Engines: overhaul etc. of non-CAT engines; responsible for functioning only as far as test bed performance; installed engine functioning certified with a Category 'C' holder; excludes ignition apparatus, instrument and electrical equipment; no making of parts.
- Categories 'A' and 'C' Rotorcraft: maintenance (excl. overhaul) of rotorcraft and its engines; excluded: bolted joints needing special techniques; complete riveted/glued joints, bonded assemblies or composite primary structures; welded/brazed joints (except minor weld repairs by Approved Welder); piston engine dismantling limits; turbine casing limits; gearbox casing dismantling (except separation for internal inspection per manufacturer); bench-tested unit disturbance. May not certify engines in aeroplanes. Systems: instruments and electrical on 7.4 rotorcraft replacements only; autopilot/stabiliser 13.4/13.5 replacements only; radio none except VHF comms replacement if MTOM ≤ 2730 kg.
- Category 'X' Instruments / Electrical / Automatic Pilots: maintenance excluding overhaul of the rated systems; no disturbance of bench-tested units. Automatic stabilisation deemed automatic pilots; autopilots include yaw/roll dampers, mach trim and autothrottles. Combined X Instrument/Autopilots = both ratings.
- Category 'R' Radio: maintenance excl. overhaul; if endorsed for overhaul of radio apparatus, may certify workshop periodic check/repair/overhaul; if endorsed for GPWS, may certify GPWS maintenance excluding overhaul or scheduled maintenance inspection.
- Compass compensation and adjustment: H-30 para 15 rating permits CRS for direct and remote reading compasses on any aircraft.

### 3.2 H-20 Part-66 categories
- Category A: minor scheduled line maintenance and simple defect rectification; no privileges until a Certification Authorisation from a Part-145 organisation; tasks per AMC 145.A.30(g); task training per type. Unrestricted B1 holder qualifies as Category A in same sub-category.
- B1: sub-categories B1.1 Aeroplanes Turbine, B1.2 Aeroplanes Piston, B1.3 Helicopters Turbine, B1.4 Helicopters Piston. Scope: airframe, engines (incl. APU), electrical power generation/distribution incl. lighting and electrical components in mechanical systems, avionic LRUs where serviceability shown by BITE or simple test (organisation to keep a list per type). No overhaul certification. Part 145.A.30(h) requires B1/B2 as prerequisite for base maintenance inspectors/supervisors. Limited privileges under M.A.801(b)2.
- B2: instruments, autopilots (incl. autoland), radio comm/nav, radar, electrical power for avionic systems. Not electrical power distribution or electrical components in mechanical systems. No overhaul. Cannot hold Cat A authorisation unless Cat A licence held.
- B3: 'piston-engine non-pressurised aeroplanes of 2000 kg MTOM and below'; similar to B1, not type rated; B1.2 holders automatically qualify and get B3 at next submission.
- C: release following base maintenance, whole aircraft, Part-145 only; supported by B1/B2. C licences "at present only apply to aircraft greater than 5700 kg MTOM". C cannot act as B certifying staff unless B1/B2 also endorsed. B1/B2 with type endorsements eligible for C type authorisation if basic C held.
- Since 28 September 2008 a CRS may be issued on a non-large, non-CAT aircraft on the basis of a Part-66 licence with type rating under Part-M, limited to items not in Part-M Appendix VII.
- Differences training may be required; responsibility for determining it remains with Part-145 organisation.

- Category 'B' Aeroplanes not granted for: unpressurised aeroplane > 5700 kg MTOM; primary structure of reinforced plastic/epoxy; any pressurised aeroplane.
- Category 'D' Engines not granted for piston engine > 500 kW (670 bhp) or any jet/propeller-turbine engine (not available for new issue).
- Category 'B' Rotorcraft not granted for turbine rotorcraft > 2730 kg MTOM.
- Group ratings for A and C: sub-paras 5.0, 5.1, 5.7, 5.7.1, 5.9.1, 6.0, 6.3, 7.1, 7.3 only. Category B groups: 5.1, 5.7, 5.7.1, 7.1, 7.3 (excluding rotorcraft > 2730 kg). Category D groups (existing licences): 6.0 and 6.3, includes rotorcraft and airship engines, excludes > 500 kW. Legacy groups (5.2, 5.2.1, 5.3, 5.3.1, 5.4, 5.5, 5.5.1, 6.1, 6.2, 6.3.1, 6.4) continue for existing holders. Items marked * not obtainable as new endorsements.
- Aeroplane groups: 5.0 Composite aeroplanes ≤ 5700 kg (not Cat B); 5.1 Wooden and wood-and-metal; *5.2 unpressurised metal ≤ 2730 kg; *5.2.1 same fixed gear; *5.3 unpressurised metal ≤ 5700 kg; *5.3.1 same fixed gear; *5.4 unpressurised metal excl. para 14; *5.5 pressurised metal ≤ 5700 kg and all unpressurised metal excl. para 14; *5.5.1 pressurised/unpressurised metal ≤ 2730 kg; 5.6 reserved; 5.7 unpressurised metal ≤ 5700 kg; 5.7.1 unpressurised metal ≤ 2730 kg; 5.8 unpressurised metal > 5700 kg; 5.9 individual pressurised metal > 2730 kg ≤ 5700 kg; 5.9.1 pressurised metal ≤ 2730 kg.
- Engine groups: 6.0 Diesel engines in aeroplanes; *6.1 unsupercharged piston (not diesel) fixed pitch propeller; *6.2 same fixed or variable pitch; 6.3 Cat C piston engines in aeroplanes (not diesel) / *Cat D piston ≤ 500 kW in aeroplanes/rotorcraft/airships; *6.3.1 piston in aeroplanes ≤ 2730 kg; 6.4 jet-turbine ≤ 22.25 kN (5000 lbf) static thrust incl. APU where endorsed; 6.5 individual propeller turbines (Allison 250, Garrett AiResearch TPE 331, P&WC PT6, Turbomeca Astazou); *6.6 jet turbine > 22.25 kN.
- Rotorcraft: 7.1 piston-engined; 7.3 turbine ≤ 2730 kg (incl. Allison 250 and Turbomeca Arriel with symbol); 7.4 individual turbine types > 2730 kg: Aerospatiale SA 365 (Arriel), Agusta 109E (PW206), Bell 212 (PT6T), Bell 222 (LTS 101), Bell 412 (PT6), Eurocopter EC135 (Arrius, increased AUW), EC155 (Arriel), MBB BK 117 (LTS 101), McDonnell Douglas 900 (PW206, increased AUW), MD902 (PW206), Sikorsky S76 (Allison 250 / PT6B / Arriel), Westland W30 (RR Gem), Westland S55 (BS Gnome).
- Category X Instruments: 8.1 general instrument systems excluding aircraft with Flight Director; 8.2 Smiths Flight System, Sperry Zero Reader ZL1/ZL2; 8.3 FD systems with air-driven attitude gyros; 8.4 FD with electrically driven gyros (excl. 8.2); 8.8 compasses (compensation/adjustment). 8.2/8.3/8.4 include 8.1. X Instruments includes INS, GPWS and compass systems (excl. compensation) whether separately endorsed or not.
- Category X Electrical: 9.1 DC main generation (incl. alternators with self-contained rectifiers) with secondary alternators ≤ 1.5 kVA; 9.2 DC main with frequency-wild alternators > 1.5 kVA for auxiliary services; 9.3 frequency-wild AC main with TRU DC; 9.4 constant-frequency AC (CSD or VSCF) with TRU DC. 9.2/9.3 include 9.1.
- Category R Radio: *12.1 Airborne Communication; 12.2 Communication and Navigation; 12.2.1 comm/nav in aircraft below 5700 kg excluding HF, passenger entertainment, multiplex, CVR, satcom; 12.3 Airborne radar; 12.3.1 radar in aircraft below 5700 kg excluding weather radar and TCAS. GPWS only if endorsed.
- Category X Autopilots (aircraft ≤ 5700 kg): 13.1 non-radio-coupled (aeroplanes); 13.2 radio-coupled excl. ILS; 13.3 ILS-coupled (LOC and GS); 13.4 non-radio-coupled (rotorcraft); 13.5 radio-coupled (rotorcraft). 13.2 includes 13.1; 13.3 includes 13.1 and 13.2; 13.5 includes 13.4.
- Para 15: compass compensation and adjustment. Para 16: Part-66 B1/B2/C type ratings per Appendix 1 to Part-66 AMC. Para 17: type lists on CAA website.

### 3.4 H-40 (sailplane/balloon authorisations in Part M Subpart F org)
- Procedure submitted via CAA Regional Office; once approved, held in the MOM.
- Age ≥ 18.
- Experience for full (entire aircraft) authorisation: 4 years relevant maintenance, or 2 years with approved training course. Restricted authorisation: period agreed by CAA, not less than 2 years.
- Knowledge: training course with practical evidence, or demonstration + documented experience + assessment covering regulations, operational requirements, MOM, type sample knowledge, maintenance practices.
- Records: assessment results, syllabi, names and positions of assessors.
- Continued validity: at least 6 months actual relevant maintenance experience in any consecutive 2-year period.
- Authorisations cannot be used outside a Part-M Subpart F organisation; become obsolete once Part-66 sailplane/balloon provisions in force.

### 3.5 H-50 (Railways and Transport Safety Act 2003, Part 5 in force 30 March 2004)
- Applies to flight and cabin crew, ATCOs and licensed aircraft maintenance engineers in the UK; also crew of UK-registered aircraft worldwide.
- s.92 unfit through drink/drugs; s.93 prescribed limits; s.94 includes preparing/standby and ancillary activities; s.95 penalty (same as aircrew/ATC under ANO 2009 Art. 241); s.96 tests; s.97 arrest without warrant; s.98 right of entry (board aircraft/enter any place, reasonable force).
- Limits for aircrew/ATC: breath 9 µg/100 ml; blood 20 mg/100 ml; urine 27 mg/100 ml.
- Limits for licensed aircraft maintenance engineers: breath 35 µg/100 ml; blood 80 mg/100 ml; urine 107 mg/100 ml.
- No random testing; testing on reasonable suspicion or after an accident ("unintended event with adverse physical effect").
- Failure to provide a specimen without reasonable excuse is an offence.
- Disclosure to employer/professional body only with authority of Assistant Chief (Constable) rank officer.
- CAA protocol: if dependency posing flight safety risk, licence may be suspended (or recommendation to foreign issuing authority); treatment and rehabilitation; suspension lifted on success.

### 3.6 H-60
- ANO 2009 Art. 33(8): must not exercise licence privileges if knows or suspects physically/medically unfit.
- Part 66.B.500: competent authority must suspend, limit or revoke licence where clear evidence of maintenance/CRS while adversely affected by alcohol or drugs.
- Organisation management must review the leaflet and implement policies/procedures.
- Hearing test: average conversational voice in quiet room at 2 metres (6 feet); failure requires an audiogram. Control of Noise at Work Regulations 2005.
- Medication: personal trial for at least 24 hours before duty. Alcohol: may be unfit even 8 hours after heavy drinking. Anaesthetics: may need 24 or 48 hours before return.
- Use of "pep" pills while working cannot be permitted.

### 3.7 Chapter 5 numbers
- Utilisation: annual utilisation stated in schedule; review if actual varies by more than 25% (5-10 says "within plus or minus 25%").
- Schedule data reviewed at least annually.
- Flying hours calculated "Take-Off to Touch-Down".
- Fuel system water drain checks at periods not exceeding 24 hours elapsed time.
- Portable valise liferafts: at overhaul period, 10% of all liferafts in fleets using system bottle and release mechanism to be inflated and tested.
- Typical permitted extension about 10% (5-10 para 6.5); further one-off extension needs CAA approval with TCH support.
- Permitted variations (5-30 Appendix A and 5-40 Appendix C):
  - Flying hours: ≤ 5000 h period → 10%; > 5000 h → 500 flying hours.
  - Calendar: ≤ 1 year → 10% or 1 month, whichever lesser; > 1 year ≤ 3 years → 2 months; > 3 years → 3 months.
  - Landings/cycles: ≤ 500 → 10% or 25, whichever lesser; > 500 → 10% or 500 (5-30 App A) / 10% or 50 (5-40 App C) — the two tables differ; flag inconsistency.
  - Multiple limits: the more restrictive applies.
  - Items under CAA trial extension programme (5-30 only): max 50 flying hours unless excluded.
  - Not applicable to: ultimate (scrap)/retirement life components (fatigue-limited primary structure, uncontained high-energy rotating parts); periods classified mandatory by CAA (CAP 747); Annual Inspection periods of schedules that are alternatives to CAA/LAMS/FW or CAA/LAMS/H (5-30). 5-40: CAP 747 may override; Annual Maintenance Review not variable.
  - Only for circumstances not reasonably foreseeable; decision only by Chief Inspector/Quality Manager or equivalent (5-40: acceptable as signatory for the check); every variation entered in log book(s).
- Compass check swings (5-40): ≤ 5700 kg MTWA every 2 years max; > 5700 kg every 3 years max; may be varied with CAA approval where inertial reference allows in-flight cross-check.
- CVR/FDR: ANO 2009 Art. 153(b) data record for retention every 12 months.
- Battery capacity checks default: lead acid 3 months; Ni-Cad 4 months (if manufacturer does not specify).
- 5-60: fleet minimum of 5 aircraft normally needed for statistical reliability element (5-10 says fleets of six or fewer give insufficient data, monitor events instead of trends). CMM programme needed even for single aircraft if MRB/MSG introduced type has condition monitoring tasks.
- Alert levels: base period 2–3 years; one-monthly or three-monthly; new type first 2 years all malfunctions significant. Pirep methods need minimum 12 months data (1.3 × Mean; Mean + 3 SD; Mean + SD of mean of means + 3 SD). Component removal methods need 7 quarters (21 months) (Mean + 2 SD; Poisson 95% cumulative; Weibull). Confirmed failures: corrected Mean + 1 SD (quarterly rates outside ±50% of uncorrected mean replaced by the mean). Large fleets may use 12 months with one-month comparison. Alert levels may range from 0.00 per 1,000 h to ~70 Pireps per 1,000 h (ATA 25). Alert level changes normally require CAA approval. Displays cover minimum 12 months; engine shutdown trend over 2–3 years. Annual programme review with CAA.
- Engine unscheduled shutdowns, pireps per 1,000 h or per 100 departures; delays per 100 departures; component removals per 1,000 h.
- 5-70: log entries as soon as practicable, no more than 7 days after expiry of the CMR in force at time of occurrence; engine/VP propeller aggregated flight time entries at time of work requiring next CRS; retention until 2 years after aircraft/engine/propeller destroyed or permanently withdrawn.
- Log book formats: ≤ 2730 kg MTWA must be CAA approved — CAP 398 (aircraft), CAP 399 (engine), CAP 400 (propeller). > 2730 kg any form acceptable to CAA — CAP 408 (aircraft), CAP 391 (engine), CAP 388 (propeller). Modification Record Book required > 2730 kg MTWA — CAP 395.
- 5-50: Aircraft > 2730 kg not CAT maintained to CAA Approved Maintenance Schedule from 1 April 1995; submit two copies of SMP 19 plus manufacturer schedule to CAA regional office.
- MRB formed for type certification of aircraft > 5700 kg MTOM.

## 4. Records, Forms and Documents

- **Certificate of Release to Service (CRS)**: issued by licence holder within privileges (H-20); for repairs by person appropriately authorised for scope; for welding repairs the CRS by person taking responsibility plus welder's Approval number; for checks per manufacturer programme, CRS to cross-refer CAA Maintenance Schedule Approval reference (5-40 para 15.1).
- **Certificate of Maintenance Review (CMR)**: governs log entry timescales (7 days after expiry); BCAR A6-2. (Note acronym clash with Certification Maintenance Requirement.)
- **Certificate of Fitness for Flight** ('A' Conditions Flight Release Certificate), BCAR A3-8/B3-8: log book endorsed with reason and specific 'A' Condition; copy included in log book.
- **Duplicate inspection** certification (BCAR A6-2): recorded in log book or cross-referred (e.g. Technical Log). Required after disturbance of vital points / flying or engine control systems, with free movement, range, direction and tension checks.
- **Maintenance Schedule Approval Document** (Form **AD271**): issued on CAA approval with "CAA Approval Reference" (format MS/....); certification requirements and endorsements; may require 'X'/'R' licensed certification for scheduled inspections.
- **SMP 19 / Leaflet 5-40 document** fields: manufacturer's manual references and revision status (airframe, engine, propeller); aircraft type, registration(s), serial number(s); Compliance Statement signed by Organisation/Operator person with date; CAA use: Schedule Approval Reference MS/..., CAA stamp; anticipated annual utilisation (hours and cycles); Supplementary Appendix A (equipment manufacturer requirements, reference list incl. manuals, SBs, STCs, mods, life limits) — columns: Date Entered, System, Detail, Reference Information, signature of person submitting, CAA approval and date; Appendix B (operator-required tasks from SBs, SLs) — same columns, can be amended without CAA approval; Appendix C variations.
- **Annual review statement** (5-40 para 15.3) in Aircraft Log Book: "Annual review carried out in accordance with: CAA APPROVED MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE REF: ___ Signed: ___ Approved Organisation Ref: ___", per AD271. Review content minimum: (1) mandatory mod, AD and inspection status; (2) audit of aircraft records; (3) conformity with CAA approved schedule; (4) conformity with CAA approved radio installation (AC968NR); (5) outstanding deferred defects; (6) effectiveness of schedule; (7) aircraft and equipment life limits. 5-50: at C of A renewal an annual check in the form of a Maintenance Review with a log book statement.
- **Forms AD981C** (programme compliance checklist submitted with new schedule), **AD981D** (for LAMS-based programmes), **AD981M** (application for other programmes).
- **CAP 543** "Time Limited Task, Additional Inspections and Component Change Record" — used with LAMS to control overhaul lives of engines, propellers, equipment.
- **Maintenance schedule introductory pages** (5-30): schedule reference, anticipated annual utilisation, applicability table (Registration, Type, Serial No.; changes need prior CAA approval), statement on flying times, CAP 520 certification requirements for ≤ 2730 kg, certification of maintenance per Approval Document, variations, AD/SB evaluation, fatigue lives, practices, vital points, fuel drain, liferafts, zonal definition, inspection standards, CMM/Reliability Programme document reference.
- **Condition Monitored Maintenance Programme Document**: content per BCAR A6-2 Appendix No.1; within or referenced by AMS; objectives, applicable items, glossary, data, displays, analysis, corrective action, amendment procedures, responsibilities, org chart, quality management.
- **Reliability data records**: component unscheduled removal report (component identity, precise reason, aircraft registration and location, date and airframe hours/running hours/landings at removal, component hours since new/repair/overhaul/calibration); workshop report (confirm/deny defect, cause, corrective action, replacement items); Fleet Reliability Summary (aircraft in fleet, in service, operating days less checks, total flying hours, average daily utilisation, average flight duration, total landings, delays/cancellations, technical incidents); figure examples show revenue/non-revenue/training hours and landings, delays > 15 minutes, interrupted flights, shutdowns, fire warnings, fuel dumpings; engine removals display (unscheduled with reason/investigation/follow-up, scheduled, HSI and overhaul approved lives); component table (ATA ref, part number, number per aircraft, unscheduled removals, failure rate per 1,000 h, non-confirmed, alert level, cumulative confirmed failures, MTBF); workshop report (serial no., aircraft & position, TSR/TSN, defect, results, conclusions, remedial action, report ref, part no.).
- **Technical Log** (Pireps entered by flight crew; log book defect entries reference tech log).
- **Log books** (5-70): aircraft log book content (manufacturer, type, manufacturer's number, date of manufacture; nationality and registration marks; operator name and address; date of each flight and take-off-to-landing duration, or number of flights and total per day; maintenance; defects in aircraft or ANO-required equipment and rectification with tech log reference; overhauls, repairs, replacements, modifications). Engine log book: same identity data, each aircraft fitted, operators, either per-day flight times or aggregate since last maintenance on engine; maintenance; defects; overhauls etc. incl. accessories. VP propeller log book: identity, each aircraft and engine type/number fitted, operators, times as engine, maintenance, defects, overhauls etc. Plus record of continued compliance with all applicable mandatory requirements.
- **Engine Inspection and Test Certificate**: fixed in engine log; data per BCAR A4-2, date and reference of CAA Approval Letter or latest Engine TCDS, category of release (e.g. Experimental Flight, Transport Category – Passenger), inspection/test record references, list of individually life-controlled parts.
- **Module logs** for modular engines: identity, history, flight times and cycles, maintenance; life-limited parts with limiting parameter and life remaining; data plate module carries whole-engine identity and module change record; each module has manufacturer/overhauler certificate equivalent.
- **C of A for Export** copy attached when raising log books for imported items.
- **Modification Record Book** (CAP 395) for > 2730 kg MTWA.
- **Component cards / component listings**: date and aircraft hours (or other parameter) at fitting and remaining life.
- **Approved Certificate** or acceptable foreign certification for overhauls — cross-referenced and retained.
- H-40 assessment records: results, syllabi, assessor names and positions.

## 5. Practice Guidance

### 5.1 Maintenance schedule compilation (5-10)
- Sources: OEM/TCH data (MPD for large aircraft; MM Chapter 5 for smaller). Intervals in flying hours, cycles, calendar or combinations; first-occurring governs. Tasks may be done early; later only with CAA agreement in exceptional circumstances.
- Adjust for operation profile (hours per cycle), environment (salt water, sand, rough strips), aircraft age (ageing structure, significant repairs), engine/propeller/APU fit, modification state.
- MRB (aircraft > 5700 kg): ISC, working groups, Policy and Procedures Handbook; STC on MRB type may require review / MRB.
- MSG logic (owned by ATA); MPD contains MSG-3 tasks plus ALIs.
- Mandatory task types: CMR* (one star – cannot change without state of certification approval), CMR** (two star – changes need approved procedure and monitoring, agreement of state of registration); terminology may be reversed by some TCHs; CMRs must be identified in the schedule; ALIs (fatigue/damage-tolerance critical structure, treated as CMR*, may be in MRB supplement, not MPD); CDCCL (fuel system design features, in ALI section); other mandatory lives (engine rotating parts, helicopter transmission, landing gear) — control method in schedule.
- Zonal inspections: GVI-level security and condition of everything in zone.
- Lubrication: monitor effectiveness; reschedule if check cycle or operating pattern changes.
- Inspection levels: General Visual Inspection (within touching distance, available lighting, mirror); Detailed Inspection (supplementary lighting, mirrors/magnifiers, cleaning); Special Detailed Inspection (NDT, boroscope, intensive cleaning); Functional Check (quantitative).
- Structural inspections are flight-cycle based; threshold and repeat.
- Turbine engines: Condition Monitored EMP (part of the programme); SOAP, mag plugs, boroscope; module lives; rotating parts cyclic lives; workscopes and strip reports.
- Piston engines: manufacturer's recommended overhaul life treated as THE life; non-CAT may extend per CAP 747 GR No. 24.
- Reliability programmes required (M.A.302(d)) for MSG-based or condition-monitored programmes; non-"Large" aircraft exempt; small fleets monitor events.
- Annual review with maintainer for small aircraft/fleets.
- Low utilisation programmes; SSIP frequencies vary by operation.
- Task escalation with supporting data; ISC pooled data.
- One aircraft = one approved schedule at a time; moving schedules requires "B" amendment to both and a Bridging Check. Registration change or adding aircraft requires amendment. Mixed fleets: task effectivity per aircraft (e.g. STC tasks for specific aircraft).
- Work packaging: LAMS grouped by frequency (50 h, 100/150 h) usable as worksheets; large aircraft by ATA; equalised programmes replace A/B/C/D letter checks, increasing CAMO burden for repeat inspection control.
- Repairs with damage tolerance interim inspections must be added to schedule.
- AWO, RVSM maintenance must be in schedule.

### 5.2 Standard clauses (5-30)
- Vital points: after work, check for loose articles; disturbed control systems get duplicate inspection plus full/free movement, range, direction, tension checks.
- Inspection without removal/dismantling unless stated; definitions per TCH, JAR, BCAR or World Airlines Technical Operations Glossary; standards per TCH, else CAP 562 or FAA AC 43-13-1A.
- Fuel quality: operator monitors supplier.

### 5.3 Non-CAT aircraft supplementary items (5-40)
- Pre-flight per flight/operations manual; tyre condition and inflation in pre-flight (large aircraft multi-wheel: Leaflet B-180 Appendix 32-2); avionics maintenance programme where manufacturer silent; compass swings; CVR/FDR; battery capacity; safety equipment serviceability, overhaul and retirement per manufacturer; post-manufacture equipment (autopilots, FD) requires Appendix A update; structural modifications assessed; fatigue lives mandatory; corrosion control and inhibitors.

### 5.4 Condition Monitored Maintenance (5-60)
- Three primary maintenance processes: Hard Time (preventive, time in service; overhaul/replace), On-Condition (preventive, periodic inspection/test to standard; not "fit until failure"), Condition Monitoring (not preventive; data collected/analysed for corrective action; only for items whose failure has no direct adverse effect on safety).
- Selection without MSG: Hard Time where failure directly affects airworthiness and wear exists; hidden function not checkable in situ; economic; life sampling; warranty. On-Condition where inspection/test determines deterioration. CM where no direct adverse safety effect and no age-reliability relationship.
- Statistical reliability element can be applied to HT/OC items as Quality Surveillance.
- Reliability Control Committee: headed by Quality or Engineering Manager (responsible to CAA); members typically Reliability Engineer/Co-ordinator, Chief Development Engineer, Chief Production Engineer; frequent meetings; formal CAA review meetings.
- Data sources: Pireps (tech log, routed daily), engine unscheduled shutdowns, mechanical delays/cancellations (ATA 100 order), component unscheduled removals and confirmed failures, MEL dispatches, incidents, air turn-backs, line/hangar/workshop findings, AIDS, SBs, other operators.
- Alert Levels are indicators, not minimum airworthiness levels.
- Problem identification: immediate individual analysis (MORs, safety reports, fleet campaigns) then collective trend analysis.
- Corrective actions list: operational/fault-finding procedure changes; maintenance scope/frequency changes; modifications; non-routine inspections; materials/fuels/lubricants; repair agencies; spares sources; storage conditions; training and literature; programme policy changes.
- Threshold sampling: hard time "threshold upper limit" verified/varied by samples; ends with fixed Hard Time; engine release period set by lowest unit hard time limit (e.g. turbine disc lives).
- Definitions (5.2): Analysis, ATA 100, Check, Condition Monitoring, Document, Failure Mode, Hard Time Limit, Hidden Function (active function with no crew indication on cessation; inactive function with no prior indication of unavailability), Item, Maintenance Significant Items, MEL, On-Condition, Overhaul, Partial Overhaul, Pireps, Programme, Quality, Quality Control, Quality Surveillance, Redundancy (Active, Standby), Replace, Scheduled Maintenance, Servicing, Test.

### 5.5 Log books (5-70)
- Electronic/alternative records acceptable if: ANO compliance; indelible hard copy or safeguarded computer memory printable on demand; total quarantine of an aircraft/engine/propeller record on command; defences against fraud, malpractice, incompetence, power failure; no erase function once committed; maintains update timescale; transferable without loss; coherent, legible print-out, preferably discrete from extra data. ANO 2009 Art. 231 applies to all technical records. Subsidiary records (NDT, test houses) are legally part of the log book and must be cross-referenced.
- Recording practice: all mandatory requirement sources (state of design + CAA Additional Directives); flag repetitive requirements; record optional inspections adopted, or justification for not adopting strongly recommended ones; record compliance method/options and extent of progressive compliance; imported items: attach Export C of A copy; after C of A renewal rule off page, total columns; adjustable-pitch propellers sensibly treated as VP (log book) or recorded in aircraft log.
- Replacements: life-controlled parts status readily established; component lists/cards; record in manufacturer's life parameter; on-condition parameters recorded progressively; identity of items removed/fitted, life available/expended, reason; transfer of parts between major components with remaining life, origin and prior location; recovered parts acceptable only with genuinely known history and condition established by approved person/organisation.
- Repairs: summary, reason, supporting docs, CRS; proof of origin of materials/parts unless CAA approved organisation with procurement control; welder Approval number.
- Modifications: title and authorisation (AAN, AD 261, ASB, SB, approved design organisation document, acceptable foreign authority); supporting docs (drawings, STC) listed/cross-referred; mandatory mods flagged and cross-referenced to mandatory compliance record.
- Aircraft log: engine and propeller identity (fixed-pitch propellers only recorded here); rigging data with datums and tolerances.
- VP propeller: basic pitch setting recorded whenever set/altered/verified; blade identities maintained (log associates with hub).
- Upkeep: ink or indelible pencil; attachments secure, not obscuring; continuation books numbered consecutively with continuity data; column totals carried forward; errors corrected legibly and signed; electronic corrections by new entry annotated as correction with authorisation code, not erasure.
- Transfer: previous keeper retains records unless new operator demands custody (must comply, responsibility transfers); new operator updates records for new situation/ownership; continuing an alternative records system by a new owner/operator/maintenance organisation requires prior CAA approval.

### 5.6 Human factors (H-60)
- Fitness, fatigue (shift work, overtime; UK Operators Technical Group), stress (management duty to identify and minimise), eyesight (near and distance; correction worn as prescribed; colour discrimination for wiring and airside driving; organisations to identify requirements), hearing, drugs/alcohol, medication three questions (Must I take it? 24 h personal trial? Do I feel fit?), alcohol + sleeping tablets/antihistamines dangerous. Appendix 1: sleeping tablets, anti-depressants (stop work when starting), antibiotics, antihistamines, pep pills (not permitted), blood pressure drugs, anti-malarials.

### Maintenance programme
- `MaintenanceSchedule`/`Programme`: reference, type (LAMS CAA/LAMS/FW, CAA/LAMS/H, manufacturer-based SMP 19, alternative, MRB-based AMP, Permit maintenance arrangements, A8-20 ex-military), CAA approval reference (MS/...), AD271 issue/date, approval forms (AD981C/D/M), applicable aircraft list (registration, type, serial — changes require CAA-approved amendment), manufacturer manual references and revision status (airframe/engine/propeller), anticipated annual utilisation (hours and cycles), amendment history (type "B" amendment, CAA approval status), linked CMM/Reliability Programme document reference and issue, Appendix A/B/C content.
- Constraint: an aircraft can be on only one approved schedule at a time; transfer workflow creates Bridging/Alignment Check task set and requires amendments to both schedules.
- `Task`: ATA reference, description, task type (CMR*, CMR**, ALI, CDCCL, mandatory life, zonal, lubrication, GVI, DET, SDI, functional check, servicing, hard time, on-condition, condition monitored), intervals (FH, cycles/landings, calendar; threshold and repeat), "whichever first" logic, effectivity per aircraft (mods/STCs), source (MPD, MM ch5, SB, SL, AD, SMP, operator), mandatory flag (blocks variation), trial-extension flag.
- Tolerance / variation engine implementing 5-30 App A / 5-40 App C tables (note the > 500 cycles difference: 500 vs 50) with exclusions (retirement/scrap lives, CAA mandatory periods/CAP 747, LAMS annual inspection, annual maintenance review). Variation record: task, original due, new due, reason (unforeseeable), approved by (Chief Inspector/Quality Manager role), date, log book entry generated. Further extensions beyond tables need CAA one-off approval with TCH support (separate status).
- Utilisation monitor: compare actual annual utilisation to anticipated; alert if deviation > 25%; prompt schedule review. Annual schedule review reminder (at least annually).
- Annual Maintenance Review checklist (7 items from 5-40 para 15.2) with log book statement template and sign-off by approved organisation reference; aligned to C of A renewal (5-50).
- Default SMP tasks to seed where manufacturer silent: compass swing (2 y ≤ 5700 kg, 3 y > 5700 kg), FDR/CVR data record every 12 months, battery capacity check (lead acid 3 months, Ni-Cad 4 months), fuel water drain ≤ 24 h, liferaft 10% inflation at overhaul, safety equipment per manufacturer, corrosion programme consideration, tyre checks pre-flight.
- Vital point / control system work: auto-require duplicate inspection record with movement/range/direction/tension check fields.
- Piston engine overhaul life: treat manufacturer recommended TBO as hard limit; allow extension only with CAP 747 GR 24 programme record (non-CAT).
- Turbine EMP: module lives, LLP cyclic lives, SOAP/mag plug/borescope trend records.

### Reliability (CMM)
- Data objects: Pirep (tech log entry, ATA chapter, date, aircraft), DelayCancellation (cause, ATA system, duration, corrective action; > 15 min threshold in examples), EngineShutdown (indications, cause), ComponentRemoval (part number, serial, reason, aircraft reg, position, date, airframe hours/landings, component TSN/TSO/TSR/since calibration, scheduled/unscheduled), WorkshopReport (confirmed/NFF, cause, action, replaced items), FleetSummary per period.
- Metrics: rates per 1,000 FH, per 100 departures, per 1,000 component hours; 3-month running averages; MTBF/MTBUR.
- Alert level calculators: 1.3 × mean; mean + 3 SD; mean + SD(mean of means) + 3 SD; mean + 2 SD (7 quarters); Poisson 95% cumulative; Weibull; corrected mean + 1 SD (±50% correction). Store method per programme; alert level versions with CAA approval status; recalculation after known changes.
- Alerts when rate exceeds alert level; workflow to corrective action (types list) and follow-up; MOR flagging; committee meeting minutes; annual CAA review.
- Minimum data checks: warn if fleet < 5 (or ≤ 6) aircraft — switch to event monitoring; new type first 2 years: treat all malfunctions as significant.

### Records / log books
- Separate logs per aircraft, engine, variable-pitch propeller (and engine module logs); adjustable-pitch propeller option to treat as VP.
- Entry timeliness check: flag entries made > 7 days after expiry of CMR in force at time of occurrence.
- Immutability: no deletion after commit; corrections as new annotated entries with authorisation code; audit trail; quarantine (freeze) of a record set on command; printable coherent export separate from extra data; transfer/export of complete records without loss.

- Modification record: title, authorisation type (AAN, AD 261, ASB, SB, design org doc, foreign authority), supporting docs (STC, drawings), mandatory flag cross-ref. Separate Modification Record Book for > 2730 kg MTWA.
- Repair record: reason, supporting data, CRS signatory and authorisation, parts/material proof of origin (unless approved org), welder approval number.
- Engine Inspection and Test Certificate attachment with category of release and LLP list; Export C of A attachment for imports; Certificate of Fitness for Flight copy with 'A' Condition reference.
- Owner-pilot maintenance (Special/Private category ≤ 2730 kg): entries signed by the person performing work without CRS.
- Flight time recording on take-off-to-touch-down (or landing) basis; per-day aggregation; engine/propeller aggregated hours since last work.
- Log book format reference list: CAP 398/399/400 (≤ 2730 kg), CAP 408/391/388 (> 2730 kg), CAP 395, CAP 543.
