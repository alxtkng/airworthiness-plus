## 2. Obligations (who, what, when, limits)

### 2.1 Error capture and independent inspection (AMC3/AMC4 145.A.48(c)(2), AMC1/GM1 145.A.48(c)(3))
- Organisation defines error-capturing methods adequate for the work and system disturbance; may need combinations (visual inspection, operational check, functional test, rigging check).
- Independent inspection (one possible method): task done by the "authorised person"; inspected by an "independent qualified person" (IQP) who attests satisfactory completion and no deficiencies; IQP does not issue CRS and need not hold certification privileges. The authorised person issues the CRS / sign-off only after a satisfactory independent inspection.
- Work card system must record identity of both persons and inspection details before the CRS or sign-off.
- IQP qualification: procedures to show training and experience in the specific inspection. Candidates: holders of certifying/support/sign-off authorisation for that critical task; or for similar task on a similar category product plus specific practical training; or a commander holding a limited certification authorisation under 145.A.30(j)(4) with training and experience.
- How to inspect control systems: disturbed parts for assembly and locking; full and free movement over full range; cable tension and clearance at secondary stops; correct sense of operation; interconnected systems checked through full range; software in a critical task checked (version, compatibility with aircraft configuration).
- Reinspection: same person acts as authorised person and IQP; only in unforeseen circumstances where only one person is available. Not "unforeseen" if no suitable IQP was assigned to that line station or shift. CRS issued after satisfactory reinspection; work card records reinspection details first.
- Procedures must: sign off each task only after completion; define grouping of tasks so critical steps are clearly identified; ensure supervised work (temporary staff, trainees) is checked and signed by an authorised person; ensure no single person removes/installs or assembles/disassembles several components of the same type fitted to more than one system (where failure affects safety) on the same aircraft or component during a particular check (reinspection allowed in unforeseen single-person cases). GM: plan different persons for the same task in different systems.

### 2.2 Certificate of release to service (145.A.50)
- (a) CRS issued by appropriately authorised certifying staff on behalf of the organisation once satisfied all ordered maintenance was properly done per MOE procedures (145.A.70), considering maintenance data (145.A.45), and there are no known non-compliances which endanger flight safety.
- (b) CRS must be issued **before flight at the completion of any maintenance**.
- (c) New defects or incomplete work orders found during maintenance must be raised with the person/organisation responsible for continuing airworthiness (owner/CAMO/CAO) to get agreement to rectify or complete; if they decline, (e) applies.
- (d) Components maintained off-aircraft: CRS issued by certifying staff; **EASA Form 1** (Part-M Appendix II) is the component CRS unless M.A.502 or ML.A.502 says otherwise. Own-use components may not need a Form 1 if MOE internal release procedures provide for it.
- (e) If unable to complete all ordered maintenance, CRS may be issued within approved aircraft limitations; the fact must be entered on the aircraft CRS before issue.
- (f) AOG away from main line station or main base for lack of a properly released component: the contracted organisation may temporarily fit a component without the appropriate release certificate for **a maximum of 30 flight hours or until the aircraft first returns to the main line station or main maintenance base, whichever is sooner**, subject to agreement of the person/organisation responsible for continuing airworthiness, and the component having a "suitable release certificate" and otherwise meeting maintenance and operational requirements. Component must be removed by that limit unless a proper release certificate is obtained meanwhile (145.A.50(a) and 145.A.42).
- AMC1 145.A.50(e): deferment needs agreement of the person/organisation responsible for continuing airworthiness; details of deferment, including the competent authority of the State of Registry, endorsed on the CRS. If in doubt about their authority to defer, inform own competent authority before issuing CRS. Procedure must say what mechanic, supervisor and certifying staff do, and keep 145.A.30(b) nominated persons informed in writing of such possible non-compliance situations.
- Maintenance check flight case: where the AMM requires an in-flight task, certifying staff release the incomplete maintenance before flight (within aircraft limitations); after the flight and any further work, a full 145.A.50(a) CRS is issued. Operator retains responsibility for the maintenance check flight (MCF) (GM M.A.301(i) / GM1 ML.A.301(f)).
- Standard Changes / Standard Repairs: release per AMC M.A.801 / AMC1 ML.A.801.

### 2.3 Records (145.A.55) - retention periods
- Maintenance records, CRSs and associated maintenance data: **3 years** from the date the aircraft or component was issued with a CRS.
- Copy of each CRS plus detailed records needed for M.A.305 / ML.A.305 must be provided to the operator or customer.
- On termination of operation: transfer records covering **the last 3 years** to the last customer or owner, or store as specified by the competent authority.
- Airworthiness review records (if 145.A.75(f) privilege): copy of each ARC and all supporting documents, retained **3 years after ARC issue**; available on request to the owner; on termination transfer last 3 years to last owner or operator, or store as authority specifies.
- Management system key process records (145.A.200) and contracts (contracting and subcontracting, 145.A.205): **minimum 5 years**.
- Personnel records: for as long as the person works for the organisation, and **at least 3 years after the person leaves or after an authorisation issued to them is withdrawn**.
- Staff right of access to own records on request; copy furnished on leaving, on request.
- AMC default: absent other indications, all records kept minimum **3 years**. Retention period starts when the record is created or was last amended.
- Electronic systems: at least one backup **updated within 24 hours of any new entry**; backup hardware stored in a different location from working data; safeguards against unauthorised alteration; data must stay accessible through hardware/software changes.
- Independent audit and feedback records: retain for the 145.A.55(c) period (5 years) or longer if needed to support changes to the audit planning cycle, whichever is longer (AMC4 145.A.200(a)(6)(f)).

### 2.4 Occurrence reporting (145.A.60)
- Establish occurrence reporting system (mandatory and voluntary) within the management system. EU-based organisations may use a single system for Reg (EU) 376/2014 and 2018/1139.
- Report to (b) competent authority **and** the design organisation of the aircraft/component (design approval holder: TC, RTC, STC, ETSO authorisation, major repair/change approval; or declarant of design compliance under Part 21 Light): any safety-related event/condition that endangers or could endanger aircraft, occupants or any other person, in particular accidents and serious incidents.
- (c) Also report aircraft events to the person/organisation responsible for continuing airworthiness (M.A.201 / ML.A.201); component events to whoever requested the maintenance.
- (d) Non-EU organisations: initial mandatory report **as soon as practicable, but in any case within 72 hours** of becoming aware, unless exceptional circumstances; protect confidentiality of reporter and persons mentioned; in the form set by the authority; with all pertinent information. Follow-up report with preventive actions as soon as identified, to the same recipients.
- (EU organisations follow Reg 376/2014 timelines, also 72 hours, which are not restated here.)
- AMC: assign one or more qualified persons to coordinate occurrence action and investigations; if several, one main focal point with a single channel to the accountable manager. Integrated system across multiple certificates allowed; single report only if it covers all certificate perspectives and mandatory data fields, identifies all certificate holders, same competent authority, and agreed with that authority.
- AMC2: share relevant reports with the design approval holder/declarant.
- GM: Regulation (EU) 2015/1018 lists mandatorily reportable occurrences but is not exhaustive; AMC 20-8A gives further detail.

### 2.5 Maintenance procedures (145.A.65)
- Procedures covering human factors and good maintenance practice (including subcontracted work), agreed with the competent authority.
- A clear work order or contract agreed with the requester **before** maintenance, defining work so that release under 145.A.50 is possible (GM: use Appendix XI to AMC M.A.708(c) or Appendix IV to AMC1 CAMO.A.315(c) contract elements).
- Procedures cover all aspects including specialised services (e.g. NDT) and the standards worked to.
- AMC: keep procedures current; staff report differences via internal safety reporting; verify and validate procedures and changes before use where practicable; design to human factors principles (GM: involve users, accuracy, user expertise, environment, explain reasons, critical step order, consistent terminology, simplified English for English documents).

### 2.6 MOE (145.A.70)
- Establish and maintain an MOE with the 17 items listed in section 3. Initial issue approved by competent authority; amended so it stays up to date.
- Amendments outside the scope of the "changes not requiring prior approval" procedure, and amendments related to 145.A.85(a) changes, need authority approval.
- Accountable manager signs compliance statement; CEO countersigns if the AM is not the CEO. New AM signs "at the earliest opportunity".
- Designate a person responsible for monitoring and amending the MOE. Personnel familiar with MOE parts relevant to their tasks.

### 2.7 Privileges (145.A.75)
- Subcontracting limit: not a base maintenance check of an aircraft, nor a complete workshop maintenance check or overhaul of an engine or engine module (AMC: anything not requiring disassembly of the core engine/core module is allowed).
- Away-from-base maintenance only for unserviceability or occasional line maintenance support, per MOE conditions.
- Line maintenance locations: minor maintenance only, only if MOE permits and lists them.
- Airworthiness reviews/ARCs: only if specifically approved, for Part-ML aircraft, principal place of business in a Member State, under ML.A.903.

### 2.8 Changes (145.A.85)
- Prior approval needed for: (1) certificate/terms of approval; (2) persons under 145.A.30(a), (b), (c), (ca) (accountable manager, nominated persons, compliance monitoring manager, safety manager); (3) reporting lines between those nominated persons and the AM; (4) the procedure for changes not requiring prior approval; (5) additional locations (other than 145.A.75(c) away-from-base).
- Apply before the change; supply documentation; implement only on receipt of formal approval (145.B.330); operate under authority conditions during the change.
- AMC timeframes: certificate change application **at least 30 working days** before intended change; planned change of a nominated person, inform **at least 20 working days** before; unforeseen changes notified at the earliest opportunity.
- AMC2: changes requiring prior approval need a risk assessment provided on request.
- Name change: apply "as a matter of urgency" for certificate re-issue.
- Unplanned nominated person change: a deputy may ensure continuity during approval.
- GM1 145.A.85(b) additional prior-approval changes: AltMoC (145.A.120(b)); alternative tooling procedure (145.A.40(a)(i)); B-rated organisation maintaining installed engine (Appendix II (f)); C-rated organisation maintaining installed components (Appendix II (g)); personnel competency procedures (145.A.30(e)); safety performance reporting system where oversight cycle extended beyond **36 months** (145.B.305(d)).
- Other changes: managed and notified per an authority-approved procedure (145.B.310(h)).

### 2.9 Continued validity (145.A.90)
- Certificate valid while compliant (handling of findings per 145.B.350), access granted, and not surrendered/suspended/revoked (145.B.355). No fixed expiry period: unlimited duration subject to conditions.
- Return certificate to the authority without delay on surrender or revocation.

### 2.10 Findings and observations (145.A.95)
- On notification of findings (145.B.350): identify root cause(s) and contributing factor(s); define corrective action plan; demonstrate implementation to authority's satisfaction; all within the period agreed with the authority.
- CAP should include correction, corrective and preventive actions and planning; submitted timely for acceptance before implementation; on completion inform the authority within the agreed period with evidence.
- Observations (145.B.350(f)): give due consideration and record decisions (analysis, actions or reasons for no action).

### 2.11 AltMoC (145.A.120)
- Before use: full description to authority including manual/procedure revisions and explanation of compliance; use only after prior approval. Approval is individual to the applicant.

### 2.12 Access (145.A.140) and immediate reaction (145.A.155)
- Grant access to competent authority (145.1) and authority performing oversight under 145.B.300(d), including at subcontractors (subcontract contracts must provide for this, AMC1 145.A.75(b) 4.7).
- Implement authority-mandated safety measures (145.B.135) and relevant EASA mandatory safety information (e.g. ADs are separate, but Safety Directives etc.).

### 2.13 Management system (145.A.200) and compliance monitoring
- Elements (a)(1) to (a)(6); scaled to size/complexity; may be integrated with other certificates.
- Safety manager and safety review board (SRB) chaired by AM, members the 145.A.30 nominated persons; SRB may be dropped for small organisations subject to risk assessment and authority agreement (tasks go to safety manager).
- Safety policy endorsed by AM, communicated, periodically reviewed; commitments incl. just culture, human factors and fatigue; safety objectives defined and periodically reviewed.
- Risk management: analysis (probability and severity), assessment (tolerability), control (mitigation); specify management levels authorised to decide risk tolerability.
- Immediate safety action: procedures for prompt contact with owner/operator/CAMO, including outside normal business hours; support operator ERP if triggered.
- Compliance monitoring independence: auditors not responsible for the function audited.
- Audit plan: all aspects of Part-145 compliance verified **every year** (including subcontracted activities), single exercise or spread over the year. Common procedures need not be checked per product line if verified yearly without findings; if findings, verify on other product lines until closed.
- Product audit: **one product per product line every year** (product line = each Appendix II class rating in the terms of approval; e.g. aircraft, engines, brakes, autopilots = at least four product audits a year). Include a percentage of unannounced audits, some at night for night-working organisations.
- If independent audit is contracted out: audit **twice every year**.
- Line stations listed under 145.A.75(d): audited at frequency consistent with flight activity and hazards, maximum **2 years** between audits of a particular line station.
- Planning cycle may be increased by **up to 100 %** if no safety-related findings, subject to risk assessment/mitigation and authority agreement (not applicable to the product audit cycle per cross-reference in (j)).
- Audit report issued each time.
- Organisations with **a maximum of 10 maintenance staff** actively engaged in maintenance may subcontract the whole independent audit element, or contract a qualified person, with authority agreement. External auditors work under the compliance monitoring manager's responsibility.
- Feedback system must **not** be contracted out. Findings go to departments with agreed target closure dates; departments report status; procedure for overdue actions.
- AM meets senior staff **at least twice per year** to review compliance monitoring performance and receives **at least a half-yearly summary report** on non-compliance findings (unless this is given to the SRB).
- Compliance monitoring function itself is subject to independent monitoring.

### 2.14 ISMS (145.A.200A)
- Establish, implement and maintain ISMS per Reg (EU) 2023/203, applicable from **22 February 2026**.

### 2.15 Internal safety reporting (145.A.202)
- Collect and evaluate 145.A.60 occurrences, plus internally reported errors, near misses and hazards; identify causes and contributing factors and feed safety risk management; evaluate info including inability to follow procedures; circulate information; arrangements to collect safety issues from subcontracted activities.
- AMC: confidential, just culture, closed loop, investigation selection process, trend analysis if appropriate, corrective actions, initial and recurrent training for investigators, cooperation with owner/operator/CAMO, feed recurrent training (AMC3 145.A.30(e)), individual and general feedback to staff. GM: retain **all** reports.

### 2.16 Contracting and subcontracting (145.A.205)
- Ensure conformity; hazards considered in management system; subcontractors work under subcontracting organisation's approval scope.
- GM: compliance monitoring checks contracted organisations' approval covers the work and is still valid; occurrence reporting interfaces with contracted organisations (does not replace their own reporting duty); internal safety reporting interfaces with subcontractors.
- Contracting (handing release to another Part-145) only when allowed by the person/organisation that requested the maintenance.

## 3. Records and Documents

### 3.1 Aircraft certificate of release to service (CRS)
- Issued by appropriately authorised certifying staff on behalf of the organisation, before flight, at completion of any maintenance.
- Content (AMC 145.A.50(b)):
  - Statement: "Certifies that the work specified, except as otherwise specified, was carried out in accordance with Part-145 and in respect to that work the aircraft/aircraft component is considered ready for release to service".
  - Alternative abbreviated statement "Part-145 release to service" allowed if the tech log introductory section contains an example of the full statement.
  - EASA Part-145 approval number; identity of the person issuing the release.
  - Reference to the task in the (S)TC holder/declarant/operator instructions or AMP (which may cross-refer to data).
  - Date of maintenance, including position relative to life or overhaul limits (date/flying hours/cycles/landings etc.).
  - For extensive maintenance, a summary with a unique cross-reference to the work package; dimensional information retained in work pack.
  - Incomplete maintenance (145.A.50(e)): entry of that fact before issue; deferment details incl. State of Registry competent authority endorsed.
- GM: the CRS certifies the ordered work, not that the aircraft is airworthy; airworthiness before each flight remains with the person/organisation managing continuing airworthiness. An overdue AD counts as endangering flight safety. Not expected to find hidden non-compliances outside the ordered work.

### 3.2 EASA Form 1 (authorised release certificate, Part-M Appendix II)
- Component CRS after off-aircraft maintenance; used domestically and for export/import; only issued by organisations approved by the relevant authority within scope.
- May be used as a rotable tag (reverse side, two copies, one returned with the item), or use existing rotable tags plus a Form 1 copy.
- Not to be issued for a known unserviceable item, except where an item goes through a series of processes at several Part-145 organisations; then a clear limitation statement in Block 12.
- Separate CRS needed for installation on aircraft after an off-aircraft Form 1.
- Own-use components: Form 1 may be unnecessary if MOE internal release procedure provides.
- Special case issue (AMC2 145.A.50(d) para 2) for components stored before Part-145/Part-21, used and removed serviceable (leased/loaned), from withdrawn-from-service aircraft, or after abnormal occurrences, or maintained by unapproved organisations:
  - Issued by "appropriately rated" organisation (class rating for that component type or for the product in which it may be installed), per MOE procedure.
  - Sign Block 14b, Block 11 "Inspected/Tested".
  - Block 12: when and by whom last maintained; if unused, when and by whom manufactured with cross-reference to original documentation; list of ADs, repairs and modifications known incorporated (or a statement that none are known); life used for life-limited parts and time-controlled components (fatigue, overhaul, storage); reference to component maintenance history record (attached with acceptance test report or statement, if applicable).
  - New/unused stored parts: acceptance test report/statement; inspection against storage instructions and condition (damage, corrosion, leakage); storage life used established. If not possible: disassemble, check ADs/repairs/mods, inspect/test, replace seals, lubricants and life-limited parts, then issue Form 1 stating work and maintenance data reference. This is a maintenance release, not a Part-21 production release.
  - Used parts from serviceable Member State-registered aircraft: removed by qualified person; last flight revealed no faults; condition inspection; aircraft record researched for accidents, incidents, heavy landings, lightning strikes (never issue if suspected extremes of stress, temperature or immersion); maintenance history record for serialised components; mods/repairs/ADs established; hours/cycles/landings and time since overhaul established; Form 1 includes the aircraft it was removed from.
  - From non-Member State-registered aircraft: only if leased/loaned from the Part-145 that retains control of airworthiness status.
  - From aircraft withdrawn from service (part-out): a maintenance activity under Part-145 control; structured disassembly plan; supervision by certifying staff; review all recorded defects; dedicated control documentation; unserviceable parts identified and quarantined; suitable facilities and storage per manufacturer.
  - Maintained by non-Part-145 organisations: dismantle and inspect, replace LLPs/time-controlled parts lacking evidence, reassemble and test, full 145.A.50 certification.
  - From accident/incident aircraft: per part-out process plus a specific work order with additional tests/inspections (may need TC holder/declarant/manufacturer input); work order referenced in Block 12.
- GM Block 12 "Remarks" content: maintenance documentation used incl. revision status ("in accordance with the CMM" alone is not acceptable); NDT methods and documentation; AD/SB compliance; repairs; modifications; replacement parts; LLP status; shelf life limits; deviations from customer work order; release statements for foreign CAA requirements; shipment shortages or reassembly info; traceability references such as batch numbers.

### 3.3 "Suitable release certificate" (145.A.50(f) temporary fit)
- Clearly states component is serviceable; identifies releasing organisation and the authority under whose approval it works, with approval/authorisation reference.
- Plus entry in the aircraft continuing airworthiness record system or tech log; compatibility check against approved design (mods, repairs, ADs, life limits, condition); where, when and why the aircraft was grounded.

### 3.4 Maintenance records (145.A.55(a))
- Details of maintenance within approval scope; all records needed to prove CRS requirements met, including subcontractors' release documents.
- Copy of each CRS and detailed records to operator/customer.
- Associated maintenance data: specific data like repair or modification embodiment data; need not retain entire AMM/CMM/IPC; records must reference the revision status of data used.
- Aircraft record should contain basic details of all serialised components and other significant components installed, for traceability to component documentation, maintenance data and mod/repair data.
- Modular engines: total time in service and maintenance records kept per module, travelling with the module, showing mandatory compliance for that module.
- Retention 3 years from CRS.

### 3.5 Airworthiness review records (145.A.55(b))
- Copy of each ARC issued plus all supporting documents; 3 years; available to owner on request.

### 3.6 Management system and contract records (145.A.55(c)) - 5 years
- Records of management system key processes; contracts (contracting and subcontracting).
- AltMoC description elements form part of the management system records (AMC1 145.A.120(b)).
- Subcontractor audits, inspections and actions; info on when subcontractors are used (AMC1 145.A.75(b) 4.5).
- Independent audit reports and feedback system records.
- Internal safety reports (retain all, GM1 145.A.202).
- Decisions on observations (145.A.95(c)).

### 3.7 Personnel records (145.A.55(d))
- For maintenance, compliance monitoring and safety management personnel: qualifications, training, experience.
- Certifying staff and support staff record (AMC1 145.A.55(d)) minimum fields: name; date of birth; basic training; task or product/type training; recurrent training; experience; qualifications relevant to authorisation; scope of authorisation (role, product, level of maintenance, etc.); date of first issue; expiry date (if appropriate); authorisation identification number. IR adds: Part-66 licence details (or equivalent); scope of certification authorisations; particulars of limited or one-off authorisations under 145.A.30(j).
- Airworthiness review staff record (AMC2 145.A.55(d)): name; date of birth; certifying staff authorisation; experience as certifying staff on Part-ML aircraft; qualifications (knowledge of Part-ML and AR procedures); scope of AR authorisation and personal authorisation reference; date of first issue; expiry date (if appropriate). IR adds: summary of relevant continuing airworthiness experience and training; copy of AR authorisation.
- Controlled by the compliance monitoring function (but CMM need not run it); minimal number of persons with access; competent authority may access.
- Retention: employment plus at least 3 years after leaving or authorisation withdrawal.

### 3.8 Work cards / work packages
- Record identity of authorised person and IQP and independent inspection or reinspection details before CRS/sign-off.
- Work package holds full details where CRS summarises; dimensional information retained.

### 3.9 Maintenance work order / contract (145.A.65(b)(1))
- Agreed before work; defines maintenance to allow release.

### 3.10 Occurrence reports
- Initial and follow-up (see 2.4); to competent authority, design organisation, CAMO/owner or component customer.

### 3.11 MOE (145.A.70) contents (directly or by reference)
1. AM statement signed (CEO countersign if different).
2. Safety policy and safety objectives.
3. Titles and names of persons nominated under 145.A.30(b), (c), (ca).
4. Their duties and responsibilities, incl. matters they may deal with directly with the authority.
5. Organisation chart showing accountability and lines of responsibility between 145.A.30(a), (b), (c), (ca) persons.
6. List of certifying staff, support staff and airworthiness review staff with scope of authorisation.
7. Manpower resources and staff availability planning system (145.A.30(d)).
8. Facilities at each approved location.
9. Scope of work relevant to terms of approval (145.A.20).
10. Procedure for changes not requiring prior approval and notification (145.A.85(c)).
11. MOE amendment procedure.
12. Procedures showing how compliance with Part-145 is ensured.
13. List of commercial operators provided regular aircraft maintenance services, plus associated procedures.
14. List of subcontracted organisations (145.A.75(b)), where applicable.
15. List of approved locations including line maintenance locations (145.A.75(d)).
16. List of contracted organisations.
17. List of currently approved AltMoC.

- AMC1 145.A.70(a) layout (if different format, include an index):
  - Part 1 General: 1.1 AM statement; 1.2 safety policy and objectives; 1.3 management personnel; 1.4 duties; 1.5 org chart; 1.6 list of certifying/support/AR staff; 1.7 manpower; 1.8 facilities per address; 1.9 scope of work; 1.10 changes requiring prior approval (incl. ## 4. Defined Terms

- **Error-capturing methods**: actions defined by the organisation to detect maintenance errors made while performing maintenance.
- **Independent inspection**: inspection by an "independent qualified person" of a task carried out by an "authorised person".
- **Authorised person**: the person who performs or supervises the task and assumes full responsibility for completion per maintenance data; issues the CRS or sign-off after the independent inspection.
- **Independent qualified person**: performs the independent inspection and attests satisfactory completion with no deficiencies; does not issue a CRS, so need not hold certification privileges.
- **Reinspection**: error-capturing method where the authorised person also acts as the independent qualified person; only in unforeseen single-person circumstances.
- **Endanger flight safety** (GM1 145.A.50(a)): any instance where safe operation could not be assured or which could lead to an unsafe condition, e.g. significant cracking, deformation, corrosion or failure of primary structure, burning, electrical arcing, significant hydraulic fluid or fuel leakage, emergency system or total system failure; an overdue AD is a hazard to flight safety.
- **Unable to establish full compliance with 145.A.50(a)**: ordered maintenance not completed because downtime ran out, aircraft condition requires more downtime, or maintenance data requires a flight as part of maintenance.
- **Suitable release certificate**: clearly states serviceability, identifies releasing organisation and the authority and approval/authorisation reference.
- **Compliance with all applicable maintenance and operational requirements** (145.A.50(f)): entry in continuing airworthiness record system/tech log, compatibility check with approved design (mods, repairs, ADs, life limits, condition), and where/when/why grounded.
- **Appropriately rated** (AMC2 145.A.50(d) only): organisation holding an approval class rating for the component type or for the product in which it may be installed.
- **Associated maintenance data** (AMC1 145.A.55(a)(3)): specific information such as repair or modification embodiment data; not necessarily the full AMM/CMM/IPC.
- **Specialised services**: any specialised activity such as NDT requiring particular skills/qualifications.
- **Organisation responsible for the design** (GM1 145.A.60(b)): design approval holder (TC, RTC, STC, ETSO authorisation, major repair design approval, major change design approval or other approval under Reg 748/2012) or declarant of a declaration of design compliance (Part 21 Light Subpart C).
- **Subcontracting** (working under the management system of a Part-145): third party (approved or not) does maintenance under the Part-145's approval and management system; responsibility and release stay with the Part-145; the approval is temporarily extended to the subcontractor.
- **Contracting**: contracting to another maintenance organisation that releases the work under its own approval; responsibility for release transfers. Only if allowed by the requester.
- **Complete workshop maintenance check or overhaul of an engine/module** (inverse defined): other maintenance = anything that can be done without disassembly of the core engine or core module.
- **Product line** (AMC2 145.A.200(a)(6)): any product under an Appendix II approval class rating in the terms of approval.
- **Accountability vs responsibility**: accountability cannot be delegated; responsibility can.
- **Safety policy**: description of overall philosophies and principles regarding safety; "how we do business here".
- **Safety review board**: high-level committee chaired by the AM, composed of the 145.A.30 nominated persons, on strategic safety.
- **Safety action group**: standing or ad hoc group assisting the safety manager or SRB.
- **Remote audit**: audit using real-time video and audio instead of physical presence on site. **Auditing entity**: authority or organisation performing it. **Auditee**: entity audited.
- **AMC**: per Art. 76(3) Reg 2018/1139, non-binding standardised means of compliance. **AltMoC**: alternative means of compliance, either authority-established or organisation-established and authority-approved; does not allow deviation from the Regulation.

## 5. Software Implications (Airworthiness+)

### 5.1 Data objects

**WorkOrder / MaintenanceContract**
- requester (owner/operator/CAMO/CAO/component customer), responsible-for-continuing-airworthiness party, scope (tasks), agreed date, contract reference (link to contract record, retained 5 years), contracting permitted flag (requester allows onward contracting), status (draft, agreed, in work, partially complete, complete, closed).

**Task / WorkCard**
- task reference (AMM/CMM/AMP/SB/AD/SRM), maintenance data document + revision status (mandatory field), critical maintenance task flag, error-capturing method(s) (enum: independent inspection, reinspection, operational check, functional test, rigging check, visual inspection, multiple), performed_by (authorised person), supervised work flag (trainee/temporary) + checking authorised person, independent inspector (IQP) id, inspection result, timestamp, reinspection flag + unforeseen-circumstance justification text, software version/compatibility check fields for software in critical tasks, control-system checklist (assembly/locking, full and free movement, cable tension/secondary stops, correct sense, interconnected systems), sign-off timestamp, task group (with critical steps flagged).
- Rules: sign-off only when completed; for critical tasks, CRS/sign-off blocked until IQP or reinspection recorded; IQP must not equal performer unless reinspection flagged; warn if reinspection used where a suitable IQP is rostered at that station/shift; warn if the same person is assigned identical tasks on several same-type components across multiple systems on the same aircraft in the same check.

**Defect / Finding raised during maintenance**
- link to work order, description, notified to CAMO/owner (timestamp, contact), response (agreed rectify / declined / deferred), deferral reference (MEL/CDL or other), State of Registry authority detail for deferment endorsement.

**CertificateOfReleaseToService (aircraft)**
- organisation Part-145 approval number, certifying staff id + authorisation number and scope check, statement type (full / abbreviated "Part-145 release to service"), tasks/work package cross-reference (unique), date, aircraft hours/cycles/landings at release, incomplete maintenance flag + list of outstanding items + deferment endorsement (incl. State of Registry authority), MCF-pending flag (release of incomplete maintenance before a maintenance check flight, followed by final CRS), SC/SR embodiment flag, status (draft, issued, superseded).
- Validation: certifying staff authorisation valid and in scope for the aircraft/task on issue date; no open items that endanger flight safety (e.g. overdue AD check from CAMO data); all critical-task inspections complete; CRS must be issued before next flight (alert if a flight/tech log sector is recorded after maintenance without a CRS).

**ComponentRelease (EASA Form 1)**
- Form tracking number, blocks 1 to 14 (e.g. Block 11 status/work: overhauled, repaired, inspected/tested, modified, etc.; Block 12 remarks; Block 14b signature), certifying staff, approval reference, own-use internal release flag (no Form 1 per MOE), unserviceable-with-limitation flag (only for multi-organisation process chains, requires Block 12 limitation text), rotable tag copies.

- Part-out project object: disassembly plan, supervising certifying staff, defect review, control documentation, quarantine list of unserviceable parts.

- aircraft, component P/N S/N, grounding location (must not be main line station/main base), reason/when/where grounded, CAMO agreement record, suitable release certificate details (releasing org, authority, approval reference), compatibility check (mods/repairs/ADs/life limits/condition), fit date, aircraft hours at fit, limit = fit hours + 30 FH, or first return to main line station/base, whichever sooner.
- Alerts: countdown in flight hours; trigger on arrival at main base; overdue removal alert; close-out when proper release certificate obtained (145.A.42) or component removed.

**MaintenanceRecord / RecordRetention**
- Every record: created/last amended date (retention clock starts then), retention class, retention end date, legal hold, storage medium; immutable audit trail (protect from alteration); access control; backup within 24 hours, offsite backup location; export for transfer on termination.

- Modular engine support: time in service and records per module, moving with module.
- Aircraft record: serialised component installation details with traceability links.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM that change practice

- **CRS is not an airworthiness statement** (GM1 145.A.50(a)): the Part-145 certifies ordered work; airworthiness before flight stays with the CAMO/owner. Overdue AD = endangers flight safety, so a known overdue AD blocks CRS. Software should separate "maintenance released" from "aircraft airworthy/ready for flight" statuses.
- **Abbreviated CRS** "Part-145 release to service" is acceptable only if the tech log intro shows the full statement.
- **Life-status on CRS**: date should include hours/cycles/landings relative to limits; extensive work can be summarised with a unique work-package cross-reference.
- **Deferments on CRS** need CAMO agreement and the State of Registry authority endorsed; MCF scenario allows release of incomplete maintenance before the check flight.
- **Independent inspection model** (AMC4 145.A.48(c)(2)): IQP need not be certifying staff; reinspection only in genuinely unforeseen single-person situations, which excludes failing to roster an IQP. Software checks for software version/compatibility in critical tasks.
- **Form 1 "Inspected/Tested"** route for surplus, part-out, leased/loaned, legacy and unapproved-source components with a prescriptive Block 12 content; "in accordance with the CMM" alone is unacceptable in Block 12; revision status mandatory.
- **Records**: electronic backup within 24 hours, offsite; retention clock starts at creation or last amendment; maintenance records need not keep full AMM/CMM, but must reference revision status used.
- **Personnel records** controlled by compliance monitoring function, minimal access, specified minimum fields.
- **Occurrence reporting**: integrated multi-certificate reporting allowed under conditions; single focal point; Reg 2015/1018 list is not exhaustive.
- **MOE layout** AMC is the de facto template (Parts 1 to 5, L2, 7 to 9); a different format needs an index.
- **Change timeframes**: 30 working days (certificate), 20 working days (nominated person), urgent name change; risk assessment for prior-approval changes.
- **Subcontracting vs contracting** (AMC1 145.A.75(b), GM2 145.A.205): subcontractors work under the Part-145's approval (pre-audit by compliance monitoring, control procedure, revocation, authority access clause, CRS by Part-145 certifying staff or authorised subcontractor staff); contracting transfers release responsibility and needs requester permission. Organisation must have in-house capability for the majority of its approved work.
- **Compliance monitoring frequencies**: annual full coverage; one product audit per class rating per year; unannounced and night audits; twice yearly if independent audit contracted; line stations max 2 years; up to 100 % extension with no safety findings and authority agreement; small organisations (max 10 maintenance staff) may outsource independent audit; feedback system never outsourced; AM twice-yearly meeting and half-yearly summary.
- **SRB** may be omitted for small organisations with authority agreement.
- **Root cause analysis** (GM1 145.A.95): focus on why, not who; avoid simple linear models; peer review recommended.
- **AltMoC**: needed when means are technically different from AMC or a form differs significantly; editorial rewording/transposition does not need AltMoC; IR-mandated forms (e.g. EASA Form 1) cannot be changed. Where no AMC exists, no AltMoC process needed.
- **Remote audits** (GM1 145.A.200(a)(6) and 145.B.300): permitted with documented procedure, agreement with auditee, platform/security tests, and records stating extent of ICT use.
- **Just culture** in safety policy; internal reports retained indefinitely; investigation scope wider than mandatory occurrences.
