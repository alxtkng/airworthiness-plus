# Chunk 21: Annex Vd (Part-CAO), Combined Airworthiness Organisation

Source: EASA Easy Access Rules for Continuing Airworthiness (Reg. (EU) No 1321/2014), Sep 2025 consolidation. The chunk covers CAO.1 General, Section A (CAO.A.010 to CAO.A.115), Section B (CAO.B.010 to CAO.B.070), Appendix I (EASA Form 3-CAO), and the appendices to the AMC/GM (EASA Form 613, the organisational review checklist, and EASA Form 2). Lines 1318 to the end of the file are page footers, book footnotes that belong to other annexes, and empty "[Regulatory source]" / "Tagger" placeholders. They carry no Part-CAO content, apart from some footnote definitions that are useful context (listed in section 4).

Amending acts cited in the text: IR from Reg. (EU) 2019/1383 (original), 2020/270 and 2021/700. AMC/GM from ED Decisions 2020/002/R, 2021/009/R, 2022/011/R, 2023/013/R and 2023/019/R.

**UK caveat (guesses, to be verified against the CAA UK Reg 1321/2014 and the CAA AMC/GM):** Part-CAO (Reg 2019/1383) applied from March 2020, before the end of the Brexit transition period, so UK retained law very probably contains Annex Vd. Likely differences:
- The competent authority is the UK CAA, and there is no "Agency" role for third-country organisations.
- "Member State" becomes UK.
- The forms are probably named "CAA Form 3-CAO", "CAA Form 1" and "CAA Form 2", and the reference is probably "UK.CAO.xxxx".
- Amendments adopted after 31 Dec 2020 may be absent or different in the UK. These include the 2021/700 rewording of CAO.A.045 and CAO.A.105, and the AMC changes from ED 2021/009, 2022/011 (ICT remote audits), 2023/013 and 2023/019 (Part 21 Light references and declared design data, which do not exist in UK law).
- The FAA/TCCA MAG alleviation for components is EASA-specific. The UK has its own bilateral arrangements (UK–US BASA, UK–Canada).
- Treat every numeric limit below as the EASA value until it has been checked against the UK text.

## 1. Scope

### General
- **CAO.1 General**: Defines the competent authority. For an organisation whose principal place of business is in a Member State, it is the authority that State designates. For an organisation whose principal place of business is in a third country, it is EASA. Defines "owner" as the person responsible for the continuing airworthiness of the aircraft: the registered owner, the lessee under a leasing contract, or the operator.

## 2. Obligations (with exact numbers)

### 2.1 Applicability and eligibility
- A CAO is only for aircraft that are **not CMPA** and **not on an air carrier AOC** (Reg. 1008/2008). GM1 CAO.A.070 adds that **used components** maintained and released on EASA Form 1 by a CAO **cannot be installed on CMPA or on aircraft used by a licensed air carrier**.
- The aircraft may fall under Part-M or Part-ML. CAO procedures branch on which one applies, for example AMP approval, AR rules and records points.
- Grandfathering (CAO.A.020(a), last paragraph): an organisation converting from Part-M Subpart F or G, or from Part-145 (under Art. 4(4)), must write its scope so that its privileges are **identical** to those in the existing approval.
- Airworthiness review (AR) staff already authorised under Part-M F/G, Part-CAMO or Part-145 count as authorised under Part-CAO on conversion, with no further supervision needed (AMC1 CAO.A.045(a)).

### 2.2 Scope of work and ratings (CAO.A.020, Appendix I)
- **Approved scope** is specified in the CAE (CAE A.4). Within the classes and ratings the authority grants, the CAE scope defines the "exact limits of approval". The classes and ratings must match the CAE scope.
- **Aircraft types that must be listed individually**, where any change needs prior approval under CAO.A.105(a) and CAO.B.065(a):
  - aeroplanes **> 2 730 kg MTOM**;
  - helicopters **> 1 200 kg MTOM or certified for > 4 occupants**.
- **Complete turbine engines**: the scope gives the engine manufacturer, group, series or type, or the maintenance task(s). Changes need prior approval.
- **Everything else** is controlled by the CAO through the CAE procedure in CAO.A.025(a)(11) / CAO.A.105(b), with no prior approval. That covers lighter aircraft, components other than complete turbine engines, and NDT specialised services. GM example: adding Bell 206L to a scope that already has the Bell 206B (> 1 200 kg) needs authority approval. Adding a Rotax 914 or a LOM M 332 to a scope with the Rotax 912 A (piston engines) is controlled by the CAO's own procedure.
- **One-person organisation**: if one person both plans and performs all maintenance, the CAO cannot hold maintenance privileges for:
  - turbine-engined aeroplanes (aircraft rating);
  - helicopters with a turbine engine or more than one piston engine (aircraft rating);
  - complete piston engines of **450 HP and above** (engine rating);
  - complete turbine engines (engine rating).
  
  These exclusions appear in the LIMITATIONS block of Form 3-CAO.
- **Component system ratings C1 to C22**, for components other than complete engines:
  - C1 air conditioning and pressurisation; C2 auto flight; C3 communications and navigation; C4 doors and hatches; C5 electrical power and lights; C6 equipment;
  - C7 engine; C8 flight controls; C9 fuel; C10 helicopter and rotors; C11 helicopter transmission; C12 hydraulic power;
  - C13 indicating and recording system; C14 landing gear; C15 oxygen; C16 propellers; C17 pneumatic and vacuum systems; C18 protection from ice/rain/fire;
  - C19 windows; C20 structural; C21 water ballast; C22 propulsion augmentation.
- **Certificate classes** (Form 3-CAO):
  - AIRCRAFT:
    - Aeroplanes, other than CMPA;
    - Aeroplanes up to 2 730 kg MTOM;
    - Helicopters, other than CMPA;
    - Helicopters up to 1 200 kg MTOM and up to 4 occupants;
    - Airships; Balloons; Sailplanes.
    
    Each aircraft class has tick-boxes for Maintenance, CAM, Airworthiness review and Permit to fly.
  - COMPONENTS: Complete turbine engines, Complete piston engines, Electrical engines, Components other than complete engines. Only Maintenance applies.
  - SPECIALISED SERVICES: NDT.
- **GM1 CAO.A.020: minimum aircraft information per class in the CAE scope**:
  - Aeroplanes > 2 730 kg: the specific types (the Part-66 AMC type rating list is acceptable).
  - Aeroplanes ≤ 2 730 kg: propulsion type (turbine or piston) and category (ELA1, ELA2, or up to 2 730 kg).
  - Helicopters > 1 200 kg and 4 occupants: the specific types (Appendix I to AMC Part-66 list).
  - Helicopters ≤ 1 200 kg and 4 occupants: propulsion type.
  - Sailplanes: ELA1.
  - Balloons: hot-air, gas, or Rozière.
  - Airships: the specific type for non-ELA2 airships. For ELA2 airships, hot-air or gas.
  
  Each category or type is annotated with the privileges held for it (Maintenance, CAM, AR, PtF).
- **What each rating permits** (Appendix I):
  - **Aircraft rating**: maintenance on the aircraft and any component, including engines, using aircraft maintenance data. Component maintenance data may be used if the authority agrees, but only while the component is fitted. A component may be temporarily removed to improve access, unless the removal creates extra maintenance not eligible under (b). This needs an approved CAE procedure (CAE C.12).
  - **Engine rating** (turbine, piston or electrical): maintenance on uninstalled engines and on engine components while they are fitted to the engine. Temporary removal for access is allowed. Installed engines may be maintained during base and line maintenance under an approved CAE procedure (CAE C.13).
  - **Component rating** (other than complete engines): uninstalled components. Installed components may also be maintained during base or line maintenance, or at an engine facility, under an approved CAE procedure.
  - **NDT rating**: self-contained, and needed only when the CAO does NDT as a task for another organisation. An organisation with an aircraft, engine or component rating can do NDT on products it maintains without an NDT rating, provided the CAE has NDT procedures.
- **Fabrication (CAO.A.020(c), AMC1 CAO.A.020(c))**: a restricted range of parts may be fabricated, in conformity with maintenance data, for use only on work in the CAO's **own facilities**, as indicated in the CAE (C.8). Details are in section 7.

### 2.3 Privileges (CAO.A.095)
- **(a) Maintenance**:
  1. Maintain approved aircraft or components at the locations in the certificate and the CAE.
  2. Arrange specialised services at another qualified organisation under CAO control, using approved CAE procedures (subcontracting, CAE B.7).
  3. Maintain at **any location** where the need arises from unserviceability or from occasional maintenance support, under CAE conditions (CAE C.11).
  4. Issue CRS under CAO.A.065 or CAO.A.070.
- **(b) CAM**:
  1. Manage the continuing airworthiness of the approved aircraft.
  2. Approve the AMP under ML.A.302(b)(2) for Part-ML aircraft.
  3. Carry out limited CAM tasks with any contracted organisation working under its quality system, as listed on the certificate.
  4. Extend an ARC issued by the authority, another organisation or a person, under M.A.901(f) or ML.A.901(c).
- **(c) Airworthiness review**, only for a CAO with its principal place of business in a Member State:
  1. If the CAO holds the CAM privilege (b): it may perform ARs under M.A.901 or ML.A.903, issue the ARC or a recommendation, and extend an ARC.
  2. If the CAO holds the maintenance privilege (a): it may perform ARs under ML.A.903 (Part-ML aircraft only) and issue the ARC.
  
  GM: a CAO may therefore hold "maintenance" and "airworthiness review" without CAM. That is allowed for Part-ML aircraft.
- **(d) Permit to fly**: only for a Member State CAO that holds privilege (c). It may issue a PtF under 21.A.711(d) for aircraft for which it can issue the ARC, when it attests conformity with the approved flight conditions, following a CAE procedure (B.11 and B.12).
- **(e)** A CAO may be approved for one or more privileges.

### 2.4 Personnel
- **Accountable manager (CAO.A.035(a))**: has the authority to ensure all activities can be financed and are carried out in accordance with Part-CAO. Signs the CAE compliance statement (CAO.A.025(a)(1)). The authority meets the AM at least once during the initial investigation (CAO.B.045(d)) and at least once every **24 months** after that (CAO.B.055(f)).
- **Nominated person(s) (CAO.A.035(b))**: the AM nominates a person or group responsible for ensuring continued compliance. They are ultimately responsible to the AM.
  - AMC1 CAO.A.035(c) qualifications:
    - (a) practical experience in applying aviation safety standards and safe operating practices;
    - (b) comprehensive knowledge of Part-M, Part-ML and the CAE;
    - (c) **5 years' aviation experience, of which at least 2 years in the aeronautical industry in an appropriate position**;
    - (d) knowledge of a relevant sample of the aircraft or component types in scope, shown by documented evidence or by an authority assessment. Training used as evidence should be at least **Part-66 Appendix III Level 1 General Familiarisation**, from a Part-147 organisation, the manufacturer or another accepted organisation;
    - (e) knowledge of maintenance standards (including human factors) and of the quality system or organisational review.
  - Authority acceptance: approval of the CAE, which lists the AM and nominated persons by name, is the formal acceptance (GM1 CAO.B.045(a)).
- **Quality manager**: must be designated (CAO.A.100(a)). In a small CAO that uses organisational review, the person responsible for the review programme is by default the AM, unless delegated to a CAO.A.035(b) person.
- **Staff sufficiency (CAO.A.035(d))**: enough appropriately qualified staff for the planned work. Temporarily subcontracted staff are allowed. The CAE manpower resources section (A.9) covers this.
- **Qualification assessment (CAO.A.035(e))**: assess and **record** the qualification of all personnel. AMC: assess competence by on-the-job evaluation and/or examination before any unsupervised work. Give and record initial and recurrent training.
- **Specialised tasks (CAO.A.035(f))**: welding staff and NDT staff (other than for colour contrast inspections) are qualified to an officially recognised standard.
- **Certifying staff (CAO.A.040)**:
  - Must comply with Art. 5. They release only once the CAO has ensured (1) that they meet **66.A.20(b)** (recency and privilege conditions), or national rules where Art. 5(6) applies, and (2) that they have adequate understanding of the aircraft or component and of the organisation's procedures.
  - **One-off certification authorisation (CAO.A.040(b))** applies in unforeseen circumstances, with the aircraft grounded away from the main base and no appropriate certifying staff available. The CAO contracted for maintenance support may issue it to either:
    1. its own employee holding type qualifications for aircraft of similar technology, construction and systems; or
    2. any person with **≥ 3 years' maintenance experience** and a valid **ICAO** AML rated for the type. This route requires that no Part-CAO organisation exists at that location, and the CAO obtains and keeps evidence of the person's experience and licence on file.
    
    The CAO **reports the issue to the authority within 7 days** and ensures that any maintenance that could affect flight safety is **rechecked**. CAE B.5 covers this.
  - **Limited certification authorisation (CAO.A.040(c))**: for maintenance support to operators in **commercial** operations, under approved CAE procedures (B.6):
    1. Pilot-in-command, on the basis of the flight crew licence, for a **repetitive pre-flight AD** that states flight crew may carry it out. The CAO ensures sufficient practical training.
    2. PIC for an aircraft operating away from a supported location, with sufficient practical training.
  - **List (CAO.A.040(d))**: record certifying staff details and keep an up-to-date list with each person's scope of approval as part of the exposition (CAE A.10).
- **AMP staff**: list of staff responsible for developing and approving AMPs, with their scope (CAE A.11, CAO.A.025(a)(6)).
- **Permit-to-fly staff**: list of staff responsible for issuing PtFs (CAE A.13).
- **Airworthiness review staff (CAO.A.045)**:
  - (a)(1) Experience in continuing airworthiness of **≥ 1 year for sailplanes and balloons, ≥ 3 years for all other aircraft**.
  - (a)(2) **and** one of: an appropriate licence under Art. 5 (Part-66), an aeronautical degree or equivalent, or **additional** CAW experience of **≥ 2 years for sailplanes and balloons, ≥ 4 years for all other aircraft**.
  - (a)(3) **and** appropriate aeronautical maintenance training.
  - (b) Nomination: before authorising a person, the CAO nominates them to perform an AR under supervision of the authority, or of a person already authorised as AR staff of the CAO. If the supervision is satisfactory, the **authority formally accepts** the person.
  - (c) Recency: the CAO ensures appropriate recent CAW experience. AMC1 CAO.A.045(c): either involvement in CAM activities for **≥ 6 months in every 2-year period**, **or** at least **one AR in the last 12 months**.
  - Restoring a lapsed authorisation (AMC (d)): perform one AR to a satisfactory level under supervision of the authority or, if the authority accepts it, of another currently valid AR staff member of the CAO, under an approved procedure.
  - "Experience in continuing airworthiness" (AMC (b)) means any appropriate combination of aircraft maintenance, CAM, and surveillance of those tasks.
  - "Equivalent to an aeronautical degree" (AMC (e)) means a relevant engineering degree (mechanical, electrical, electronic, avionic or other relevant studies) or an aircraft maintenance technician qualification with additional education.
  - (d) Each AR staff member appears in a list in the CAE that includes their AR authorisation (CAE A.12).
  - (e) Record for each AR staff member: qualifications, a summary of relevant CAW experience and training, and a copy of the authorisation. Retained **≥ 2 years after the person leaves** the CAO.

### 2.5 Combined Airworthiness Exposition (CAO.A.025)
- The manual contains at least:
  1. the AM's signed statement that the CAO will at all times work to Part-CAO and the CAE;
  2. the scope of work;
  3. titles and names of the AM and nominated persons;
  4. an organisation chart showing chains of responsibility between them;
  5. the list of certifying staff with scope, if any;
  6. the list of AMP development and approval staff with scope, if any;
  7. the list of AR staff with scope, if any;
  8. the list of PtF issuance staff, if any;
  9. a general description and location of facilities;
  10. procedures showing how the CAO ensures compliance;
  11. the CAE amendment procedure under CAO.A.105(b).
- The initial CAE is approved by the authority. Amendments follow CAO.A.105.
- AMC2: personnel should be familiar with the parts of the CAE relevant to their tasks. The CAE may be published by electronic data processing (EDP), with attention to compatibility for internal and external dissemination.
- The full AMC1 layout is in section 3.

### 2.6 Facilities (CAO.A.030, AMC1)
- All necessary facilities, including adequate office accommodation, are needed for all planned work.
- If the scope includes maintenance:
  - (a) Specialised workshops, hangars and bays protect from contamination and the environment.
  - (b) **Secure storage** for components, equipment, tools and material, with:
    - unserviceable components and materials **segregated** from everything else;
    - the **manufacturer's storage instructions complied with**;
    - **access restricted to authorised personnel**.
- AMC1 CAO.A.030:
  - (a) If the CAO does not own the hangar, it may need proof of tenancy. Sufficiency of hangar space is shown by a **projected aircraft hangar visit plan** relative to the AMP, updated regularly.
  - (b) For balloons and airships, a hangar may not be needed when envelope and bottom-end maintenance is better done outside, provided ML.A.402 is met. Complex repairs or component work needing EASA Form 1 require suitable workshops. Facilities and environmental conditions are defined in the CAE.
  - (c) With the authority's agreement, alternative facilities other than a hangar may be used at the approved location for certain tasks, if protection is adequate.
  - (d) Weather protection is assessed against normal local weather over any **12-month period**. Structures keep out rain, hail, ice, snow, wind and dust. Floors are sealed to minimise dust.
  - (e) Maintenance staff have an area to study instructions and complete records.
  - (f) **Part-ML special case**:
    1. No hangar or dedicated workshop is required. Other facilities are acceptable, including working in the field or in non-aviation premises, closed or not, if there is protection from weather and contamination.
    2. These facilities do not need individual authority approval, provided the CAE describes for **each type of facility** the scope of work, the tooling and equipment available, and the permitted environmental conditions.
    3. The quality system or organisational review samples compliance with these conditions during maintenance events.
  - (g) Office requirements may be combined into one office if there is enough room.
  - Stores: see 2.7.

### 2.7 Stores, storage, receiving and acceptance, tools and calibration (exactly what the text says)
- **Rule text**:
  - CAO.A.030(b): secure storage, segregation of unserviceable items, manufacturer's storage instructions, and access restricted to authorised personnel.
  - CAO.A.050(c): the CAO "shall inspect, classify and appropriately segregate all incoming components" in accordance with **M.A.501 and M.A.504** (Part-M) or **ML.A.501 and ML.A.504** (Part-ML).
- **AMC1 CAO.A.030, storage detail**:
  - (h) Storage for serviceable components is clean, well ventilated and kept at an even dry temperature to minimise condensation. Manufacturer storage recommendations are followed where published.
  - (i) Storage racks are adequate and strong enough to support large components without damage.
  - (j) Components stay in protective packaging wherever practicable. A **shelf-life control system** is used, and **identity tags** identify components.
  - (k) "Segregation" means storing unserviceable components in a **separate secured location** from serviceable ones.
  - (l) Segregation and management of unserviceable components follow the organisation's approved procedure.
  - (m) A procedure defines the **decision process for the status of unserviceable components**, identifying at least:
    1. roles and responsibilities of the persons managing the decision;
    2. how the choice between maintaining, storing or **mutilating** a component is made;
    3. traceability of the decision.
  - (n) Components identified as **unsalvageable** (M.A.501(a)(3) or ML.A.504(c)) go to secure areas that prevent unauthorised access. They are managed through a procedure that ensures appropriate final disposal (M.A.504(b), ML.A.504(d) or (e)). **"The person responsible for the implementation of this procedure should be identified."**
- **Named stores or logistics roles**: the text names **no** storekeeper, stores inspector or logistics manager. The only role-like items are:
  - "persons managing the decision process" for unserviceable component status (AMC1 CAO.A.030(m)(1));
  - "the person responsible for the implementation of" the unsalvageable component procedure (AMC1 CAO.A.030(n));
  - "authorised personnel" with access to storage (CAO.A.030(b)).
- **CAE C.3**: "Components, equipment, tools and material (supply, acceptance, segregation, storage, calibration, etc.)", referencing CAO.A.050, CAO.A.060(d) and CAO.A.030(b). There is no further AMC on receiving inspection in this chunk. The receiving and acceptance detail lives in M.A.501/ML.A.501 (other chunks).
- **Organisational review checklist item 4, "Stores"**: "Do the stores meet the criteria of the CAE procedures?" and "Check by sampling some items in the store for presence of proper documentation and any overdue items."
- **Tools and equipment (CAO.A.050(a), (b))**:
  - Hold the equipment and tools specified in the maintenance data, or **verified equivalents as listed in the CAE**, as needed for day-to-day maintenance in scope.
  - Have a procedure to access other tools that are used only occasionally.
  - Tools and equipment are **controlled and calibrated to an officially recognised standard**. Keep **records of calibrations and the standards used**, and comply with CAO.A.090 (records).
  - AMC1 CAO.A.050(a): "necessary for day-to-day maintenance" means standard practices, plus normal servicing, plus tasks up to the **annual / 100-hour** or equivalent inspection, common to the majority of aircraft in scope. Rarely used tools are handled by the access procedure.
  - Checklist item 3: check tool and equipment availability against the CAE lists and their continued adequacy against the maintenance data, and sample-check calibration.
  - AMC2 CAO.B.045: before approval, the CAO must be able to show maintenance capability and comply with CAO.A.050(a) even if it does not yet hold all maintenance data.
- **Material use (CAO.A.060(d))**: use the tools, equipment and material specified in CAO.A.050.

### 2.8 Maintenance data and work orders (CAO.A.055)
- Hold and use applicable current data (M.A.401 / ML.A.401), including for modifications and repairs. Customer-provided data are held only while the work is in progress.
- AMC: data need not be held continuously. A procedure may ensure specific data are available before the work.
- **A written work order is agreed before maintenance starts** between the CAO and the requester, clearly establishing the work (CAE C.2).
- Checklist item 2: data present and up to date, and no change to DAH or declarant data made without notifying them.

### 2.9 Maintenance standards and production (CAO.A.060)
- (a) Qualified persons. (b) Clean, organised area. (c) Methods per data and work orders. (d) Specified tools and material. (e) Environmental limits in the data. (f) Proper facilities in inclement weather or for lengthy work.
- (g) Minimise the risk of multiple errors and of errors repeated in identical tasks.
- (h) **Error-capturing method after any critical maintenance task**.
- (i) **General verification** after completion: clear of tools, equipment and extraneous parts or material, and all access panels refitted.
- (j) All maintenance properly recorded and documented.
- AMC1 (g):
  - Each task is signed off only after completion.
  - Tasks are grouped for sign-off so critical steps stay identifiable.
  - Work by supervised personnel (temporary staff, trainees) is checked and signed off by an authorised person.
  - For identical tasks on several systems (same type of component fitted to more than one system, where failure affects safety), plan **different persons** for each system. If only one person is available, they perform a reinspection.
- Critical tasks and independent inspection: see section 7.
- **Production planning**: Part-CAO has no explicit production-planning point (unlike 145.A.47). The nearest items are:
  - the hangar visit plan relative to the AMP (AMC1 CAO.A.030(a));
  - staff sufficiency for planned work;
  - the one-person "planning and carrying out" limitation;
  - CAM coordination of scheduled maintenance, ADs, life-limited part replacement and component inspection (CAO.A.075(b)(8)).

### 2.10 Certification of maintenance
- **Aircraft CRS**: under M.A.801 or ML.A.801 at the completion of any aircraft maintenance (CAO.A.065).
- **Component CRS**: under M.A.802 or ML.A.802. An **EASA Form 1** is issued per Part-M Appendix II, except:
  - the M.A.502(b) or (d) and ML.A.502 cases;
  - locally **fabricated parts** (CAO.A.020(c)), which never get a Form 1.
  
  Form 1 may be generated from a computer database (CAO.A.070(b)).
- GM1 CAO.A.070: Part-M Appendix II point 5, blocks 12 and 14a, describe how the CAO formalises a component release on Form 1. Used components released by a CAO on Form 1 cannot be installed on CMPA or air carrier aircraft.
- AMC1 CAO.A.070(a): component maintenance off-aircraft needs a CRS, and installation needs a separate CRS. For components maintained for the CAO's own use, a Form 1 may not be needed, depending on the internal CAE release procedure. Release of stored, removed or unapproved-maintained components is covered in section 7.
- The CRS is issued by the "authorised person" only after independent inspection or reinspection is satisfactory, for critical tasks.
- A copy of each CRS goes to the aircraft owner, together with any specific repair or modification data used (CAO.A.090(a)(1)).

### 2.11 Continuing airworthiness management (CAO.A.075, CAO.A.080)
- CAM is carried out per **Part-M Subpart C** or **Part-ML Subpart C**.
- For every aircraft managed, the CAO:
  1. Develops and controls the AMP. For **Part-ML** aircraft, it **approves** the AMP and its amendments. For **Part-M** aircraft, it presents the AMP to the authority for approval, unless an indirect approval procedure (M.A.302(c)) covers it.
  2. Provides a copy of the AMP to the owner.
  3. Ensures modification and repair data comply with M.A.304 / ML.A.304.
  4. Ensures all maintenance follows the AMP and is released under Part-M Subpart H, Part-145 Section A or Part-ML Subpart H.
  5. Ensures all applicable ADs, and operational directives with a CAW impact, are implemented.
  6. Ensures all defects found or reported are corrected by an appropriately approved MO or by independent certifying staff.
  7. Ensures the aircraft is brought to an appropriate MO or to independent certifying staff whenever necessary.
  8. Coordinates scheduled maintenance, ADs, replacement of life-limited parts and component inspections.
  9. Manages and archives all CAW records and, if applicable, the technical log.
  10. Ensures the mass and balance statement reflects the current aircraft status.
- AMC1 CAO.A.075:
  - The CAM privilege includes the tasks in M.A.301(b), (c), (f), (g), (h) and ML.A.301(b), (c), (d), (e).
  - Without the maintenance privilege, the CAO contracts MO(s) in agreement with the owner or operator.
  - The CAO is **responsible for the airworthy condition** of the managed aircraft and should be satisfied before the intended flight that all required maintenance has been done.
  - The CAO may check any aspect of contracted work at the MO's facilities.
  - The contract details each party's responsibilities and work.
- CAO.A.080: hold current data for CAM tasks. The owner may provide the data under contract (M.A.201(h)(2), (i)(1), (i)(3); ML.A.201(e)(1), (f)), in which case they are held only for the duration of the contract unless CAO.A.090(b) retention applies. AMC: with no CAM contract yet, no need to hold data.
- The checklist (items 9 and 13 to 16) expects:
  - a CAM contract per Part-M or Part-ML Appendix I with **each external owner/operator**;
  - correct pre-flight checks;
  - correct technical log use (hours and cycles, pilot-reported defects, next maintenance due);
  - no flights with overdue maintenance or improperly deferred defects;
  - no maintenance done without notifying the CAO;
  - fleet status (AD, AMP, LLPs, deferred maintenance, ARC validity) with no expired items, and aircraft grounded if any are expired;
  - Part-ML AMPs approved by the CAO and **reviewed annually**;
  - Part-M AMPs incorporating all DAH ICA revisions since the last review;
  - modifications and repairs reflected in the AMP;
  - amendments approved at the correct level (CAO, authority, or indirect);
  - tolerances and variations, and deviations from AMP tasks, managed;
  - deviations from DAH ICA justified and recorded;
  - all ADs since the last review in the AD status, with applicability, compliance date and periodicity correct;
  - modifications and repairs approved under M.A.304 / ML.A.304 and incorporated in the status.
- **Subcontracting of CAM tasks**: see section 7. A **small CAO using organisational review cannot contract CAM tasks to other parties** (CAO.A.100(f)).

### 2.12 Airworthiness review and ARC
- ARs follow **M.A.901** (Part-M) or **ML.A.903** (Part-ML) (CAO.A.085).
- An ARC may be issued under the CAM route (D.11) or under the maintenance privilege for Part-ML (C.15). Extensions are covered by D.12.
- Retain a copy of each ARC issued under ML.A.901(a), and each recommendation issued or extended, **with all supporting documents** (CAO.A.090(a)(3)).
- CAE B.10 covers carrying out the AR.

### 2.13 Permit to fly
- Issued under 21.A.711(d) for aircraft for which the CAO can issue the ARC, when it attests conformity with the **approved flight conditions** (CAE B.11 and B.12).
- Retain a copy of each PtF issued under 21.A.729 (CAO.A.090(a)(4)).

### 2.14 Records and retention (CAO.A.090)
- **(a)(1) Maintenance records**: those needed to show compliance for issuing the CRS, including **subcontractors' release documents**. A copy of each CRS, with specific repair or modification data, goes to the owner. **Retention: 3 years from the date the aircraft or component was released to service**, together with associated maintenance data (b).
- **(a)(2) CAM records**: M.A.305 and, if applicable, M.A.306 (technical log), or ML.A.305.
- **(a)(3)**: copies of ARCs and recommendations issued or extended, with supporting documents.
- **(a)(4)**: copies of PtFs.
- **(c) Retention for (a)(2) to (a)(4): 2 years from the date the aircraft is permanently withdrawn from service.**
- (d) Storage protects from damage, alteration and theft.
- (e) Backup hardware is stored in a **different location** from the main data, in good environmental conditions.
- (f) If CAM is transferred to another organisation or person, all (a)(2) to (a)(4) records go to them, and they take on the retention duties.
- (g) If the CAO terminates: (a)(1) records go to the **last owner or customer**, or are stored as the authority specifies. (a)(2) to (a)(4) records go to the **owner**.
- Other retention periods:
  - quality monitoring records: **at least the previous 2 years** (CAO.A.100(c));
  - AR staff records: 2 years after they leave (CAO.A.045(e));
  - calibration records: per CAO.A.090;
  - one-off authorisation evidence: held "on file" (no period stated);
  - fabrication records (heat treatment, final inspections): "adequate records" (no period stated);
  - authority records: ≥ 5 years (CAO.B.020(c)).
- AMC1 CAO.A.100(f)(d)(2): contracting a **software tool for CAM data (CAO.A.080) and records (CAO.A.090) is not subcontracting**, so it needs no quality system cover, but it must be described in the CAE and approved. Conditions:
  - in a multi-organisation tool, each organisation accesses only its own data;
  - data entry is done only by CAO personnel;
  - data can be retrieved at any time.
  
  This is **directly relevant to Airworthiness+**. The same applies to subscriptions to technical publishers.

### 2.15 Occurrence reporting
- Part-CAO has **no dedicated occurrence reporting point**. That obligation comes from Reg. (EU) 376/2014 and Part-M/ML (other chunks; the footnotes cite 376/2014 and 2015/1018).
- In this chunk:
  - Organisational review (Appendix II (e)): findings that lower the safety standard and seriously hazard flight safety are **immediately notified to the authority**, and immediate action is taken on aircraft in service. All occurrence reports are reviewed for continuous improvement (prior indicators, dismissed alerts), with corrective and preventive actions.
  - Checklist item 18: "Check that reporting is properly performed, actions taken and recorded."
  - AMC1 CAO.B.060(a)(1): for a Level 1 finding directly related to an aircraft, the authority informs the State of registry.
  - One-off authorisation: report to the authority within 7 days.

### 2.16 Quality system, organisational review and internal reviews (CAO.A.100)
- **Quality system plus a designated quality manager.** It monitors:
  - activities done per approved procedures;
  - **contracted maintenance done per contract**;
  - continued compliance.
  
  Records are kept at least the previous **2 years**. It may be combined with the quality system of another approval.
- **Small CAO** (any one of these conditions):
  1. the scope contains only Part-ML aircraft;
  2. **≤ 10 FTE** involved in maintenance;
  3. **≤ 5 FTE** involved in CAM.
  
  GM: an organisation with both privileges can be small for one and not for the other, in which case it is **not** small as a whole.
- **A small CAO may replace the quality system with regular organisational reviews**, subject to authority approval. It then **cannot contract CAM tasks**.
- Part-CAO has **no SMS or "internal safety review" requirement**, unlike Part-CAMO and the newer Part-145. The quality system or organisational review is the internal oversight mechanism.
- Quality system detail (GM1 and AMC1 to (a), AMC1 (b)):
  - **Independence**: auditors are not responsible for the function audited.
  - **Feedback system**: findings are investigated and corrected promptly. The procedure states who rectifies, and the escalation if rectification is not done in time. The AM is kept informed.
  - Audit reports go to the department, with target rectification dates agreed with the department before the quality department or auditor confirms them. The department reports rectification to the QM or auditor.
  - If the AM delegates day-to-day progress meetings to the QM, the AM still **meets senior staff at least twice a year** and receives at least a **half-yearly summary** of findings.
  - **Independent audit**: routine sample checks of all aspects, including **product sampling**.
  - An **audit plan** covers all Part-CAO aspects **every year**, including subcontracted activities, as one exercise or subdivided. A common procedure need not be verified against every product line if verified yearly without findings. Where there are findings, it is verified against other lines until they are rectified.
  - The cycle may be **increased by up to 100 %** (so up to 2 years) if there are no safety-related findings and the authority agrees.
  - **Multiple locations**: each location is audited at a frequency matching its activity, **not exceeding 2 years**.
  - A **report for each audit** states what was checked and the findings.
- GM1 CAO.A.100(b) and CAO.B.055: ICT remote audits follow GM1 145.A.200(a)(6) / 145.B.300 (ED 2022/011; may not be in UK AMC).
- Organisational review: see section 7 and Appendix II.

### 2.17 Changes (CAO.A.105, CAO.B.065)
- **Prior notification and approval** (before the change), for changes to:
  1. information on the certificate or the terms of approval;
  2. the AM or nominated persons;
  3. aircraft types in scope for aeroplanes > 2 730 kg MTOM and helicopters > 1 200 kg MTOM or > 4 occupants;
  4. the scope for complete turbine engines;
  5. the CAO.A.105(b) control procedure itself.
- **Other changes** (locations, facilities, equipment, tools, material, procedures, scope, staff) are controlled by the CAE procedure. The CAO submits a description and the CAE amendments **within 15 days** of the change taking place.
- Authority side: verifies compliance before approving. It may set conditions for operating during the change, or suspend if the nature or extent of the change warrants it. Non-approval changes are assessed during oversight. Audits focus particularly on changes notified under CAO.A.105(b).
- Subcontracted CAM organisations notify the CAO of changes affecting the contract as soon as practical, and the CAO informs the authority. Failure to do so may invalidate the contract's acceptance.

### 2.18 Findings (CAO.A.115, CAO.B.060)
- **Level 1**: "any significant non-compliance with Part-CAO requirements which lowers the safety standard and seriously hazards flight safety". The authority takes immediate action to revoke, limit or suspend the approval, in whole or part, until successful corrective action. The State of registry is informed if the finding relates to an aircraft. Re-certification of affected products may be required.
- **Level 2**: "any non-compliance with the Part-CAO requirements which may lower the safety standard and possibly hazard flight safety". The corrective action period is **no more than 3 months**, extendable at the end of the first period subject to a satisfactory corrective action plan.
- The CAO adopts a **corrective action plan** and demonstrates corrective action within the authority's period.
- Missing the deadline leads to suspension in whole or part.
- Findings are confirmed in writing and recorded with closure actions and recommendations.
- Internal findings follow the quality feedback system or the organisational review findings management.

### 2.19 Continued validity
- **Unlimited duration**, subject to compliance (including findings handling), authority access, and no surrender or revocation.
- The certificate is returned on surrender or revocation.
- The certificate text also mentions "superseded" and "suspended".

### 2.20 Authority oversight figures (relevant to the CAO's audit readiness)
- The CAO is audited at intervals **of 24 months or less**.
- A **sample of the aircraft managed** is surveyed every **24 months**, with the sample size based on prior audits and product surveys.
- An AM meeting is held at least every **24 months**.
- Subcontracted CAM organisations are audited by the authority at intervals of 24 months or less. Subcontracted specialised maintenance organisations are audited at the authority's discretion. A senior technical CAO member accompanies the inspector.
- Authority records are kept ≥ **5 years**.

## 4. Defined terms
- **Competent authority** (CAO.1(1)): the Member State-designated authority, or EASA for a third-country principal place of business.
- **Owner** (CAO.1(2)): "the person responsible for the continuing airworthiness of the aircraft", including (i) the registered owner, (ii) the lessee under a leasing contract, (iii) the operator.
- **Combined airworthiness organisation (CAO)** and **combined airworthiness exposition (CAE)** (CAO.A.010, CAO.A.020).
- **Level 1 finding** / **Level 2 finding** (CAO.A.115): quoted in 2.18.
- **Small CAO** (CAO.A.100(e)): Part-ML only, or ≤ 10 FTE in maintenance, or ≤ 5 FTE in CAM. See the GM on mixed privileges.
- **Aircraft rating / engine rating / component rating / NDT rating** (Appendix I (b) to (e)).
- **System ratings C1 to C22** (CAO.A.020(a)(4)).
- **"Tools necessary for day-to-day maintenance"** (AMC1 CAO.A.050(a)).
- **"Segregation"** (AMC1 CAO.A.030(k)): "storing unserviceable components in a separate secured location from serviceable components".
- **"Experience in continuing airworthiness"** (AMC1 CAO.A.045(b)).
- **"Appropriate recent continuing airworthiness experience"** (AMC1 CAO.A.045(c)): 6 months of CAM in every 2 years, or 1 AR in the last 12 months.
- **Equivalent to aeronautical degree** / **"relevant engineering degree"** (AMC1 CAO.A.045(e)).
- **Critical maintenance task** (AMC1 CAO.A.060(h)): see section 7.
- **Independent inspection**, **"authorised person"**, **"independent qualified person"**, **reinspection** (AMC2 CAO.A.060(h)).
- **"Appropriately rated"** (for the AMC1 CAO.A.070(a) point 2 only): an organisation with an approval class rating for the type of component, or for the product in which it may be installed.
- **Footnote definitions** that appear at the end of the book (informal, from other parts of the EAR, useful for UI labels):
  - "Commercial": a balloon operated under Part-BOP Subpart-ADD, a sailplane under Part-SAO Subpart-DEC, or other aircraft not operated under Part-NCO; includes commercial ATOs and DTOs.
  - "CMPA": complex motor-powered aircraft (Art. 3(j) of Reg. 216/2008).
  - "'Light' a/c" (informal): aeroplanes ≤ 2 730 kg MTOM, rotorcraft ≤ 1 200 kg MTOM and max 4 occupants, and other ELA2.
  - "Individual CAM" (informal): continuing airworthiness managed by the owner under its own responsibility.
  - "CAO-CAM" (informal): a Part-CAO organisation with the CAM privilege.
  - "Individual maintenance" (informal): maintenance released by a pilot-owner or independent certifying staff.
  - "CAO-M" (informal): a Part-CAO organisation with the maintenance privilege.
  - Other footnotes in the tail belong to Part-66 (duration reductions: −50 % for non-pressurised piston aeroplanes < 2 000 kg MTOM, −30 % for Group 2 helicopters; Module 8L; licence limitations). They are not Part-CAO content.

### 5.2 Workflows
1. **Initial approval and conversion**: build Form 2; draft the CAE from the A to E template; nominate personnel; record the AM statement signature; track authority findings to closure; record the issued Form 3-CAO. For conversions, import the existing Part-M F/G or 145 scope and check that privileges are identical; carry AR authorisations over.
2. **Scope change**: a rules engine classifies a new type, engine or C-rating as prior-approval or internal. Prior-approval changes block use until approval. Internal changes start the 15-day submission clock.
3. **AR staff onboarding**: check the experience thresholds, nominate, run a supervised AR, record authority acceptance, issue the authorisation, list the person in CAE A.12, and monitor recency.
4. **One-off authorisation**: an AOG-away-from-base wizard checks the eligibility branch, collects evidence, issues the authorisation, sets a 7-day authority report task and a flight-safety recheck task.
5. **Maintenance job**: agree the work order, then confirm data availability, then plan staff (with a different-person check for identical tasks), execute task cards, run independent inspection or reinspection for critical tasks, do the general verification, issue the CRS (if all are complete), and send the CRS and repair/mod data to the owner. Component path: component CRS / Form 1.
6. **Goods-in**: receive, then incoming inspection per M.A.501/ML.A.501 (documents, condition), then classify, then store in serviceable or quarantine. For unserviceable items: decision workflow (maintain / store / mutilate), recorded traceably. Unsalvageable items go to a secure area and are disposed of per ML.A.504 / M.A.504.
7. **Form 1 for stored, used or removed components** (point 2 of AMC1 CAO.A.070(a)): checklists by case (new/unused; removed serviceable from a Member State aircraft; non-Member State aircraft only if leased or loaned from the CAO; withdrawn aircraft with a disassembly plan; unapproved MO with strip, LLP replacement and test; FAA 8130-3 / TCCA alleviation; accident aircraft with a specific work order).
8. **CAM per aircraft**: maintain the AMP (approve it if ML; annual review), track AD/mod/LLP status, raise work orders to an MO, check the returned CRS, the component list and Form 1s, update M&B, and confirm airworthy status before flight.
9. **AR / ARC**: plan the AR from the ARC expiry, assign authorised AR staff, issue the ARC, recommendation or extension, and archive with supporting documents.
10. **PtF**: check flight conditions, attest conformity, issue, archive.
11. **Quality / organisational review cycle**: generate the annual audit plan from the CAE sections and Part-CAO points; ensure every item is covered every 12 months; per-location cadence; record findings with agreed target dates; produce a half-yearly AM summary; hold a twice-yearly AM and senior staff review.
12. **Record transfer**: on a CAM transfer, export the (a)(2) to (a)(4) records package. On termination, assign records to the owner or customer.

### 5.3 Alerts and validations
- AR staff recency: alert before CAM activity in the rolling 2 years falls below 6 months **and** there has been no AR in 12 months. Mark lapsed, require a supervised restoration.
- Certifying staff 66.A.20(b) recency and licence validity.
- One-off authorisation report due in 7 days; recheck task open.
- Tool calibration due or overdue (block use on task cards).
- Component shelf-life expiry; storage-life-limited parts.
- CAE-controlled change submission due at +15 days.
- L2 finding due (≤ 3 months, or the authority-set date); internal finding overdue escalates to the AM.
- Audit plan coverage gaps (a CAE section or CAO point not audited in 12 months); location not audited in 2 years.
- Part-ML AMP annual review due; ARC expiry; AD due; LLP due; expired items, so ground the aircraft.
- Authority audit, sample survey and AM meeting cycle (24 months), for readiness.
- Retention timers: maintenance records at CRS date + 3 years; CAM, ARC and PtF records at permanent withdrawal + 2 years; quality records ≥ 2 years; AR staff records at leaving + 2 years. Block deletion before expiry.
- Validations:
  - Work order must exist before task start.
  - CRS cannot be issued before an independent inspection is recorded on a critical task.
  - Same person on identical tasks across systems: warning.
  - Fabricated part: no Form 1, no external issue.
  - Used component Form 1: warn that it cannot be installed on CMPA or air carrier aircraft.
  - Small CAO on organisational review: block CAM subcontracting.
  - CAM subcontractor must appear on the Form 3-CAO list.
  - Only one CAM subcontractor per aircraft type (except engine/APU), otherwise require a coordination note.
  - AR privilege requires a Member State principal place of business, plus CAM or maintenance (maintenance route for Part-ML only).
  - PtF requires the AR privilege.
  - One-person organisation: block turbine and multi-piston helicopter scope.

### 5.4 Permissions (role-based)
- Only authorised AR staff can issue or extend ARCs.
- Only listed PtF staff can issue PtFs.
- Only authorised certifying staff can issue a CRS or Form 1, within their scope.
- Only AMP staff can approve a Part-ML AMP.
- The independent qualified person can sign the independent inspection without CRS privileges.
- Trainees and temporary staff work only under supervision and cannot sign final.
- Stores access is limited to authorised personnel.
- Unserviceable and unsalvageable decisions are made by designated persons.
- Quality and audit roles cannot audit their own area (independence check).
- The AM signs the CAE statement.
- Only CAO personnel may enter data into the shared software tool (AMC1 CAO.A.100(f)(d)(2)). **Multi-tenant isolation is a regulatory condition**: each organisation sees only its own data, and data must be retrievable at any time.
- Owners and customers get read access to CRS copies, the AMP and their aircraft records.
- An authority/auditor read-only view (Form 613 evidence pack).
- Records are tamper-evident (protection from alteration), with off-site backup in a separate location (CAO.A.090(d), (e)).

## 7. Notable AMC and GM (detail)

### 7.1 AMC1 CAO.A.020(c): fabrication of parts
- The authority's agreement is formalised by approving a detailed CAE procedure (C.8).
- Fabrication, inspection, assembly and test must be within the organisation's technical and procedural capability.
- **Data**: approved by EASA, the TC holder, a Part 21 DOA holder or an STC holder, or declared by a declarant of design compliance (Part 21 Light Subpart C).
- Parts are used only by the CAO, during overhaul, maintenance, modification or repair of items in its own facilities. **This is not a manufacturing approval. No external supply. No EASA Form 1.** In bulk transfers or surplus inventory, locally fabricated parts are physically segregated and excluded from delivery certification.
- No fabrication of parts or modification kits for onward supply or sale.
- Repair data may include fabrication if sufficient: P/N, dimensions, materials, processes, special techniques, raw material spec, incoming inspection. The capability is defined in the CAE. If the data call for special processes the CAO lacks, it cannot fabricate unless the TC/STC holder or declarant gives an approved alternative.
- **Examples**:
  - bushes, sleeves, shims;
  - secondary structural elements and skin panels;
  - control cables;
  - flexible and rigid pipes;
  - electrical looms and assemblies;
  - formed or machined sheet metal repair panels.
- **No fabrication "to pattern"** unless an engineering drawing including the processes is produced and accepted by the authority.
- When a TC holder, declarant or production organisation (Part 21 Light Subparts G and R are mentioned) supplies manufacturing drawings for parts-list items not referenced in manuals or SBs, fabricating those items is **outside** CAO scope unless the authority agrees via a CAE procedure.
- **Inspection and identification**:
  - an inspection stage before, and separate from (preferably independent of), the installation inspection;
  - full conformity with the data;
  - the part is identified as fit for use by a statement of conformity;
  - adequate records, including heat treatment and final inspections;
  - a P/N linked to the data (unless there is no space), plus the **CAO identity marking** for traceability.

### 7.2 AMC1 CAO.A.030: facilities and stores
Points (a) to (n) are summarised in 2.6 and 2.7. Key product points:

### 7.6 AMC1 CAO.A.060(g) and (h), AMC2 CAO.A.060(h): error management
- **Critical maintenance tasks**, reviewed primarily for safety impact:
  - (a) tasks affecting control of flight path and attitude (installation, rigging and adjustment of flight controls);
  - (b) aircraft stability control systems (autopilot, fuel transfer);
  - (c) propulsive force (installation of engines, propellers, rotors);
  - (d) overhaul, calibration or rigging of engines, propellers, transmissions and gearboxes.
- **Independent inspection** is one possible error-capturing method:
  - The "authorised person" does or supervises the task and takes full responsibility.
  - The "independent qualified person" inspects and attests satisfactory completion with no deficiencies. They do **not** issue a CRS, so they need no certification privileges.
  - The CRS is issued by the authorised person after a satisfactory inspection.
  - The work card records each person's identity, the date and the details before the CRS.
- **IQP qualification**, shown by any of:
  - a Part-66 licence in the same subcategory (or equivalent) needed to release the task;
  - a Part-66 licence in the same category plus specific task training;
  - appropriate training and relevant experience in the specific task.
- **How to inspect control systems**:
  1. all disturbed parts checked for assembly and locking;
  2. full and free movement over the complete range;
  3. cable tension correct, with clearance at secondary stops;
  4. correct sense of operation;
  5. interactions of interconnected systems checked through the full range;
  6. **software that is part of the task checked (version, compatibility with configuration)**.
- **Reinspection**: the authorised person also acts as IQP. Only in **unforeseen** circumstances when only one person is available. It is not unforeseen if no suitable IQP was assigned. The CRS is issued after reinspection, and the work card records the details.
- AMC1 (g): see 2.9 (sign-off only after completion, grouping, supervised work, different persons on identical tasks).

### 7.7 AMC1 CAO.A.070(a): Form 1 for components without prior release
1. Off-aircraft maintenance needs a CRS for the maintenance and another CRS for installation. Own-use components may not need a Form 1, depending on the CAE.
2. Applies to:
   - components in storage from before Part-145, Part-M or Part 21;
   - components removed serviceable from aircraft, including leased or loaned ones;
   - components from withdrawn aircraft, or from aircraft involved in accidents, incidents, heavy landings or lightning strikes;
   - components maintained by an unapproved organisation.
   
   The certifying organisation must be an **appropriately rated** Part-CAO organisation (see section 4), using CAE procedures, and is responsible for taking all reasonable measures.
   - 2.4 Form 1 content: sign **block 14b**, state **"Inspected/Tested" in block 11**. **Block 12** gives:
     - the last maintenance, when and by whom;
     - if unused, the manufacture date and maker, with a cross-reference to original documents (to be included);
     - all known ADs, repairs and modifications, or a statement that none are known;
     - life used for LLPs (fatigue, overhaul, storage);
     - a reference to the component's own maintenance history record if it has the required detail.
     
     The history record and acceptance test report are attached.
   - 2.5 New/unused: applies to components manufactured by an acceptable organisation before Part 21 dates, stored without a Form 1.
     - Note: this is a maintenance release, not a production release, and must not bypass production release for the manufacturer's own line.
     - Conditions: (a) acceptance test report or statement available where acceptance testing applies; (b) inspection against the manufacturer's storage instructions and limits (storage life, inhibitors, climate, containers), and for damage, corrosion and leakage; (c) storage life used established.
     - If these cannot be satisfied: disassemble, check ADs, mods and repairs, inspect or test per data, replace seals, lubricants and LLPs as relevant, reassemble, then issue a Form 1 stating what was done and the data reference.
   - 2.6 Removed from a serviceable Member State aircraft. Conditions:
     - removal by a qualified person;
     - the last flight with the component fitted showed no faults on it or its system;
     - condition inspection (damage, corrosion, leakage) plus any additional data;
     - aircraft records researched for accidents, incidents, heavy landings or lightning strikes. **Never** issue a Form 1 if extreme stress, temperature or immersion is suspected;
     - maintenance history record for serialised parts;
     - modifications and repairs established;
     - LLP hours, cycles and landings, and TSO, established;
     - ADs established.
     
     The Form 1 includes the source aircraft. For a non-Member State aircraft, only if the component is leased or loaned from the CAO, which retains control of its airworthiness status.
   - 2.7 Aircraft withdrawn from service:
     - Dismantling for spares is maintenance, under Part-CAO approved procedures.
     - An assessment to the 2.5 and 2.6 standards, including any alignment of scheduled maintenance with the receiving aircraft's AMP.
     - Removal and storage compatible with Part-CAO, whether or not the aircraft has a CofA.
     - A **structured disassembly plan**, with work done by an appropriately rated organisation under certifying staff supervision.
     - Review of recorded defects and their effects on normal and standby functions.
     - **Dedicated control documentation**.
     - Unserviceable components are identified and **quarantined** pending a decision.
     - Serviceability records form part of the component history.
     - Suitable facilities (environment, lighting, access equipment, tooling, storage). Removal may be done outdoors if local conditions allow, but disassembly and storage follow manufacturer recommendations.
   - 2.8 Maintained by an organisation not approved under Part-M F, Part-CAO or Part-145: dismantle and inspect, replace LLPs without evidence, reassemble and test, then certify per CAO.A.070. **Alleviation for FAA Part-145 and TCCA CAR573 organisations** (based on the FAA–EASA and TCCA–EASA MAGs):
     - Form 8130-3, TCCA 24-0078 or Authorized Release Certificate Form One available;
     - AD compliance verified;
     - no repairs or modifications not approved under Part 21;
     - condition inspection;
     - Form 1 issued per 2.2 to 2.4.
   - 2.9 Accident or incident aircraft: process per 2.7 plus a **specific work order** with the additional tests and inspections, possibly with TC holder or OEM input. The work order is referenced in block 12.
3. No certificate for a known unserviceable component, except when a component moves between approved MOs during a series of processes. In that case a clear limitation statement goes in block 12.
4. The Form 1 serves export, import and domestic purposes. It is issued only by approved organisations within their scope.

### 7.8 AMC1 CAO.A.075 and CAO.A.080

### 7.9 AMC1 CAO.A.095(b)(3): subcontracting CAM tasks (ED 2021/009)
- The subcontractor works as an integral part of the CAO quality system, whatever its own approvals.
- The CAO remains accountable. It manages the work by active control through direct involvement and/or by endorsing the subcontractor's recommendations.
- **Permitted tasks**:
  - AD analysis and planning;
  - SB analysis;
  - maintenance planning;
  - reliability and engine health monitoring;
  - AMP development and amendments;
  - others agreed by the authority that do not limit CAO responsibility.
- Controls are reflected in the contract and the CAE, and the quality system extends to the subcontractor.
- **One organisation per aircraft type** for any combination of tasks, except engines and APUs. With more than one, the CAO demonstrates coordination controls and defines responsibilities.
- **No further subcontracting** by the subcontractor.
- The authority oversees via the CAO approval and must find the contracts acceptable. The CAO subcontracts only to organisations **listed on Form 3-CAO page 2**.
- The subcontractor notifies changes to the CAO as soon as practical, and the CAO informs the authority, otherwise the contract acceptance may be invalidated.
- The Part-CAMO Appendix II principles may be applied.

### 7.11 AMC1 CAO.A.100(f) and Appendix II: organisational review
- **Objectives**: monitor compliance and request action.
- **The CAO identifies**:
  - the responsible person;
  - the frequency;
  - the scope and content;
  - the reviewers;
  - the procedure for planning, performing and processing findings;
  - the procedure for timely corrective action.
- **Not subcontracting** (no quality system cover needed, but described in the CAE and approved):
  1. a technical publisher subscription (AMM, IPC, SBs);
  2. a **software tool for CAM data and records**, provided that each organisation sees only its own data, only CAO staff enter data, and the data are retrievable at any time.
- **Appendix II features**. The system can range from a quality-system-like design (without independence) to a simplified one, depending on complexity (number and type of aircraft, fleets, AR privilege).
  - (a) Responsible person: the AM by default, or delegated to a CAO.A.035(b) person.
  - (b) Reviewers: thorough knowledge of the regulations and procedures, plus audit knowledge from training or experience (for example, having taken part in authority audits).
  - (c) Programme: checklist(s) covering all CAE procedures, and a schedule in which **each item is checked at least every 12 months** (one full review or several partial ones).
  - (d) Method: an appropriate combination of:
    - review of records and documents;
    - sample check of aircraft under contract or work order;
    - interviews;
    - review of discrepancies and internal reports;
    - review of customer complaints.
  - (e) Findings and occurrences:
    - all findings are recorded and notified to the affected persons;
    - serious-hazard findings are notified **immediately** to the authority, with immediate action on aircraft in service;
    - occurrence reports are reviewed for prior indicators and dismissed alerts;
    - CAPA is approved by the responsible person and implemented in a specified time frame;
    - closure is recorded with a corrective action summary once the action is effective;
    - the AM is notified of significant findings and of global results regularly.
- **Example checklist (19 headings)**. This is a ready-made audit template for the product.
  1. **Scope of work**:
     - all contracted aircraft covered by Form 3-CAO;
     - CAE scope consistent with Form 3-CAO;
     - no work outside scope;
     - whether types with no aircraft under contract should still be retained.
  2. **Maintenance data**: present and current; no unnotified changes to DAH or declarant data.
  3. **Equipment and tools**: availability against the CAE lists, still appropriate to the data; calibration sample check.
  4. **Stores**: meet CAE criteria; sample items for documentation and overdue items.
  5. **Certification of maintenance**: properly certified; mods and repairs properly approved (sample).
  6. **Maintenance contracted**: sample records for:
     - a work order;
     - a valid CRS, including deferred maintenance;
     - a list of removed and installed components with Form 1 copies;
     - a copy of the contracted MO's current EASA Form 3.
  7. **Maintenance subcontracted**: specialised service subcontractors controlled.
  8. **Owners and operators, maintenance**: suitable work orders; contract obligations respected.
  9. **Owners and operators, CAM**:
     - a contract per Part-M or Part-ML Appendix I with each external owner, covering all aircraft;
     - owner obligations met: pre-flight checks (interview pilots), tech log use (hours and cycles, defects, next due), no flights with overdue maintenance or unrectified or undeferred defects, no maintenance without notifying the CAO.
  10. **Maintenance records**: proper recording; sample check including Form 1s and certificates of conformity for completeness and retention.
  11. **CAW records**: completeness and retention; computerised storage ensured.
  12. **AR and PtF records**: sample check.
  13. **Fleet airworthiness status**: AD, AMP, LLP, deferred maintenance and ARC validity, with no expired items; if any, the aircraft is grounded.
  14. **AMP**:
      - Part-ML AMP approved by the CAO and annually reviewed;
      - Part-M AMP incorporating all DAH ICA revisions since the last review;
      - mods and repairs considered;
      - amendments approved at the right level;
      - compliance status reflects the latest AMP;
      - tolerances and variations, and deviations from tasks, managed;
      - deviations from ICA justified and recorded.
  15. **ADs**: all new ADs in the status; applicability, compliance date and periodicity correct (sample).
  16. **Mods and repairs**: approved per M.A.304 / ML.A.304; new installations incorporated in the status (sample from the logbooks).
  17. **Personnel**:
      - AM and NPs correctly identified in the CAE;
      - staffing sufficient if headcount has dropped or activity has risen;
      - new or changed staff assessed;
      - training on changes to regulations, authority publications, the CAE and procedures, the approved scope, and maintenance data (significant ADs, ICA amendments).
  18. **Occurrence reporting**: reporting done, actions taken and recorded.
  19. **AR and PtF procedures**: ARs performed properly and the ARC or recommendation issued properly; PtFs issued properly and flight conditions complied with.

### 7.12 Section B AMC and GM
- **GM1 CAO.B.017**: an AltMoC used by one CAO may be used by another only after being re-processed under CAO.B.017(d).
- **GM1 CAO.B.045(a)**: CAE approval equals formal acceptance of the AM and NPs.
- **AMC1 CAO.B.045**: the initial audit includes interviews and facility inspections, and is done only once the application documents are compliant. Focus areas:
  - management structure and NP qualifications;
  - staff numbers and qualifications;
  - validity of licences and authorisations;
  - quality system or organisational review;
  - facilities;
  - CAE procedures and the AM signature.
  
  If refused, the applicant is told of the right of appeal under national law.
- **AMC2 CAO.B.045**: holding maintenance data via a procedure is acceptable, but capability and CAO.A.050(a) compliance must be shown.
- **AMC1 CAO.B.045(c), AMC1 CAO.B.055**: EASA Form 613 is used. After the audit, the auditing surveyor completes the report with findings, closure actions and the recommendation. A competent independent person nominated by the authority reviews and signs it.
- **AMC2 CAO.B.055**: subcontracted CAM organisations are audited at intervals of 24 months or less. Specialised maintenance subcontractors are audited case by case (nature, complexity, previous results, the subcontractor's approvals or industry certification). A senior technical CAO member accompanies the inspector. The authority checks that the CAO has sufficient control over its subcontractors.
- **AMC1 CAO.B.060(a)(1)**: for L1 findings, inform the State of registry for aircraft-related findings; re-maintenance and re-certification of affected products may be required.
