## 2. Obligations

### Article 3: which rule set applies (determines the regime for each aircraft)
- **Part-M (Annex I)** applies by default to Article 1(a) aircraft and components.
- **Part-ML (Annex Vb)** applies to *other than complex motor-powered aircraft* that are:
  - (a) "aeroplanes of 2 730 kg maximum take-off mass or less";
  - (b) "rotorcraft of 1 200 kg maximum take-off mass or less, certified for a maximum of up to 4 occupants";
  - (c) "other ELA2 aircraft".
  - **Exception:** if such an aircraft is listed in the AOC of an air carrier licensed under Reg. 1008/2008, **Part-M applies**.
- **Art. 3(3): to list a Part-ML-eligible aircraft on a licensed air carrier's AOC**, all three are needed: (a) an AMP approved by the competent authority under M.A.302; (b) due maintenance under that AMP performed and certified under 145.A.48 and 145.A.50 (Part-145 CRS); (c) an airworthiness review performed and a **new ARC issued** under M.A.901.
- **Art. 3(4):** an aircraft with a **permit to fly** follows the specific CAW arrangements defined in the permit (Part-21, Reg. 748/2012).
- **Art. 3(5):** AMPs compliant with M.A.302 as applicable **before 24 March 2020** are deemed compliant with M.A.302 or ML.A.302.
- **Art. 3(6):** operators ensure CAW of Article 1(b) aircraft (third-country, dry leased-in) under **Part-T (Annex Va)**.
- **Art. 3(7):** aeroplanes with MTOM "at or below 5 700 kg" fitted with **multiple turboprop engines** (which are CMPA by definition, because they have more than one turboprop) are treated as non-CMPA for M.A.201, M.A.301, M.A.302, M.A.601, M.A.803, 145.A.30, 66.A.5, 66.A.30, 66.A.70, Part-66 Appendices V and VI, CAMO.A.315, CAO.A.010 and Part-CAO Appendix I.

### Article 4: organisation approvals and transitions
- Organisations are approved "upon their request" under Part-145, Part-CAMO or Part-CAO.
- Part-M Subpart F and G approvals could be issued until **24 September 2020** and were valid until **24 March 2022** (now lapsed in the EU).
- JAR-145 approvals valid before **29 November 2003** are deemed Part-145.
- Holders of Subpart F, Subpart G or Part-145 approvals may get a **Form 3-CAO** on request. Privileges are the same, but capped at Part-CAO Section A privileges. Part-CAO-specific findings had to close by **24 March 2022**, otherwise the approval is revoked, limited or suspended.
- Subpart G holders may get an **EASA Form 14** (Part-CAMO) on request. Part-CAMO-specific findings had to close by **24 March 2022**, with the same consequence.
- Certificates and AMP approvals issued under 1321/2014 as applicable before **24 March 2020** are deemed valid.
- Part-145 holders could close findings against requirements introduced by 2021/1963 (management system/SMS) until **2 December 2024**, derogating from 145.B.350(d)(1) and (2). After that date: revoke, limit or suspend.

### Article 5: certifying staff
- Qualified under Part-66, except under M.A.606(h), M.A.607(b), M.A.801(c), M.A.803, ML.A.801(c), ML.A.803, CAO.A.040(b) and (c), 145.A.30(j) and Part-145 Appendix IV.
- Licences without individual type ratings: privileges continue "until the first renewal or change". The licence is then converted under 66.B.125 to the ratings in 66.A.45.
- Component certifying staff: **national law** applies until EU requirements exist. For maintenance organisations outside the EU, the Agency approves the requirements.
- Limited certifying staff authorisations for flight engineers under 145.A.30(j)(3) or (4) issued before **2 December 2022** stay valid until they expire or the maintenance organisation revokes them.

### Article 6: training (Part-147)
- Type courses approved before the OSD minimum syllabus must include the mandatory OSD elements "not later than 18 December 2017 or within two years after the operational suitability data was approved, whichever is the latest".
- Basic training (or part of it) that commenced before **12 June 2024** must be finished, with its examinations, before **12 June 2026**, and the CofRs must also be issued before **12 June 2026**. They are issued under the pre-12 June 2024 rules.
- For a Part-66 licence issued or changed after 12 June 2024, the authority accepts basic knowledge exam status gained under the pre-12 June 2024 rules.

### Article 7a: competent authorities
- If a State has several authorities, their areas (responsibilities, geography) must be clearly defined and coordinated.
- Authority staff must not perform certification or oversight where there is a conflict of interest (family or financial).
- Authority powers: examine records, data and procedures; make copies or extracts; ask for oral explanations on-site; enter premises, operating sites and means of transport; perform audits, investigations, assessments and inspections, **including unannounced ones**; take enforcement measures.

### Article 8: entry into force (mostly historic)
- In force on the 20th day after OJ publication.
- Optional State opt-outs: Part-66 certifying staff for piston non-pressurised aeroplanes of 2000 kg MTOM and below not in CAT until **28 September 2014**; Part-T (Annex Va) for dry leased-in third-country aircraft until **25 August 2017**. States using these must notify the Commission and the Agency.
- 2a: requirements for SPO and non-air-carrier CAT under Reg. 965/2012 applied from **21 April 2017**, with interim application of M.A.201(f), (h), M.A.306(a), M.A.801(c), M.A.803(b) and M.A.901(g) to defined categories.
- 4: the origin of time for the Part-66 time limits (66.A.25, 66.A.30, Appendix III) for items completed before 1149/2011 applied is the date 1149/2011 applied.
- 6: old-format certificates (Part-M Appendix III; Part-147 Appendices II and III) could be issued until **31 December 2015**. Certificates issued before **1 January 2016** stay valid until changed, suspended or revoked.
- 2018/1142: applies from **5 March 2019**. For ELA1 aeroplanes not in CAT, and aircraft other than aeroplanes and helicopters, Part-66 licence issue applies from **1 October 2019** and the Part-66 certifying staff requirement (M.A.606(g), M.A.801(b)(2), 145.A.30(g),(h)) from **1 October 2020**.

### M.1: competent authority (who oversees what)
1. Individual aircraft CAW oversight and **ARC issue**: the authority of the **State of registry**.
2. Subpart F maintenance organisation: the State of its principal place of business, or the **Agency** if it is in a third country.
3. **AMP approval**: the State of registry, or, if the State of registry agrees before approval, (a) the State of the operator's principal place of business (or its place of establishment or residence if it has none), or (b) the authority overseeing the CAMO managing the aircraft, or the CAMO/CAO holding the owner's **limited contract** under M.A.201(i)(3).
4. Subpart G CAMO: the State of its principal place of business if the approval is not in an AOC; the State of the operator if it is in an AOC; the Agency if it is in a third country.

### M.A.201: responsibilities
- **(a) The owner** is responsible for CAW and shall ensure **no flight** unless all four hold:
  1. the aircraft is maintained in an airworthy condition;
  2. any operational and emergency equipment fitted is correctly installed and serviceable, **or clearly identified as unserviceable**;
  3. the **airworthiness certificate is valid**;
  4. maintenance is performed in accordance with the **AMP (M.A.302)**.
- **(b) Lease:** owner responsibilities transfer to the **lessee** if the lessee is stipulated on the **registration document** or detailed in the **leasing contract**. "Owner" in Part-M means owner or lessee, as applicable.
- **(c)** Any person or organisation performing maintenance is responsible for the tasks performed.
- **(d) Pre-flight inspection:** the responsibility of the PIC, or of the operator for licensed air carriers. It is carried out by the pilot or another qualified person and does **not** need an approved maintenance organisation or certifying staff.
- **(e) Licensed air carrier (1008/2008):** the operator is responsible for CAW and shall (1) ensure the (a) conditions are met; (2) obtain CAMO approval (Part-CAMO, or Subpart G) **as part of its AOC**; (3) hold Part-145 approval, or have a written contract under CAMO.A.315(c) or M.A.708(c) with a Part-145 organisation.
- **(ea) Shared group CAMO (2022/410):** at least two operators in a single air carrier business grouping may use the same CAMO for **all** the aircraft they operate, if: (1) the CAMO is Part-CAMO approved for those aircraft; (2) the CAMO is in the same grouping; (3) there is a contract per **Appendix I to Part-M** between the CAMO and each AOC holder that is not itself a CAMO; (4) the CAMO's principal place of business is in the EU (Treaty territory); (5) the management systems are **harmonised**.
- **(eb)** If an AOC is terminated or revoked and an air carrier no longer meets (ea), it must define and implement an **action plan** acceptable to the authority to comply with (e)(2) "as soon as practicable" (no fixed days).
- **(f) CMPA used for SPO, or CAT other than by licensed air carriers, or by commercial ATOs/DTOs:** the operator ensures the (a) conditions; CAW tasks are performed by a CAMO (Part-CAMO or Subpart G), with an Appendix I written contract if the operator is not itself a CAMO; and the CAMO is Part-145 approved or has a CAMO.A.315(c)/M.A.708(c) contract with Part-145 organisations.
- **(g) CMPA not covered by (e) or (f)** (e.g. private or non-commercial CMPA): the **owner** has the same obligations as (f): a CAMO with an Appendix I contract, and Part-145 maintenance.
- **(h) Non-CMPA used for SPO, or CAT other than by licensed air carriers, or by commercial ATOs/DTOs:** the operator ensures a **CAMO or CAO** (Appendix I contract if the operator is not one). Maintenance by Part-145, Part-M Subpart F, or a CAO with maintenance privileges, or under a written contract with such organisations.
- **(i) Non-CMPA not covered by (e) or (h), or used for limited operations:** the owner ensures the (a) conditions by **one of**: (1) contracting the M.A.301 tasks to a CAMO or CAO via an **Appendix I** contract; (2) doing the tasks **himself**; (3) doing them himself **except** the development and approval processing of the AMP, which a CAMO or CAO performs through a **limited contract** under M.A.302.
- **(j)** The owner or operator grants any person authorised by the competent authority **access** to facilities, aircraft and documents, including subcontracted activities.
- **(k)** An aircraft on an AOC used for non-commercial operations or specialised operations under ORO.GEN.310 or NCO.GEN.104 (Reg. 965/2012): CAW tasks must be done by the AOC holder's CAMO or CAO.

### M.A.202: occurrence reporting
- **Who:** any person or organisation responsible under M.A.201 (without prejudice to Part-145 and Part-CAMO reporting).
- **What:** "any identified condition of an aircraft or component which endangers flight safety".
- **To:** (1) the competent authority of the State of registry and, if different, of the State of the operator; (2) the organisation responsible for the type design or supplemental type design (TC/STC holder).
- **(c)** Where maintenance or the airworthiness review is done under a written contract, the contractor also reports to the **owner and the operator** and, when different, the **CAMO or CAO**.
- **(d) Deadline:** "as soon as possible, but no later than **72 hours** from the moment when the person or organisation identified the condition", unless exceptional circumstances prevent this.
- **(e) Follow-up report:** details of the actions intended to prevent recurrence, "as soon as those actions have been identified", in the form and manner the authority sets.
- **(b)** Reports are made in the manner the authority determines and contain all pertinent information known.

## 4. Defined Terms (Article 2, plus terms with regulatory meaning)

- **Aircraft**: "any machine that can derive support in the atmosphere from the reactions of the air other than reactions of the air against the earth's surface".
- **Certifying staff**: "personnel responsible for the release of an aircraft or a component after maintenance".
- **Component**: "any engine, propeller, part or appliance".
- **Continuing airworthiness**: "all of the processes ensuring that, at any time in its operating life, the aircraft complies with the airworthiness requirements in force and is in a condition for safe operation".
- **JAA** / **JAR**: Joint Aviation Authorities / Joint Aviation Requirements.
- **Commercial air transport (CAT) operation**: "an aircraft operation to transport passengers, cargo or mail for remuneration or other valuable consideration".
- **Maintenance**: "any one or combination of the following activities: overhaul, repair, inspection, replacement, modification or defect rectification of an aircraft or component, with the exception of pre-flight inspection".
- **Organisation**: "a natural person, a legal person or part of a legal person". It may be established at more than one location, inside or outside the Member States. (So a sole trader can be an "organisation".)
- **Pre-flight inspection**: "the inspection carried out before flight to ensure that the aircraft is fit for the intended flight".
- **ELA1 aircraft** (manned European light aircraft):
  - aeroplane, MTOM 1 200 kg or less, not CMPA;
  - sailplane or powered sailplane of 1 200 kg MTOM or less;
  - balloon with design volume of not more than 3 400 m3 (hot air), 1 050 m3 (gas), 300 m3 (tethered gas);
  - airship for not more than 4 occupants, volume of not more than 3 400 m3 (hot air) or 1 000 m3 (gas).
- **ELA2 aircraft**:
  - aeroplane, MTOM 2 000 kg or less, not CMPA;
  - sailplane or powered sailplane of 2 000 kg MTOM or less;
  - any balloon;
  - hot air ship;
  - gas airship with 3 % maximum static heaviness, non-vectored thrust (except reverse), conventional simple structure, control and ballonet design, and non-power-assisted controls;
  - Very Light Rotorcraft.
- **LSA aircraft**: a light sport aeroplane with all of:
  - MTOM not more than 600 kg;
  - VS0 not more than 45 knots CAS at maximum certificated take-off mass and most critical CG;
  - not more than 2 seats including the pilot;
  - a single non-turbine engine with a propeller;
  - a non-pressurised cabin.
- **Principal place of business**: "the head office or the registered office of the undertaking within which the principal financial functions and operational control of the activities referred to in this Regulation are exercised".
- **Critical maintenance task**: "a maintenance task that involves the assembly or any disturbance of a system or any part on an aircraft, engine or propeller that, if an error occurred during its performance, could directly endanger the flight safety".
- **Commercial specialised operations**: operations subject to Part-ORO Subpart SPO (Annex III, Reg. 965/2012).
- **Limited operations** (non-CMPA only):
  - (i) cost-shared flights by private individuals, with the direct cost shared by all occupants including the pilot, and at most **six** persons sharing;
  - (ii) competition flights or flying displays, with remuneration limited to direct cost recovery plus a proportionate share of annual costs, and prizes up to a value set by the authority;
  - (iii) introductory flights, parachute dropping, sailplane towing or aerobatic flights by an ATO (Reg. 1178/2011) with its principal place of business in a Member State, or by an aerial sport or leisure organisation, where the aircraft is owned or dry leased, the flights generate no profit distributed outside the organisation, and flights with non-members are only a marginal activity.
  - Limited operations are **not** CAT or commercial specialised operations.
- **Introductory flight**, **competition flight**, **flying display**: as defined in Article 2(9), (10), (11) of Reg. 965/2012.
- **Management systems harmonisation**: "the coordinated process by which the management systems of two or more organisations interact and share information and methods to reach common or consistent safety and compliance monitoring objectives".
- **Complex motor-powered aircraft (CMPA)**:
  - (i) an aeroplane with MTOM exceeding **5 700 kg**, or certificated for more than **19** passenger seats, or for a minimum crew of at least **two pilots**, or equipped with turbojet engine(s) or **more than one turboprop**;
  - (ii) a helicopter certificated for MTOM exceeding **3 175 kg**, or more than **9** passenger seats, or a minimum crew of at least two pilots;
  - (iii) a **tilt rotor** aircraft.
- **Owner** (M.A.201(b)): the owner or the lessee, as applicable.
- **Single air carrier business grouping** (GM2 M.A.201(ea)): a controlling undertaking and its controlled undertakings, all located in Treaty territory. Control is "dominant influence" through ownership, financial participation, the right to appoint management, governing rules, or other means. The grouping includes 1008/2008 air carriers and one or more Part-CAMO organisations.
- **Competent authority** (AMC M.1): a ministry, NAA or other designated aviation body in the Member State. Only one per area of responsibility.
- **"Light" / "non-Light"** (GM Arts 3 and 4 table): shorthand for aircraft inside or outside the Part-ML scope of Art. 3(2).
- Abbreviations used: AMP, ARC, AOC, CAMO, CAO (combined airworthiness organisation), CAO-CAM (CAO with CAW management privileges), CAO-M (CAO with maintenance privileges), ATO, DTO, SPO, NCO, SRB (safety review board), OSD, CofR, FRMS, TC, STC.

## 5. Software Implications

### Aircraft classification engine (core, drives everything else)
- **Aircraft fields:** category (aeroplane, helicopter, other rotorcraft, sailplane, powered sailplane, balloon (hot-air, gas, tethered gas), airship (hot-air, gas), tilt rotor, VLR); MTOM (kg); maximum certificated passenger seating; minimum certificated flight crew; engine type and count (piston, turboprop, turbojet/turbofan); pressurised (y/n); VS0; seats including pilot; balloon or airship envelope volume (m3); occupants (airships); airship characteristics for ELA2 (static heaviness, vectored thrust, power-assisted controls); State of registry; registration mark; permit to fly (y/n); TC or declaration-of-design-compliance holder.
- **Derived flags (computed, with an explanation):** `is_CMPA`, `is_ELA1`, `is_ELA2`, `is_LSA`, `is_multi_turboprop_le_5700kg` (Art. 3(7) special case: CMPA by definition but treated as non-CMPA for listed points).
- **Regime determination:** `regime` is one of Part-M, Part-ML, Part-T, or permit-to-fly arrangements. Rule: Part-ML if non-CMPA AND (aeroplane at or below 2 730 kg, OR rotorcraft at or below 1 200 kg with up to 4 occupants, OR other ELA2) AND not listed on a 1008/2008 air carrier AOC. Otherwise Part-M. Article 1(b) third-country dry lease-in gives Part-T. A permit to fly overrides with "permit conditions". Keep this as versioned, data-driven rules so a UK rule set can differ.
- **Operation type per aircraft (time-bounded):** licensed air carrier CAT; other CAT; commercial SPO; commercial ATO/DTO; non-commercial; limited operations (sub-type: cost-share (at most 6 sharers), competition or display, introductory, parachute, glider tow, aerobatic); AOC aircraft used for NCO or SPO (M.A.201(k)).
- **Responsibility matrix output** (M.A.201(e) to (i) and the GM table): CAMO required? CAO allowed? Self-management allowed? Is Part-145 mandatory, or are Subpart F, CAO-M or independent certifying staff allowed? Show the legal basis (e.g. "M.A.201(h)"). Re-run it whenever the operation type, AOC listing, MTOM, mod status or registration changes.

### Parties and responsibilities
- **Party entity:** owner, lessee, operator (with AOC number and 1008/2008 licence flag), CAMO (Form 14 ref), CAO (Form 3-CAO ref, with CAM and/or M privileges), Part-145 (approval ref), Subpart F (legacy), Part-147, independent certifying staff (Part-66 licence), competent authority, TC/STC holder or design declarant.
- **"Responsible person" resolution:** the owner by default. It transfers to the lessee when (flag) the lessee is on the registration document or (flag) the lease contract says so. The operator for 1008/2008 carriers. Store the evidence document and effective dates.
- **Contract objects:** type is one of Appendix I CAW management contract, limited AMP contract (scope M.A.302(d), (f), (h)), CAMO-to-145 maintenance contract (CAMO.A.315(c) / M.A.708(c)), CAO/CAMO-to-maintenance contract, or group (ea) contract. Fields: parties, aircraft covered, start and end dates, scope, the group management board list (for (ea)), linked group standards document, status (draft, active, terminated). Validation: (ea) needs at least 2 operators, CAMO in the same grouping, EU principal place of business (UK variant to configure), covering all aircraft of each operator, and a harmonisation record.
- **Compliance checks:** for CMPA or commercial cases, block "self-managed" status; require an active CAMO/CAO contract and an appropriate maintenance organisation approval.
- **Competent authority mapping per function (M.1):** the ARC/aircraft oversight authority (State of registry), the AMP approval authority (State of registry, or a transferred one, with a reference to the transfer agreement), and the CAMO or Subpart F oversight authority.

### No-fly gating (M.A.201(a))
An aircraft **airworthy / dispatch status** computed from four inputs:
1. open defects and overdue maintenance (airworthy condition);
2. operational and emergency equipment: each item has a status (serviceable, or unserviceable with "clearly identified" placard confirmed);
3. CofA valid, which in turn depends on ARC validity (later chunk);
4. all maintenance per the **approved current AMP revision**.

Show a red/amber/green status with the reasons. Alert when any input is about to fail.

### AMP record
- Fields: approval method (direct or indirect per M.A.302(c)), approving authority, approval date, revision, grandfathered (pre-24 March 2020) flag, developer (owner, CAMO or CAO via limited contract).

### Pre-flight inspection
- Log it as a separate activity, explicitly **not** maintenance. Performer: PIC or another qualified person. No CRS needed. The responsible party is the PIC, or the operator for air carriers.

### Occurrence reporting workflow (M.A.202)
- **Report object:**
  - reporter (name, organisation, approval reference);
  - aircraft (registration, serial, type) and/or component (P/N, S/N);
  - time-since data at discovery: hours, cycles, landings, and time since overhaul (auto-populate from the tracking data);
  - description, "identified at" timestamp, recipients, submission timestamps, method.
- **Recipient auto-list:**
  - State of registry authority;
  - State of operator authority if different;
  - TC/STC holder or declarant;
  - if under contract: owner, operator, CAMO/CAO.
- **Deadline:** identified_at + **72 h**. Countdown, escalation alerts (e.g. at 24 h and 48 h), and an overdue status with a field to justify "exceptional circumstances".
- **Follow-up report:** linked child record for preventive actions; due "as soon as identified"; prompt after the root cause is closed.
- **Statuses:** draft, submitted (per recipient), follow-up pending, follow-up submitted, closed.
- **Reportability triage prompts (AMC M.A.202(a)):** serious cracks, permanent deformation, burning or serious corrosion of structure found in scheduled maintenance; failure of any emergency system during scheduled testing.
- The organisation must have a nominated occurrence coordinator role with defined authority.
- UK users report via the UK CAA's system (UK MOR). Make the recipient endpoints configurable per jurisdiction.

### Personnel and licences
- **Part-66 licence:** category and sub-category, type ratings or group ratings (66.A.45), technical limitations, legacy JAA origin flag, "conversion due at next renewal or change" flag (Art. 5(4)).
- **Limited authorisations:** e.g. flight engineer (145.A.30(j)(3)/(4)), with expiry and revocation status.
- **Component certifying staff:** qualification basis = national rule (configurable).
- **Training records / CofR:** course type (basic, type), syllabus version (pre- or post-12 June 2024 / 2023/989), part (1 or 2 for transition), list of modules, module mapping per the correlation table (7A/7B become 7; 9A/9B become 9; 11A/11B/11C become 11; 17A/17B become 17; the L modules are renamed). A deadline flag applies to pre-12 June 2024 courses (complete and CofR issued before 12 June 2026). That date is past as of now, so treat it as a validation rule for historic records.

### Organisation approvals
- Fields: approval type (Part-145, Part-CAMO Form 14, Part-CAO Form 3-CAO, Part-147, legacy Subpart F/G, legacy JAR-145), reference, issuing authority, privileges, scope, status (valid, limited, suspended, revoked), transition flags.
- **Findings register:** finding, requirement, deadline, status. Supports transition deadlines (24 March 2022, 2 December 2024) and the consequence (revoke, limit or suspend).
- **Authority access/audit log** (M.A.201(j), Art. 7a): record authority visits, including unannounced inspections, documents provided, and copies taken. Give authority inspectors a read-only role that covers subcontracted activity data.

### Group CAMO module (EU carriers; probably not UK)
- Group, members (AOC holders, CAMO), group management board (accountable managers plus coordinating member), group standards document with versions, harmonisation activities log (joint SRB meetings, exchange of compliance monitoring and oversight results), a common language setting, and an (eb) action plan tracker.

### Permissions (suggested)
- **Owner/lessee:** view everything; approve contracts; self-manage only if the responsibility matrix allows.
- **CAMO/CAO staff:** manage the aircraft under contract (scoped by contract dates and aircraft).
- **Maintenance organisation or certifying staff:** record tasks they performed (M.A.201(c)).
- **Pilot:** pre-flight inspection entry; defect reporting.
- **Authority inspector:** read-only.
- **Occurrence coordinator:** submit reports.

### Rule versioning
Store the applicable regulation amendment per rule (e.g. 2021/700 for Art. 3) and allow parallel EASA and UK rule sets, with effective-date logic for the many transitional dates.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM

- **GM Articles 3 and 4 (applicability matrix):**
  - Non-commercial, non-CMPA "Light" (Part-ML): CAW management by individual, CAO-CAM or CAMO; maintenance by individual certifying staff, CAO-M or Part-145.
  - Non-commercial non-Light non-CMPA: Part-M. Commercial non-CMPA: CAO-CAM or CAMO.
  - All CMPA: Part-CAMO and Part-145 mandatory.
  - Licensed air carrier: always Part-M, Part-CAMO and Part-145 mandatory (Part-ML N/A).
  - Treat this as the product's master "regime and organisation requirement" lookup.
- **GM M.A.201 quick summary table** (non-Part-ML aircraft only):

  | Operation | CMPA | Non-CMPA |
  |---|---|---|
  | Licensed air carrier CAT | CAMO in AOC (unless (ea)) + Part-145 | Same |
  | Other CAT, SPO, commercial ATO | CAMO + Part-145 (M.A.201(f)) | CAMO or CAO + Subpart F / CAO / Part-145 (M.A.201(h)) |
  | Non-commercial, including limited operations | CAMO + Part-145 (M.A.201(g)) | No CAMO/CAO required; maintenance organisation "not always required" (M.A.201(i)) |

- **GM M.A.201(e):** ground de-icing and anti-icing are not Part-145 activities, but the **inspection for fluid residues is maintenance** and needs suitably authorised personnel. The system should classify those tasks as maintenance requiring a CRS or authorisation.
- **AMC M.A.201(e)(2):**
  - A licensed air carrier needs a CAMO approval within its AOC only for the aircraft on its AOC.
  - The airworthiness review privilege is **optional**.
  - There is no independent CAMO approval to manage for air carriers, apart from the (ea) group derogation.
  - The operator remains ultimately accountable.
- **AMC1 M.A.201(ea) harmonisation elements:**
  - a forum sharing SRB results, attended by accountable managers and safety managers (e.g. joint SRB meetings);
  - regular exchange of compliance monitoring and authority oversight results;
  - a common or consistent safety policy and objectives (local extra objectives allowed);
  - common safety management key processes (except organisation-specific ones such as FRMS);
  - cooperation mechanisms for serious concerns, group-level findings, and synchronised changes;
  - procedures describing the interface and the **records to retain**.
- **AMC2 M.A.201(ea):** one common language (e.g. English) for manuals, procedures and communication when aircraft are registered in a State other than the CAMO's, agreed with the authorities.
- **GM1 M.A.201(ea):**
  - group standards endorsed by a group management board (accountable managers plus a coordinating member, possibly a CAMO employee);
  - board names go in, or are referenced from, the contract and are updated on change;
  - the coordinating member's duties are listed;
  - group standards are documented in a common document, in each exposition, or in the contract;
  - they include safety management key processes, identification of the operators and CAMO, board contacts, and change management.
- **GM2 M.A.201(ea):**
  - each organisation stays responsible for its own management system;
  - the air carrier stays **ultimately responsible** for CAW even when it contracts a CAMO, but the responsibility for *performing* the CAW tasks transfers to the CAMO;
  - the operator must still assess the CAMO's capability and capacity (Appendix I point 7).
- **GM3 M.A.201(ea):**
  - Compliant configurations: several AOCs contracting one group CAMO, possibly integrated into another AOC; several CAMOs within one group.
  - Non-compliant configurations: only **one** AOC uses the contracted CAMO; or the CAMO is not contracted for **all** of an AOC's aircraft.
  - Validation rules: at least 2 AOC users per contracted CAMO; full fleet coverage.
- **GM M.A.201(i):** a self-managing owner is fully responsible and should self-assess competence or get expertise. The app could offer a competence self-assessment or acknowledgement step at onboarding.
- **AMC M.A.201(i)(3):** the limited contract covers M.A.302(d), (f) and (h) (AMP development, review and approval processing; details in a later chunk).
- **GM1 M.A.201(k):** using AOC aircraft for NCO or SPO does not relax (f) to (i).
- **GM1 M.1(3)(ii):** AMP approval can move to another State's authority by written State-to-State contract. If it moves to the CAMO's or CAO's authority, the CAMO/CAO's **indirect approval procedure** may be used unless the terms say otherwise.
- **AMC M.1:** only one authority per area of responsibility, even when a State designates several.
- **AMC M.A.202(a):**
  - Report to the **TC holder or the declarant of design compliance**, and liaise with them about service information.
  - Approved organisations must designate a qualified occurrence coordinator with clear authority.
  - Maintenance reportables are "normally limited to" serious structural cracks, permanent deformation, burning or serious corrosion found in scheduled maintenance, and emergency system failures during scheduled testing.
- **AMC M.A.202(b):** any transmission method is acceptable. Minimum content includes **life or overhaul status** (hours, cycles, landings), so occurrence forms should pull time-since figures automatically.
- **GM1 Article 6(4)/(5):** transitional basic training options. Option 1: the whole course on the old syllabus, finished before the end of the transition. Option 2: split into Part 1 (old) and Part 2 (new), within the **same organisation**, with two CofRs carrying the exact notes:
  - Part 1: "This certificate is issued for Part 1 of the basic training course which started before the applicability date of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/989, covering the modules listed below:"
  - Part 2: "This certificate is issued for Part 2 of the basic training course which started after the applicability date of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/989, covering the modules listed below:"
  - Courses finishing after the transition can only use Option 2.
- **GM1 Article 6(6):** old and new modules and levels for the same licence (sub)category are equivalent, and pre-applicability CofRs are equivalent. Merges: 7A/7B into 7, 9A/9B into 9, 11A/11B/11C into 11, 17A/17B into 17. Category L modules were renamed (e.g. 4L "Airframe wooden/metal tube and fabric" became "Wooden and/or metal-tube structure covered with fabric").

## UK flags (guesses, not facts; verify against UK Reg 1321/2014 as retained and amended, and CAA CAP publications)
