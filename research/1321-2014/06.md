# Chunk 06 — Part-M Section B (all Subparts) and Appendices to Annex I

Source: `/home/claude/r1321/chunks/06.txt` (2,092 lines, read in full). EASA Easy Access Rules for Continuing Airworthiness, Regulation (EU) No 1321/2014, Annex I (Part-M).

What this chunk is: **Section B** is the procedure for **competent authorities** (CAs): how they oversee Part-M organisations and aircraft. It is followed by the **Appendices to Annex I** (contract, forms, rating systems, complex tasks, pilot-owner tasks). Section B speaks to authorities, not to industry. For Airworthiness+ it still matters because:
- (a) it sets what the authority will ask for, how often, and on what timescale;
- (b) it holds the finding levels and deadlines that organisations must meet;
- (c) the Appendices define the forms and contract content the app must produce and store.

Amendment sources appear under each heading: Reg 1321/2014, 2015/1536, 2019/1383, 2020/270, 2021/700, 2021/1963, 2022/1360; ED Decisions 2015/029/R, 2016/011/R, 2019/009/R, 2020/002/R, 2021/009/R, 2023/013/R.

**UK note (guess, not fact):** UK retained Reg 1321/2014 is roughly the EU text as at 31 Dec 2020. EU amendments made after that date are probably absent from the UK text, or were made differently by UK statutory instruments or CAA decisions. In this chunk that means:
- Reg 2021/700 (form issue numbers, the 24 March 2022 validity dates on Forms 3-MF and 14-MG);
- Reg 2021/1963 (Appendix IV and Appendix VII wording);
- Reg 2022/1360 (the Appendix I contract, including M.A.201(ea) and Part 21 Light);
- ED Decisions 2021/009/R and 2023/013/R (AMC wording that mentions the "declarant of a declaration of design compliance").

Other UK differences to expect:
- UK forms are CAA-branded: "CAA Form 1", "CAA Form 15a/15b/15c", "CAA Form 3", "CAA Form 14".
- References to EASA/"the Agency" and to Member States become the UK CAA.
- Article 72 of Reg 2018/1139 (exchange of information among EU authorities) has no UK equivalent.
- Part 21 Light (Annex Ib to 748/2012) is very likely not in UK law.

## 1. Scope (point by point)

### Section B, Subpart A — General
- **M.B.101 Scope**: sets the administrative requirements for CAs that apply and enforce Section A of Part-M.
- **M.B.102 Competent authority**:
  - (a) each Member State designates a CA responsible for issuing, continuing, changing, suspending and revoking certificates and for continuing-airworthiness oversight, with documented procedures and an organisational structure;
  - (b) enough staff;
  - (c) staff are qualified and receive initial and continuation training;
  - (d) procedures show how Part-M compliance is achieved, and are reviewed and amended.
- **M.B.103 Findings and enforcement measures – persons**: if a CA finds that a holder of a licence, certificate, rating or attestation under 2018/1139 does not comply, it takes enforcement measures to stop the non-compliance continuing.
- **M.B.104 Record-keeping**: the CA's record system gives traceability for each certificate. It sets minimum records for organisation oversight (kept 5 years) and for each aircraft (kept until 2 years after permanent withdrawal from service). Records are available to other Member States and to EASA on request.
- **M.B.105 Mutual exchange of information**: CAs share safety information (Art. 72 of 2018/1139) and help each other when a safety threat involves several Member States.

### Subpart B — Accountability
- **M.B.201 Responsibilities**: the CAs named in M.1 carry out audits, inspections and investigations to verify compliance.
- **M.B.202 Information to the Agency**: the CA notifies EASA without undue delay of significant implementation problems, and passes on safety-significant information from occurrence reports received under M.A.202.

### Subpart C — Continuing airworthiness
- **M.B.301 Aircraft maintenance programme (AMP)**: the CA checks that the AMP complies with M.A.302. It approves the AMP and amendments directly, unless M.A.302(c) indirect approval applies; in that case it approves the CAO/CAMO's AMP approval procedure through the exposition (CAO.A.025, M.A.704 or CAMO.A.300). The CA has access to the M.A.302(d), (e) and (f) data.
- **M.B.302 Exemptions**: the CA records and keeps all exemptions granted under Art. 71 of 2018/1139.
- **M.B.303 Aircraft continuing airworthiness monitoring (ACAM)**: the CA runs a risk-based survey programme over the fleet on its register. The programme includes sample product surveys covering all key risk elements. Findings are categorised, confirmed in writing to the person accountable under M.A.201, and analysed for safety significance. The CA records findings and closure actions, deals with non-compliance with other Parts under those Parts, and exchanges information with other CAs.
- **M.B.304 Revocation and suspension**: the CA suspends an ARC on reasonable grounds of a potential safety threat, or suspends or revokes an ARC under M.B.903(1).
- **M.B.305 Aircraft technical log system**: the CA approves the initial tech log system (M.A.306). It also approves the procedure that lets the organisation change the system without prior approval (CAMO.A.300(c), M.A.704(c) or CAO.A.025(c)).

### Subpart D — Maintenance standards
"(to be developed as appropriate)". No content.

### Subpart E — Components
"(to be developed as appropriate)". No content.

### Subpart F — Maintenance organisation (Part-M Subpart F AMO; legacy, see the note on validity under Appendix V)
- **M.B.601 Application**: if facilities are in more than one Member State, investigation and oversight are done jointly with those States' CAs.
- **M.B.602 Initial approval**: the CA:
  - accepts M.A.606(a) and (b) personnel in writing;
  - confirms the MOM procedures comply with Subpart F, and that the accountable manager (AM) signs the commitment statement;
  - verifies compliance;
  - meets the AM at least once during the investigation;
  - confirms findings in writing and records findings, closure actions and recommendations.
  All findings must be closed before the approval is issued.
- **M.B.603 Issue of approval**: the CA issues EASA Form 3 (Appendix V) with the extent of approval, any conditions, and a reference number in the format EASA specifies.
- **M.B.604 Continuing oversight**: the CA keeps an audit programme with due dates and dates done. Each organisation is completely audited at least every 24 months, and the CA meets the AM at least once every 24 months. Findings are confirmed in writing and recorded.
- **M.B.605 Findings**:
  - Level 1: immediate revocation, limitation or suspension, in whole or in part.
  - Level 2: corrective action period of no more than 3 months, which can be extended if there is a satisfactory corrective action plan.
  - If the organisation misses the timescale, the approval is suspended in whole or in part.
  - Covers non-compliance with Part-M or Part-ML.
- **M.B.606 Changes**: the CA applies the relevant initial-approval elements to changes notified under M.A.617, and may set conditions for operating during the change. MOM changes are either directly approved (M.A.604(b)) or indirectly approved (M.A.604(c)). For indirect approval the changes must stay minor and the CA keeps adequate control.
- **M.B.607 Revocation, suspension and limitation**: on reasonable grounds of a potential safety threat, or under M.B.605.

### Subpart G — CAMO (Part-M Subpart G; legacy, superseded by Part-CAMO)
- **M.B.701 Application**:
  - (a) for licensed air carriers (Reg 1008/2008), the CA receives with the initial AOC application, any variation, and for each aircraft type: the CAME, the operator's AMPs, the aircraft technical log, and where appropriate the technical specification of the CAMO–Part-145 maintenance contracts;
  - (b) multi-State facilities are handled jointly.
- **M.B.702 Initial approval**: the same pattern as M.B.602, for M.A.706(a), (c), (d) and M.A.707 personnel and the CAME. All findings closed before issue.
- **M.B.703 Issue of approval**: the CA issues EASA Form 14-MG (Appendix VI) with the extent and validity of approval and a reference number. For licensed air carriers, the Form 14 information is included on the AOC.
- **M.B.704 Continuing oversight**: audit programme. Complete audit at least every 24 months. **A relevant sample of the managed aircraft is surveyed in every 24-month period**, with the sample size based on prior audits and product surveys. AM meeting at least once every 24 months.
- **M.B.705 Findings**: Level 1 and Level 2 rules as in M.B.605 (3 months, extendable), for Part-M or Part-ML non-compliance. Suspension if the organisation misses the timescale.
- **M.B.706 Changes**: as M.B.606, for changes notified under M.A.713 and CAME changes under M.A.704(b) (direct) or M.A.704(c) (indirect).
- **M.B.707 Revocation, suspension and limitation**: on reasonable grounds of a safety threat, or under M.B.705.

### Subpart H — Certificate of release to service (CRS)
"(to be developed as appropriate)". No content.

### Subpart I — Airworthiness review certificate
- **M.B.901 Assessment of recommendations**: when it receives an application and an ARC recommendation under M.A.901, qualified CA staff verify that the compliance statement shows a complete airworthiness review was done. The CA investigates and may ask for more information.
- **M.B.902 Airworthiness review by the CA**: when the CA itself reviews an aircraft and issues **EASA Form 15a**, it follows M.A.901. It sets airworthiness review staff qualifications (see section 2), keeps a staff record, has access to M.A.305, M.A.306 and M.A.401 data, and the staff member issues Form 15a after a satisfactory review.
- **M.B.903 Findings (aircraft)**:
  - Level 1: corrective action before further flight, and immediate revocation or suspension of the ARC.
  - Level 2: corrective action appropriate to the nature of the finding.
- **M.B.904 Exchange of information**: on an aircraft transfer between Member States (M.A.903), the current State of registry's CA tells the new State's CA of any known problems. The new State's CA makes sure the old CA has been notified.

### Appendices to Annex I
- **Appendix I — Continuing airworthiness management contract**: minimum content, a mandatory text, CAMO/CAO obligations, owner/operator obligations, occurrence reporting under 376/2014, and extra requirements for M.A.201(ea) intra-group contracts. GM: the operator coordinates flight ops with the CAO/CAMO.
- **Appendix II — Authorised Release Certificate, EASA Form 1** (maintenance use): purpose, format, copies, error correction, block-by-block completion, user/installer statement and the form layout ("EASA Form 1 — MF/CAO/145 Issue 3"). AMC covers electronic signature and electronic exchange. GM covers Block 12.
- **Appendix III — ARC, EASA Form 15b** (issued by a CAMO, Subpart G or CAO organisation; Issue 6, Reg 2021/700) and **EASA Form 15a** (issued by the CA; Issue 5, Reg 2019/1383). Both have spaces for 1st and 2nd extensions. Form 15c (Part-ML) is not in this chunk.
- **Appendix IV — Class and rating system for Part-M Subpart F AMO terms of approval**: classes A (aircraft), B (engines/APU), C (components C1–C22), D (D1 NDT); limits for one-person organisations; the rating table.
- **Appendix V — Maintenance Organisation Certificate, EASA Form 3-MF** (Issue 6), with page 2 terms of approval.
- **Appendix VI — CAMO certificate, EASA Form 14-MG** (Issue 6), with page 2 terms of approval.
- **Appendix VII — Complex maintenance tasks** (referred to in M.A.801(b)): a structural, control, piston engine and propeller list, plus a catch-all.
- **Appendix VIII — Limited pilot-owner maintenance**: principles, 8 exclusion criteria, records and a 30-day reporting rule. AMC tables list permitted tasks: Part A aeroplanes, Part B rotorcraft, Part C sailplanes and powered sailplanes, Part D balloons and airships.

### Airworthiness review staff of the CA (M.B.902(b))

**(1) Aircraft used by licensed air carriers (1008/2008), and aircraft above 2 730 kg MTOM.** The staff member must have:
- (i) "at least 5 years of experience in continuing airworthiness";
- (ii) an appropriate Part-66 licence, or a nationally recognised qualification (where Art 5(6) refers to national rules), or an aeronautical degree or equivalent;
- (iii) formal aeronautical maintenance training;
- (iv) a position with appropriate responsibilities.

Item (ii) may be replaced by **5 years** of continuing airworthiness experience on top of the 5 already required.

**(2) Aircraft not used by licensed air carriers, at 2 730 kg MTOM and below.** The staff member must have:
- (i) "at least 3 years of experience in continuing airworthiness";
- (ii) as above;
- (iii) "appropriate" aeronautical maintenance training;
- (iv) as above.

Item (ii) may be replaced by **4 years** of additional experience.

Note: the IR text says "Notwithstanding points (a) to (d)" and "(b)(1)(b)" while the list uses (i) to (iv). This is a drafting inconsistency in the source.

### CA inspector qualifications (AMC1 M.B.102(c))
- Practical experience.
- Comprehensive knowledge of the rules, procedures, inspector rights, quality systems, CAM and operational procedures.
- Audit technique training.
- "five years relevant work experience" before working independently.
- A relevant engineering degree, or an AMT qualification plus additional education.
- Type knowledge through a formal course including Fuel Tank Safety (FTS), at least at Part-66 Appendix III Level 1.
- Knowledge of maintenance standards.
- Integrity and impartiality.
- Continuation training at regular intervals.

### Owner / operator obligations (Appendix I, 5.2)
1. General understanding of the approved AMP.
2. General understanding of Part-M.
3. Present the aircraft to the agreed AMO at the due time the CAMO/CAO requests.
4. Do not modify the aircraft without first consulting the CAMO/CAO.
5. Tell the CAMO/CAO about maintenance done exceptionally without its knowledge.
6. Report all defects found in operation to the CAMO/CAO through the logbook.
7. Inform the CA of the State of registry if either party denounces the contract.
8. Inform the CAMO/CAO and the CA when the aircraft is sold.
9. Carry out all mandated occurrence reporting.
10. Give the CAMO/CAO flying hours and other utilisation data regularly, as agreed.
11. Enter the CRS in the logbooks (M.A.803(d)) for pilot-owner maintenance, within the AMP task list (M.A.803(c)).
12. **Inform the CAMO/CAO "not later than 30 days" after completing any pilot-owner maintenance task** (M.A.305(a)).
13. Ensure compliance with the AMP, and coordinate with the CAMO/CAO on any request to the CA for a one-time extension of an AMP interval.
14. Inform the CAMO/CAO of any non-compliance with operational requirements that may affect continuing airworthiness.
15. Inform the CAMO/CAO of operational requirements (for example specific approvals) needed to keep the required configuration.

From the mandatory contract text: if the contract is cancelled for non-conformity, the owner/operator takes back full responsibility and must **inform the CA of the State of registry "within 2 weeks"** about the non-conformity. Under M.A.201(ea) contracts the CA must be informed "immediately".

### CAMO / CAO obligations (Appendix I, 5.1)
1. Have the aircraft type in its terms of approval.
2. Maintain continuing airworthiness by doing the following:
   - (a) develop the AMP, including any reliability programme;
   - (b) declare the pilot-owner tasks in the AMP (M.A.803(c));
   - (c) organise AMP approval;
   - (d) once approved, give the owner/operator a copy of the AMP;
   - (e) establish and order bridging maintenance from the former AMP;
   - (f) have all maintenance done by an approved maintenance organisation;
   - (g) have all applicable ADs applied;
   - (h) have defects (found in scheduled maintenance or airworthiness review, or reported by the owner) rectified by an approved organisation;
   - (i) coordinate scheduled maintenance, component inspections, LLP replacement and ADs, and ensure compliance with operational requirements that affect continuing airworthiness, EASA continuing airworthiness requirements and CA immediate safety measures;
   - (j) inform the owner/operator each time the aircraft is to go to an AMO;
   - (k) manage and archive the continuing airworthiness records;
   - (l) coordinate on AMP deviation requests;
   - (m) support the operator or pilot-owner during maintenance check flights.
3. Organise approval of any modification under Part 21 or Part 21 Light before it is embodied. For aircraft under a declaration of design compliance, organise the declaration under Part 21 Light Subpart F.
4. Organise approval of any repair before it is carried out (Part 21 Light Subpart N for declared designs).
5. Inform the CA of the State of registry when the owner does not present the aircraft to the AMO as requested.
6. Inform the CA when the contract is not respected.
7. Ensure the airworthiness review is done when needed, and that the ARC is issued or a recommendation sent to the CA.
8. **Send a copy of any ARC issued or extended to the CA of the State of registry "within 10 days"**.
9. Carry out mandated occurrence reporting.
10. Inform the CA when either party denounces the contract.

### M.A.201(ea) intra-group contracts (Appendix I, 7)
Before signing, the operator assesses the CAMO's capability and capacity.

**7.1 Eligibility**: only for an air carrier licensed under 1008/2008 where the CAMO is in the same air carrier business grouping. The contract describes how the M.A.201(ea) conditions are met and how the management systems are harmonised.

**7.2 Additional CAMO obligations**:
- know the operator's contract-monitoring procedure;
- get the operator's agreement before subcontracting;
- inform the CA **immediately** of non-presentation of the aircraft, contract breach or denunciation;
- train operator staff on CAMO policies, communication lines and CAMO-specific procedures (software, reliability, tech log, interoperability).

**7.3 Additional operator obligations**:
- interface procedures for ARC issue and renewal;
- inform the CAMO **immediately** of unexpected maintenance needs where no Part-145 is contracted;
- inform the CA **immediately** of contract denunciation;
- train CAMO staff on the operator's procedures.

### Pilot-owner (Appendix VIII)
- Always responsible for the maintenance they do.
- Must be satisfied they are competent before doing a task; if not competent, they cannot release it.
- The pilot-owner, or their CAMO/CAO, identifies the pilot-owner tasks in the AMP and keeps that document updated "in a timely manner".
- The AMP is approved under M.A.302.
- Maintenance data (M.A.401) must be available and complied with, and referenced in the CRS (M.A.803(d)).
- **Must inform the CAMO "not later than 30 days after completion"** (M.A.305(a)).

### EASA Form 1 originator (Appendix II)
- Keep the certificate in a form that allows the original data to be verified.
- If the end user reports an error in writing, the originator may reissue only if the error can be verified and corrected. The reissue has a new tracking number, signature and date, and refers to the original. Both certificates are kept for the retention period of the first.

## 3. Records and documents

### CA records (M.B.104)

**Organisation oversight records, kept at least 5 years:**
1. Application for organisation approval.
2. Approval certificate, including changes.
3. Audit programme, with due dates and dates done.
4. Continued oversight records, including all audit records.
5. Relevant correspondence.
6. Exemptions and enforcement actions.
7. Reports from other CAs.
8. Exposition or manual and its amendments.
9. Any other directly approved document.

**Aircraft oversight records, kept until 2 years after permanent withdrawal; at least a copy of:**
1. Certificate of airworthiness (CofA).
2. ARCs.
3. ARC recommendations issued by a CAO or CAMO.
4. Reports of airworthiness reviews done by the CA.
5. Aircraft correspondence.
6. Exemptions and enforcement actions.
7. Any document approved under Part-M or Part-ARO (965/2012).

**AMC M.B.104(a):**
- records accessible within a reasonable time and organised consistently;
- sensitive data stored securely with controlled access;
- backup hardware kept at a different location from the working data;
- data stays accessible through hardware and software changes for the full retention period.

**AMC M.B.104(f):** records are shared only for:
- incidents or accidents;
- ACAM findings that involve organisations approved by another CA (root cause);
- aircraft mainly operated in another MS;
- aircraft previously operated in another MS;
- organisations with approvals in several MS.

The reason for the request is stated. Records are shared by copy or by consultation.

### CA procedures document (AMC M.B.102(d))
- MS designation of the CA.
- Titles and names of managers, with duties.
- Organisation charts.
- Staff qualification procedure, and a list of staff authorised to sign certificates.
- Facilities description.
- Part-M compliance procedures.

### Exemptions register (M.B.302)
All Art. 71 exemptions, recorded and kept.

### ACAM records (M.B.303)
- Survey programme and its changes (documented).
- Each inspection record identifies which KREs were inspected (AMC2 M.B.303(b)).
- Findings and closure actions.

### Airworthiness review staff record (M.B.902(c); AMC minimum content)
- Name.
- Date of birth.
- Basic education.
- Experience.
- Aeronautical degree and/or Part-66 qualification.
- Initial training.
- Type training.
- Continuation training.
- Experience in continuing airworthiness and within the organisation.
- Responsibilities of current job.

Under AMC M.B.902(b)(2), an assessment by the CA of knowledge for staff reviewing aircraft at or below 2 730 kg must be recorded.

### Continuing airworthiness management contract (Appendix I)
- Signed by both parties.
- On request, a copy is sent by the owner/operator to the CA of the State of registry.
- Minimum data:
  - aircraft registration, type and serial number;
  - owner's or registered lessee's name or company details, with address;
  - contracted CAMO/CAO details, with address;
  - type of operation.
- Mandatory statement (paraphrased): the owner entrusts the CAMO/CAO with continuing airworthiness management, including developing an AMP approved by the CA under M.1 and organising maintenance to it. Both sides undertake their obligations. The owner declares the information given is and will stay accurate, and that the aircraft will not be repaired or modified without the CAMO/CAO's prior agreement. Non-conformity is assessed for safety significance and repetition. If either party cannot fulfil its responsibilities, the contract is cancelled and the CAs are informed immediately. The owner then has full responsibility and informs the CA of registry within 2 weeks.
- Obligations of each party under 5.1 and 5.2 (section 2), plus the split of mandatory and voluntary occurrence reporting under Reg 376/2014.

### EASA Form 1 (Appendix II): block fields
"EASA Form 1 — MF/CAO/145 Issue 3"; landscape format.

**General rules:**
- Correlation with the item(s) must be established.
- It is not a delivery or shipping note.
- Aircraft are not released with it.
- It is not approval to install.
- Production-released and maintenance-released items cannot be mixed on one form.
- Block numbers and positions are fixed; block sizes may vary.
- Pre-printed or computer-generated.
- In English, plus other languages if appropriate.
- Machine-printed, or handwritten in block letters.
- Few abbreviations.
- The reverse side may carry extra information but no certification statement, and its use must be referenced on the front.
- Any number of copies.

## 4. Defined terms (regulatory meaning)

- **Level 1 / Level 2 finding**: used throughout (M.B.605, M.B.705, M.B.903). The consequences are defined here (immediate action; corrective action period of no more than 3 months). The level definitions themselves are in Section A.
- **Provisional / confirmed categorisation** (AMC M.B.602(e), M.B.702(e)): a finding is first recorded as "provisional" Level 1 or 2, then reviewed and changed to "confirmed".
- **Closure actions**: "actions required to close a finding" (M.B.602(f), M.B.604(d), M.B.702(f), M.B.704(e)).
- **"Formally indicate in writing"**: means using EASA Form 4 for each nominated person except the AM. For the AM, approval of the manual or exposition containing the signed commitment statement is acceptance; for CAMOs this applies after a satisfactory AM meeting.
- **"Relevant engineering degree"**: "an engineering degree from aeronautical, mechanical, electrical, electronic, avionic or other studies relevant to the maintenance and continuing airworthiness of aircraft/aircraft components" (AMC1 M.B.102(c)). Under AMC M.B.902(b)1 this counts as an aeronautical degree.
- **"Relevant sample"** (type training): "these courses should cover typical systems embodied in those aircraft being within the scope of approval."
- **"Experience in continuing airworthiness"**: "any appropriate combination of experience in tasks related to aircraft maintenance and/or continuing airworthiness management (engineering) and/or surveillance of such tasks" (AMC M.B.902(b)2).
- **"Appropriate licence in compliance with Part-66"**: "a category B or C licence in the subcategory of the aircraft reviewed". Recent experience and type rating are not needed (AMC M.B.902(b)3).
- **"Position with appropriate responsibilities"**: a CA position that authorises the person to sign on behalf of the CA (AMC M.B.902(b)4).
- **Formal aeronautical maintenance training** (licensed air carriers, or more than 2 730 kg other than balloons): evidenced training in continuing airworthiness regulations, operational requirements, internal procedures, and type knowledge at Part-66 App. III Level 1 or above.
- **Appropriate aeronautical maintenance training** (2 730 kg or below, non-air-carrier): demonstrated knowledge of the same subjects, gained "through training and/or work experience", shown by documented evidence or a recorded CA assessment.
- **ACAM**: Aircraft Continuing Airworthiness Monitoring. **In-depth survey**: "a sample inspection of the key risk elements (KREs) … performed during scheduled/extensive maintenance", a "deep cut". **Ramp survey**: during operations, to monitor the apparent condition. Every ACAM survey requires a physical inspection.
- **Key Risk Elements (KREs)** (AMC3 M.B.303(b)), adapted to the aircraft complexity:
  - (a) type design and changes to type design
  - (b) airworthiness limitations
  - (c) ADs
  - (d) aircraft documents
  - (e) flight manual
  - (f) mass & balance
  - (g) markings & placards
  - (h) operational requirements
  - (i) defect management
  - (j) AMP
  - (k) component control
  - (l) repairs
  - (m) records

  More detail is in Appendix III to GM1 M.B.303(b), which is not in this chunk.
- **EASA Form 1 block 11 statuses**:
  - **Overhauled**: "a process that ensures the item is in complete conformity with all the applicable service tolerances specified in the type certificate holder's, or equipment manufacturer's instructions for continued airworthiness, or in the data which is approved or accepted by the Authority". At least disassembled, cleaned, inspected, repaired as necessary, reassembled and tested.
  - **Repaired**: "Rectification of defect(s) using an applicable standard".
  - **Inspected/Tested**: "Examination, measurement, etc. in accordance with an applicable standard (e.g. visual inspection, functional testing, bench testing etc.)".
  - **Modified**: "Alteration of an item to conform to an applicable standard".
  - **Applicable standard**: "a manufacturing/design/maintenance/quality standard, method, technique or practice approved by or acceptable to the Competent Authority". It is described in block 12.
- **Electronic signature** (AMC App. II): "data in electronic form which is attached to or logically associated with other electronic data and which serves as a method of authentication". It must be uniquely linked to the signatory, able to identify them, and created by means under their sole control. It is a cryptographic value that allows the source and integrity to be verified.
- **Class ratings (App. IV)**:
  - **A**: aircraft and fitted components, using aircraft maintenance data (or component data if agreed), only while fitted. Temporary removal for access is allowed under a MOM control procedure.
  - **B**: uninstalled engine/APU and their fitted components. Installed-engine work at base or line is allowed under a control procedure.
  - **C**: uninstalled components (not engines or APUs). Installed work is allowed under a control procedure.
  - **D**: self-contained specialist services. D1 NDT is needed only when NDT is done as a task for another organisation; A, B and C organisations may do NDT on their own products if the MOM has NDT procedures.
  - **Series**: a specific type series (for example Cessna 150/172, Beech 55, Continental O-200). **Type**: a specific model (Cessna 172RG). **Group**: for example Cessna single piston engine aircraft, or Lycoming non-supercharged piston engines.
- **Complex maintenance tasks (Appendix VII)**, for M.A.801(b):
  1. Modification, repair or replacement by riveting, bonding, laminating or welding of: box beam; wing stringer or chord; spar; spar flange; truss-type beam member; beam web; keel or chine of a hull or float; corrugated sheet compression member in a wing or tail surface; wing main rib; wing or tail brace strut; engine mount; fuselage longeron or frame; side, horizontal truss or bulkhead member; seat support brace or bracket; seat rail replacement; landing gear strut or brace strut; axle; wheel; ski or ski pedestal (excluding replacement of a low-friction coating).
  2. Modification or repair of:
     - skin (including float skin) needing a support, jig or fixture;
     - pressurised skin with damage "more than 15 cm (6 inches) in any direction";
     - load-bearing control system parts, excluding swaging of a repair splice or cable fitting and replacement of a riveted push-pull tube end fitting;
     - other structure the manufacturer identifies as primary structure.
  3. Piston engine:
     - dismantling and reassembly, other than for access to piston/cylinder assemblies, or removing the rear accessory cover for oil pump work without disturbing internal gears;
     - reduction gear dismantling;
     - welding or brazing of joints, other than minor exhaust weld repairs by a suitably approved or authorised welder (component replacement excluded);
     - disturbing parts of bench-tested units, except items normally replaceable or adjustable in service.
  4. Propeller balancing, except certification of static balancing where the manual requires it, and dynamic balancing of installed propellers with electronic equipment where permitted.
  5. Any task needing specialised tooling, equipment or facilities, or significant coordination because of long duration and several people.

  AMC: "suitably approved or authorised welder" means a qualification to an officially recognised standard, or one accepted by the CA.
- **Pilot-owner maintenance (Appendix VIII)**: "simple visual inspections or operations to check for general condition and obvious damage and normal operation". It is **excluded** where the task:
  1. is a critical maintenance task;
  2. requires removal of major components or assemblies;
  3. is done for an AD or ALI, unless the AD or ALI specifically allows it;
  4. needs special or calibrated tools (except a torque wrench and crimping tool);
  5. needs test equipment or special testing (for example NDT, system tests, avionics operational checks);
  6. is an unscheduled special inspection (for example a heavy landing check);
  7. affects systems essential for IFR;
  8. is listed in Appendix VII, or is component maintenance under M.A.502(a) to (d).

  These criteria "cannot be overridden by less restrictive instructions" issued under M.A.302(d). AFM flight-preparation tasks (for example rigging glider wings, pre-flight) are **pilot tasks**, not maintenance, and need no CRS.
- **Standard proposed maintenance schedule** (AMC M.B.301(b)3): a CA may publish one for piston aircraft below 2 730 kg MTOM, or for sailplanes, powered sailplanes and balloons. Owners who use it must add all out-of-phase manufacturer recommendations.
- **Sailplane abbreviations** (AMC App. VIII Part C): SP sailplane; SSPS self-sustained powered sailplane; SLPS/TM self-launching powered sailplane/touring motorglider.

## 5. Software implications (Airworthiness+)

### Data objects
- **Organisation approval**:
  - type: Part-M Subpart F (Form 3-MF, legacy), Part-M Subpart G (Form 14-MG, legacy), with links to Part-145, Part-CAMO and Part-CAO objects;
  - reference string (patterns `XX.MF.NNNN`, `XX.MG.NNNN (ref. AOC XX.NNNN)`);
  - date of original issue, revision no., date of revision;
  - CA, conditions, status (valid, suspended in whole or part, limited, revoked, surrendered, superseded);
  - linked exposition or manual reference;
  - linked AOC, if any (AOC loss invalidates Form 14-MG for the AOC registrations).
- **Terms of approval lines**:
  - MF: class (A/B/C/D), rating (A2, A3, A4, B1–B3, C1–C22, D1), limitation text, base Y/N, line Y/N, ARC authorised Y/N (only ELA1 non-commercial, alongside the annual inspection), NDT methods;
  - MG: aircraft type/series/group, AR authorised Y/N, permit to fly authorised Y/N, organisations under quality system.

  Validate that the exposition scope of work is within the certificate ratings. Flag a one-person AMO that holds ratings beyond its maximums:
  - A2 piston, 5 700 kg and below;
  - A3 single piston helicopter, 3 175 kg and below;
  - A4 no limitation;
  - B2 piston "less than 450 HP";
  - C1–C22 as per capability list;
  - D1 with method specified.
- **Capability list**: versioned. Amendments may follow the indirect approval route.
- **Nominated persons**: role, Form 4 acceptance date and reference, CA acceptance status. AM acceptance is tied to the commitment statement in the approved exposition and to an AM meeting record.
- **Exposition / MOM / CAME amendment log** (the "status sheet"): amendment no., submitted date, route (direct or indirect), CA received date, CA approval or acknowledgement date, and whether it affects certificate content (if so, the certificate must be reissued). Changes notified under M.A.617 or M.A.713 can never be "minor" or indirect.
- **Audit** (CA or internal mirror):
  - organisation, scope element, planned date, done date, auditor;
  - report form (6F or 13), quality review signature;
  - AM meeting held Y/N;
  - back-credit items (date of last item audit, and that the item is re-audited within 24 months).
- **Finding**:
  - id, source (audit, ACAM, other), requirement reference, level (1/2), categorisation status (provisional or confirmed);
  - date raised, date confirmed in writing (target ≤ 2 weeks after the visit, from initial audit AMC);
  - corrective action period due date (Level 2 ≤ 3 months), extension requested or granted with a corrective action plan reference;
  - closure action, date cleared, CA closure letter or report reference;
  - status (open, CAP submitted, extended, closed, overdue leading to suspension risk);
  - affected aircraft (Level 1 can trigger owner and CA notification and rectification before further flight);
  - KRE tag (for ACAM).
- **Aircraft**: State of registry CA, CofA copy, ARC history, ARC recommendations, CA review reports, correspondence, exemptions, enforcement, approved documents, withdrawn-from-service date. CA-side retention runs until withdrawal + 2 years; mirror this as a minimum for archive policy.
- **ARC**:
  - form type (15a CA / 15b organisation; 15c Part-ML is elsewhere);
  - ARC reference;
  - aircraft manufacturer, designation, registration, serial number;
  - issuer (CA or organisation, with name, address and approval reference);
  - issue date, expiry date, airframe FH at issue (not for airships);
  - signatory and authorisation no.;
  - extension 1 and 2, each with issue date, expiry, FH, signatory, authorisation no., company, approval ref, and a "controlled environment for the last year" attestation;
  - status (valid, suspended, revoked, expired).
- **ARC recommendation** (sent to the CA): submitted date, compliance statement, CA response due (30 days, AMC M.B.901), CA requests for information or physical survey, outcome.
- **Airworthiness review staff record**: the AMC M.B.902(c) fields plus an applicability tier:
  - Tier 1 (air carrier or more than 2 730 kg): 5 yrs, or 10 yrs with no licence or degree;
  - Tier 2 (2 730 kg or below, non-air-carrier): 3 yrs, or 7 yrs with no licence or degree.

  Part-66 B or C licence in the subcategory; no type rating or recency needed.
- **CAM contract (Appendix I)**:
  - parties (owner or registered lessee, with address; CAMO/CAO, with address);
  - aircraft registration, type and S/N; type of operation;
  - mandatory statement text;
  - obligations checklist (5.1 and 5.2), occurrence reporting split (376/2014);
  - (ea) flag with eligibility justification (same air carrier business grouping, 1008/2008 licence), operator pre-assessment of the CAMO, harmonisation description, training records both ways;
  - signed dates, copy-to-CA-on-request log;
  - status (active, denounced, cancelled for non-conformity), denunciation date;
  - CA notification records.
- **EASA Form 1 (maintenance)**: blocks 1–14e as above.
  - Tracking no. is unique per organisation.
  - Block 11 is an enum (Overhauled, Repaired, Inspected/Tested, Modified), exactly one.
  - Block 12 is structured: maintenance data and revision, ADs/SBs, repairs, mods, parts, LLP status, shelf life, NDT, batch numbers, deviations.
  - Block 14a is a multi-select with at least one selected.
  - Block 14e is formatted dd MMM yyyy.
  - Blocks 13a–13e are disabled or shaded for maintenance.
  - Per line item: item no., description, P/N, qty, S/N (or "N/A").
  - Correction link: `corrects_form_id`, corrected blocks, and the standard text; the retention period is inherited from the original.
- **Pilot-owner task catalogue**: ATA chapter, area, task text, and applicability by category:
  - aeroplane ≤ 2 730 kg;
  - single-engine rotorcraft ≤ 2 730 kg;
  - SP, SSPS, SLPS/TM;
  - hot air airship, hot air balloon, gas balloon.

  Include the `**` flag (task excludes IFR unless released by a licensed engineer). The AMP marks which tasks are declared pilot-owner tasks.
- **Complex maintenance task list** (Appendix VII): a classification table used to block non-licensed or pilot-owner release and to route work to an approved organisation.
- **Tech log system**: initial CA approval reference, and the approved procedure for indirect changes.
- **AMP**: approval route (direct by CA, or indirect via the CAMO/CAO procedure).
  - Time-limited approval for an incomplete AMP at start of operation (AMC M.B.301(b)7).
  - CA-published standard schedule flag, with the out-of-phase manufacturer tasks attached.
  - Fields noting who may issue a CRS and which tasks are complex or pilot-owner (AMC M.B.301(b)5).
  - Programme control provisions (AMC M.B.301(c)): task escalation, programme review, SB assessment, component and structures in-service performance review, revision, effectiveness review, MRBR/MPD review, AD review, liaison, training.
  - Escalation evidence (task repeated satisfactorily several times at the current frequency).
  - Status, including suspended or revoked (for example on a change in utilisation).

### Workflows
1. **CAM contract lifecycle**:
   - Create from template (Appendix I text).
   - Verify the CAMO/CAO terms of approval cover the aircraft type before signing.
   - Generate obligations tasks.
   - On non-conformity, run a safety-significance and repetition assessment. If cancelled, notify the CA. The owner reports to the CA within 2 weeks (immediately for (ea)).
   - On denunciation or sale, send CA notifications.
2. **ARC issue / extension**:
   - Generate Form 15b (organisation) or prepare a recommendation for the CA (which issues 15a).
   - Alert: **send a copy of the ARC issued or extended to the CA of registry within 10 days**.
   - Extension requires the "controlled environment for the last year" check (M.A.901 logic is in another chunk).
   - Track the CA's 30-day assessment of a recommendation.
3. **Pilot-owner maintenance**:
   - Check the task against the AMP-declared list and the 8 exclusion criteria (AD/ALI, special tools, IFR, Appendix VII, M.A.502 component tasks).
   - Require maintenance data references in the CRS.
   - Record the CRS in the logbook.
   - Alert and countdown: **inform the CAMO/CAO within 30 days** of completion.
   - Block IFR-flagged tasks for IFR operation unless released by a licensed engineer.
4. **Form 1 issue and receipt**:
   - Electronic signing must:
     - be personal to the signatory, generated only in their presence, and cryptographic;
     - never sign a blank form;
     - lock data after signature (changes need a new form with a new number referencing the original);
     - work only at the release location;
     - keep an audit trail and backups.
   - Print with the "PRINTED FROM ELECTRONIC FILE" watermark and "Electronic Signature on File" in block 14b.
   - Electronic exchange is voluntary and must be agreed by both parties. It carries all data plus authentication data, uses Spec 2000 Ch.16 where possible, and must allow lossless regeneration; otherwise fall back to paper.
   - On receipt, check that block 14a and the block 12 statement match the issuer type (Part-145 vs Part-M Subpart F / CAO). Warn that the certificate is not authority to install, and require a separate installation CRS in the aircraft records.
   - Error handling: the end user notifies the originator in writing, and a correction form is issued.
5. **Finding management**:
   - Level 1: immediate restriction, and notify affected aircraft owners and CAs (AMC M.B.705(a)(1)). Possibly re-certify affected products (AMC M.B.605(a)(1)).
   - Level 2: 3-month due date, with extension only against an approved CAP.
   - Overdue: suspension.
   - Aircraft Level 1: grounding ("before further flight") and ARC suspension or revocation.
6. **Oversight readiness**:
   - 24-month audit cycle tracker (complete audit, AM meeting, aircraft product sample for CAMOs, subcontractor audits).
   - A CA audit programme view that shows which aspects are covered on each visit.
   - Prepare internal self-monitoring or quality reports and the quality manager's concession count (the CA will look at these).
7. **Exposition / MOM change**:
   - Classify direct vs indirect.
   - Changes under M.A.617 or M.A.713 cannot be indirect.
   - Submit every amendment to the CA. Record the written approval (direct) or the CA's written acknowledgement (indirect).
   - Reissue the certificate only if its content is affected.
8. **Aircraft transfer between States**: checklist for notifying both CAs and passing on known problems (M.B.904, AMC M.B.105(a)).
9. **AOC applicant pack** (licensed air carriers): CAME, AMPs, tech log, Part-145 contract technical specifications. Submit drafts early; the approval cannot be granted until the documents are complete.

### Alerts / timers summary
- ARC copy to CA: 10 days.
- Pilot-owner maintenance report to CAMO: 30 days.
- Contract non-conformity report to CA by owner: 2 weeks (immediately for (ea)).
- Level 2 finding: no more than 3 months (extendable).
- Findings confirmed in writing by CA: within 2 weeks of the audit (expect the letter).
- CA response to ARC recommendation: 30 days.
- Org audit cycle, AM meeting and CAMO aircraft sample survey: 24 months.
- Organisation records (CA): 5 years. Aircraft records (CA): withdrawal + 2 years.
- ARC: original, plus up to two extensions on the form (the "last year" wording implies 1-year validity per period; this is in Section A).
- Legacy Form 3-MF and 14-MG validity: 24 March 2022. Flag legacy certificates past this date as needing conversion to Part-CAO or Part-CAMO (EU). UK date may differ (guess).

### Permissions / roles
- Form 1 signatory: only "persons specifically authorised under the rules and policies of the Competent Authority". Store the authorisation number and scope, and enforce secure per-person access.
- ARC signatory: authorisation number. Airworthiness review staff eligibility is tiered by aircraft (see above).
- Pilot-owner: may release only declared AMP pilot-owner tasks, must be competent (self-declared), and must hold a current pilot licence for the type (AMC).
- CAMO/CAO: AMP approval only via an approved indirect procedure; otherwise submit to the CA.
- Owner: must not modify or repair without the CAMO/CAO's prior agreement (contract). Add a hard warning to work order flows.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM (practical effect)

- **AMC M.B.102(a)**: CAs must not delegate key inspection functions so far that industry in effect "regulate themselves", and must not depend on single individuals.
- **AMC2 M.B.102(c)**: ACAM in-depth surveys need M.B.102(c)-qualified inspectors. Ramp surveys may be done by ramp-qualified inspectors from other Parts.
- **AMC M.B.301(b)**:
  - the CA checks the AMP against the operating environment and utilisation;
  - the CA keeps a copy of the AMP unless a CAMO approved it;
  - the approval letter may say who can issue a CRS, and define complex and pilot-owner tasks;
  - an incomplete AMP may get a time-limited approval;
  - escalation needs repeated satisfactory performance at the current interval;
  - the approval can be suspended or revoked if utilisation changes or the owner/CAMO fails to keep the AMP current.
- **AMC M.B.301(c)**: for indirect AMP approval the CAO/CAMO must show it can analyse reliability. It needs RCM and condition monitoring procedures as appropriate, and programme control provisions (a)–(j). The CA may attend reliability meetings.
- **ACAM AMC/GM**:
  - risk-based selection;
  - a percentage of **unannounced** ramp surveys;
  - every KRE must be covered over the programme (none "never inspected");
  - credit is possible against organisation oversight and other qualifying inspections;
  - combined surveys: ACAM, AR, permit-to-fly inspection, export CofA, M.B.704(c) product survey, Part-145/CAO/Subpart F product audit, AR staff supervision review, ARO ramp;
  - findings analysis targets systemic issues and root causes.

  For the app: be inspection-ready on all 13 KREs per aircraft.
- **AMC M.B.602(c) / M.B.702(c)**: audits may be done by product line, with a full check on one type and a sample on others. An organisation senior person (for a CAMO, normally the quality manager) accompanies the auditor. Findings are briefed at close.
- **AMC M.B.602(e) / M.B.702(e)**: provisional findings become confirmed, with written confirmation within 2 weeks. Doubtful items are reviewed afterwards; if there is no finding, verbal confirmation is enough.
- **AMC M.B.603(a) / M.B.703**:
  - approval is based on capability, not individual products (rate "A2 Cessna 100 series", not "Cessna 172RG");
  - ELA1 standard ratings: "ELA1 sailplanes"; "ELA1 powered sailplanes and ELA1 aeroplanes"; "ELA1 balloons"; "ELA1 airships". Each is granted after capability is shown on at least one type;
  - Form 14 scope can be a type, a series, or a group (for example "Group 3 aircraft", "aircraft below 2 730 kg MTOM"). The CAME scope defines the actual approval;
  - adding types within the endorsed group may use **indirect approval**, not M.A.713;
  - a full Group 3 approval should not be granted on a Cessna 172-only intended scope.
- **AMC M.B.604(b) / M.B.704(b)**:
  - audits look at internal self-monitoring reports and (for CAMOs) the number of quality manager concessions;
  - back-credit is possible for item audits within 23 months (four conditions);
  - CAMO subcontractors are audited every 24 months, accompanied by CAMO staff, and findings go to the CAMO;
  - multiple approvals are audited together.
- **AMC M.B.605(a)(1)**: a Level 1 finding may require further maintenance and re-certification of all affected products.
- **AMC M.B.705(a)(1)**: for a Level 1 CAMO finding, the CA informs owners/operators and the CAs of affected aircraft so unsafe conditions are corrected before further flight. It may become an aircraft finding under M.B.303(f).
- **AMC M.B.701(a)**: submit drafts early. The CA may ask for Part-145 contract technical specifications showing base and line maintenance is arranged "for an appropriate period of time".
- **AMC M.B.702(b)**: CAMO subcontract contracts are included in the CAME and checked against AMC M.A.711(a)(3). The CA considers all of the subcontractor's other contracts, whatever the State of registry.
- **AMC M.B.901**:
  - the CA does not repeat the review, but checks it was complete and accurate;
  - the depth of investigation depends on aircraft history and the organisation's track record;
  - the CA may order a physical survey or a full or partial AR, with notice to the CAMO/CAO (M.A.901(i)), done by M.B.902(b)-qualified staff;
  - the ARC is issued only when the inspector is satisfied the aircraft is airworthy;
  - result within 30 days.
- **AMC M.B.902(b)**: grandfathers pre-Part-M CA staff. Part-66 B/C licence in the subcategory, with no type rating or recency needed. For 2 730 kg and below, knowledge may come from work experience and be assessed by the CA.
- **AMC to Appendix II (electronic Form 1)**:
  - an organisation using e-signature or e-exchange must document the procedure and submit it with its exposition documents;
  - paper with signature (originals and copies), a printout from an electronic system, and a fully electronic form are all acceptable;
  - the system must be under a policy and management structure (administrators, signatories, authorisation scope, passwords and authentication, change tracking, minimum blocks completed, archives);
  - extra data (manufacturer code, customer code, workshop report, inspection results) is allowed, preferably in block 12;
  - hyperlinked data must print legibly and be identified as referenced from the Form 1.

  These are design requirements for Airworthiness+ if it generates Form 1s.
- **GM to Appendix II**: "A statement such as 'in accordance with the CMM' is not acceptable". Block 12 must give the documentation **with revision status** for all work. Block 12 can hold vital airworthiness information (for example shelf life, LLP status, NDT, batch numbers) that may require action by the receiver. **Parse and surface block 12 on receipt.**
- **AMC to Appendix V / VI**: the certificate dates and revision no. track the exposition revisions that affect certificate content. Other revisions need no reissue.
- **AMC to Appendix VIII**:
  - the task lists are the "representative scope";
  - they apply to an owner holding a current, valid pilot licence for the type;
  - periodic inspections of any interval may be done if every task is in the lists. Pilot-owner maintenance cannot be generalised to "50 Hrs, 100 Hrs or 6 Month" inspections, since content varies by TCH. A "50 Hrs/6 Month periodic inspection for a fixed wing aeroplane as well as the one-year inspection on a glider may normally be eligible";
  - `**` tasks exclude IFR operations afterwards, unless released by an appropriately licensed engineer.

  Notable list contents:
  - **Part A (aeroplanes ≤ 2 730 kg)**: oil and filter change; spark plugs; wheels and bearings; brake pads; batteries (not Ni-Cd; `**`); ELT removal and refit; nav database updates (not AFCS or transponder); cowlings (no prop removal); fabric patches over no more than one rib; side windows without riveting or bonding; placards; lights; fuses; safety belts (not airbag belts); seats; portable oxygen; wiring repairs in non-critical equipment; pitot-static simple leak check (`**`); panel removal (`**`); comms and nav device swap (`**`).
  - **Part B (single-engine rotorcraft ≤ 2 730 kg)**: similar, plus main rotor blade removal and refit of the same blades in the original position where no special tools are needed (tail rotor excluded); snow pads; emergency flotation gear with quick disconnects; spin-on oil filters only; seats excluding flight crew seats; no surface finish work on main or tail rotors.
  - **Part C (SP / SSPS / SLPS-TM)**: adds trim plan recalculation without reweighing; free play measurement and minor adjustment; control travel measurement; gas dampers; turbulator tapes; water ballast flexible tanks; canopies; simple wiring for non-required equipment, including soldering and crimping of switches; non-required instruments; static probes; air brake chute; fire warning sensors (powered only). Exceptions by type:
    - fuel lines with self-sealing couplings: SSPS Yes, SLPS/TM **NO**;
    - powerplant unit removal/installation: SSPS Yes, SLPS/TM **NO**;
    - engine controls minor adjustment: SSPS Yes, SLPS/TM **NO**;
    - lights: SLPS/TM only;
    - no IFR exclusions apart from the instrument panel.
  - **Part D (hot air airship / hot air balloon / gas balloon)**:
    - envelope fabric repairs (not complete panels, no load tape): airship and hot air balloon Yes, gas **No**;
    - nose line: airship only;
    - banners;
    - melting link;
    - temperature transmitter;
    - crown line: hot air balloon only, airship No;
    - scoop or skirt;
    - burner cleaning, piezo adjustment, jets, buffers, valve adjustment (hot air only);
    - basket trim, runners, handles, seat covers and belts;
    - liquid valve O-rings: gas No;
    - batteries and comm/nav swap;
    - airship engine tasks, including powerplant removal and installation, spark plugs, chip detectors, oil, fuel lines.

  The rotorcraft table in the source has a stray empty ATA "63" row before "65 Transmission" (an extraction artefact).
