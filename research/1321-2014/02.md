## 1. Scope (IR points)

| Point | Title | One-line plain summary |
|---|---|---|
| M.A.301 | Continuing airworthiness tasks | Lists the tasks (a)–(i) that together ensure continuing airworthiness and serviceability of operational/emergency equipment: pre-flight inspections, defect rectification (with MEL/CDL), AMP maintenance, release per Subpart H, AMP effectiveness analysis (CMPA/air carriers), ADs/ODs/Agency requirements/authority safety measures, mods and repairs per M.A.304, mass and balance statement to PIC/operator, maintenance check flights. |
| M.A.302 | Aircraft maintenance programme (AMP) | Every aircraft maintained to an approved AMP; approval by the competent authority or by indirect approval via a CAMO/CAO; content basis (authority instructions, ICA from DAHs, CS references, Part-26); escalation allowed with review data; reliability programme for CMPA where MSG/condition monitoring; periodic review. |
| M.A.303 | Airworthiness directives | Every applicable AD must be carried out within the AD's requirements, unless the Agency specifies otherwise. |
| M.A.304 | Data for modifications and repairs | Damage must be assessed; mods/repairs must use data approved by the Agency, by a Part 21 DOA, contained in CS requirements (21.A.90B/21.A.431B; 21L.A.62/102/202/222), or declared under Part 21 Light. |
| M.A.305 | Aircraft continuing airworthiness record system | CRS into the record system within 30 days; required contents (dates, in-service life, status of ADs, mods/repairs, AMP compliance, deferrals, LLPs, time-controlled components, mass and balance); retention periods (36 months / superseded / 12 months after permanent withdrawal); clear, correctable entries. |
| M.A.306 | Aircraft technical log system | For CAT, commercial specialised operations (SPO) and commercial ATO/DTO operations: technical log with flight info, current CRS, maintenance statement, outstanding deferred defects, maintenance support guidance; initial issue approved by the competent authority, amendments via CAME/CAE procedures. |
| M.A.307 | Transfer of aircraft continuing airworthiness records | On permanent transfer to another owner/operator, records (and tech log if applicable) must go too; on contracting a CAMO/CAO, records go to it; retention periods continue to apply to the recipient. |

## 2. Obligations

### M.A.301 Continuing airworthiness tasks (who: the person/organisation responsible under M.A.201: owner, operator, CAMO or CAO)
Continuing airworthiness and serviceability of operational and emergency equipment "shall be ensured by":
- (a) accomplishment of pre-flight inspections;
- (b) rectification of any defect and damage affecting safe operation, using data per M.A.304 and M.A.401, "taking into account the minimum equipment list ('MEL') and configuration deviation list, when they exist";
- (c) all maintenance per the AMP (M.A.302);
- (d) release of all maintenance per Subpart H (M.A.801 etc.);
- (e) for all complex motor-powered aircraft (CMPA) or aircraft used by air carriers licensed under Reg. (EC) No 1008/2008: analysis of the effectiveness of the approved AMP;
- (f) accomplishment of any applicable: (1) AD; (2) operational directive with a continuing airworthiness impact; (3) continuing airworthiness requirement established by the Agency; (4) measures required by the competent authority in immediate reaction to a safety problem;
- (g) modifications and repairs per M.A.304;
- (h) delivering the mass and balance statement reflecting the current configuration to the pilot-in-command (or to the operator, for licensed air carriers);
- (i) maintenance check flights (MCF), when necessary.

AMC-level obligations:
- Defects affecting safe operation must be rectified within limits prescribed by the approved MEL, CDL or maintenance data; postponement only if agreed by the operator and per a procedure approved by the competent authority (AMC M.A.301(b)).
- Maintenance checks must be performed within AMP limits; postponement only per a procedure agreed by the competent authority (AMC M.A.301(c)).
- Licensed air carriers: CAMO publishes pre-flight inspection guidance; pre-flight personnel training must be demonstrated to the authority; training standard described in the CAME (AMC M.A.301(a) para 3).
- OD incorporation: operator responsible; CAMO assesses airworthiness impact; process set out in an arrangement or common procedure (AMC M.A.301(f)).

### M.A.302 AMP
- (a) Maintenance of each aircraft "shall be organised in accordance with an AMP".
- (b) The AMP and any subsequent amendments approved by the competent authority.
- (c) Indirect approval allowed when continuing airworthiness is managed by a CAMO or CAO, or under a limited contract per M.A.201(i)(3). The indirect approval procedure is part of the CAME (CAMO.A.300 or M.A.704) or the CAE (CAO.A.025), approved by the authority responsible for that CAMO/CAO. Only usable where the CAMO/CAO is overseen by the State of registry, unless a written contract under M.1 point 3 transfers AMP approval responsibility to the CAMO/CAO's authority.
- (d) The AMP must demonstrate compliance with: (1) competent authority instructions; (2) ICA from TC, RTC, STC, major repair design approval, ETSO authorisation holders, declarants of design compliance, or any other Part 21 / Part 21 Light approval holder; CS items per 21.A.90B/21.A.431B; 21L.A.62/102/202/222; (3) applicable provisions of Part-26 (Reg. (EU) 2015/640).
- (e) Derogation: owner/CAMO/CAO may deviate from (d)(2) ICA and propose escalated intervals based on sufficient reviews per (h). "Indirect approval is not permitted for the escalation of safety-related tasks." Additional instructions may also be proposed.
- (f) AMP contains all maintenance, including frequency and tasks linked to type and specificity of operations.
- (g) CMPA with AMP based on MSG logic or condition monitoring: AMP must include a reliability programme.
- (h) Periodic reviews and amendment when necessary, taking into account operating experience, authority instructions, new/modified TC/STC/declarant instructions.
- AMC: review "at least annually"; mandatory Part 21 requirements incorporated "as soon as possible".
- AMC: an aircraft maintained to only one approved AMP at a time; switching may require a transfer check/inspection.
- AMC: escalation of tasks directly approved by the competent authority, except ALIs (Airworthiness Limitation Items), which are approved by the Agency.

### M.A.303 ADs
- Carry out any applicable AD "within the requirements of that airworthiness directive, unless otherwise specified by the Agency."

### M.A.304 Mods and repairs
- Any person or organisation repairing an aircraft or component "shall assess any damage".
- Use only approved/declared data from the five listed sources (a)–(e).

### M.A.305 Records — time limits and retention (exact)
- (a) CRS (per M.A.801 or 145.A.50) entered in the record system "as soon as practicable and no later than 30 days after the completion of any maintenance".
- (e)(1) Technical log: "covering the 36 months period prior to the last entry" (AMC: may be extended if a tech log entry needs longer retention elsewhere in Part-M).
- (e)(2)(i) CRS + detailed records for AD / authority safety measure compliance (aircraft, engines, propellers, components): until superseded by equivalent information "but covering a period not shorter than 36 months".
- (e)(2)(ii) CRS + detailed records demonstrating compliance with M.A.304 data for current mods and repairs (aircraft, engines, propellers, components subject to airworthiness limitations): no end stated, i.e. kept while the mod/repair is current.
- (e)(2)(iii) CRS + detailed records of all scheduled maintenance or other maintenance required for continuing airworthiness: until superseded, not shorter than 36 months.
- (e)(3)(i) In-service history record for each LLP: no end stated (kept for life of part).
- (e)(3)(ii) CRS + detailed records for last scheduled maintenance and subsequent unscheduled maintenance of LLPs and time-controlled components: until superseded by equivalent scheduled maintenance, not shorter than 36 months.
- (e)(3)(iii) ELA2 component fitted without EASA Form 1 per 21.A.307(b)(2): CRS + owner's acceptance statement, not shorter than 36 months.
- (e)(3)(iv) Component fitted without EASA Form 1 per 21L.A.193(b)(2): CRS + owner's acceptance statement, not shorter than 36 months.
- (e)(4) Aircraft permanently withdrawn from service: (b)(1) data (dates, in-service life), last effective (c)/(d) statuses, and most recent CRS(s)/records under (e)(2)(ii) and (e)(3)(i): retained "for at least 12 months".
- (f) Person/organisation responsible under M.A.201 complies with record system requirements and presents records to the competent authority on request.
- (g) Entries "clear and accurate"; corrections made "in a manner that clearly shows the original entry".
- GM interpretation of "superseded ... not shorter than 36 months": for 36 months both old and superseding records are kept; after 36 months only the new one. Task interval < 36 months means more than one set kept; interval > 36 months means only the last set.
- AMC M.A.305(e) IT backup "updated at least within 24 hours of any entry in the primary system".

### M.A.306 Technical log
- Who: operator, for CAT, commercial SPO, commercial ATO or commercial DTO operations.
- Initial issue approved by the competent authority per CAMO.A.105, M.1, or CAO.1(1); amendments managed per CAMO.A.300(c), M.A.704(b)/(c), or CAO.A.025(c) (i.e. via CAME/CAE procedures).

### M.A.307 Transfer
- (a) Transferring owner/operator ensures M.A.305 records (and M.A.306 tech log if applicable) are transferred on permanent transfer.
- (b) Owner contracting a CAMO/CAO ensures M.A.305 records are transferred to it.
- (c) M.A.305(e) retention periods continue to apply to the new owner, operator, CAMO or CAO.
- AMC: dry lease-out of "less than 6 months" generally not a permanent transfer, but records needed for the lease must be transferred to or accessible by the lessee. On termination of operation, records passed to the new owner/operator or stored.
- AMC M.A.305(f): if a maintenance organisation keeps records on behalf of the owner/CAMO, the owner/CAMO remains responsible for retention and for transfer to a new owner/organisation.

### ATL contents (AMC M.A.306(a), 5-section example)
- Section 1: operator registered name and address, aircraft type, complete international registration marks.
- Section 2: when next scheduled maintenance is due (including out-of-phase component changes due before next check); current CRS for the complete aircraft (normally from last check). Flight crew need not get these if next maintenance controlled by other acceptable means.
- Section 3 (sector record): type and registration; date and place of take-off and landing; take-off and landing times; running total of flying hours (to determine hours to next scheduled maintenance); failures/defects/malfunctions affecting airworthiness or safe operation incl. emergency systems and cabin/galley defects affecting safety known to the commander, with commander date and signature, including "nil defect" entries; CRS after rectification identifying the defect(s) or check; fuel and oil uplifted, fuel per tank at start and end of each flight, planned vs actual uplift in same units; de-icing/anti-icing start time, fluid type, mixture ratio; pre-flight inspection signature. Supplementary: time in engine power ranges affecting engine life; number of landings; flight cycles / pressure cycles. Layout: one copy stays on aircraft, one on ground until flight completed; separate after-flight and pre-next-flight parts.
- Abbreviated CRS: "Part-145 release to service" acceptable instead of the full AMC 145.A.50(b) statement, provided the introductory section shows the full statement.
- Section 4 (deferred defects): pre-printed operator name and page serial number; cross-reference to original Section 3 entry; original date of occurrence; brief details; eventual rectification and its CRS or cross-reference.
- Section 5: maintenance support information (how to contact maintenance en route etc.).
- Cabin/galley defects affecting safe operation or occupant safety count as part of the log even if recorded by another means.

## 4. Defined Terms

- **Pre-flight inspection** (AMC M.A.301(a)): "all of the actions necessary to ensure that the aircraft is fit to make the intended flight". Typically: walk-around for condition (wear, damage, leakage) and presence of required/emergency equipment; check of record system/ATL for outstanding deferred defects and that no required maintenance is overdue "or will become due during the flight"; fluids/gases uplifted correct spec, uncontaminated, recorded; doors secure; locks, covers, restraints, blanks removed; surfaces/engines free of ice, snow, sand, dust; de-/anti-icing fluid residue assessment. Oil/hydraulic uplift and tyre inflation may be included, with procedures to detect abnormal consumption.
- **Deferred defects** (AMC M.A.301(b)): "defects reported in operational service which are deferred for later rectification".
- **Carried forward defects**: "defects arising during maintenance which are carried forward for rectification at a later maintenance input".
- **Operational directives with a continuing airworthiness impact**: e.g. ETOPS/LROPS, RVSM, MNPS, AWOPS, RNAV.
- **Other continuing airworthiness requirement established by the Agency**: TC-related requirements such as CMRs, life-limited parts, airworthiness limitations in CS-25 Book 1 Appendix H para H25.4, fuel tank system limitations incl. CDCCL.
- **Maintenance programme** vs **maintenance schedule** (AMC M.A.302): programme includes scheduled tasks, associated procedures and standard maintenance practices; schedule is the scheduled tasks alone.
- **Reliability programme**: means to ensure AMP tasks are effective and periodicity adequate; can escalate, delete, de-escalate or add tasks.
- **Applicable (airworthiness limitation) parameter** (GM M.A.305(b)): flight hours and/or flight cycles and/or landings and/or calendar time and/or any other utilisation unit (AMC also cites APU cycles, engine power range time, pressure cycles).
- **Life-limited part** (GM M.A.305(c)): part the AMP maintenance schedule requires to be permanently removed from service when, or before, the mandatory life limitation (per Reg. 748/2012) is reached in any applicable parameter. Examples: engine HPT disc, landing gear sliding tube.
- **Time-controlled component** (GM M.A.305(e)): component the AMP requires to be periodically removed for workshop maintenance to return it to a specified standard, sub-component replacement, or inspection/test of performance, after a service period controlled at component level per a specified airworthiness limitation. Examples: horizontal stabiliser actuator, landing gear gearbox, ELT functional check, slide/raft batteries, extinguisher cartridges. Under MSG-based AMPs this means Hard Time and On-Condition. A part can be both LLP and time-controlled.
- **Current status** (GM M.A.305(l)): data accurately establishing the level of compliance with a requirement; must identify the item, be dated, and include total in-service life in the applicable parameter at that date. For LLPs: limit, accumulated, remaining.
- **Detailed maintenance records** (GM M.A.305(g)): those the M.A.201 responsible person needs to fulfil Part-M obligations; a subset of what maintenance organisations keep under M.A.614, CAO.A.090(a), 145.A.55(a). Incoming CoCs, batch numbers and individual task card sign-offs need not be kept by the owner/CAMO, but dimensional data may be requested.
- **Airworthiness limitation** (GM M.A.305(h)): "a boundary beyond which an aircraft or a component thereof must not be operated, unless the instruction(s) associated with this airworthiness limitation is (are) complied with."
- **Other maintenance required for continuing airworthiness** (GM M.A.305(i)): unscheduled or out-of-phase maintenance due to abnormal events, e.g. lightning strikes, hard landings, long-term storage, propeller/rotor overspeed, over-torque, main rotor blade impact.
- **In-service history record** (GM M.A.305(j)): records from which LLP current status can be determined (fields above).
- **Permanently withdrawn from service** (GM M.A.305(k)): moving the aircraft or component to a location not used for storage and/or future return to service.
- **Hard Time**: preventive process, actions at periods related to time in service restore the utility margin.
- **On-Condition**: preventive inspection/test at specified periods to decide if it can continue in service.
- **Condition Monitoring**: monitoring a parameter (vibration, temperature, oil consumption) to identify developing faults; may remain in service without preventive maintenance until functional failure.
- **Fly-to-failure**: components outside these processes; corrective maintenance after failure detection.
- **Data equivalent in scope and detail**: logbooks/log cards in the record system.
- **Permanent transfer** (AMC M.A.307(a)): does not generally include dry lease-out under 6 months.
- **Maintenance check flight (MCF)**: defined in the Air Operations Regulation; operated under operator control.
- **Interoperable aircraft technical log system** (GM1 M.A.306(b)): for a CAMO contracted under M.A.201(ea) by operators in a single air carrier business grouping regularly moving aircraft between AOCs; common data formats and data exchange defined.

## 5. Software Implications

### Core data objects
- **Aircraft**: registration, type, MSN, operator, owner, operation type (CAT / commercial SPO / commercial ATO / commercial DTO / non-commercial), CMPA flag, licensed air carrier (1008/2008) flag, CAMO/CAO managing, regime (Part-M vs Part-ML), status (active, stored, permanently withdrawn with date). Drives whether ATL, AMP effectiveness analysis, reliability programme and defect-analysis features are mandatory.
- **Utilisation counters** per aircraft, engine, propeller, engine module, APU and component: flight hours, flight cycles, landings, calendar, APU cycles, pressure cycles, time in engine power ranges, custom units. Each status snapshot must be dated with counters at that date.
- **Engine module** records where modular life tracking is used (module-level total life, in-service history, records travel with the module).
- **AMP**: version, approval route (direct authority / indirect via CAME/CAE), approval reference and date, effectivity per registration (one AMP can cover several registrations, with task-level effectivity), preface (inspection standards, permitted tolerances, escalation procedure), basis documents (MRB report, MPD, AMM chapters, component data, Part-26, authority instructions) with revisions, next annual review due date, reliability programme link. Rule: one active AMP per aircraft at a time; changing AMP may need a transfer check.
- **AMP task**: task ref, title, source (MRB/MPD/ICA/CMR/ALI/AD repetitive/mod- or repair-derived/authority/operator additional), MRB cross-reference, control level (aircraft/engine/propeller/component), interval(s) with parameter(s), tolerances, safety-related/ALI flag (blocks indirect approval of escalation; ALI escalation needs Agency approval), escalation history (any change in periodicity or parameter must be recorded).
- **Task compliance record**: last done (CRS date + counters), next due, terminating action flag, linked CRS.
- **AD**: number, revision/amendment, applicability, status (applicable / not applicable with reason / superseded / cancelled), one-time vs repetitive, multi-part steps with method-of-compliance choice, intermediate assessments, last and next for repetitive, due limits in multiple parameters, linked CRS and detailed records, software part numbers.
- **Authority safety measures** (immediate reaction to a safety problem), **operational directives** (ETOPS, RVSM, etc.) and **Agency requirements** (CMR, CDCCL, ALIs): track like ADs.
- **Modification / repair**: data reference and source type (Agency approved / DOA approved / CS-based / Part 21 Light declared), embodied on (aircraft/engine/propeller/component), CRS date, multi-part status, method of compliance, derived AMP tasks, impacts (mass and balance, AFM supplement, ICA). Structural repair fields: description type (doubler, blend, crack, dent), location (stringer/frame refs), dimensions, remaining material for blend-outs; repaired and non-repaired damage.
- **Loadable software part** configuration: P/N tracked independently of hardware where the DAH data identifies it; nav databases and IFE excluded.
- **Component**: P/N, S/N, classification (LLP / time-controlled (hard time / on-condition) / condition monitored / fly-to-failure / LLP + time-controlled), current location (installed on with position, or removed), life limit, accumulated, remaining, since last overhaul/scheduled maintenance, Form 1 reference, owner's acceptance statement (no Form 1 cases), alternate interchangeable part flag (not a mod if DAH published).
- **In-service history entry (LLP)**: date on/off, host type/S/N/weight variant/thrust rating, counters at on/off, events changing life limit or parameter.
- **Defect**: source (in flight / during maintenance), type (deferred vs carried forward), MEL/CDL reference and category limits, deferral procedure reference, raised date, sector cross-reference, rectification CRS, cumulative-effect review flag, crew notification flag. Repetitive and significant flags.
- **CRS**: unique reference, date, counters at release, issuing organisation (Part-145, M Subpart F, CAO, independent certifying staff), scope, linked work items, "certificate after incomplete maintenance" type, abbreviated "Part-145 release to service" variant.
- **Technical log** (for CAT/commercial SPO/ATO/DTO): sector records (date, place and times of take-off/landing, block or flight time, running totals, fuel/oil uplifts planned vs actual, fuel per tank before/after, de-icing start time, fluid type and mix ratio, defects, nil-defect entry, commander signature, pre-flight inspection signature), deferred defect pages with page serial numbers, maintenance statement (next due scheduled and out-of-phase), current CRS, maintenance support info. Approval reference for initial ATL system; amendments via CAME/CAE.
- **Mass and balance report**: current version, linked to mods; delivery record to PIC/operator.
- **Maintenance check flight**: scenario (1 incomplete maintenance + certificate, 2 operator/CAMO choice after CRS, 3 post-troubleshooting confirmation after CRS, 4 permit to fly needed), ATL entry, data to be gathered, appointed crew briefing record, post-flight completion and final CRS.
- **Reliability / effectiveness analysis** (CMPA and air carriers): significant defects, repetitive defects, deferred/carried forward trends, unscheduled removals, system performance; outputs feed AMP amendments.
- **Record transfer**: from/to party, date, scope, lease (dry lease < 6 months not permanent), records accessible to lessee, retention obligations carried over.

### Workflows
- CRS ingestion: alert if a CRS is not entered within 30 days of maintenance completion (configurable warning earlier, hard flag at day 30).
- AD/task forecasting: compute next due per parameter, "whichever first", using utilisation forecasts; show remaining.
- Pre-flight/dispatch check: block or warn if any required maintenance is overdue or "will become due during the flight" (needs planned flight duration/cycles), and show outstanding deferred defects affecting operation.
- Deferral workflow: raise defect, apply MEL/CDL category limit, approval per authority-approved procedure, extension requests, cumulative-effect review, crew notification, closure with CRS.
- Maintenance postponement (task tolerance/extension) per authority-agreed procedure, with audit.
- AMP amendment workflow: draft, review, approval (direct to authority or indirect by CAMO/CAO with eligibility check: CAMO/CAO under State of registry oversight or M.1 point 3 contract; block indirect approval for escalation of safety-related tasks; ALI escalation flagged for Agency approval). Annual review reminder ("at least annually"); prompt to import new DAH ICA revisions and mandatory Part 21 changes "as soon as possible".
- Reliability programme: required flag if CMPA and AMP based on MSG or condition monitoring.
- MCF workflow (four scenarios with differing certificate requirements).
- Record reconstruction workflow for lost/destroyed records, with statement, submission to authority and acceptance status; conservative-method LLP back-to-birth handling with endorsement.
- Aircraft transfer/CAMO change: export full record set in an industry-standard format (e.g. Spec 2000 / ATA style) and printable form; track handover.
- Permanent withdrawal: switch retention clock to "at least 12 months" for specified items.
- Retention engine: per record type, compute earliest deletion date (36 months from last ATL entry; superseded plus 36 months; life of mod/LLP; 12 months after withdrawal). Never purge automatically without review; carry obligations to new holder.

### Integrity, IT and permissions (AMC M.A.305(e) is effectively a spec for the product)
- Search and production of status reports.
- Export to industry-wide data format and/or printing; ability to print a paper version of any retained record.
- Role-based access preventing unauthorised alteration; immutable audit trail with traceability of amendments.
- Corrections must show the original entry (no destructive edits; strike-through/versioning).
- Backup updated within 24 hours of any entry, preferably remote, access-protected.
- Digitised records: store creation date of scan, one record per document, legible (signatures, stamps, dates), authenticity assurance that dates are not changed after creation, retrieval within reasonable time, protection against technological obsolescence.
- Accepted retention formats: original paper or electronic (via approved electronically signed form), paper copy, electronic copy, print of electronic, digitised paper, microfilm/scan. Paper may be disposed of once authority accepts the digitised system.
- Roles: owner, operator, CAMO/CAO staff (planning, records, airworthiness review), maintenance organisation (CRS issuer), certifying staff, PIC/commander (ATL entries, signatures, nil defect, pre-flight signature), pre-flight inspection personnel (training records), authority read access (present records on request).
- Group CAMO serving several AOCs: interoperable ATL and common data formats.

### Alerts
- AD/task/LLP/time-controlled component approaching or past due (per parameter).
- CRS not entered within 30 days.
- Deferred defect approaching MEL/CDL limit; repetitive defect detection.
- AMP annual review due; new DAH revision not yet assessed.
- Mass and balance report not updated after a mod affecting it.
- Records approaching retention end (review, not auto-delete).
- Missing Form 1 for LLP/time-controlled components where required; missing owner's acceptance statement for components fitted without Form 1.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM

- **AMC M.A.301(a)**: pre-flight inspection includes checking that no maintenance will "become due during the flight", which implies forecasting against planned flight duration. De-icing fluid residue may alternatively be controlled by scheduled AMP inspections/cleanings.
- **AMC M.A.301(b)**: for air carriers and CMPA, a continuous defect analysis system covering significant, repetitive, deferred/carried forward defects, and unscheduled removals/system performance. Distinguishes deferred (in service) from carried forward (in maintenance) defects.
- **AMC M.A.301(c)**: owner/CAO/CAMO must have a system to keep checks within AMP limits; postponement only via an authority-agreed procedure.
- **AMC M.A.301(f)**: ODs are the operator's responsibility; CAMO assesses airworthiness impact; process in an arrangement/common procedure.
- **GM M.A.301(i)**: four MCF scenarios determining whether flight is under the CofA (after certificate after incomplete maintenance, or after CRS) or under a permit to fly. Crew may be appointed and briefed by the maintenance organisation to collect in-flight data for the final CRS.
- **AMC M.A.302**: annual AMP review minimum; one AMP at a time; transfer check when changing; preface requirements; repetitive tasks from mods/repairs into AMP.
- **GM M.A.302(a)**: one AMP may cover several registrations with clear task effectivity.
- **AMC M.A.302(d)**: AMP normally based on MRB report/MPD/AMM; MRB cross-reference mandatory where MRB exists; associated reliability/health monitoring is part of the AMP; ALI escalation approved by the Agency, other escalations directly by the competent authority.
- **AMC M.A.302(g)**: reliability programme needed for MSG-based, condition-monitored, or AMPs lacking overhaul periods for all significant components; not needed for non-CMPA or AMPs with overhaul times for all significant components.
- **AMC M.A.304**: options when damage is beyond published data: replacement, TC holder/declarant/Part 21 organisation support, Agency approval.
- **GM M.A.305**: core definitions (LLP, time-controlled, current status, airworthiness limitation, in-service history, permanently withdrawn). Owner/CAMO need not keep incoming CoCs, batch numbers or task card sign-offs.
- **AMC M.A.305(b)1**: modular engine/propeller life tracking; APU cycles where relevant.
- **AMC M.A.305(c)1/2/3**: detailed field-level content of AD, mod/repair and AMP status (key for data model).
- **GM M.A.305(d) and (d)(2)**: tables of which records are needed per component class (LLP, hard time, on-condition, condition monitoring, ELA2 without Form 1). Off-wing maintenance on on-condition items needs the Form 1 kept; condition monitoring items generally do not. DAH-recommended component-level tasks that are not time-controlled need status but no Form 1 retention.
- **AMC M.A.305(e)**: IT system requirements (search, status, export in industry-wide format or print, access control, integrity and amendment traceability), backup within 24 hours, digitised record rules, lost record reconstruction.
- **AMC M.A.305(e)(1)**: 36-month ATL retention may be extended where another Part-M provision requires.
- **AMC M.A.305(e)(2)**: Form 1s and CoCs of parts used in a mod/repair are not substantiation data (kept by the maintenance organisation). Intermediate AD steps form part of the detailed records.
- **GM M.A.305(e)(2)**: worked meaning of "superseded but not shorter than 36 months".
- **AMC M.A.305(e)(3)**: no need for Form 1 for every LLP installation/removal in the history; conservative methods for missing history acceptable if supporting documents are endorsed; consider DAH guidance on reconstruction.
- **AMC M.A.305(f)**: outsourcing record storage does not transfer responsibility.
- **AMC M.A.306(a)**: 5-section ATL model; abbreviated "Part-145 release to service" CRS.
- **AMC M.A.306(b)**: ATL can be paper, computer or mixed; computer systems need safeguards against unauthorised alteration.
- **GM1 M.A.306(b)**: interoperable ATL for group CAMOs.
- **AMC M.A.307(a)**: dry lease under 6 months generally not a permanent transfer.
