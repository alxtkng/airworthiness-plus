## 1. Scope (every IR point)

### Subpart D — Maintenance standards
| Point | Plain summary |
|---|---|
| M.A.401 Maintenance data | Whoever maintains an aircraft must have access to and use only applicable, current maintenance data (incl. for mods and repairs); (b) lists what counts as maintenance data; (c) data must be current and readily available, and a work card / worksheet system must be used (transcribe the data accurately or reference the exact task). |
| M.A.402 Performance of maintenance | For anyone other than a Part-145 organisation: be qualified; clean, organised work area; use the methods, tools, equipment and materials in the data; calibrate tools to an officially recognised standard; respect environmental limits; proper facilities; minimise multiple and repeated errors; use an error-capturing method after any critical maintenance task; do a general verification at the end (no tools or loose items left, panels refitted). |
| M.A.403 Aircraft defects | A defect that seriously hazards flight safety must be rectified before further flight. Only specified certifying staff or authorised persons decide whether it does (except when the MEL is used by the pilot or certifying staff). Other defects: fix as soon as practicable, within maintenance-data or MEL limits. Any defect not fixed before flight must be recorded in the M.A.305 record system or the M.A.306 tech log. |

### Subpart E — Components
| Point | Plain summary |
|---|---|
| M.A.501 Classification and installation | Five component categories: (1) serviceable, released on EASA Form 1 or equivalent and marked per Part 21 Subpart Q; (2) unserviceable; (3) unsalvageable; (4) standard parts; (5) raw and consumable material. Install only if in satisfactory condition, in one of these categories, and specified by the applicable maintenance data. |
| M.A.502 Component maintenance | Component maintenance must be done by a Subpart F, Part-145 or Part-CAO organisation, with derogations: (b) on-aircraft by an aircraft maintenance org or M.A.801(b)(1) independent certifying staff (no Form 1, aircraft CRS instead); (c) on-engine/APU by an engine maintenance org; (d) 21.A.307(b)(2) components, which can also be done by the pilot-owner; (e) 21.A.307(b)(3)–(b)(6) components can be maintained by anyone and released with a "declaration of maintenance accomplished". |
| M.A.503 Life-limited parts and time-controlled components | Installed LLPs and time-controlled components must not exceed the approved limit in the AMP and ADs. At expiry the part must be removed for maintenance, or for disposal if it is an LLP. |
| M.A.504 Segregation of components | Unserviceable and unsalvageable components must be segregated from serviceable items. Unsalvageable items cannot re-enter the supply system unless the life limit is extended or a repair is approved under 748/2012. |

## 2. Obligations (who, what, when)

### Persons and organisations performing maintenance (Subpart D)
- **Maintenance data (M.A.401)**: the person or organisation maintaining must have access to and use only applicable current data, keep it current and "readily available for use when required", and run a work card / worksheet system.
  - AMC: data must be "in close proximity" to the aircraft or component; there must be enough computer terminals for the size of the work programme (unless paper copies can be printed); data is kept current by subscribing to amendment schemes, checking that amendments arrive and monitoring their status.
- **Performance (M.A.402)**, for everyone except Part-145 organisations: items (a)–(i), see Scope. The error-capturing method is mandatory "after the performance of any critical maintenance task".
  - AMC: work is done by CRS-authorised persons or under their supervision, and the supervisor must be readily available. The supervisor must ensure supervised staff are trained or experienced, and that specialised tasks (e.g. welding) are done by people qualified to a recognised standard.
  - AMC M.A.402(g): sign each task off only after it is complete; group tasks so critical steps are visible; supervised work (temporary staff, trainees) must be checked and signed off by an authorised person; plan different people for identical tasks on different systems, and if only one person is available, use reinspection.
- **Defects (M.A.403)**:
  - A defect that seriously hazards flight safety must be rectified **before further flight**.
  - The decision (whether it is serious, what action, what can be deferred) can only be made by: certifying staff under M.A.801(b)(1) (independent), Subpart F, Part-145 or Part-CAO certifying staff, or a person authorised under M.A.801(c). The decision must use M.A.401 data. Exception: when the MEL is used by the pilot or certifying staff.
  - A non-serious defect must be rectified "as soon as practicable, after the date the aircraft defect was first identified and within any limits specified in the maintenance data or the MEL".
  - A defect not rectified before flight must be recorded in the M.A.305 records or the M.A.306 tech log.
  - AMC: assess cause and potential hazard to find the root cause. Tell the crew about deferred defects before they arrive at the aircraft if possible. Transfer deferred defects to worksheets at the next appropriate check; if still not fixed, re-enter them on a new deferred defect record sheet and **keep the original defect date**. Order the parts on a priority basis.

### Components (Subpart E)
- Install a component, standard part or material only if it is in satisfactory condition, in an M.A.501(a) category, and specified in the applicable maintenance data (M.A.501(b)).
  - GM: an incoming physical inspection must be done **before installation**, by M.A.801(b)(1), (b)(2) or (c) persons or by the organisation.
  - GM: check part number, modification status and limitations against the data for that specific aircraft or component. Outside an organisation, the individual is responsible for this; Subpart F and CAO organisations need a procedure.
- **Component maintenance (M.A.502)**:
  - Default: done by a Subpart F, Part-145 or Part-CAO organisation.
  - (b) Fitted to the aircraft: an aircraft maintenance org or M.A.801(b)(1) staff may do it, using aircraft maintenance data (component data only if the competent authority agrees). The component may be removed temporarily for access, unless the removal itself creates extra maintenance. No Form 1; released under the M.A.801 aircraft CRS.
  - (c) Fitted to an engine or APU: an engine maintenance org may do it (same conditions).
  - (d) 21.A.307(b)(2) / 21L.A.193(b)(2) components on the aircraft or temporarily removed for access: may be done by an aircraft maintenance org, M.A.801(b)(1) certifying staff or an M.A.801(b)(2) pilot-owner. No Form 1; aircraft CRS.
  - (e) 21.A.307(b)(3)–(b)(6) components: may be done by the organisations in (a) **or by any person or organisation**, released with a "declaration of maintenance accomplished".
  - AMC: competent-authority agreement for (b)/(c) comes from the overseeing authority (or the State of Registry for independent certifying staff), and only for simple component maintenance.
- **LLPs and time-controlled components (M.A.503)**: must not exceed the AMP or AD limit (except as M.A.504(b)). When the limit expires: remove for maintenance, or for disposal if an LLP.
- **Segregation (M.A.504)**: segregate unserviceable and unsalvageable items. Unsalvageable items must not re-enter supply unless the life is extended or a repair is approved.
  - AMC: independent certifying staff must send unserviceable parts, with the owner's agreement, to a maintenance organisation for controlled storage. Alternatively, custody may pass to the owner/lessee after the part has been identified as unserviceable, but only if the transfer is reflected in the aircraft, engine or component logbook.
  - AMC: unsalvageable parts must be kept in the secure location, mutilated before disposal, or marked unsalvageable for legitimate non-flight use (or the P/N or data plate removed, or a disposal record kept).

### Subpart F maintenance organisation
- **No activity without approval** (M.A.603(a)).
- **Application** on EASA Form 2 or equivalent (AMC M.A.602).
- **MOM**: the accountable manager signs the compliance statement; the MOM and amendments are approved by the competent authority; minor amendments by indirect approval.
- **Accountable manager**: has corporate authority to finance and carry out all customer-required maintenance. **Nominated person(s)** (M.A.606(b)) report to the AM.
  - AMC M.A.606(c): nominated persons need "five years aviation experience of which at least three years should be practical maintenance experience". Type knowledge must be at least equivalent to Part-66 Appendix III Level 1 General Familiarisation.
- **Staffing**: temporarily subcontracted staff only for higher than normal workload, and "only for personnel not issuing a certificate of release to service".
- **Qualification of personnel** in maintenance and airworthiness reviews must be "demonstrated and recorded".
  - AMC: assess competence before unsupervised work; provide recorded initial and recurrent training.
- **NDT**: qualified to EN 4179 level 1, 2 or 3 (colour contrast dye penetrant is excluded from the IR requirement). The MOM must contain NDT qualification procedures. Borescope and coin tapping are inspections, not NDT, but still need training and competence procedures.
- **Limited certification authorisation for the aircraft commander** (M.A.606(h)), when supporting commercial operators:
  - (1) for a repetitive pre-flight AD that says flight crew may do it, or (2) when operating away from a supported location;
  - sufficient practical training is required;
  - AMC: the commander holds an ATPL or CPL; regulatory, task and procedural training are required; "The authorisation should have a finite life of **twelve months** subject to satisfactory recurrent training on the applicable aircraft type."
- **Airworthiness review staff (ELA1, not commercial; M.A.606(i))**, all of:
  - a certifying staff authorisation for the aircraft;
  - "at least **three years** of experience as certifying staff";
  - independence from, or overall authority over, the CAM process for that aircraft;
  - knowledge of Part-M Subpart C or Part-ML Subpart C, and of the organisation's ARC procedures;
  - formally accepted by the competent authority after a supervised review;
  - "at least **one** airworthiness review in the last **twelve-month** period" (recency).
- **Certifying staff privileges (M.A.607(a))**: the organisation must ensure they meet 66.A.20(b) (recency and experience) or national law, and have adequate understanding of the type and procedures.
  - AMC: assess competence by supervised work (could be "a few weeks"); written confirmation from a previous approved organisation is acceptable; hold copies of qualification and recent-experience documents.
- **One-off certification authorisation (M.A.607(b))**: for an unforeseen AOG away from main base with no appropriate certifying staff. Issued to (1) an employee with type qualifications on similar technology, or (2) any person with "not less than **three years** maintenance experience" holding a valid ICAO licence rated for the type, if no appropriately approved organisation is at that location (evidence held on file).
  - **Report to the competent authority "within seven days"** after issue.
  - Any maintenance that could affect flight safety must be re-checked.
- **Tools (M.A.608)**: hold the tools needed for day-to-day work; demonstrate access to occasional ones; calibrate to an officially recognised standard and keep calibration records and the standard used; inspect, classify and segregate incoming parts.
  - AMC: keep a control register (including approved personal tools); label every tool with the next due date; calibration intervals follow the manufacturer's instructions unless results justify otherwise.
- **Data (M.A.609)**: customer-provided data is held and used only while the work is in progress.
  - AMC: if the organisation relies on customer data, its scope is limited to the contracted individual aircraft unless it holds its own complete data set.
- **Work order (M.A.610)**: a written work order must be agreed **before the commencement** of maintenance.
- **CRS**: aircraft CRS per M.A.801 / ML.A.801. Component CRS per M.A.802 / ML.A.802, normally an EASA Form 1.
- **Records (M.A.614)**: see section 3. Keep **3 years** from release, and ARC records **3 years** from issue. Give copies to the owner or operator.
  - AMC: back up computer records "at least within **24 hours** of any maintenance".
- **Organisational review (M.A.616)**: "on a regular basis". The frequency is defined by the organisation (AMC).
- **Changes (M.A.617)**: notify the competent authority **before** the change. Personnel changes not known in advance must be notified "at the earliest opportunity".
  - AMC: without adequate notification "the approval would automatically be suspended".
- **Validity (M.A.618)**: valid "until **24 March 2022**" (EU sunset of Subpart F approvals; the EU replacement is Part-CAO, Annex Vd). The certificate must be returned to the competent authority on surrender or revocation.

## 4. Defined terms

- **Applicable maintenance data** (M.A.401(b)): (1) requirements, procedures, standards or information issued by the competent authority or the Agency; (2) applicable ADs; (3) ICA and other instructions from the TCH, STCH, DDC declarant or other Part 21 / Part 21 Light publishers; (4) component manufacturer instructions acceptable to the DAH or declarant; (5) data issued under 145.A.45(d).
- **Data "available"** (AMC M.A.401(c)): "available in close proximity to the aircraft or component being maintained".
- **Critical maintenance tasks** (AMC1 M.A.402(h)), mainly: flight control installation, rigging and adjustment; stability control systems (autopilot, fuel transfer); propulsive force (installation of engines, propellers, rotors); overhaul, calibration or rigging of engines, propellers, transmissions and gearboxes. GM sources for identifying them: DAH information, accident reports, incidents, occurrence reporting, flight data analysis, audits, normal operations monitoring, training feedback, information exchange.
- **Independent inspection**: "one possible error-capturing method", performed by an **independent qualified person** on a task done by an **authorised person**.
  - **Authorised person**: performs or supervises the task, takes full responsibility, and issues the CRS.
  - **Independent qualified person**: attests satisfactory completion; does not issue the CRS and needs no certification privileges.
- **Reinspection**: the authorised person also acts as the independent qualified person. Only for critical tasks in **unforeseen** circumstances when only one person is available. The circumstances are not unforeseen if no suitable independent person had been assigned.
- **Officially recognised standard**: "those standards established or published by an official body, being either a natural or legal person, and which are widely recognised by the air transport sector as constituting good practice."
- **Component categories** (M.A.501(a)): serviceable (Form 1 or equivalent), unserviceable, unsalvageable, standard parts, material (raw and consumable).
- **Unsalvageable** (AMC1 M.A.501(a)(3)): non-repairable defects (visible or not); cannot conform to design; irreversible unacceptable modification or rework; LLPs at or beyond life **or with missing or incomplete records**; airworthiness cannot be restored after extreme forces, heat or environment; AD conformity cannot be accomplished; records or traceability to the manufacturer cannot be retrieved.
- **Unserviceable** reasons (GM1 M.A.501(a)(2)): service life limit expired; AD or other mandated requirements not complied with; information missing to determine status or eligibility; defects or malfunctions; installed on an aircraft in an accident or incident likely to affect it.
- **Standard parts** (AMC1 M.A.501(a)(4)): made in complete compliance with a published industry, Agency, authority or government specification (design, manufacture, test, acceptance, identification), e.g. NAS, AN, SAE, JEDEC, ANSI, EN. Designated by the TCH or declarant via a standard parts manual or a parts catalogue reference. AMC2: for sailplanes and powered sailplanes, non-required instruments and equipment under CS 22.1301(b) that are not a safety hazard (e.g. electrical variometers, ball-type slip indicators, TE probes, final glide calculators, nav computers, loggers, bug-wipers, anti-collision systems). Oxygen equipment is not a standard part.
- **Consumable material**: "any material which is only used once" (lubricants, cements, compounds, paints, dyes, sealants). **Raw material**: "any material that requires further work to make it into a component part" (metals, plastics, wood, fabric).
- **Declaration of maintenance accomplished**: a certificate "in any shape/form" for 21.A.307(b)(3)–(b)(6) components, issued without any approval.
- **Secure location under the control of an approved maintenance organisation**: managed by the organisation, prevents reuse or tampering, may be off the main site, and is identified in procedures.
- **Mutilation**: makes the part permanently unusable, and it cannot be reworked or camouflaged. Acceptable methods: grinding, burning, removing a major lug, permanent distortion, cutting holes by torch or saw, melting, sawing into many small pieces, other accepted methods. Less effective: stamping or vibro-etching, paint, small distortions, tags or markings, small holes, sawing in two pieces only.
- **Accountable manager**: normally the CEO with overall (incl. financial) responsibility. May serve more than one organisation and need not be technical.
- **Sufficient staff**: employs or contracts directly, "even on a volunteer basis". **Temporarily sub-contracted**: employed by another organisation and contracted to this one.
- **NDT** (AMC M.A.606(f)): testing specified by the TCH in the maintenance data for in-service products. **Particular NDT**: dye penetrant, magnetic particle, eddy current, ultrasonic, radiographic (X-ray, gamma). Borescope and coin tapping = **non-destructive inspection**, not NDT (not in class rating D1).
- **Adequate understanding** (AMC M.A.607): training and experience on the product type and procedures, so the person understands how it functions and the common defects and their consequences.
- **Appropriately rated** (AMC M.A.613 para 2.3): having a class rating for the component type or for the product it may be installed in.
- **Associated maintenance data** (AMC M.A.614(c)): specific information such as repair and mod data. The full AMM, CMM or IPC need not be retained, but records should refer to the data revision status.
- **Specialised services**: NDT, surface treatment, heat treatment, welding, fabrication of parts for minor repairs and mods, etc. **Under the control of** the Subpart F organisation: capability vetted, instructions and data provided, work recorded in the Subpart F records, deviations notified.
- **Level 1 finding**: "significant non-compliance ... which lowers the safety standard and seriously endangers flight safety". **Level 2 finding**: "non-compliance ... which may lower the safety standard and may endanger flight safety".
- **Indirect approval**: approval of minor MOM amendments through an approved procedure.
- **C ratings** (AMC M.A.603(a), ATA Spec 2200 chapters):

## 5. Software implications

### Data objects
- **MaintenanceDataDocument**: type (AMP/MPD/MRBR, AMM incl. ALS, SRM, SSID, CPCP, SB, SL, WDM, TSM, IPC, CMM, EMM, NDT manual, AD, authority publication, 145.A.45(d) data); issuer (TCH, STCH, ETSO holder, DAH, DDC declarant, component manufacturer, authority); acceptable-to-DAH flag (for component manufacturer data); applicability (type, serial, component P/N); revision number and date; amendment subscription status; last-checked date; customer-provided flag (with an access window limited to an active work order).
- **WorkOrder** (M.A.610): requester (owner, operator or CAMO), organisation, scope, agreed date (must be before work starts), linked logbook defect entry option.
- **WorkCard / TaskCard**: task reference, data document and **revision status**, stages (disassembly, accomplishment, reassembly, test), performer(s) per stage, supervisor, sign-off timestamps (only allowed after completion), critical task flag, error-capture method (independent inspection or reinspection), independent inspector identity, date and details, general verification (tools and panels) checkbox.
- **Defect**: date first identified (**immutable**, carried across re-entries), description, assessed-by (must hold a qualifying role), hazard assessment (serious or not), MEL item reference and category or data limit, deferral limit date / hours / cycles, root cause notes, rectification action, parts on order (priority), status, crew-notified flag, transferred-to-check reference, re-entry history.
- **Component**: P/N, S/N, modification status, category (`serviceable`, `unserviceable`, `unsalvageable`, `standard_part`, `material`), release document (Form 1 or equivalent type, other foreign release, declaration of maintenance accomplished, 21.A.307(b)(x) basis), Part 21 Subpart Q marking, life-limited / time-controlled flags, limits from AMP and ADs (hours, cycles, landings, calendar, storage or shelf life), life used, time since overhaul, maintenance history record, AD/mod/repair status, accident or incident exposure flag, location / custody (store, quarantine, owner custody with logbook reference), installed-on (aircraft, engine, APU).
- **UnserviceableTag**: the fields in GM1 M.A.501(a)(2).
- **MaterialBatch / StandardPartLot**: specification or standard, P/N, batch number, quantity, manufacturer source, supplier source, conformity statement, storage conditions, shelf-life expiry.
- **ReleaseCertificate (EASA Form 1)**: blocks incl. 11 (status/work), 12 (remarks with the para 2.4 contents), 14b (signature); limitation statement; can be system-generated (M.A.613(b)). Block numbering follows the Form 1 itself (in the Part-M appendices / Part 21).
- **DeclarationOfMaintenanceAccomplished**: basic details, completion date, issuer identity; component eligibility (first installed new under M.A.502(e)).
- **Disposal record**: mutilation method, marked unsalvageable, P/N removed, non-flight-use disposal record, owner agreement.
- **Organisation (Subpart F)**: approval reference, certificate (Appendix V), class/rating scope (Appendix IV, C1–C22, D1 etc.), locations, ELA2 facility types, MOM with versions, approval type (direct or indirect), accountable manager, nominated persons, subcontracted specialised service providers with qualifications, validity (EU: hard end date 24 Mar 2022), status (active, surrendered, revoked, suspended).
- **Person / Staff**: role (accountable manager, nominated person, certifying staff, ARS, mechanic, NDT level 1/2/3 to EN 4179, temporary subcontracted), Part-66 licence, pilot licence (ATPL/CPL for limited authorisation), training (basic, type, recurrent, specialised), experience, date of birth, authorisation reference, scope, first issue date, expiry date, documents on file.
- **Authorisations**: standard certifying; limited commander authorisation (12-month validity, recurrent training); one-off authorisation (event-based, 7-day report deadline, re-check required); ARS authorisation (recency: 1 review per 12 months).
- **Tool**: ID, type, owner (organisation or personal), calibration interval and standard, last calibration date, next due date, status (serviceable, unserviceable, overdue), occasional-access flag, verified equivalent reference.
- **OrganisationalReview**: planned frequency, scope, reviewers, findings (level 1 or 2), corrective actions and due dates.
- **ChangeNotification** (M.A.617): change type (name, location, additional location, AM, nominated person, facilities, tools, procedures, scope, staff), proposed date, notified date (must be before the change; for unforeseen personnel changes, at the earliest opportunity), authority response.

### Workflows and rules
- **Defect triage**: only users with qualifying roles (M.A.801(b)(1) independent certifying staff, Subpart F/145/CAO certifying staff, M.A.801(c) authorised persons) can classify a defect as non-hazardous or deferred, unless the deferral is under the MEL (a pilot can apply the MEL). A serious defect blocks the aircraft (no flight). A deferred defect needs a limit from the maintenance data or MEL and is logged to the tech log or CAW record. On carry-over at a check, re-enter it while keeping the original identified date.
- **Pre-installation check**: block installation unless the component category is valid (serviceable, standard part or material), the P/N and mod status are in the IPC or data for that aircraft, the release document type is acceptable, shelf life is in date, and the incoming inspection is recorded. Reject unreadable or unsatisfactory declarations of maintenance accomplished.
- **LLP / time-controlled tracking**: alerts when nearing the AMP or AD limit; at expiry, force a removal task (for an LLP: disposal; a time-controlled part goes to maintenance). An LLP with missing or incomplete records is classed unsalvageable.
- **Component status state machine**: serviceable → installed → removed (serviceable or unserviceable) → quarantine (unserviceable, segregated) → maintenance → serviceable (new Form 1), or → unsalvageable → mutilated / marked for non-flight use / disposed. Unsalvageable can only go back to serviceable with an approved life extension or repair (M.A.504(b)).
- **Release document routing**: maintenance under M.A.502(b)/(d) gives an aircraft CRS with no Form 1. M.A.502(e) gives a declaration. Off-aircraft maintenance by an approved org gives a Form 1 plus a separate installation CRS (AMC M.A.613 para 1). Fabricated parts (M.A.603(c)) get no Form 1 and stay in-house only.
- **Form 1 for stored, used or parted-out components** (AMC M.A.613 para 2): checklists per case (new/unused, removed serviceable, withdrawn aircraft, unapproved maintainer, FAA/TCCA release, accident or incident with a specific work order referenced in block 12).
- **Critical tasks**: flag at task-card level (default categories per AMC1). Require an independent inspection sign-off by a different user before the CRS can be signed. Allow single-person reinspection only with an "unforeseen circumstances" reason and only if no independent qualified person was assigned. Show checklist items: assembly and locking, full and free movement, cable tension and secondary stops, correct sense, interactions, software version and compatibility.
- **Identical tasks on multiple systems**: warn if the same person is assigned identical tasks on different systems (AMC M.A.402(g)).
- **Supervised work**: tasks by trainees or temporary staff need sign-off by an authorised person. Temporary subcontracted staff can never sign a CRS.
- **CRS permission**: only staff with a current authorisation covering the scope (type, rating, location), meeting 66.A.20(b) recency, and with the organisation's tools, data and facilities available (M.A.615). Customer-provided data only within the contracted aircraft scope.
- **One-off authorisation**: create with an issue date; start a 7-day countdown to the authority report; create a mandatory re-check task for safety-affecting work.
- **Limited commander authorisation**: 12-month expiry; recurrent training tracked; limited to the listed simple tasks or a specified repetitive pre-flight AD.
- **ARS eligibility**: computed checks (certifying authorisation for the type, ≥3 years as certifying staff, independence, authority acceptance, ≥1 review in the last 12 months); expiry alert. ARC issue only if the organisation's approval includes the ELA1 airworthiness review privilege and the aircraft is ELA1 and not in commercial operations.
- **Tool calibration**: due-date alerts; block issuing a tool that is overdue or unserviceable.
- **Records**: retain **3 years** after release (work records) or after ARC issue. Auto-send the CRS and detailed records to the owner or operator. Back up within **24 hours** of any maintenance, stored off-site. Audit trail and role-restricted access. Records remain legible for the full retention period. Closure workflow: export the last 3 years to the last owner or customer.
- **Change management**: pre-notification gate for M.A.617 changes; MOM revision workflow with direct or indirect approval paths.
- **Organisational review**: scheduler using the organisation-defined frequency; findings tracked as level 1 or level 2 (levels from M.A.619; corrective action periods are in M.A.619(c), next chunk); corrective actions owned by the aircraft maintenance manager or workshop manager.
- **Unserviceable custody**: record logbook entries when custody passes to the owner/lessee.

### Permissions (roles)
Owner / pilot-owner (limited, M.A.801(b)(2) and M.A.502(d)), independent certifying staff (M.A.801(b)(1)), authorised person (M.A.801(c)), organisation certifying staff, independent qualified person, mechanic, supervised trainee or temporary worker, ARS, NDT technician (level 1/2/3), accountable manager, nominated persons (aircraft maintenance manager, workshop manager), stores (incoming inspection, quarantine), competent authority (read access to staff records on request).

## 7. Notable AMC and GM (practical effect)

1. **Maintenance data revision status must be recorded** on work cards and in records (AMC M.A.401(c) point 4, AMC M.A.614(c)). An organisation does not need to retain full AMMs or CMMs, only specific data such as repair and mod data.
2. **Component manufacturer data** counts as acceptable to the DAH when it is referenced with the ICA or listed by the DAH (GM1 M.A.401(b)(4)). On-wing engine, APU or component work may also use aircraft data.
3. **Independent inspection framework** (AMC1/AMC2 M.A.402(h)) defines the critical-task categories, inspector qualifications and reinspection limits.
   - Outside a Subpart F organisation, the independent inspector can be any Part-66 licence holder or **a pilot licensed for the type**, which matters for owner-maintained GA.
   - The inspector must not be shown how to inspect after the work is finished.
4. **Deferred defects keep their original date** when re-entered after a check (AMC M.A.403(d)). Deferral age and limits therefore run from the first identification.
5. **Form 1 equivalents and cut-off dates** (AMC1 M.A.501(a)(1)): JAA Form One dates 28 Nov 2004, 28 Sep 2005 and 28 Sep 2008. A stored item without a Form 1 needs a new Form 1 from an approved organisation before installation (AMC1 M.A.501(a)(1)(b)).
6. **Incoming inspection before installation** (GM1 M.A.501(b)): condition, shelf life, packaging (ATA 300, ESD), plugs and caps, and release certificate validity per 21.A.307. Tape on connectors can leave residues.
7. **LLP with missing or incomplete records = unsalvageable** (AMC1 M.A.501(a)(3)(d)). Lost back-to-birth traceability is an airworthiness outcome, not just admin, so the software should warn strongly on record gaps.
8. **Owner custody of unserviceable parts** is allowed if logged in the aircraft, engine or component logbook (AMC1 M.A.504(a)). Independent engineers should otherwise send parts to an organisation for controlled storage.
9. **Component removal and installation is aircraft maintenance**, not component maintenance (AMC M.A.502).
10. **M.A.502(b)/(c) component maintenance with component data** needs authority agreement and only for simple work (AMC M.A.502(b)(c)).
11. **ELA2 facilities**: no hangar is required. Field or non-aviation premises are allowed if the MOM describes the facility types, scope, tooling and permitted conditions, and the organisational review samples compliance (AMC M.A.605(a)).
12. **Shelf-life control and identity tags** in stores; a documented procedure for deciding the fate of unserviceable items (maintain, store or mutilate), with traceability of the decision (AMC M.A.605(c)).
13. **Nominated persons**: 5 years aviation experience including 3 years practical maintenance; type knowledge at least Part-66 Level 1 (AMC M.A.606(c)).
14. **Limited commander authorisation**: ATPL or CPL holders, simple tasks (lights, cowlings, role changes, de-icing fluid residue checks); **12-month** validity (AMC M.A.606(h)(2)).
15. **Certifying staff record field list** (AMC M.A.607(c)) maps directly to a staff database schema. Access must be minimal and the authority must have access on request.
16. **Tool labelling with the next due date** and a register of precision tools (AMC M.A.608(b)). The interval can differ from the manufacturer's if results justify it.
17. **Customer-provided data limits the approval scope** to the contracted aircraft (AMC M.A.609).
18. **A work order can be a logbook entry** specifying the defect (AMC M.A.610).
19. **AMC M.A.613(a)** sets the detailed process for issuing a Form 1 to stored, used, removed, parted-out or accident-exposed components, including the block 11, 12 and 14b content.
    - FAA 8130-3 and TCCA releases from non-EASA-approved shops are accepted with alleviated checks (ADs, unapproved repairs or mods, condition).
    - An internal release can be used instead of a Form 1 for components used within the same organisation.
    - A component maintained off the aircraft needs two CRSs (component and installation).
20. **Computer records**: at least one backup, updated within **24 hours** of any maintenance; terminals protected against unauthorised alteration; paper must be robust; records legible throughout retention (AMC M.A.614(a)). The aircraft record should list all serialised and significant components for traceability.
21. **Subcontracted specialised services** (NDT, heat treatment, welding, etc.) need no approval but must be vetted and listed in the MOM. The CRS is always issued under the Subpart F organisation's reference (AMC M.A.615(b)).
22. **Temporary lack of tools** does not require removing a type from the approval if the organisation commits to re-acquiring them (GM M.A.615).
23. **Organisations above 10 maintenance staff** should use Part-145-style MOE and quality system structures (AMC M.A.604, AMC M.A.616).
24. **Unnotified changes** lead to automatic suspension (AMC M.A.617).

## UK divergence flags (guesses, not verified)

- **M.A.618 sunset (24 March 2022)** came from Reg. (EU) 2021/700, adopted after the Brexit transition ended on 31 Dec 2020. I suspect UK retained Part-M still treats Subpart F approvals as unlimited in duration, and UK CAA still issues M.A. Subpart F approvals. I also think the UK may not have implemented Part-CAO in the same way, so references to Annex Vd (Part-CAO) in M.A.403, M.A.501, M.A.502 and others may be absent or different in UK text. Check the UK CAA consolidated text.
- **Post-2020 EU amendments probably absent or different in the UK**: Reg. 2021/1963 (M.A.403 wording incl. Part-CAO), 2022/1360 (Part 21 Light, declarations of design compliance, 21L.* references in M.A.401 and M.A.501), 2023/989 (M.A.502 wording), and ED Decisions 2021/009/R and 2023/013/R AMC/GM. UK AMC/GM is published separately by the UK CAA and may lag or differ.
- **Terminology**: "EASA Form 1" is likely "CAA Form 1" (UK); "EASA Form 2" is likely "CAA Form 2"; "the Agency" and "Member State" become the CAA / UK; "aircraft registered in a Member State" becomes the UK register. EU bilateral agreements become UK bilaterals (e.g. UK-US BASA, UK-Canada). The acceptance of EASA Form 1s in the UK (and the UK CAA's treatment of transitional periods) should be handled as configurable data.
- **Part-ML (2019/1383)** applied before the end of the transition period and is, I believe, in UK retained law. Its ELA1 ARC privileges for Subpart F organisations (M.A.606(i), M.A.615(e)) should therefore apply in the UK too, but confirm.
- **The UK has made its own amendments** (e.g. via UK SIs and CAA decisions on Part-ML and Part-66), so retention periods, NDT standards and authorisation rules should be data-driven per jurisdiction rather than hard-coded.
