## 2. Structure and Numbers

### Introductory rules (quoted or near-verbatim)
- **Purpose:** "The following aircraft type ratings should be used to ensure a common standard throughout the Member States." If a Member State needs a TR that is not on the list, it should send the information to EASA through the "Contact us" webpage.
- **Annex I aircraft:** the tables "may erroneously contain aircraft models that fall within the definition of Annex I aircraft of Regulation (EU) 2018/1139". Part-66 does not apply to those aircraft.
- **TR covering several models or variants:** endorsement "does not automatically imply that the AML holder has acquired the appropriate knowledge on each model/variant". The AML holder and/or the maintenance organisation that contracts or employs them is responsible for verifying "that the model/variant has been adequately covered by the TR course or gained experience and is up to date". See AMC 66.A.20(b)3 and AMC 145.A.35(a).
- **Implementation timing:** licensing authorities "should implement the new type rating list **within 6 months** after publication of this Decision".
  - During that period the old TRs may still be endorsed.
  - New applications for TRs that are no longer certified by EASA should not be accepted.
  - Licences with old TRs "shall be endorsed with the amended type ratings, whenever the licensing authority deems necessary or the holder requests it; however, **no later than the next renewal** of the licence."
  - Instructions for combined or split TRs are in "Details of the changes" in the explanatory note of the Decision. That note is not in this chunk.
- **Engine-change STCs:**
  - The list includes TRs from STCs that install a different engine: STCs approved by EASA, and STCs approved by Member States **before 2003** and grandfathered by EASA. STCs other than engine STCs are not considered.
  - Example: the JET AVIATION AG STC (LBA approved) replacing the GE CF 700 with the Honeywell TFE731 on the Fan Jet Falcon Series E gives the new rating "Falcon 20E (Honeywell TFE731)".
  - No new rating is listed where the STC engine is fitted to only part of the original airframe models, or comes from the same manufacturer and is very similar to the original. In those cases the result is an existing rating. Examples: Silverhawk PT6A-135A on the Beech C90/C90A/E90 falls under "Beech 90 Series (PWC PT6)"; Air-Service Wildgruber PT6A-20 to PT6A-27 falls under "De Havilland DHC-6 (PWC PT6)".
  - EASA has not received all Member State STC information, so "not all STCs are considered".
  - If the STC engine falls in a **different subcategory** (for example piston to turboprop), the new TR endorsement requires compliance with **all** relevant criteria: basic knowledge, experience, type training and OJT.
  - If a TR resulting from an STC is not yet defined, the competent authority shall contact EASA to agree a new TR.
- **Table conventions:**
  - Rows are sorted alphabetically by TC/STC holder, then TR endorsement, then model.
  - "TC Holder" is as shown in the TCDS (EASA, FAA or other) or the SAS. "STC Holder" is as shown in the STCDS.
  - "Aircraft with an SAS" marks an "orphan aircraft" or a GA aircraft from CIS countries.
- **Group 3 "Type of structure"** helps authorities work out what experience is needed to remove existing licence limitations. **Group 4 "Type of structure"** helps identify the required "L" subcategories.
  - "Wooden structure covered with fabric is considered to fall under wooden structure."
  - Mixed structures need both experiences. Example: metal tubing fuselage with wooden wings needs both "metal tube covered with fabric" and "wooden structure".
- **Group 3 MTOM column:**
  - **above 2 t** requires "a **B1.2 and B2 or B2L** licence";
  - **2 t and below** requires "a **B1.2 or B3 and B2 or B2L** licence".
- **NOTE column** can contain:
  - ELA1 or ELA2;
  - "OSD Approved" or "Pending OSD Approval" (an OSD-MCS exists or is still being approved; the OSD data is owned by the TCH);
  - the STC reference number;
  - "TC (or STC) not yet released".
- **OSD rule:** "Type training courses approved before the approval of the OSD-MCS shall include the OSD elements **within 2 years** after the OSD-MCS approval."
- **"TC not yet released":** the TR can already be defined. Initial training and licensing may start, and the TR may be used to approve type training courses and to endorse Part-66 licences. The **maintenance organisation rating** can be granted only after type certification (or STC approval).

### Table columns
- Group 1, 2a, 2b, 2c and airships: **TC Holder | Model | Com. des. (commercial designation) | Part-66 type rating endorsement | Note**. STC tables use "STC holder" in place of "TC Holder".
- Group 3: **TC Holder | Model | Type of structure | Part-66 type rating endorsement | Note | MTOM ≤2T | >2T** (an "X" in one column).

## 4. Defined Terms

- **Type rating (TR) endorsement:** the standardised title written on the AML (for example "Airbus A318/A319/A320/A321 (CFM56)"). It can cover several models/variants.
- **AML:** aircraft maintenance licence.
- **TC / STC / TCDS / STCDS / TCH:** type certificate, supplemental type certificate, their data sheets, and the type certificate holder.
- **SAS (specific airworthiness specification):** "Aircraft with an SAS" marks an orphan aircraft or a GA aircraft from CIS (former Soviet Union) countries.
- **Orphan aircraft:** an aircraft without an active TC holder. Example in the table: "WITHOUT TC HOLDER - ORPHANED (ex Fournier, René)" for RF 3, RF 4, RF 47 and RF 6B.
- **OSD-MCS:** operational suitability data for maintenance certifying staff. It is owned by the TCH. Existing type courses must include it within 2 years of its approval.
- **ELA1 / ELA2:** European Light Aircraft categories, flagged in the NOTE column.
- **MTOM:** maximum take-off mass. The 2 t threshold decides which licence (sub)categories apply in Group 3.
- **Type of structure** (Group 3/4): Metal, Wood (including wood covered with fabric), Composite, Metal tubing (covered with) Fabric, Pressurised, or combinations. It drives the experience needed to remove licence limitations (Group 3) and the "L" subcategories (Group 4).
- **Annex I aircraft** (Reg. (EU) 2018/1139): aircraft outside EASA scope, to which Part-66 does not apply.
- **OJT:** on-the-job training.
- **Group 1 / Subgroups 2a, 2b, 2c / Group 3 / Group 4:** Part-66 aircraft groupings (defined in 66.A.5; see cross-references). The table headings here: 2a single turboprop aeroplanes, 2b single turbine helicopters, 2c single piston helicopters, 3 piston aeroplanes, each "other than those in Group 1".

## 5. Software Implications (Airworthiness+)

1. **TR master reference table.** Hold a versioned table mapping {TC/STC holder, model, commercial designation} to the Part-66 TR endorsement string, with group/subgroup, note flags (ELA1/ELA2, OSD status and date, "TC/STC not yet released", STC number, SAS reference), Group 3 structure type and MTOM band. Store the ED Decision source with each table (2023/019/R or 2019/024/R) so changes can be tracked.
2. **Aircraft to TR resolution.** From an aircraft's TCDS model and fitted engine, plus any engine-change STC, work out the TR it falls under. The engine is often decisive (A320 CEO with CFM56 versus V2500; C172 with Continental versus Lycoming). Serial-number-dependent designations (Cessna 525, Challenger 604/605/650, Premier I/IA) and mod-dependent MTOM (DA42 with MÄM 42-659/42-678/OÄM 42-260) need serial-number and modification fields on the aircraft record.
3. **Engineer licence / experience logbook.**
   - Record the TR endorsements on each engineer's licence using the exact strings.
   - Log training and experience at **model/variant** level under each TR, so the "verify the model/variant has been adequately covered … and is up to date" duty (AMC 66.A.20(b)3, AMC 145.A.35(a)) can be shown and reported. Flag gaps where an engineer holds a TR but has no course or experience on the specific variant they are about to certify.
4. **Licence re-endorsement tracking.** Flag licences with superseded or legacy TR titles. Track the "no later than next renewal" deadline against the licence expiry date. Support mapping from old TR names to new ones (split or combined TRs).
5. **Group 3 limitation logic.**
   - Use the structure type to decide which structural experience (metal, wood, composite, metal tubing and fabric) an engineer needs to lift limitations. Mixed structures need both experiences.
   - Use the MTOM band to check licence category eligibility: >2 t needs B1.2 and B2/B2L; ≤2 t needs B1.2 or B3 and B2/B2L.
   - Group 4 "L" subcategory logic will need the Group 4 table from the next chunk.
6. **Training course compliance.** For type training providers, track OSD-MCS approval dates per TR and alert when a course approved before the OSD has to include OSD elements (2-year window).
7. **Part-145 / CAO scope checks.** A TR may exist for licensing before a TC is released, but the **organisation rating** cannot be granted until type certification. Keep these two concepts separate in the scope/ratings module.
8. **Engine-change STC rule.** If an STC moves an aircraft into a different subcategory (for example a piston single converted to turboprop under Subgroup 2a STCs), flag that the engineer needs full basic knowledge, experience, type training and OJT for the new TR.
9. **UK caveat.** UK operations use the UK-retained Part-66 and the UK CAA's AMC, which does not automatically take later EASA ED Decisions such as 2023/019/R. Source the UK type rating list separately and check endorsement strings against the UK CAA version. Keep EASA and UK lists side by side, with a jurisdiction field.
10. **Data ingestion.** The source tables are long (thousands of rows) and change through ED Decisions. Plan for import from the official PDF or Excel with manual validation, especially for the MTOM column, which flattened text cannot resolve reliably.
