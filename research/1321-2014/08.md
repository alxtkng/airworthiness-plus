## 2. Obligations (who, what, when; numbers quoted exactly)

### 145.1 Competent authority
- Principal place of business in an EU MS (Chicago Convention territory): authority designated by that MS (or another MS per Art 64 of 2018/1139, or EASA if reallocated per Arts 64/65).
- Principal place of business outside: EASA.

### 145.A.15 Application
- Applicant: apply on a form and in a manner established by the competent authority, covering Part-M, Part-ML and Part-145 (AMC1: EASA Form 2 or equivalent; one Form 2 can cover several certificates under 1321/2014).
- Initial applicant must supply: (1) results of a pre-audit performed by the organisation against Part-M, Part-ML and Part-145; (2) documentation demonstrating how it will comply.
- AMC2: submit draft documents at the earliest opportunity; initial certification or approval of changes cannot occur until completed documents received.

### 145.A.20 Terms of approval
- Scope of work specified in the MOE (145.A.70); comply with certificate terms of approval and MOE scope.

### 145.A.25 Facilities (organisation)
- Weather protection; segregated specialised workshops/bays.
- Base maintenance: hangars available and large enough for planned base maintenance. AMC: proof of tenancy if hangar not owned; projected "aircraft hangar visit plan", updated regularly. Weather protection = normal prevailing local weather expected "throughout any twelve month period". Floors sealed.
- AMC: subject to risk assessment and competent authority agreement, other facilities with equivalent protection may be used for certain base maintenance tasks; this does not remove the need for a base maintenance hangar at that location.
- Line maintenance: hangars not essential; recommended access to hangar for inclement weather.
- Office accommodation for management and certifying staff; AMC: an area to study maintenance instructions and complete records.
- Working environment: temperature without undue discomfort; dust kept down, seal susceptible systems if visible contamination; lighting adequate; noise not distracting (personal equipment if source cannot be controlled); special environmental conditions from maintenance data observed; line maintenance tasks must be suspended when temperature, moisture, hail, ice, snow, wind, light, dust deteriorate to unacceptable level until satisfactory conditions restored.
- Secure storage; serviceable segregated from unserviceable; storage per manufacturer instructions; access restricted to authorised personnel. AMC: clean, ventilated, constant dry temperature; strong racks; keep components packaged.

### 145.A.30 Personnel
- (a) Accountable manager (AM): corporate authority to finance and carry out all maintenance; ensure resources; establish and promote the safety policy (145.A.200(a)(2)); demonstrate a basic understanding of the Regulation.
- (b) AM nominates person(s) for maintenance management structure (ensuring work per MOE and procedures). Procedures must state who deputises in "lengthy absence".
- (c) AM nominates person(s) to manage the compliance monitoring function.
- (ca) AM nominates person(s) to manage safety management processes.
- (cb) Nominated persons responsible to and with direct access to the AM.
- (cc) Nominated persons demonstrate relevant knowledge, background, experience in aircraft/component maintenance and working knowledge of the Regulation.
- (d) Maintenance man-hour plan for sufficient qualified staff to plan, perform, supervise, inspect and monitor; plus a procedure to reassess intended work when actual staff availability falls below planned staffing for a shift or period.
  - AMC: at least half the staff performing maintenance in each workshop, hangar or flight line on any shift should be employed (not contracted); temporary increase of contracted proportion only with competent authority permission under an approved procedure.
  - AMC: man-hour plan reviewed "at least every 3 months" and updated when necessary.
  - AMC: "significant deviation" = "more than a 25% shortfall in available man-hours during a calendar month" for any function in 145.A.30(d); report through departmental manager to compliance monitoring manager and AM (may also go through internal safety reporting).
  - AMC: plan accounts for work outside Part-145 scope, planned absences, base maintenance hangar visit plan, component planned maintenance, compliance monitoring man-hours.
  - AMC: risk assessment/mitigation procedure when staff below plan or when contracted proportion is temporarily increased.
- (e) Establish and control competence of personnel involved in maintenance, airworthiness reviews, safety management and compliance monitoring, per a procedure and standard agreed with the competent authority; must include understanding of safety management principles, human factors and human performance.
  - AMC1: assess before unsupervised work commences; control continuously; applies to employed and contracted.
  - AMC4: initial safety (incl. HF) training before starting the job function unless competency assessment justifies no need; new, directly employed staff working under direct supervision may be trained "within 6 months after joining". Recurrent safety training of appropriate duration "in each 2-year period". Safety training procedures in MOE.
  - AMC3: compliance monitoring staff trained in compliance monitoring, audit techniques, reporting, recording. Recurrent training to use internal safety reporting information.
- (f) NDT personnel qualified per European or equivalent standard (AMC: EN 4179 Level 1, 2 or 3); other specialised tasks per officially recognised standards. Derogation: B1, B3 or L licensed staff under (g), (h)(1), (h)(2) may carry out/control colour contrast dye penetrant tests.
- (g) Line maintenance: aircraft-rated certifying staff B1, B2, B2L, B3, L as appropriate (Part-66 + 145.A.35). May also use task-trained staff with 66.A.20(a)(1) (cat A) and 66.A.20(a)(3)(ii) (B2 limited) privileges for minor scheduled line maintenance and simple defect rectification; these do not replace B-category staff.
- (h)(1) Base maintenance of complex motor-powered aircraft (CMPA): type-rated category C certifying staff plus sufficient type-rated B1 and B2 support staff.
  - (i) B1/B2 support staff ensure tasks/inspections are done to standard before cat C issues CRS.
  - (ii) Organisation maintains a register of B1 and B2 support staff.
  - (iii) Cat C ensures (i) is met, all customer-requested work accomplished, and assesses impact of work not carried out, either requiring accomplishment or agreeing with the operator to defer to another specified check or time limit.
- (h)(2) Base maintenance of non-CMPA: either B1/B2/B2L/B3/L aircraft-rated certifying staff, or cat C assisted by support staff.
- (i) Component certifying staff qualified per Article 5(6) and 145.A.35.
- (j) Derogations from Part-66:
  - (1) Base maintenance outside EU MS territory: staff may be qualified to local national regulations, conditions in Appendix IV.
  - (2) Line station outside EU MS territory: certifying staff qualified per national regs of the station State or of the organisation's principal place of business State, Appendix IV conditions.
  - (3) Repetitive pre-flight AD that states flight crew may carry it out: limited certification authorisation to the pilot based on flight crew licence; ensure sufficient practical training.
  - (4) Aircraft operated away from a supported location: limited certification authorisation to pilot, subject to sufficient practical training. AMC: pilot holds ATPL or CPL (Reg 1178/2011, and 2020/723 as applicable); MOE procedures for CAW regulation training, task training, procedural training; validity "limited to twelve months", renewable after satisfactory recurrent training.
  - (5) Unforeseen grounding away from main base with no certifying staff: contracted maintenance organisation may issue a one-off certification authorisation to (i) its employee with equivalent type authorisations on similar technology aircraft, or (ii) any person with "not less than five years maintenance experience" holding a valid ICAO AML rated for the type, if no Part-145 approved organisation at that location and evidence of experience/licence obtained and held on file. All such cases reported to the competent authority "within seven days after issuing". Maintenance that could affect flight safety must be re-checked by an appropriately approved organisation.
- (k) If issuing ARCs per ML.A.903, have ARS per 145.A.37.

### 145.A.35 Certifying and support staff
- (a) Ensure adequate understanding of relevant aircraft/components and organisation procedures; for certifying staff, before issue or reissue of authorisation.
- (b) Authorisation only within licence basic categories/subcategories and type ratings on the Part-66 licence (except cat A, and except 145.A.30(j) and 66.A.20(a)3(ii) cases); licence must remain valid throughout the authorisation's validity; holder must stay compliant with Part-66. AMC: may need to confirm licence validity with the issuing authority.
- (c) Certifying and support staff involved in "at least 6 months of actual relevant aircraft or component maintenance experience in any consecutive 2-year period" (exercised privileges and/or actually carried out maintenance on some of the type/group systems in the authorisation). AMC: interpret per AMC 66.A.20(b)2.
- (d) Sufficient recurrent training "in each 2-year period" (technologies, procedures, safety management, human factors). AMC: review the recurrent training "at least once in every 24-month period"; duration scaled to audit findings and hazards (days to weeks; component staff hours to days).
- (e) Recurrent training programme, with procedures ensuring compliance with this point and with Part-66.
- (f) Assess competency, qualifications and capability before issue or reissue of certification authorisation per MOE procedure (except 145.A.30(j)(5) one-off cases).
- (g) Issue authorisation clearly stating scope and limits once (a), (b), (d), (f) and where applicable (c) are met. Continued validity depends on continued compliance with (a), (b), (d) and where applicable (c).
- (h) Authorisation must be clear in scope to holder and any authorised person; code translation readily available if codes used.
- (i) Compliance monitoring person(s) (145.A.30(c)) responsible for issuing authorisations; may nominate others to issue or revoke per MOE procedure.
- (j) Provide holder a copy (documented or electronic).
- (k) Holder produces authorisation to an authorised person "within 24 hours".
- (l) Minimum age "21 years" for certifying and support staff.
- (m) Cat A holder: certification on a specific type only after relevant cat A task training by a Part-145 or Part-147 organisation appropriately approved; practical and theoretical; demonstrated by examination or workplace assessment.
- (n) B2 holder exercising 66.A.20(a)(3)(ii): cat A task training plus "6 months of documented practical experience covering the scope of the authorisation"; training, exam/assessment and experience all within the issuing maintenance organisation. AMC: privilege not transferable to another Part-145; new cat A tasks on an already-authorised type need task training and exam but not another 6 months; several types can share one 6-month period; new types need another 6 months unless similar per AMC 66.A.20(b)2; 6 months can be full or part time.

### 145.A.37 Airworthiness review staff (Part-ML ARCs)
- (a)(1) Experience in continuing airworthiness: "at least 1 year for sailplanes and balloons and of at least 3 years for all other aircraft".
- (a)(2) Hold certifying staff authorisation for the corresponding aircraft.
- (a)(3) Knowledge of Part-M Subpart C or Part-ML Subpart C. (a)(4) Knowledge of organisation's AR/ARC procedures.
- (b) Before authorisation: perform an airworthiness review under supervision of the competent authority or an already-authorised ARS; if satisfactory, competent authority formally accepts the person.
- (c) Recent experience. AMC1: either involved in CAW management "for at least 6 months in every 2-year period", or "conducted at least one airworthiness review in the last 12-month period". To restore validity: supervised AR (competent authority or, if accepted, another authorised ARS per approved procedure).
- GM1 process: verify (a) compliance; supervised AR; application for change requiring prior approval (145.A.85) with MOE amendment adding ARS to list (145.A.70(a)(6)); competent authority accepts candidate; competent authority approves MOE; organisation issues AR authorisation.

### 145.A.40 Equipment and tools
- Have and use necessary equipment and tools; manufacturer-specified tools unless alternative agreed via exposition procedures; permanently available unless infrequently used (cases detailed in exposition procedure); base maintenance: access equipment and inspection platforms/docking.
- Control and calibrate to officially recognised standard at a frequency ensuring serviceability and accuracy; keep calibration records and traceability to the standard.
- AMC: control register listing tools needing servicing/calibration (incl. agreed personal tools); labelling showing next inspection/service/calibration due and unserviceability; register of precision tooling with calibration records and standards used; intervals per manufacturer unless results justify different.

### 145.A.42 Components
- Classify into (i) serviceable with EASA Form 1 or equivalent and Part 21 Subpart Q marking (unless 21.A.307, 21L.A.193, M.A.502, ML.A.502 or Part-145 says otherwise); (ii) unserviceable; (iii) unsalvageable (life limit reached or non-repairable defect); (iv) standard parts with evidence of conformity traceable to standard, when specified in maintenance data; (v) material, raw and consumable, meeting specification with traceability, with documentation containing conformity statement plus manufacturing and supplier source.
- Procedures for acceptance; procedures to install only when satisfactory, meets (a), and the maintenance data specifies it.
- Restricted fabrication of parts for own use only, per exposition procedures.
- Components under 21.A.307(b)(2) / 21L.A.193(b)(2) installed only if the owner considers them eligible for their own aircraft.
- Segregate unserviceable and unsalvageable from serviceable; unsalvageable not re-entering supply system unless life limit extended or repair approved under Reg 748/2012.
- GM1 145.A.42(b): used components maintained by a CAO and released on EASA Form 1 cannot be installed on CMPA or aircraft used by an air carrier licensed under Reg 1008/2008.

### 145.A.45 Maintenance data
- Hold and use applicable current data (relevant to aircraft/components/processes in terms of approval and capability list). Customer-provided data held while work in progress (subject to 145.A.55(a)(3) retention).
- Procedure: inaccurate, incomplete or ambiguous data recorded in internal safety reporting (145.A.202) and notified to the author. AMC: notify "in a timely manner"; keep record of communications until author clarifies.
- Modify maintenance instructions only per MOE procedure; demonstrate equivalent or improved standards; inform author. Excludes engineering design of repairs/mods. AMC: practical demonstration to compliance monitoring personnel; risk assessment as needed; compliance monitoring approves; full traceability; modification clearly identified; only for (a) more practical/efficient, (b) original intent not achievable, (c) alternative tools/equipment. CDCCL-linked instruction changes are type certificate changes needing Part 21 / Part 21 Light approval.
- Common work card/worksheet system across relevant parts; transcribe accurately or make precise reference to tasks. Electronic: protected against unauthorised alteration; backup database "updated within 24 hours after an entry is made". Complex or long tasks transcribed and subdivided into clear stages.
- Operator's own work card system may be used, with a procedure ensuring correct completion; AMC: assess it for compliance (e.g. subdivision).
- Data readily available (AMC: close proximity to aircraft; enough terminals unless paper printout).
- Procedure keeping controlled data up to date; for customer data, written confirmation, work orders stating amendment status, or being on the customer's amendment list. AMC: monitor amendment status, subscribe to amendment schemes, special attention to mandatory instructions and airworthiness limitations.

### 145.A.47 Production planning
- Planning system for personnel, tools, equipment, material, data, facilities.
- Planning of tasks and shifts must take into account human performance limitations including fatigue.
- Handover: relevant information adequately communicated between outgoing and incoming personnel.
- Hazards of external working teams considered by the management system.
- AMC: shift schedule shared sufficiently in advance; shift durations not exceeded merely for management convenience, even if staff willing; procedure (with mitigations) for significantly increased hours or modified shift patterns; otherwise a specific risk assessment. Schedule critical maintenance tasks when staff likely most alert.

### 145.A.48 Performance of maintenance
- Only when all facilities, equipment, tooling, material, data and personnel available (AMC: temporary unavailability does not require amending the approval if the organisation commits to reacquire before resuming).
- Organisation responsible for maintenance within its approval.
- After completion: general verification aircraft/component clear of tools, equipment, extraneous parts/material, and removed access panels refitted.
- Error-capturing method after every critical maintenance task.
- Minimise risk of errors and of errors repeated in identical tasks.
- Damage assessment, repairs and modifications per M.A.304 / ML.A.304; defect assessment per M.A.403(b) / ML.A.403(b).

### Small organisations (GM1 145.A.10)
- One-person organisations limited to ratings A2 (aeroplanes 5 700 kg MTOM or less, piston only), A3 (single-engine helicopters 3 175 kg MTOM or less), A4, B2 (piston engines under 450 HP), C, D1. One full-time person (Part-66 certifying staff) holds AM, safety manager, maintenance engineer, certifying staff and, if applicable, ARS. No CRS can be issued in their absence.
- "Full-time" = "not less than 35 hrs per week except during vacation periods".
- Independent audit of compliance monitoring (145.A.200(a)(6)) may be subcontracted to another Part-145 or a qualified person, with competent authority agreement; "minimum of two on-site audits every year", one pre-announced and one unannounced. Part-145 remains responsible for corrective actions.
- Up to 10 persons: normally two full-time persons meeting certifying staff requirements, a "maintenance engineer" (issues CRS, may be ARS for ML.A.903) and a "compliance monitoring engineer" (independence retained). Either may be AM and safety manager.

### Nominated persons (AMC1 145.A.30(cc))
- "5 years of relevant work experience, of which at least 2 years should be from the aeronautical industry in an appropriate position".
- Relevant engineering/technical degree or aircraft technician/maintenance engineer qualification with additional education; replaceable by "2 years of experience" extra. For CMM/SM already holding equivalent role under another certificate within 2018/1139 scope, replaceable by a specific training programme.
- Type familiarisation at least Part-66 Appendix III Level 1; for balloons and other aircraft of "2 730 kg MTOM or less" may be replaced by documented evidence or recorded assessment.

## 4. Defined Terms

From GM1 to Annex II (for Part-145 AMC/GM):
- **Audit**: systematic, independent, documented process for obtaining evidence and evaluating it objectively to determine extent of compliance; may include inspections.
- **Assessment**: planned, documented activity by competent personnel to evaluate achieved performance and maturity against policy and objectives (whole-organisation, strengths/weaknesses).
- **Base maintenance**: any aircraft maintenance other than line maintenance (AMC1 145.A.10).
- **Base maintenance hangar**: closed facility that can house an aircraft and protect it from environmental conditions.
- **Competency**: combination of individual skills, practical and theoretical knowledge, attitude, training and experience.
- **Correction**: action to eliminate a detected non-compliance.
- **Corrective action**: action to eliminate or mitigate root cause(s) and prevent recurrence.
- **Error**: action or inaction that may lead to deviation from accepted procedures or regulations.
- **Fatigue**: physiological state of reduced mental or physical performance capability from sleep loss, extended wakefulness, circadian phase or workload.
- **Hazard**: condition or object with potential to cause or contribute to an aircraft incident or accident.
- **Human factors**: anything that affects human performance; principles seeking a safe interface between human and other system components.
- **Human performance**: human capabilities and limitations affecting safety and efficiency.
- **Inspection** (compliance monitoring context): independent documented conformity evaluation by observation and judgement with measurement/testing as appropriate.
- **Just culture**: see Reg (EU) No 376/2014 Art 2.
- **Line maintenance**: limited maintenance suitable while aircraft remains in the air operation environment; may include troubleshooting, defect rectification, component replacement (incl. engines and propellers) with external test equipment, checks for obvious discrepancies (incl. through quick opening panels), repairs/mods not needing extensive disassembly.
- **Near miss**: event in which an occurrence mandatorily reportable under Reg 376/2014 was narrowly averted.
- **Organisational factor**: condition affecting effectiveness of safety risk controls (culture, policies, processes, resources, workplace).
- **Oversight planning cycle**: time frame within which all approval areas and processes should be reviewed by the authority.
- **Oversight programme**: detailed authority audit/inspection schedule.
- **Preventive action**: action to eliminate cause of a potential non-compliance.
- **Risk assessment**: evaluation to establish whether achieved or perceived risk is acceptable or tolerable.
- **Safety culture**: enduring values, norms, attitudes and practices concerned with minimising exposure to hazards.
- **Safety risk**: predicted probability and severity of consequences of a hazard.
- **Safety training**: dedicated training supporting safety management, including human factors. "Safety management training" is specific training for staff in 145.A.30(ca) / 145.A.200(a)(3) functions.
- **Working days**: Monday to Friday inclusive, not including public holidays.

From rules/AMC/GM in this chunk:

## 5. Software Implications (Airworthiness+)

### 5.1 Organisation and approval
- `Organisation`: approval number, competent authority (derived from principal place of business; EASA if outside), certificate (Form 3) terms of approval, classes/ratings (A1-A4, B1-B3, C1-C22, D1), line stations and remote facilities (stores, workshops, subcontractor workshops) listed in MOE but not on certificate.
- `ApprovalRating` with ATA chapter mapping table (C1 to C22) so a component work order can be validated against the rating by ATA chapter.
- `CapabilityList` items (aircraft types/variants, components by P/N, processes); validate work orders against it (145.A.45(a) "applicable" data and 145.A.20).
- `Application` record (Form 2), pre-audit results attachment, change requests requiring prior approval (e.g. new ARS, 145.A.85).
- `MOE` document with versioning, sections (e.g. 3.9 competency assessment), and list of ARS; link procedures to rule points.
- Flag "temporarily unable" status per type/component (145.A.48(a) AMC): block work orders for that type until tools/data reacquired, without removing the rating.

### 5.2 Facilities and environment
- `Facility` (hangar, workshop, store, line station, office) with ownership/tenancy evidence, size/aircraft capacity, environmental controls.
- Hangar visit plan (projected aircraft slots) linked to man-hour plan.
- Line maintenance environmental suspension log: record task suspension due to weather/light/contamination and resumption.
- Store locations with access control (authorised personnel only), segregation zones (serviceable, unserviceable, unsalvageable/quarantine).

### 5.3 People, roles and nominated persons
- `Person` with employment type (employed / contracted via agency / independent), date of birth (enforce minimum age 21 for certifying and support staff), full-time flag.
- `NominatedPostholder` roles: accountable manager, maintenance nominated person(s) (base, line, workshop managers), compliance monitoring manager, safety manager; deputy for lengthy absence; validation rule: compliance monitoring cannot be combined with a maintenance function (except small-organisation arrangements by authority agreement); CMM cannot be a 145.A.30(b) person. One nominated person per Part-145 function.
- Nominated person qualification checklist (5 years experience / 2 aeronautical, degree or +2 years, type familiarisation Level 1 or documented assessment for aircraft 2 730 kg MTOM or less, HF and management system knowledge).
- `ManHourPlan` per function/shift/period: planned vs available hours, planned absences, contracted ratio; alerts: review due every 3 months; flag >25% shortfall in calendar month for any function, routed to CMM and AM; flag when contracted staff exceed 50% of maintenance staff on a shift in a workshop/hangar/line (unless temporary authority permission recorded); reduced-staffing risk assessment workflow.

### 5.4 Competence and training
- `JobFunction` (manager, planner, supervisor, certifying staff, support staff, mechanic, specialised services, compliance monitoring, safety management, stores, purchasing, ground equipment operator) with competency element matrix (GM2 table).
- `CompetencyAssessment`: person, function, method (supervised work, OJT, knowledge test, record review), assessor, dates, credits from previous organisation (experience credential), result, actions if unsatisfactory, resulting scope of authorised tasks and supervision level. Block unsupervised work until assessed.
- `TrainingRecord` with types: initial safety/HF, recurrent safety/HF, compliance monitoring training, EWIS, fuel tank safety/CDCCL, type/differences, cat A task training, procedural training, engine run-up, NDT. Track syllabus coverage (GM1 topics 1 to 10).
- Rules: initial safety training before starting job, or within 6 months for new direct employees under direct supervision; recurrent safety training each 2-year period; recurrent training programme for certifying/support staff with planned dates and "done on time" indicator; review of recurrent training content at least every 24 months.
- Recurrent training feedback capture routed to internal safety reporting.
- Experience credential generator (GM3 template) on staff leaving; retain copy at least 3 years.
- NDT qualifications: method (PT, MT, ET, UT, RT), EN 4179 level 1/2/3, national NDT board; non-destructive inspections (borescope, tap test) as separate trained competencies; derogation allowing B1/B3/L licence holders to do dye penetrant.

### 5.5 Certification authorisations
- `Part66Licence`: categories (A, B1, B2, B2L, B3, L, C), subcategories, type ratings, limitations, expiry; verification status with issuing authority.
- `CertificationAuthorisation`: holder, authorisation number/code, scope (categories, types/groups, components, tasks for cat A), limits, issue date, issuer (CMM or nominee), validity end, status (draft, active, suspended, revoked, expired), code translation reference, copy delivered (paper/electronic).
- Validity rules: cannot exceed licence validity; auto-suspend alert if licence lapses; must satisfy: understanding of type/variant/configuration differences, 6 months relevant experience in any consecutive 2 years, recurrent training in each 2-year period, competency assessment before issue/reissue. Alerts ahead of these windows rolling.
- Experience log per person per type/system to compute rolling 6-in-24-month currency.
- Cat A: authorisation per task only after task training with exam/workplace assessment; check training covered all tasks if trained elsewhere.
- B2 limited (66.A.20(a)(3)(ii)) privileges: requires cat A task training plus 6 months documented experience within the issuing organisation; non-transferable; new type needs another 6 months unless similar; new tasks on existing type need training only.
- Support staff register (B1/B2 for CMPA base maintenance), flag support staff vs certifying role per work package.
- Special authorisations: pilot limited authorisation (ATPL/CPL, task list, 12-month validity, renewal on recurrent training); one-off authorisation (reason "unforeseen", issuer CMM, type (i) employee with similar types or (ii) external person with 5 years experience and ICAO licence plus evidence on file, step-by-step worksheet, 7-day report-to-authority deadline alert, re-check required on return to approved facility).
- "Produce within 24 hours" requirement: provide holder mobile/PDF view of current authorisation.
- Component certifying staff qualified per Article 5(6).
- Permissions: only CMM or designated nominees can issue/revoke authorisations; maintenance managers cannot self-authorise.

### 5.6 Airworthiness review staff
- `ARSAuthorisation`: experience check (1 year sailplanes/balloons, 3 years others), linked certifying staff authorisation for corresponding aircraft, knowledge evidence (Part-M/ML Subpart C), supervised AR record (supervisor = authority or existing ARS), authority acceptance date, MOE amendment reference, issue date.
- Recency: valid if 6 months CAW management activity in every 2-year period OR at least one AR in last 12 months; otherwise status "lapsed" until supervised AR performed. Alerts.
- Only Part-ML aircraft ARCs (ML.A.903) via Part-145 route.

### 5.7 Tools and calibration
- `Tool` / `Equipment`: ID, type, manufacturer-specified flag and P/N, alternative tool approval reference, personal tool flag, permanent availability vs infrequent-use (with loan/arrangement), location, calibration required flag, interval (manufacturer or justified alternative), standard used, last calibration, next due, certificate/traceability record, status (serviceable, due, overdue, unserviceable, quarantined).
- Labels/QR showing next due date; block tool checkout and task sign-off if overdue/unserviceable; record which tool serials used on each task card step for traceability.
- Base maintenance access equipment/docking inventory.

### 5.8 Components, parts, materials and suppliers
- `Part` status classification: serviceable (with release document type: EASA Form 1, equivalent such as FAA 8130-3 under bilateral, JAA Form One with date limits, declaration of maintenance accomplished), unserviceable, unsalvageable, standard part, raw material, consumable material, owner-eligible 21.A.307(b)(2) item.
- Receiving inspection workflow: physical checks (damage, shelf life, packaging incl. ATA 300/ESD, caps/plugs), document review; batch fields (spec/standard, P/N, batch number, quantity, manufacturing source, supplier source, conformity statement); separate acceptance per batch.
- Shelf-life and storage condition tracking with expiry alerts.
- Unserviceable tag data: identification, in-service times, maintenance/preservation status, defects, adverse environment exposure, accident/incident aircraft flag, reason (life limit, AD non-compliance, missing info, defect, accident).
- Unsalvageable workflow: quarantine location, mutilation (method from accepted list, evidence/photo) or marking for non-flight use with owner agreement, or disposal record; prevent re-entry unless life extension or approved repair.
- Custody transfer of unserviceable part to owner recorded in aircraft/engine/component logbook.
- Installation check: P/N, mod status, limitations must match applicable maintenance data/IPC for that aircraft before install; block install of CAO-released used parts (EASA Form 1 by CAO) on CMPA or air carrier (Reg 1008/2008) aircraft.
- `Supplier` with type (Part-145, Part 21G, operator, stockist, distributor, broker, owner), certifications (EN/AS9120 in OASIS, ASA-100, EASO 2012, FAA AC 00-56), evaluation method (questionnaire, desktop, on-site audit), evaluation date and recurrent due date; still requires incoming inspection.
- Locally fabricated parts: allowed type list, approved data reference, inspection separate from installation inspection, conformity statement, P/N and organisation ID marking, heat-treatment records; flag "not eligible for Form 1, not for external supply" and segregate from deliveries.

### 5.9 Maintenance data and work cards
- `MaintenanceDataSource`: owner (organisation-controlled vs customer-provided), revision/amendment status, subscription status, customer currency evidence (written confirmation, work order amendment status, or on amendment list), mandatory instructions/ALS emphasis.
- Data deficiency report type in internal safety reporting, with "notified to author" date and open until author clarifies.
- Modified maintenance instruction workflow: proposer, justification type (more practical, intent not achievable, alternative tool), practical demonstration, risk assessment, CMM approval, author informed, clear identification; hard block on CDCCL-linked instructions (needs Part 21 approval).
- `WorkCard`/`Worksheet`: common template per organisation area; task reference (precise reference or transcription); stages with interruption points; disassembly/accomplishment/reassembly/test steps; error-capturing method required flag (independent inspection, re-inspection); per-step sign-off by authorised person (distinct from CRS); supplementary cards per individual for long tasks; operator-supplied card system option with completion check procedure.
- Electronic records integrity: audit trail, protection against unauthorised alteration, backup replication within 24 hours (ideally real-time), monitor backup lag.
- Data access: availability near aircraft (tablet/terminals), offline/print fallback.

### 5.10 Planning, fatigue, handover
- Production planning: resource availability check (people with valid authorisations, tools in calibration, parts, data revision, hangar slot) before releasing a work package; block start if not all available (145.A.48(a)).
- Line vs base classification of tasks per procedure (duration, task count, shifts, disciplines, environment); exceptional base task at line station requires a task assessment record; progressive/equalised checks assessed individually.
- Shift rostering with fatigue rules: rest between shifts, avoid alternating day/night, publish rosters in advance, flag extended hours or pattern changes requiring procedure/risk assessment and mitigations (extra supervision/independent inspection, non-critical tasks only, extra breaks); plan critical tasks when staff most alert.
- Fatigue reports as a category in internal safety reporting.
- Shift/task handover record: open tasks, stage reached, outstanding steps, parts, warnings; acknowledgement by incoming person; planned overlap.
- External working teams register at a facility with hazard coordination notes (e.g. aircraft configuration/electrical power conflicts).

### 5.11 Performance of maintenance and critical tasks
- Close-out checklist: tool control (all tools accounted for), no extraneous parts/material (FOD), access panels refitted (panel list from task cards).
- Critical maintenance task flag on task definitions (flight controls installation/rigging, stability systems such as autopilot and fuel transfer, engines/propellers/rotors installation, overhaul/calibration/rigging of engines, propellers, transmissions, gearboxes), with data source for identification (DAH data, accident reports, incidents, occurrence reporting, FDM, audits, training feedback).
- Enforce error-capturing method (e.g. independent inspection by a different qualified person) before CRS; record inspector qualification.
- Repeated identical task risk: flag when the same person performs the same critical task on multiple systems/engines of one aircraft (classic multiple engine error) for separation or additional checks (inferred from 145.A.48(c)(3); more detail likely in chunk 09).
- Defect assessment link to M.A.403(b)/ML.A.403(b) and repair/mod data per M.A.304/ML.A.304.
- Cat C CRS workflow for CMPA base maintenance: confirm support staff sign-offs complete, all customer work done, assessment of work not carried out with either completion or deferral agreed with operator to a specified check/time limit.

### 5.12 Safety management and compliance monitoring hooks
- Safety manager functions: hazard log, risk assessments, safety action plan tracking, periodic safety performance reports to safety review board, internal occurrence investigations, safety training oversight.
- Compliance monitoring: audit plan, findings, corrections vs corrective actions vs preventive actions (definitions), responsibility of base/line/workshop managers for corrective actions in their areas, monitoring of contracted maintenance.
- Small organisation mode: one-person setup with subcontracted independent audit, two on-site audits per year (one unannounced); 2-person mode with maintenance engineer issuing CRS and compliance monitoring engineer independent.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM changing practical application

- Line vs base (AMC1 145.A.10): organisations need a procedure to classify tasks, and may (temporary/occasional) do a base task in a line environment after a task assessment. Engine and propeller changes can be line maintenance.
- Small organisations (GM1 145.A.10): one person can be AM, safety manager, maintenance engineer, certifying staff and ARS; independent audit outsourced with two audits a year (one unannounced); Form 3 replaces a separate authorisation.
- Hangar alternatives (AMC1 145.A.25(a) point 4): non-hangar facilities with equivalent protection may be used for certain base tasks after risk assessment and authority agreement.
- Remote facilities (AMC2 145.A.20) need not appear on the certificate if covered in MOE.
- Accountable manager need not be technical (AMC1 145.A.30(a)) and can serve several organisations; can also be CMM and/or safety manager in small organisations subject to risk assessment and authority agreement (AMC1 145.A.30(c);(ca)(e)).
- Staffing ratios (AMC1 145.A.30(d)): at least 50% employed per shift/area; 3-monthly plan review; 25% monthly shortfall trigger. These are numeric thresholds directly implementable.
- Competency assessment (AMC1-AMC2 145.A.30(e)): a licence is not enough (also AMC 145.A.35(a)); assessment may be supervised work over days to weeks; written confirmation from previous organisation can shorten it; GM3 credential template supports portability between organisations.
- Safety training timing (AMC4 145.A.30(e)): new direct employees under supervision get 6 months to complete initial safety training; recurrent each 2 years, with duration driven by audit findings. Wide staff population (stores, purchasing, ground equipment operators included).
- Recurrent training duration (AMC1 145.A.35(d)): no fixed hours; scaled to findings and hazards (days vs weeks; hours for simple component release).
- Cat A / B2 limited task list (AMC 145.A.30(g)): explicit list of allowed simple tasks (wheels, brakes, lights, wiper blades, seats, batteries, fluids replenishment, MEL deactivation agreed as simple, de-icing residue checks, helicopter role items) and "minor scheduled line maintenance" up to and including weekly check. No troubleshooting allowed. Not all B categories needed at every line station; MOE procedure for defects needing other categories.
- Support staff can also hold line certification privileges (AMC1 145.A.30(h)); same licence/recency/training requirements as certifying staff.
- Pilot authorisations (AMC1 145.A.30(j)(4)): ATPL/CPL; 12-month validity; task list includes role changes (stretcher, dual controls, FLIR, doors, camera equipment).
- One-off authorisations (AMC 145.A.30(j)(5)): decided by compliance monitoring personnel case by case; step-by-step worksheet signed by holder; verification on return to an approved facility by visual examination and/or system operation.
- ARS recency (AMC1 145.A.37): either 6 months CAW activity per 2 years or one AR per 12 months; this mirrors CAMO ARS rules.
- Tools (AMC 145.A.40): personal tools allowed if listed in the control register; calibration intervals can deviate from manufacturer if justified by results.
- Form 1 equivalents (AMC1 145.A.42(a)(i)) include the Part-M/ML "declaration of maintenance accomplished" by an independent certifying staff; standard parts and materials never come with Form 1.
- CAO-released components (GM1 145.A.42(b)) are barred from CMPA and air carrier aircraft: important parts eligibility rule by aircraft category.
- Supplier certifications (EN/AS9120 OASIS, ASA-100, EASO 2012, FAA AC 00-56) ease evaluation but do not remove incoming inspection.
- Unsalvageable disposition (AMC1 145.A.42(c)) and mutilation methods (GM1 145.A.42(c)(i)); stamping, paint, tags, small holes and two-piece sawing are poor mutilation methods.
- Fabrication (AMC1 145.A.42(b)(iii)): typical allowed items (bushes, shims, secondary structure, skins, control cables, pipes, looms, sheet metal repair panels); no fabricating to pattern without an acceptable engineering drawing; no onward supply.
- Modified maintenance instructions (AMC1 145.A.45(d)): approved by compliance monitoring after practical demonstration; never for CDCCL.
- Work cards (AMC1 145.A.45(e)): must show error-capturing method; human factors in card design; subdivision at interruption points and discipline changes; operator cards assessed for compliance.
- Fatigue (AMC1/GM1 145.A.47(b)): no numeric duty limits in EASA text; principles: publish shifts in advance, adequate rest, avoid alternating day/night, do not extend shifts for management convenience, procedure and mitigations for extensions.
- Handover (AMC 145.A.47(c)): formalised process, planned overlap and a place for handover.
- Critical maintenance tasks (AMC2 145.A.48(c)(2)): categories to review (flight path/attitude controls, stability systems, propulsive force, overhaul/rigging of engines/props/transmissions); multiple data sources. Text continues into next chunk.
- CDCCL (GM1 145.A.48(c)): controlled through training and standardised practice (wire routing/splicing, fuel quantity indication wiring segregation), not periodic inspection.
