### 2.4 Examination format (Appendix II, section 1: quoted exactly)
- 1.1 "All basic examinations shall be carried out using the multi-choice question format and essay questions as specified below." Distractors must "seem equally plausible to anyone ignorant of the subject"; numerical distractors "shall correspond to procedural errors … they shall not be mere random numbers."
- 1.2 "Each multi-choice question shall have three alternative answers of which only one shall be the correct answer and the candidate shall be allowed a time per module which is based upon a nominal average of 75 seconds per question."
- 1.3 "Each essay question requires the preparation of a written answer and the candidate shall be allowed 20 minutes to answer each such question."
- 1.4 Essay questions are drafted and evaluated using the Appendix I Module 7 syllabus.
- 1.5/1.6 Each question has a model answer, broken down into "Key Points".
- 1.7 "The pass mark for each module and sub-module multi-choice part of the examination is 75 %."
- 1.8 "The pass mark for each essay question is 75 % in that the candidates answer shall contain 75 % of the required key points addressed by the question and no significant error related to any required key point."
- 1.9 "If either the multi-choice part only or the essay part only is failed, then it is only necessary to retake the multi-choice or essay part, as appropriate."
- 1.10 "Penalty marking systems shall not be used to determine whether a candidate has passed."
- 1.11 "An examination in a module may not be retaken earlier than 90 days following the date of a failed examination in that module, except in the case of a maintenance training organisation approved in accordance with Annex IV (Part-147) which delivers a course of retraining tailored to the failed subjects in the particular module; the failed module may be retaken after 30 days."
- 1.12 "Basic knowledge examinations with a maximum allowed time of more than 90 or more than 180 minutes may be split in two or three partial exams respectively." Each partial exam must: (a) be complementary, together meeting the module requirements; (b) be of similar allowed time; (c) "be passed with 75 % or more of the questions answered correctly"; (d) "contain a number of questions that is multiple of four"; (e) be listed on the same certificate of recognition issued after the last partial exam is passed, listing "the dates and the results of the partial exams – without averaging the results"; (f) be taken within the same organisation, following normal retake provisions.
- 1.13 "The maximum number of attempts for each examination is three in a 12-month period." The applicant must give the MTO or competent authority a written statement of the number and dates of attempts in the preceding 12 months and where they took place. The MTO or authority is responsible for checking.
- 1.14 Questions used in an MBT learning programme "shall not be used in examinations".

### 2.6 Appendix III: type training (this chunk's portion)
**1. General**
- Type training = theoretical training and examination, plus practical training and assessment ("except for the category C ratings").
- Theory: conducted by a Part-147 MTO or by another organisation "as directly approved by the competent authority". Must comply with point 3.1 and with any OSD elements (Reg. 748/2012). For a category C person qualified by academic degree (66.A.30(a)(5)), the first type theoretical course must be at B1 or B2 level. Theory "Shall have been started and completed within the 3 years preceding the application for a type rating endorsement."
- Practical: same approval routes; complies with point 3.2 and OSD; must include "a representative cross section of maintenance activities relevant to the aircraft type" and demonstrations using equipment, components, MSTDs, MTDs or real aircraft. Also within the 3 years preceding application.
- **Differences training** covers differences between (a) two type ratings of the same manufacturer as determined by the Agency, or (b) two licence categories for the same type rating. It is defined case by case. A type rating is endorsed after differences training only if the applicant already holds the "from" type rating or has completed type training for the "from" aircraft. It must be started and completed within 3 years before the application.

**3.1 Theoretical element**
- After the first type course for category C, "all subsequent courses need only be to level 1".
- In level 3 training, level 1/2 material may be used, but "the majority of the course material and training time shall be at the higher level".
- **Minimum tuition hours** (theory):

| Aircraft | B1.1 | B1.2 | B1.3 | B1.4 | B2 | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aeroplanes MTOM > 30 000 kg | 150 | 120 | – | – | 100 | 30 |
| Aeroplanes > 5 700 kg and ≤ 30 000 kg | 120 | 100 | – | – | 100 | 25 |
| Aeroplanes ≤ 5 700 kg | 80 | 60 | – | – | 60 | 15 |
| Helicopters | – | – | 120 | 100 | 100 | 25 |

  "a tuition hour means 60 minutes of teaching and exclude any breaks, examination, revision, preparation and aircraft visit." The hours apply only to complete aircraft/engine combinations per the Agency-defined type rating.
- Course duration must be justified by a **training needs analysis (TNA)** based on aircraft design, maintenance needs and types of operation, chapter analysis and competency analysis. More hours are required if the TNA shows a need. Differences courses, combined B1/B2 courses and courses below the minimum hours must be justified to the competent authority by TNA. The course must also state the minimum physical/virtual classroom attendance and the maximum classroom hours per day. "If the minimum attendance required is not met, the certificate of recognition shall not be issued."
- Content: at minimum the type-specific elements of the syllabus, plus elements from type variations and technology changes. The focus is mechanical/electrical for B1 and electrical/avionic for B2. Any OSD minimum syllabus must be included.
- **Syllabus table** (by ATA chapter; columns: Aeroplanes turbine B1/C, Aeroplanes piston B1/C, Helicopters turbine B1/C, Helicopters piston B1/C, Avionics B2), summarised:

## 4. Defined Terms

- **Basic knowledge levels** (Appendix I, section 1):
  - **Level 1**: "A familiarisation with the principal elements of the subject." The applicant should be familiar with basic elements, give a simple description using common words and examples, and use typical terms.
  - **Level 2**: "A general knowledge of the theoretical and practical aspects of the subject and an ability to apply that knowledge." The applicant should understand theoretical fundamentals, give a general description with typical examples, use mathematical formulae with physical laws, read and understand sketches, drawings and schematics, and apply knowledge practically "using detailed procedures".
  - **Level 3**: "A detailed knowledge of the theoretical and practical aspects of the subject and a capacity to combine and apply the separate elements of knowledge in a logical and comprehensive manner." The applicant should know theory and interrelationships, give a detailed description, use formulae, read, understand and **prepare** sketches and schematics, apply knowledge "using manufacturer's instructions", and interpret results and apply corrective action.
- **Aircraft type training levels** (Appendix III, section 2). These are different from the basic levels:
  - **Level 1**: "A brief overview of the airframe, systems and powerplant as outlined in the Systems Description Section of the Aircraft Maintenance Manual/Instructions for Continued Airworthiness."
  - **Level 2**: "Basic system overview of controls, indicators, principal components, including their location and purpose, servicing and minor troubleshooting." Includes servicing of Fuel, Power Plants, Hydraulics, Landing Gear, Water/Waste and Oxygen, and determining airworthiness per MEL/CDL.
  - **Level 3**: "Detailed description, operation, component location, removal/installation and bite and troubleshooting procedures to maintenance manual level."
- **n/a** (matrix): "the subject module is neither applicable nor required".
- **Key Points**: the breakdown of an essay model answer into its important points (App. II 1.6).
- **Partial exam**: a split of a basic exam over 90 or 180 min into 2 or 3 parts (App. II 1.12).
- **Differences training**: training covering the differences between two type ratings of the same manufacturer (as determined by the Agency) or between two licence categories on the same type rating (App. III 1(c)).
- **Tuition hour**: "60 minutes of teaching and exclude any breaks, examination, revision, preparation and aircraft visit".
- **MBT**: multimedia-based training, used "either in a physically or in a virtually controlled environment".
- **Training methods** (AMC1 App. I s.3): "instructor-centred training", "student-centred training", "blended training". Modules 7, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 17 "should not normally be taught solely through a student-centred method unless provisions are in place to verify the actual and progressive acquisition of knowledge, skills and attitude by the student."
- **MSTD / MTD**: maintenance simulation training device / maintenance training device.
- **OSD**: operational suitability data (Reg. 748/2012).
- **TNA**: training needs analysis.
- **B2L system ratings**: C/N (Communication and Navigation), Ins. (Instruments), A/F (Autoflight), Sur. (Surveillance), A/S (Airframe and Systems), plus Basic.
- **"Dirty Dozen"** (Module 9.10): lack of communication, lack of teamwork, lack of assertiveness, complacency, fatigue, stress, lack of knowledge, lack of resources, lack of awareness, distraction, pressure, norms.

## 5. Software Implications

### 5.1 Reference data (static, versioned by regulation amendment)
- `licence_category`: A1–A4, B1.1–B1.4, B2, B2L, B3, C (L handled separately). Attributes: aircraft group (aeroplane/helicopter), engine (turbine/piston), B3 scope (piston non-pressurised aeroplane MTOM ≤ 2 t).
- `b2l_system_rating`: Basic, COM/NAV, INSTRUMENTS, AUTOFLIGHT, SURVEILLANCE, AIRFRAME SYSTEMS, each mapped to Module 13 sub-modules and exam sizes. Module 14 is required only for the INSTRUMENTS and AIRFRAME SYSTEMS ratings.
- `module` (1–17) and `submodule` (e.g. 7.14.3). Mark reserved items (7.4, 7.15, 12.16).
- `module_applicability` matrix: category to module, including the per-subcategory exception that Module 4 is n/a for A1–A4. Category C is modelled as "any one of B1.x / B2 basic knowledge sets".
- `submodule_level`: (submodule, category) gives level 1/2/3 or not required. This lets a gap analysis compare an engineer's existing modules against those needed for an upgrade (e.g. B1.2 to B1.1 needs Module 15 and Module 11 at B1.1 levels; A to B1 needs higher levels on the same modules).
- `exam_spec`: (module, category or category group, B2L rating) gives MCQ count, essay count, time in minutes. Rules: 75 s per MCQ, 20 min per essay, 3 options per MCQ, pass mark 75 %. Optional AMC sub-module question allocation for exam building.
- `type_training_min_hours`: (aircraft class by MTOM band or helicopter, category) gives hours.
- `type_syllabus_chapter`: ATA chapter, with level per column (aeroplane turbine/piston, helicopter turbine/piston × B1/C, plus B2).

### 5.2 Per-person tracking (licence-tracking feature)
- Module exam results: module, sub-module/partial number, category level sat, date, score %, pass/fail, MCQ vs essay part (these pass and fail separately), examining body (Part-147 MTO or competent authority), certificate of recognition reference.
- **Retake eligibility engine**:
  - next permitted date = failed date + 90 days, or + 30 days if the candidate attended a Part-147 retraining course tailored to the failed subjects (store the retraining evidence);
  - no more than 3 attempts per exam in any rolling 12-month period (count across all organisations; the applicant's written statement of prior attempts should be captured);
  - only the failed part (MCQ or essay) needs retaking.
- **Partial exam handling**: allowed when exam time exceeds 90 min (2 parts) or 180 min (3 parts). Each part's question count must be a multiple of 4 and each part needs ≥ 75 %. All parts must be at the same organisation. Do not average results. The certificate lists each part's date and result.
- Validation that no MBT learning question is reused in an exam bank (an exam-bank feature for MTO customers).
- **Type training records**: theory course (start date, end date, level, hours, attendance %, approving organisation or direct authority approval, OSD inclusion), practical training (dates, tasks, devices used), differences training (from-type, to-type or category). Rule: theory, practical and differences training must be "started and completed within the 3 years preceding the application" for the endorsement. The app should alert when a completed course is approaching 3 years old with no endorsement application.
- Category C: flag that after the first type course, later courses need only level 1. Flag academic-degree route holders (66.A.30(a)(5)) whose first type course must be at B1/B2 level.
- Attendance: if the minimum attendance is not met, no certificate of recognition. Store the minimum attendance and maximum hours per day from the course definition.

### 5.3 For MTO/training-provider customers
- Course builder: map course content to sub-modules and levels; record training method per module (instructor-centred, student-centred or blended). Warn if Modules 7, 9, 11–17 are delivered solely student-centred without progress-verification provisions.
- TNA workflow and document storage, with justification needed when hours are below the table minimums or for differences or combined courses.
- Exam generator: draws per AMC sub-module allocations, checks totals against the IR, uses a 3-option MCQ format, no penalty marking, and supports essay key-point marking (75 % of key points and no significant error).

### 5.4 UK note
The UK CAA retained Part-66 at the EU exit, and the UK text has since diverged. The 2023/989 changes (B2L restructure, new sub-modules such as 7.14.3 additive manufacturing and 10.10 cybersecurity, MBT, partial exams, the 3-attempts-in-12-months limit) may not match UK Part-66. Store module and exam data per jurisdiction and regulation version, and check the UK CAA text before applying these EU numbers to UK licences.
