## 2. Obligations (with exact numbers)

### 2.1 Maintenance data (ML.A.401)
Whoever maintains the aircraft must use only "applicable maintenance data". That means any of:
1. requirements, procedures, standards or information issued by the competent authority or EASA;
2. any applicable AD;
3. the applicable ICA and other maintenance instructions from the TC holder, STC holder, declarant of a declaration of design compliance, or other organisation publishing under Part 21 / Part 21 Light;
4. for components approved for installation by the DAH or declarant: the component manufacturer's maintenance instructions, where the DAH or declarant accepts them;
5. data issued under 145.A.45(d).

### 2.2 Performance of maintenance (ML.A.402(b)), outside approved organisations
The person performing maintenance must:
1. be qualified for the task;
2. work in an organised, clean area;
3. use the methods, techniques and standards in the maintenance data;
4. use the tools, equipment and material in the data, with tools calibrated to an officially recognised standard where needed;
5. respect environmental limits;
6. use proper facilities in bad weather or for lengthy maintenance;
7. minimise the risk of multiple errors and of errors repeated across identical tasks;
8. apply an error-capturing method after any critical maintenance task;
9. do a general verification at the end: no tools or extraneous parts left, all access panels refitted;
10. record and document all maintenance.

AMC details:
- Acceptable records include a ticked and signed copy of the 100-h/annual checklist, and a copy of the release to service listing the tasks.
- Keep airborne contamination to a minimum, or seal susceptible systems.
- Sign each task off only after it is complete. Group tasks so critical steps are visible. Work by supervised people (temporary staff, trainees) must be checked and signed by an authorised person.
- **Critical maintenance tasks (AMC1 (b)(8))** are tasks that may affect:
  - (a) flight path and attitude control, such as installing, rigging or adjusting flight controls;
  - (b) stability control systems (autopilots, fuel transfer);
  - (c) propulsive force, including installing engines, propellers and rotors;
  - (d) overhaul, calibration or rigging of engines, propellers, transmissions and gearboxes.
- **Error capturing (AMC2)**: re-inspection when only one person is available, or independent inspection.

### 2.3 Defects (ML.A.403)
- (a) A defect that seriously endangers flight safety must be rectified before further flight.
- (b) Who may decide a defect does not seriously endanger flight safety, and defer it:
  1. the pilot, for defects affecting **non-required** equipment;
  2. the pilot using the **MEL**, for required equipment. Without an MEL, only authorised certifying staff may defer these;
  3. the pilot, for other defects, if both:
     - (i) the aircraft is operated under Part-NCO (or is a balloon not under Part-BOP Subpart-ADD, or a sailplane not following Part-SAO Subpart DEC); and
     - (ii) the pilot has the agreement of the owner or of the contracted CAMO/CAO;
  4. appropriately qualified certifying staff, for other defects where (3)(i)/(ii) are not met.
- (c) Defects that do not seriously hazard flight safety must be rectified as soon as practicable from the date first identified, and within the limits in the maintenance data.
- (d) Any defect not rectified before flight must be recorded in the ML.A.305 record system, and a record must be available to the pilot.
- AMC/GM:
  - Equipment is defective if the pilot saw a malfunction in flight, or it is found faulty after an inspection or test in the maintenance data. A pilot's observation is not automatically a defect.
  - The pilot should consult available certifying staff before deferring.
  - "Required" means required by the certification specification, the air operations rules, the rules of the air or ATM (for example a transponder in some airspace).
  - Deferred defects should be made known to the crew before they reach the aircraft. They go on the list of deferred maintenance (ML.A.305(d)(6)) and are rectified at the next appropriate maintenance event. If one is not rectified then, re-enter it **keeping the original defect date**.

### 2.4 Component classification and installation (ML.A.501–504)
**Fitting a component (ML.A.501(a))**: unless Part-M Subpart F, Part-145, Part-CAO, 21.A.307 or 21L.A.193 says otherwise, a component may be fitted only if all three hold:
- (i) it is in satisfactory condition;
- (ii) it is released on an EASA Form 1 or equivalent;
- (iii) it is marked per Part 21 Subpart Q (or Part 21 Light Subpart Q).

Other installation rules:
- **Eligibility (b)**: before installation, check the part is eligible where different modification or AD configurations apply.
- **Standard parts (c)**: fit only when the maintenance data specifies those particular standard parts. They must come with evidence of conformity to the applicable standard and appropriate traceability.
- **Raw or consumable material (d)**: use only if:
  - (i) the aircraft or component manufacturer allows it in the maintenance data (or Subpart F / Part-145 / Part-CAO allows it);
  - (ii) it meets the material specification and has traceability;
  - (iii) it comes with documentation clearly relating to that material, with a conformity-to-specification statement and the manufacturing and supplier source.
- **Balloons (e)**: the basket, burner and fuel cylinders must be eligible per the TCDS (or documents it refers to), serviceable, and have maintenance records.
  - AMC: these are often swapped between balloons. No Form 1 is needed when one is removed serviceable and installed on another balloon, provided the installer has access to the records showing serviceability, paying attention to inspection dates. Maintenance on these components must still be released on a Form 1 or equivalent, or in the balloon log book.
- **Parts that need no Form 1 (GM1 ML.A.501(a))**: 21.A.307(b) and 21L.A.193(b) list new components that do not need a Form 1. Points (c) of those rules set the requirements for the accompanying document.

**Documents equivalent to an EASA Form 1 (AMC1 ML.A.501(a)(ii))**:
- (a) a release document under an EU bilateral agreement;
- (b) a release document from an organisation approved under a JAA bilateral agreement, until the EU agreement supersedes it;
- (c) a JAA Form One issued before **28 November 2004** by a JAR-145 organisation in a JAA Full Member State;
- (d) a JAA Form One from a JAR-21 organisation (JAA Full Member State, mutual recognition system) for new components released from manufacturing before the Part 21 compliance date;
- (e) a JAA Form One issued before **28 September 2005** by a nationally approved production organisation;
- (f) a JAA Form One issued before **28 September 2008** by a nationally approved maintenance organisation;
- (g) a release document acceptable under a bilateral agreement between a competent authority and a third country (notified under Art. 68 of 2018/1139), until the EU agreement supersedes it;
- (h) a release document under Art. 4(4) of 1321/2014;
- (i) a "declaration of maintenance accomplished" under ML.A.502(c).

**"Owner-produced" and owner-accepted parts (ML.A.502(a) and (c))**:
- Components the owner accepted under 21.A.307(b)(2) or 21L.A.193(b)(2) (owner-accepted parts, for ELA aircraft) may be maintained by anyone. They are subject to re-acceptance by the owner under the same conditions. That maintenance is **not eligible for a Form 1** and is subject to the aircraft release requirements.
- Components under 21.A.307(b)(3) to (b)(6) or 21L.A.193(b)(3) to (b)(6) may be maintained by anyone. Their release is a **"declaration of maintenance accomplished"**, which must contain at least:
  - basic details of the maintenance;
  - the completion date;
  - the identity of the issuing organisation or person.
- That declaration counts as a maintenance record and is equivalent to a Form 1 for the component. GM adds:
  - it may be in any shape or form, and the issuer needs no approval;
  - together with other records, it must show the component was first installed "new" as such a component;
  - do not install the component if the certificate is unreadable, not understandable, or says the component is not in satisfactory condition.
- (Note: the exact categories in 21.A.307(b)(2)–(6) are in Part 21, not in this chunk. Typically (b)(2) is owner-accepted parts for ELA1/ELA2 aircraft, and (b)(3)–(6) include standard parts and parts with no airworthiness impact. Verify against Part 21.)

**Component release table (ML.A.502(b))**

| Maintenance data used | Type of maintenance | Released on EASA Form 1 by | Released at aircraft level (ML.A.801, no Form 1 possible) by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Component maintenance data (from the component manufacturer) | Maintenance other than overhaul | Engine-rated (engines) or component-rated (other components) maintenance organisations | Aircraft-rated maintenance organisations and/or independent certifying staff |
| Component maintenance data | Overhaul of components other than engines and propellers | Component-rated maintenance organisations | Not possible |
| Component maintenance data | Overhaul of engines and propellers for **CS-VLA, CS-22 and LSA** aircraft | Engine-rated (engine) or component-rated (propeller) organisations | Aircraft-rated maintenance organisations and/or independent certifying staff |
| Component maintenance data | Overhaul of engines and propellers for **other** aircraft | Engine-rated or component-rated organisations | Not possible |
| Aircraft maintenance data (from the aircraft manufacturer) | All components, all types of maintenance | Engine-rated or component-rated organisations | Aircraft-rated maintenance organisations and/or independent certifying staff |

- GM1 ML.A.502: independent certifying staff may release component maintenance only when ML.A.201 allows them to maintain the aircraft and they are competent for it. Example: not for balloons operated under Subpart-ADD (ML.A.201(e)).

**Service-life-limited components (ML.A.503)** cover:
- (1) components with a **certified life limit**, after which they must be retired;
- (2) components with a **service life limit**, after which they need maintenance to restore serviceability.

Rules:
- Installed life-limited components must not exceed the approved limit in the AMP and ADs, except under ML.A.504(c).
- Limits are expressed in **calendar time, flight hours, landings or cycles**.
- At the limit, remove the component for maintenance, or for disposal if it has a certified life limit.

**Unserviceable (ML.A.504(a))**: a component is unserviceable if any of these apply:
1. its service life limit (as defined in the AMP) has expired;
2. it does not comply with applicable ADs or other EASA-mandated continued-airworthiness requirements;
3. there is not enough information to determine its airworthiness status or eligibility;
4. there is evidence of defects or malfunctions;
5. it was involved in an incident or accident likely to affect its serviceability.

**Identifying and holding unserviceable components (b)**: either
1. unserviceable and stored in a secure location under the control of an approved maintenance organisation or independent certifying staff, until its future status is decided; or
2. marked unserviceable by the person or organisation that declared it so, with custody transferred to the **aircraft owner**, the transfer documented in the ML.A.305 record system.

**Unsalvageable (c)**: components that have reached their certified life limit, or have a non-repairable defect or malfunction. They must not re-enter the supply system unless the certified life limit is extended or a repair is approved under ML.A.304.

The person or organisation responsible under ML.A.201 must, for an unsalvageable component, either:
- (d)(1) retain it in a secure location as in (b)(1); or
- (d)(2) have it mutilated beyond economic salvage or repair before relinquishing responsibility; or
- (e) transfer it without mutilation to an organisation for **training or research**.

**Serviceable**: there is no formal definition. It is implied by ML.A.501(a): satisfactory condition, Form 1 or equivalent, Part 21 Subpart Q marking, and eligible.

### 2.5 Aircraft CRS (ML.A.801)
- (a) A CRS is issued after the required maintenance has been properly carried out.
- (b) Issuers, alternatively:
  1. certifying staff on behalf of an approved maintenance organisation;
  2. independent certifying staff;
  3. the pilot-owner, per ML.A.803.
- (c) **Unforeseen circumstances (AOG away from base)**: when no approved organisation and no certifying staff are available, the owner may authorise any person with **at least 3 years** of appropriate maintenance experience and proper qualifications to maintain and release the aircraft to Subpart D standards. The owner must:
  1. obtain and keep, in the aircraft records, details of the work and the person's qualifications;
  2. have the work rechecked and released under (b) at the earliest opportunity, within **7 days**. For Part-NCO aircraft (and balloons not under ADD, sailplanes not under DEC) the limit is **30 days**;
  3. notify the contracted CAMO/CAO, or the competent authority if there is no contract, within **7 days** of issuing the authorisation.
- (d) Certifying staff (b)(1)/(b)(2) may be assisted by people under their direct and continuous control.
- (e) **CRS minimum content**:
  1. basic details of the maintenance carried out;
  2. the completion date;
  3. the identity of the issuer, meaning either (i) the maintenance organisation's approval reference and the certifying staff, or (ii) for independent certifying staff, their identity and licence number if applicable;
  4. any limitations to airworthiness or operations.
- (f) **Incomplete maintenance**: a CRS may be issued within the approved aircraft limitations when the required maintenance cannot be completed. It must say the maintenance was not completed and state the limitations in (e)(4).
- (g) No CRS may be issued if there is any known non-compliance with Part-ML that endangers flight safety.

**AMC1 ML.A.801(e)**:
- Standard statement: "certifies that the work specified, except as otherwise specified, was carried out in accordance with Part-ML, and in respect to that work, the aircraft is considered ready for release to service."
- Pilot-owner statement: "certifies that the limited pilot-owner maintenance specified, except as otherwise specified, was carried out in accordance with Part-ML, and in respect to that work, the aircraft is considered ready for release to service."
- The CRS should refer to the task in the DAH, declarant or operator instruction, or in the AMP, and give the revision status of that instruction.
- It should give the date of the maintenance relative to any life or overhaul limit (date, FH, cycles, landings).
- Extensive maintenance may be summarised if there is a unique cross-reference to the work pack. Dimensional information stays in the work pack.
- Use the normal signature. For a computer release-to-service system, the authority must be satisfied that only that person can issue the CRS. Example: a magnetic or optical personal card plus a PIN known only to the individual. A certification stamp is optional.
- Owners, certifying staff, operators and organisations should hold a clear, concise and legible record of the work.
- Independent certifying staff (b)(2) should keep all records proving the CRS requirements were met.

**AMC1 ML.A.801(f)**: where the data requires an in-flight task to complete maintenance, the certifier releases the incomplete maintenance (within limits) before the flight. The operator keeps responsibility for the maintenance check flight (see GM1 ML.A.301(f)). After the flight and any further work, the full CRS is issued.

**AMC1 ML.A.801, Standard Changes and Standard Repairs (SC/SR, CS-STAN)**:
- Only persons entitled to release the aircraft under ML.A.801(b) are eligible installers.
- The installer takes responsibility for meeting CS-STAN: design, parts, identification, documentation, manual amendments, embodiment, release and record-keeping.
- CS-STAN may restrict some SC/SR so the pilot-owner cannot release them.
- Transitional note: until **1 October 2020**, national certifying-staff qualifications could release. Releases on behalf of an organisation were valid in any Member State. Independent releases were valid only in the qualifying State, and only where the aircraft is registered.
- Parts used in an SC/SR need no separate design approval. Where CS-STAN requires an ETSO article, equivalent articles under international agreements or grandfathered articles are acceptable. Specifically designed parts needing POA production must come with a Form 1. The 21.A.122(b) arrangement does not apply.
- Eligibility of parts follows Part 21, Part 21 Light, Part-ML and the organisation rules. Less restrictive rules apply to ELA1/ELA2 (21.A.307) and sailplanes (AMC 21.A.303). Organisations may fabricate parts under M.A.603(c), CAO.A.020(c) and 145.A.42(c).
- Parts must be permanently marked per Part 21 Subpart Q or Part 21 Light Subpart Q.
- Documentation: an engineering file in proportion to complexity (drawings, parts list, analysis, test results, statement of compliance, manual/ICA/IPC/wiring amendments). Use **EASA Form 123**. The logbook entry refers to the Form 123. Form 123 and the CRS must be signed by **the same person**. Use controlled-document principles (reference numbers, issue dates, revisions, names).
- The owner, CAMO or CAO keeps the status of changes and repairs (ML.A.305). This information is consulted at the airworthiness review.
- Under ML.A.302, the owner, CAMO or CAO assesses whether ICA changes require an AMP amendment.
- Two or more related Subpart B SCs may be embodied as a single change on one Form 123 with one CRS, with full traceability. The restrictions of all of them apply.

### 2.6 Component CRS (ML.A.802)
- Except for ML.A.502(c) cases (the declaration of maintenance accomplished), a component CRS is issued after the required maintenance under ML.A.502.
- The **EASA Form 1** (Appendix II to Part-M) is the component CRS, except where maintenance is released at aircraft level per the ML.A.502(b) table.

### 2.7 Pilot-owner (ML.A.803, Appendices II and III)
**Who qualifies (a)**:
1. holds a valid pilot licence, or equivalent, issued or validated by a Member State for the type or class rating;
2. owns the aircraft, solely or jointly, and is either:
   - (i) a natural person on the registration form; or
   - (ii) a member of a **non-profit recreational legal entity** named on the registration document as owner or operator, who is directly involved in its decision-making and designated by it to do pilot-owner maintenance.

**Applicability (b)**: only for Part-NCO aircraft, balloons not under Part-BOP Subpart-ADD, and sailplanes not following Part-SAO Subpart DEC. The pilot-owner may issue a CRS after **limited** pilot-owner maintenance per Appendix II.

**CRS content (c)**: entered in the **logbooks**, with:
- basic details of the maintenance;
- the **maintenance data used**;
- the completion date;
- the pilot-owner's identity, **signature** and **pilot licence number** (or equivalent).

**AMC1 ML.A.803**:
- A pilot-owner may only certify maintenance they performed themselves.
- For jointly owned aircraft, the AMP lists all competent, designated pilot-owners (ML.A.302(c)(6)), or contains a procedure for keeping that list current.
- An equivalent licence is any pilot qualification document recognised by the Member State.
- **Not holding a valid medical does not invalidate the licence** for pilot-owner purposes.

**Appendix II, basic principles**:
- (a) The pilot-owner is always responsible for maintenance they perform. They must be competent and familiar with standard practices and with the AMP.
- (b) **Permitted tasks**: simple visual inspections or operations to check the airframe, engines, systems and components for general condition, obvious damage and normal operation.
- **The pilot-owner must not release a task if** it:
  1. is a critical maintenance task;
  2. requires removing major components or a major assembly;
  3. is done to comply with an AD or an airworthiness limitation item (ALI), unless the AD or ALI specifically allows it;
  4. needs special or calibrated tools. A **torque wrench and crimping tool are allowed**;
  5. needs test equipment or special testing (for example NDT, system tests, avionics operational checks);
  6. is an unscheduled special inspection (for example a heavy-landing check);
  7. affects systems essential for IFR operations;
  8. is a complex task under Appendix III, or a component maintenance task under ML.A.502(a) or (b);
  9. is part of the 100-h/annual check, where that check is combined with the airworthiness review by an organisation or independent certifying staff.
- Points 1–9 **cannot be overridden** by less restrictive AMP instructions.
- Tasks described in the AFM or other operational manuals are **not maintenance** and need no CRS. Examples: preparing for flight, rigging sailplane wings, the pre-flight inspection, assembling a balloon basket, burner, cylinders and envelope. The person assembling remains responsible for the eligibility and serviceability of the parts.
- (c) Maintenance data (ML.A.401) must be available and followed. The data used must be referenced in the CRS ("point (d) of ML.A.803" in the text, apparently a drafting error for (c)).
- The pilot-owner must inform the contracted CAMO/CAO **no later than 30 days** after completing the tasks (ML.A.305(a)).

**AMC1 to Appendix II**:
- The lists are representative, and any other task meeting Appendix II may also be done.
- Part A covers aeroplanes, Part B rotorcraft, Part C sailplanes and powered sailplanes, Part D balloons and airships.
- Periodic inspections of any periodicity in the AMP may be done if every task complies with Appendix II. Do not generalise to "the 50-h", "the 100-h" or "the 6-month" inspection, because content differs by type. A **50-h / 6-month inspection for a fixed-wing aeroplane and the 1-year inspection for a glider may normally be eligible** for pilot-owner maintenance.
- `**` in Parts A/B means the task excludes IFR operation after pilot-owner maintenance. For IFR, appropriate certifying staff must release the task.

**Part A — aeroplanes (ATA chapter: task; all "Yes"; `**` = not for IFR)**
- 09 Towing: tow release unit and tow-cable retraction mechanism (clean, lubricate, replace tow cable including weak links); mirrors (install or replace).
- 11 Placards: install or renew placards and markings required by the AFM/AMM.
- 12 Servicing: lubrication not needing disassembly beyond non-structural items (cover plates, cowlings, fairings).
- 20 Standard practices: replace defective safety wiring or cotter keys, except in engine, transmission and flight controls. Replace or adjust simple non-structural standard fasteners, except receptacles or anchor nuts that need riveting.
- 21 Air conditioning: replace flexible hoses and ducts.
- 23 Communication: remove or replace self-contained, panel-mounted comms devices with quick-disconnect connectors, excluding IFR **.
- 24 Electrical: replace and service batteries. Repair broken circuits in non-critical equipment, except the ignition system, primary generating system, required comms/nav and primary flight instruments. Replace broken bonding cable. Replace fuses with the correct rating.
- 25 Equipment:
  - safety belts and harnesses (not airbag belts);
  - seats or seat parts, with no disassembly of primary structure or control systems;
  - non-essential self-contained panel equipment with quick-disconnect;

### 2.8 Airworthiness review and ARC (ML.A.901–907)
**Purpose**: a periodic review of the aircraft and its records keeps the airworthiness certificate valid.

**Validity**: the ARC (EASA Form 15c, Appendix IV) is issued after a satisfactory review and is **valid for 1 year** (ML.A.901(a)).

**Who may review and issue (ML.A.901(b))**, in accordance with ML.A.903:
1. the competent authority;
2. an appropriately approved CAMO or CAO (with the AR privilege);
3. an approved maintenance organisation **while performing the 100-h/annual inspection** in the AMP;
4. for Part-NCO aircraft (and balloons not under ADD, sailplanes not under DEC): independent certifying staff while performing the 100-h/annual inspection, if they hold:
   - (i) a Part-66 licence rated for the aircraft, or a national certifying-staff qualification valid for it where Part-66 does not apply; and
   - (ii) an authorisation from the authority that issued the Part-66 licence, or that is responsible for the national qualification.
- Part-66 holders may review aircraft registered in any Member State. National-qualification holders may review only aircraft registered in the State responsible for their qualification. Their ARCs get **no mutual recognition** on transfer to another Member State.
- If circumstances reveal a potential safety threat, the competent authority must do the review and issue the ARC itself (also ML.B.902(f)).
- GM1: a contracted CAMO/CAO with AR privilege does not have to perform the review. The owner may choose another CAMO/CAO, or a maintenance organisation at the annual inspection, or eligible independent certifying staff. None of those listed is obliged to accept a request.

**Extension (ML.A.901(c))**: an appropriately approved CAMO or CAO may extend the ARC **a maximum of two consecutive times, for 1 year each**, if:
1. it has continuously managed the aircraft for the previous **12 months**;
2. the aircraft has been maintained for the previous 12 months by approved maintenance organisations (including pilot-owner tasks released by the pilot-owner or by independent certifying staff);
3. it has no evidence or reason to believe the aircraft is not airworthy.

This applies whoever originally issued the ARC. The result is a full review at least every 3 years in a CAMO/CAO-managed chain: issue, extension 1, extension 2, then a new review.

**Anticipating an extension (ML.A.901(d))**: the extension may be done up to **30 days early** without losing continuity of the review pattern, so the original ARC can be placed on board. In other words the expiry date continues from the previous expiry, not from the extension date.

**Anticipating a review (ML.A.903(d))**: the review may be done up to **90 days early** without losing continuity, so the physical review can happen during a maintenance check. The new expiry is based on the previous expiry date.

**When the authority does the review (ML.A.901(e))**, the owner must provide:
- the documentation the authority requires;
- suitable accommodation for its staff at the appropriate location;
- where necessary, support from certifying staff.

**ARC invalid (ML.A.902(a))** if:
1. it is suspended or revoked;
2. the airworthiness certificate is suspended or revoked;
3. the aircraft is not on a Member State register;
4. the type certificate is suspended or revoked.

**No-fly (ML.A.902(b))**: the aircraft must not fly if the ARC is invalid, or if:
1. the continuing airworthiness of the aircraft or a fitted component does not meet Part-ML;
2. it does not conform to the EASA-approved type design;
3. it has been operated beyond AFM or airworthiness-certificate limits without appropriate action;
4. it has had an accident or incident affecting airworthiness without action to restore it;
5. a modification or repair does not comply with Part 21 or Part 21 Light.
- (c) On surrender or revocation, the ARC is returned to the competent authority.

**Documented records review (ML.A.903(a))**, 11 checks:
1. airframe, engine and propeller flight hours and cycles properly recorded;
2. the flight manual applies to the configuration and is at the latest revision;
3. all maintenance due under the AMP has been done;
4. all known defects corrected, or deferred in a controlled manner;
5. all applicable ADs applied and properly registered;
6. all modifications and repairs registered and compliant with Part 21 / Part 21 Light;
7. all life-limited components identified, registered and within limits;
8. all maintenance certified per Part-ML;
9. if required, the current mass-and-balance statement reflects the configuration and is valid;
10. the aircraft complies with the latest revision of its EASA-approved type design;
11. if required, a noise certificate matches the current configuration (Part 21 Subpart I).

**Physical survey (ML.A.903(b), (c))**: mandatory. Review staff not qualified under Part-66 must be assisted by qualified personnel. The survey must confirm:
1. required markings and placards are properly installed;
2. the aircraft complies with its approved flight manual;
3. the configuration complies with the approved documentation;
4. there is no evident defect not addressed under ML.A.403;
5. there are no inconsistencies between the aircraft and the records review.

**Issue conditions (ML.A.903(e))**: only:
1. by appropriately authorised review staff;
2. when the review is completely carried out and **all findings are closed**;
3. when any AMP discrepancy under (h) has been satisfactorily addressed.

Other ML.A.903 rules:
- **(f)** Send a copy of any ARC **issued or extended** to the Member State of registry **within 10 days**. The contract (Appendix I) repeats this as a CAMO/CAO obligation.
- **(g)** Airworthiness review tasks **must not be subcontracted**.
- **(h) AMP effectiveness review**: may be done together with the airworthiness review (ML.A.302(c)(9)), by the same person who did the review.
  - If aircraft deficiencies are linked to deficiencies in the AMP, the AMP must be amended.
  - If the reviewer disagrees with the amendment made by the owner, CAMO or CAO, they inform the competent authority of the State of registry. The authority decides, raises ML.B.903 findings, and may act under ML.B.304.
  - AMC: the reviewer may be from the authority, a CAMO, a CAO, a maintenance organisation or independent certifying staff. They are not responsible for the completeness of the AMP but may sample. See AMC1 ML.A.302(c)(9).

**Review staff qualification (ML.A.904)**:
- (a) Authority staff: per ML.B.902. That means at least **3 years** of continuing-airworthiness experience, plus a Part-66 licence, national qualification, aeronautical degree or equivalent, plus aeronautical maintenance training, plus a position authorised to sign for the authority. The licence/degree requirement can be replaced by **4 more years** of experience, so 7 years in total.
- (b) Organisation staff: per Part-M Subpart F/G, Part-145, Part-CAMO or Part-CAO.
- (c) Independent certifying staff: a Part-66 licence rated for the aircraft (or a national qualification), plus an authority authorisation.
- (d) The authorisation is issued when the authority has assessed the person's knowledge of the relevant Part-ML (CAW management, reviews, ARC issue) **and** the person has satisfactorily performed a review **under the authority's supervision**.
  - It is valid for **5 years**, provided the holder performs **at least 1 airworthiness review every 12 months**. Otherwise a new supervised review is needed.
  - It is renewable for 5 years at a time, with fresh compliance with (d)(1) and (d)(2). There is no limit on renewals.
  - The holder must keep records of all reviews performed, available on request to any competent authority and to any owner they are reviewing for.
  - The authority may revoke it at any time for competence or misuse (ML.B.304 requires revocation for poor performance or misuse).
  - GM: "corresponding/particular aircraft" means the person meets the certifying-staff requirements for that aircraft at the time of the review.

**Transfer within the EU (ML.A.905)**:
- The applicant first tells the former State the name of the new State, then applies to the new State for a new airworthiness certificate.
- The former ARC stays valid until expiry, notwithstanding ML.A.902(a)(3).
- Exception: an ARC issued by independent certifying staff with a national qualification. ML.A.906 then applies.
- ML.A.906 also applies if the aircraft was non-airworthy in the former State, or its status cannot be determined from the records.

**Import (ML.A.906)**, from a third country or a non-2018/1139 system:
- (a) The applicant applies for a new airworthiness certificate. For non-new aircraft, a satisfactory review under ML.A.901 is needed. All maintenance must comply with the approved or declared AMP.
- (b) If compliant, the reviewer (authority, CAMO/CAO, maintenance organisation or independent certifying staff) issues the ARC and sends a copy to the State of registry.
- (c) The owner allows the authority access to inspect.
- (d) The authority issues the new airworthiness certificate if Part 21 / Part 21 Light is met.

**Findings (ML.A.907)**:
- **Level 1**: significant non-compliance that lowers the safety standard and **seriously endangers** flight safety.
- **Level 2**: non-compliance that **may** lower the safety standard and **may** endanger flight safety.
- After an ML.B.903 notification, the person or organisation responsible under ML.A.201 must define and demonstrate a corrective action plan, addressing the root cause and preventing recurrence, within a period agreed with the authority.
- Authority response (ML.B.903):
  - Level 1: corrective action before further flight, and **immediate revocation or suspension** of the ARC.
  - Level 2: corrective action appropriate to the finding.

### 2.9 Section B (authority) obligations, briefly
- **ML.B.102**: designate a competent authority with documented procedures, an organisational structure, adequate staff, qualified and trained staff, and procedures kept under review.
- **ML.B.104 records**: a traceable system for issuing, continuing, changing, suspending and revoking certificates and authorisations. The per-aircraft file contains at least:
  1. the certificate of airworthiness;
  2. ARCs;
  3. reports of reviews the State performed itself;
  4. relevant correspondence;
  5. exemptions and enforcement actions;
  6. any document approved under Part-ML or Reg. 965/2012.
  - Kept until **2 years after permanent withdrawal from service**, and available to other Member States and EASA.
- **ML.B.105**: mutual exchange of information (Art. 72 of 2018/1139). Authorities assist each other on multi-State safety threats.
- **ML.B.201**: inspections and investigations.
- **ML.B.302**: record and retain exemptions under Art. 71.
- **ML.B.303**:
  - a risk-based survey programme over the register, including sample product surveys covering airworthiness key risk elements;
  - findings categorised per ML.B.903 and confirmed in writing to the person responsible under ML.A.201;
  - a procedure to analyse the safety significance of findings;
  - all findings and closure actions recorded;
  - non-compliances with other Annexes handled under those Annexes, with information exchanged as needed;
  - AMC: the Part-M M.B.303 programme is an acceptable basic structure.
- **ML.B.304**: suspend an ARC on reasonable grounds of potential safety threat, or suspend/revoke under ML.B.903(a). Revoke an independent certifying staff review authorisation for poor performance or misuse.
- **ML.B.902**:
  - reviews per ML.A.903;
  - staff qualification as above;
  - a record of all review staff (qualifications, CAW experience summary, training);
  - access to ML.A.305 and ML.A.401 data during reviews;
  - staff issue the Form 15c after a satisfactory review.

## 4. Defined terms
- **Applicable maintenance data** (ML.A.401(b)): the five-category list in 2.1.
- **Service-life-limited components** (ML.A.503(a)): components with a **certified life limit** (retire) and components with a **service life limit** (maintain to restore serviceability). Expressed in calendar time, FH, landings or cycles.
- **Unserviceable component** (ML.A.504(a)): any of the five conditions in 2.4.
- **Unsalvageable** (ML.A.504(c)): reached its certified life limit, or has a non-repairable defect or malfunction.
- **Declaration of maintenance accomplished** (ML.A.502(c), GM): a certificate in any shape or form from whoever maintained a 21.A.307(b)(3)–(6) component. It is equivalent to a Form 1.
- **Critical maintenance task** (AMC1 ML.A.402(b)(8)): tasks affecting flight path and attitude control, stability control systems, propulsive force, or overhaul, calibration or rigging of engines, propellers, transmissions and gearboxes.
- **Error-capturing method** (AMC2): re-inspection (one person) or independent inspection.
- **Required equipment** (GM1 ML.A.403): equipment required by the certification specification, operations rules, rules of the air or ATM.
- **Pilot-owner** (ML.A.803(a)): holds a valid pilot licence for the type or class and owns the aircraft as a natural person on the registration, or is a designated, decision-involved member of a non-profit recreational entity named as owner or operator.
- **Limited pilot-owner maintenance** (Appendix II): simple visual inspections or operations for general condition, obvious damage and normal operation, subject to the 9 exclusions.
- **Complex maintenance task** (Appendix III): the list in 2.7.
- **Airworthiness review staff**: the people who perform reviews, qualified per ML.A.904 / ML.B.902.
- **Level 1 / Level 2 finding** (ML.A.907): see 2.8.
- **Independent certifying staff**: certifying staff acting on their own behalf, not for an organisation (the term is used throughout, not defined here).
- **Equivalent to EASA Form 1**: the list in AMC1 ML.A.501(a)(ii).
- **SC/SR**: Standard Change / Standard Repair per CS-STAN (21.A.90B / 21.A.431B).
- **Bridging inspection**: an inspection organised using the aircraft's prior AMP when it moves to a new AMP (Appendix I(e)(1)(ii)(C)). Not defined further here.
- Abbreviations: SP = sailplane; SSPS = self-sustained powered sailplane; SLPS/TMG = self-launching powered sailplane / touring motor glider; ALI = airworthiness limitation item; NDT = non-destructive testing; MIP = minimum inspection programme; MCF = maintenance check flight; DAH = design approval holder; LSA = light sport aeroplane; CS-VLA = very light aeroplanes; CS-22 = sailplanes and powered sailplanes.

## 5. Software implications for Airworthiness+

### 5.1 Core data objects
- **Aircraft**:
  - registration, type, manufacturer, manufacturer's designation, serial number;
  - State of registry;
  - category (aeroplane / rotorcraft / sailplane / SSPS / SLPS-TMG / hot-air balloon / gas balloon / hot-air airship);
  - certification basis flags: CS-VLA, CS-22, LSA, ELA1, ELA2, declared-design (Part 21 Light);
  - operating regime: Part-NCO (yes/no), balloon under Part-BOP Subpart-ADD (yes/no), sailplane under Part-SAO Subpart DEC (yes/no). This gates pilot-owner maintenance, deferral rights, independent certifying staff ARCs and the 7-versus-30-day AOG recheck window;
  - IFR capable or operated (gates `**` tasks);
  - airworthiness certificate status; TC status;
  - mass-and-balance statement (date, validity); noise certificate;
  - AFM revision;
  - totals: airframe FH, engine and propeller hours and cycles, landings (balloons and airships do not need FH on the ARC);
  - contracted CAMO/CAO (link to Contract).
- **Contract (Appendix I)**:
  - owner or registered lessee (name or company, address); CAMO/CAO (name, address, approval reference); aircraft (registration, type, serial number);
  - type of operation;
  - mandatory statement accepted (versioned text);
  - signatures of both parties with dates;
  - status: draft / active / terminated (denounced by owner or by CAMO/CAO) / null (non-conformity);
  - check that the type is in the CAMO/CAO's terms of approval;
  - utilisation-reporting frequency agreed.
  - Termination creates a task to inform the authority within **2 weeks**. Sale of the aircraft creates a task to inform the authority and the CAMO/CAO.
- **Component**:
  - P/N, S/N, description, ATA chapter;
  - classification: `serviceable` / `unserviceable` / `unsalvageable` / `quarantined`. Plus `owner_accepted_21A307b2` and `declaration_of_maintenance_accomplished` categories;
  - release document (type: EASA Form 1, an equivalent with subtype a–i from the AMC list, a declaration of maintenance accomplished, or a 21.A.307(b) new-part document; plus reference, date, issuer, scan);
  - Part 21 Subpart Q marking;
  - eligibility against modification and AD configuration;
  - life limits (type: certified/retire or service/restore; unit: calendar, FH, landings or cycles; limit; consumed; remaining; source AMP or AD);
  - location and custody (secure store under an approved maintenance organisation or independent certifying staff, or owner custody with a transfer record);
  - disposition for unsalvageable parts (retained / mutilated with evidence / transferred to a training or research organisation).
  - **Balloon modules**: basket, burner and cylinder as interchangeable components with their own inspection dates and eligibility (TCDS) per envelope.
- **Standard part / raw material lot**:
  - specification, conformity evidence, traceability, supplier and manufacturer source;
  - a link showing that the maintenance data specifies or allows it.
- **Defect**:
  - date first identified (immutable, kept when re-deferred), description, reported by;
  - affects required equipment (yes/no); MEL used (yes/no);
  - seriously endangers flight safety (yes/no, which blocks flight);
  - deferral authority (pilot for non-required / pilot under MEL / pilot under NCO with owner or CAMO agreement, agreement record attached / certifying staff);
  - rectification limit from the maintenance data;
  - status: open / deferred / re-deferred / rectified;
  - link to the rectifying CRS.
  - Show deferred defects to the pilot before flight.
- **Maintenance task / work order**:
  - task reference, data source and revision (ML.A.401 categories), critical-task flag, error-capture method (re-inspection or independent inspection) with the inspector;
  - performed by, supervised-by link;
  - pilot-owner eligibility evaluation;
  - environmental, tooling and calibration references;
  - work-pack ID.
- **CRS (aircraft)**: see 5.3.
- **Component CRS**: a Form 1 record, or an aircraft-level release reference, per the ML.A.502(b) table.
- **SC/SR embodiment (Form 123)**: all 12 boxes, document list with revision and asterisk flag, parts list. Links to the CRS (same signer), the logbook entry, AMP review, and owner acknowledgement (box 12, or the CAMO/CAO representative with an "inform owner before next flight" task).
- **Airworthiness review**: see 5.2.
- **ARC (Form 15c)**: reference, issuer type (authority / CAMO / CAO / maintenance organisation / independent certifying staff), issuer details (name, address, approval reference, or name and Part-66 or national licence number), authorisation number, new-aircraft flag (authority only), dates of issue and expiry, airframe FH at review, extensions 1 and 2 (issue, expiry, FH, signer, authorisation number, company, approval reference), mutual-recognition flag (false if issued by independent certifying staff with a national qualification), status (valid / expired / suspended / revoked / surrendered / invalid-derived), date sent to the State of registry, returned-to-authority date.
- **Person / credential**:
  - pilot licence (number, type/class ratings, issuing State, valid). The medical is **not** required for pilot-owner status;
  - ownership link (natural person on the registration, or designated member of a non-profit recreational entity, with the designation document);
  - Part-66 licence and ratings or national qualification;
  - ARS authorisation (issuing authority, issue date, expiry at 5 years, last review date). Alert if no review in the last 12 months;
  - log of reviews performed;
  - organisation approvals with the AR privilege.
- **Finding (Level 1 / Level 2)**: source (authority survey, ML.B.903), date notified, corrective action plan (root cause, due date agreed with the authority), status. Level 1 sets the ARC to suspended or revoked and blocks flight.

### 5.2 Airworthiness review workflow
1. **Trigger and scheduling**:
   - Compute ARC expiry.
   - Open the review window **90 days** before expiry (ML.A.903(d)). Open the extension window **30 days** before expiry (ML.A.901(d)).
   - Either way, the new expiry = previous expiry + 1 year (continuity preserved). If done earlier than the window, the expiry is based on the completion date (a pattern reset). Warn the user.
   - Alerts at, for example, 90/60/30/7 days before expiry, and on expiry (ARC invalid, aircraft grounded).
2. **Path decision**:
   - **Extension**: offered only if the issuer is a CAMO/CAO with continuous management for the last 12 months (check the contract start date), all maintenance in the last 12 months was by approved organisations (including pilot-owner tasks released by the pilot-owner or independent certifying staff), and no evidence of non-airworthiness. Allow at most 2 consecutive extensions.
   - **Full review**: required after the 2nd extension, or if the conditions fail.
   - Show eligible reviewers: authority; CAMO/CAO with AR privilege; maintenance organisation only together with the 100-h/annual inspection; independent certifying staff only for Part-NCO, non-ADD balloons and non-DEC sailplanes, together with the 100-h/annual, with a valid authorisation and an aircraft rating, and for national-qualification holders only for aircraft on their State's register.
   - The contracted CAMO is not obliged to do it, and the owner may choose another reviewer.
3. **Records review checklist**: the 11 ML.A.903(a) items, each with status (compliant / finding), evidence links (hours log, AFM revision, AMP due-list, defect list, AD compliance list, mods and repairs list including Form 123s, life-limited components list, CRS list, mass and balance, type design revision, noise certificate) and reviewer notes. Auto-populate from system data and flag gaps: overdue AMP items, ADs not recorded, life-limited parts over limit, CRSs missing required fields, open defects without a controlled deferral.
4. **Physical survey checklist**: the 5 ML.A.903(c) items, plus a record of the assisting Part-66 person if the reviewer is not Part-66 qualified. Date and location.
5. **AMP effectiveness review (optional, ML.A.903(h))**: same reviewer. Deficiencies, AMP amendment required, owner/CAMO/CAO response, reviewer agreement. If there is disagreement, a task to inform the competent authority.
6. **Findings**: each must be closed before the ARC can be issued (hard block). Link findings to defects or work orders.
7. **Issue**:
   - Generate Form 15c with prefilled fields, tailored per footnote (**).
   - The signer must be appropriately authorised.
   - No subcontracting: the review must be done entirely by the issuing reviewer or organisation. Enforce one reviewer entity.
   - Optionally put the 100-h/annual CRS on the reverse.
8. **Post-issue**:
   - A task and alert: send a copy to the State of registry **within 10 days**, with the sent date recorded. Overdue alert.
   - Add to the independent certifying staff's review log, which updates their 12-month currency.
   - Place the original on board (track that the physical ARC is on the aircraft).
9. **Invalidation monitoring**:
   - Automatic ARC invalid status if the CofA is suspended or revoked, the aircraft is deregistered, the TC is suspended or revoked, or the ARC is suspended or revoked.
   - A no-fly flag for the ML.A.902(b) conditions (non-compliant continuing airworthiness, type design non-conformity, exceedance of limits, accident or incident without action, non-compliant mod or repair).
   - A task to return the ARC to the authority on surrender or revocation.
10. **Transfer and import**:
    - EU transfer: keep the ARC to expiry unless it was issued by independent certifying staff with a national qualification, or the aircraft is non-airworthy or its status indeterminable. In those cases, route to the import-style review.
    - Import: new CofA application, review (for non-new aircraft), all AMP maintenance complied with, ARC copy to the State.
    - UK: post-Brexit, EU-to-UK transfers are likely treated as imports (a guess).

### 5.3 Certificate of release to service
- **Required fields** (validation):
  - basic details of the work;
  - completion date;
  - issuer identity, by issuer type:
    - organisation: approval reference and certifying staff;
    - independent certifying staff: name and licence number;
    - pilot-owner: name, signature, pilot licence number;
  - limitations (free text plus structured flags);
  - maintenance data reference and revision (mandatory for the pilot-owner, recommended for others);
  - hours, cycles or landings at completion;
  - certification statement chosen by issuer type (standard or pilot-owner wording, from AMC1 ML.A.801(e)).
- **States**: draft, signed (complete), signed (incomplete, with limitations), superseded or rechecked.
- **Block issue** when there is a known non-compliance that endangers flight safety (open defects flagged "seriously endangers", unresolved findings).
- **Incomplete-maintenance CRS**: a checkbox that requires the limitations text. Link to a follow-up CRS (for example after a maintenance check flight).
- **AOG derogation**:
  - The owner authorises a person with at least 3 years of experience. Capture the qualifications.
  - Countdown to recheck: 7 days, or 30 days for NCO, non-ADD balloons and non-DEC sailplanes.
  - Notify the CAMO/CAO or authority within 7 days.
  - Alert until recheck by an ML.A.801(b) issuer.
- **E-signature**: personal credential plus second factor (equivalent to card plus PIN). Show that only that person can sign. A stamp image is optional. Audit trail.
- **Supervised work**: assistants under the certifying staff's direct and continuous control. Their tasks are signed by an authorised person.
- **Work-pack summary mode**: a CRS summary with a unique work-pack ID.
- **Logbook entry**: pilot-owner CRSs must be entered in the logbooks. Generate a logbook-format entry.
- **SC/SR**: if the CRS includes an SC/SR, require a linked Form 123 signed by the same person. Block pilot-owner issue where CS-STAN restricts it.
- **Component release routing** (the ML.A.502(b) table as a rule engine). Inputs: data source (component manufacturer or aircraft manufacturer), maintenance type (overhaul or other), component type (engine, propeller, other), aircraft class (CS-VLA, CS-22, LSA or other). Outputs: Form 1 issuer allowed, and aircraft-level release allowed or not.
- A declaration of maintenance accomplished is allowed for 21.A.307(b)(3)–(6) parts.

### 5.4 Pilot-owner maintenance module
- **Eligibility gate (per user, per aircraft)**:
  - valid pilot licence with the type or class rating (the medical does not matter);
  - ownership on the registration, or a designated member of a non-profit recreational entity;
  - aircraft regime is Part-NCO, non-ADD balloon or non-DEC sailplane;
  - for joint ownership, the user is on the AMP's list of designated pilot-owners (or the AMP procedure).
- **Task library**: seed with the AMC1 Appendix II tables:
  - Part A aeroplanes, Part B rotorcraft, Part C sailplanes with SP/SSPS/SLPS-TMG columns, Part D balloons and airships with hot-air airship / hot-air balloon / gas balloon columns;
  - fields per task: ATA, area, task text, applicable categories with Yes/No/n/a, IFR-exclusion `**` flag, and conditions (for example quick-disconnect, non-electrically-indicated chip detector, spin-on filter, same blades in the original position).
  - Allow custom tasks, but run them through the **Appendix II nine-point exclusion checklist**: critical task; major component removal; AD/ALI (unless permitted); special or calibrated tools other than torque wrench or crimper; test equipment or NDT; unscheduled special inspection; IFR-essential system; Appendix III complex task or ML.A.502(a)/(b) component maintenance; part of a 100-h/annual combined with an ARC.
  - The AMP cannot override these exclusions.
- **IFR logic**: if a `**` task is released by the pilot-owner, set an "IFR not permitted until released by certifying staff" flag on the aircraft.
- **Appendix III blocklist**: structural keywords and ATA areas, the 15 cm pressurised-skin threshold, and engine dismantling, propeller balancing and similar items.
- **Execution**: maintenance data must be referenced (ML.A.401) and appear on the CRS. The pilot-owner may only certify their own work.
- **Notification**: if there is a CAMO/CAO contract, auto-notify it, with a deadline of **30 days** after completion and an overdue alert.
- **AFM tasks** (rigging, pre-flight, balloon assembly) are not maintenance and need no CRS. Still prompt an eligibility and serviceability check of the parts being assembled (balloon modules).
- A periodic inspection (for example the 50-h/6-month for aeroplanes, the 1-year for gliders) may be a pilot-owner package only if every task passes the checks. Do not mark whole periodic inspections as pilot-owner by default.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM (detail)
- **AMC1 ML.A.402**: acceptable maintenance records are the ticked and signed 100-h/annual checklist plus a CRS listing the tasks. This supports a checklist-based e-record with signatures.
- **AMC1 ML.A.402(b)(7)**: sign-off only after task completion. Grouping must keep critical steps identifiable. Supervised staff work is countersigned. Software: per-step sign-off, critical-step markers, countersignature.
- **AMC1/AMC2 ML.A.402(b)(8)**: the four categories of critical tasks and the two error-capture methods (re-inspection by the same person when alone, or independent inspection). Software: a critical-task flag requires an error-capture record before the CRS.
- **AMC1 ML.A.403**: defects are malfunctions seen in flight, or faults found by inspection or test. Pilot comments are not automatically defects. Keep a separate "observation" record type.
- **GM1 ML.A.403**: consult available certifying staff before deferring. A pilot under NCO (or a non-ADD balloon or non-DEC sailplane) can defer required equipment whether or not a CAMO/CAO is contracted, but needs owner or CAMO/CAO agreement. Defines "required".
- **AMC1 ML.A.403(d)**: deferred defects are communicated before crew arrival, listed on the deferred list, fixed at the next appropriate event, and keep the original date if re-entered.
- **GM1 ML.A.501(a)**: new components under 21.A.307(b) / 21L.A.193(b) need no Form 1. Their accompanying document conditions are in point (c).
- **AMC1 ML.A.501(a)(ii)**: the 9-item list of Form 1 equivalents with date cut-offs (see 2.4). Implement as a picklist with date validation.
- **AMC1 ML.A.501(e)**: balloon basket, burner and cylinder swaps need no Form 1 if serviceable records are accessible. Watch inspection dates. Maintenance on them still needs a Form 1, an equivalent, or a balloon logbook release.
- **GM1 ML.A.502**: independent certifying staff may release component maintenance only where ML.A.201 lets them maintain and they are competent (not for ADD balloons).
- **GM1 ML.A.502(c)**: the declaration of maintenance accomplished is free form and needs no approval. Records must show the component was first installed new as a (b)(3)–(6) component. Reject unreadable or unsatisfactory declarations.
- **AMC1 ML.A.801 (SC/SR)**: the long guidance in 2.5. Key points: the installer is responsible for CS-STAN compliance; Form 123; the same signer for Form 123 and the CRS; a logbook reference; owner copies; one Form 123 for related SCs; the owner, CAMO or CAO assesses ICA impact on the AMP; information is consulted at the airworthiness review.
- **AMC1 ML.A.801(e)**: the two statement wordings; task reference with revision; life-limit relative timing; work-pack summarising; e-signature (personal card plus PIN, authority satisfied, stamp optional); legible records; independent certifying staff keep proof records.
- **AMC1 ML.A.801(f)**: release incomplete maintenance before a required flight, with the operator responsible for the maintenance check flight, then a full CRS after.
- **AMC1 ML.A.803**: own-work-only rule; joint-owner list in the AMP; equivalent licence; medical not needed.
- **GM1 ML.A.901**: freedom to choose the reviewer. No obligation on listed parties to accept.
- **AMC1 ML.A.903(h)**: the AMP review at the airworthiness review is by the reviewer, not responsible for AMP completeness, sampling allowed.
- **GM1 ML.A.904(c);(d)**: "corresponding aircraft" means meeting certifying-staff requirements at the time of the review. The authorisation needs a knowledge assessment plus a supervised review.
- **AMC1 ML.B.201**: the AMP data-collection template (see 3.10).
- **AMC1 ML.B.303**: reuse the Part-M survey programme structure.
- **AMC1 to Appendix II**: the task lists are representative, not exhaustive. Periodic inspections are eligible only task by task. A 50-h/6-month aeroplane inspection and a 1-year glider inspection may normally qualify. `**` means no IFR until released by certifying staff.

### Possible UK differences (guesses, verify)
- Form names: "CAA Form 15c" / "UK CAA Form 1".
- The ARC copy goes to the UK CAA within 10 days.
- References to "Member State" or "Agency" become the CAA.
- ARC mutual-recognition and EU transfer provisions do not apply. Aircraft from the EU register are treated as imports.
