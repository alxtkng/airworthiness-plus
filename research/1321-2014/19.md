## 2. Obligations (with exact numbers)

### 2.1 Certification, application and AltMoC
- **Application (CAMO.A.115):** use the authority's form and manner. AMC1: **EASA Form 2** (Appendix I to AMC1 CAMO.A.115) or equivalent. One Form 2 can cover several certificate types under 1321/2014.
- **Initial application contents (CAMO.A.115(b)):** (1) results of an internal **pre-audit** against Part-M, Part-ML and Part-CAMO; (2) compliance documentation, including the **procedure for changes not requiring prior approval**.
- **AMC1 CAMO.A.115(b)(2):** the minimum documentation is the CAME, including:
  - for CAT, commercial specialised operations and commercial ATO/DTO operations, a description of the aircraft technical log (ATL) system;
  - the technical content of any subcontract for CAW management tasks.
  On request, the CAMO must show arrangements exist for all base and scheduled line maintenance "for an appropriate period of time". If M.A.201(ea) applies, the CAW management contract with the operator(s) (Appendix I to Part-M) is submitted with the CAME. UK?
- **AMC2 CAMO.A.115:** submit drafts early. Certification cannot happen until the completed documents are received. The pre-audit shows internal verification of compliance and lets the authority size its oversight.
- **GM1 CAMO.A.115(b):** for an initial certificate the authority may limit the scope of changes not requiring prior approval for the first period of operation.
- **AltMoC (CAMO.A.120):** before use, give the authority a full description, the relevant manual or procedure revisions, and a compliance assessment. Use only after **prior approval** and notification under CAMO.B.120. Changes to an AltMoC need prior approval (GM1 CAMO.A.130(b)). The CAME lists currently approved AltMoCs (CAMO.A.300(a)(14)).

### 2.2 Privileges (CAMO.A.125)
- (a) The approval is shown on the certificate (Appendix I).
- (b) For air carriers licensed under Reg. 1008/2008, the approval is part of the AOC, except when the CAMO is contracted under M.A.201(ea) by operators forming a single air carrier business grouping.
- (c) The scope of work is specified in the CAME.
- (d) Base privileges:
  1. manage CAW of aircraft listed on the certificate (not licensed air carrier aircraft);
  2. manage CAW of licensed air carrier aircraft listed on both the certificate and the AOC, or when M.A.201(ea) applies;
  3. arrange for limited CAW tasks to be done by subcontracted organisations working under its management system, as listed on the certificate;
  4. **extend an ARC** under M.A.901(f) or ML.A.901(c);
  5. **approve the AMP** under ML.A.302(b)(2) for Part-ML aircraft.
- (e) Optional, only for organisations with their principal place of business in a Member State: **airworthiness reviews** per M.A.901 or ML.A.903, and
  1. issue the ARC and extend it under M.A.901(c)(2) and (e)(2) or ML.A.901(c);
  2. issue a **recommendation** for the airworthiness review to the State of registry authority (M.A.901(d) or M.A.904(b)).
- (f) Optional, only with (e): issue a **permit to fly** per 21.A.711(d) for the particular aircraft for which it may issue ARCs, when attesting conformity with approved flight conditions. Needs an adequate CAME procedure.
  - GM1 CAMO.A.125(f): for Part-M aircraft used by licensed air carriers and aircraft above **2 730 kg MTOM**, a PtF only for aircraft in a controlled environment managed by that CAMO. For Part-M aircraft of 2 730 kg MTOM or less not used by licensed air carriers, and for all Part-ML aircraft, a PtF can be issued for any aircraft.
- GM1 CAMO.A.125(e):
  - A CAMO may hold (d) only and contract airworthiness reviews to another suitably approved CAMO for the same type, which need not be linked to an AOC holder.
  - (e) for a type requires (d) for that type.
  - The CAMO need not currently manage that type to do ARs on it. Not managing a type does not by itself lead to the type being removed, provided all Part-CAMO requirements (facilities, documentation, personnel, management system) remain met.

### 2.3 Subcontracting of CAW management tasks (AMC1 CAMO.A.125(d)(3), CAMO.A.205, GM1 CAMO.A.205)
- The subcontractor works as an integral part of the CAMO's management system, regardless of its own approvals (CAMO or Part-145). The CAMO remains **accountable** for the tasks.
- Control is exercised by active control (direct involvement) and/or by endorsing the subcontractor's recommendations.
- **Permitted subcontracted tasks (limit):**
  1. AD analysis and planning;
  2. SB analysis;
  3. planning of maintenance;
  4. reliability monitoring and engine health monitoring;
  5. AMP development and amendments;
  6. other activities agreed by the authority that do not limit the CAMO's responsibilities.
- Controls are reflected in the contract and in line with the CAME. The management system is considered extended to the subcontractor.
- Normally **one subcontractor per aircraft type** for any combination of Appendix II activities, except for engines and APUs. With more than one, the CAMO must show coordination controls and clear responsibilities.
- **No further sub-subcontracting** may be authorised.
- Contracts must be acceptable to the authority. Subcontract **only to organisations specified by the authority on EASA Form 14** (the certificate). Adding a subcontractor is a change needing prior approval (GM1 CAMO.A.130(a)(1)).
- The subcontractor notifies the CAMO of changes affecting the contract as soon as practical, and the CAMO informs the authority. Failure to do so may invalidate the authority's acceptance of the contract.
- CAMO.A.205(a): when contracting maintenance or subcontracting any part of CAW management, the activities must conform to the requirements, and the related hazards must be covered by the management system. (b) The subcontractor works under the CAMO's approval, and the authority gets access to it.
- GM1 CAMO.A.205:
  - All subcontracted activities are subject to hazard identification and risk management (CAMO.A.200(a)(3)) and compliance monitoring (CAMO.A.200(a)(6)), whatever the subcontractor's approval status.
  - Compliance monitoring must at least check that each contracted maintenance organisation's approval **covers the contracted activities and is still valid**.
  - Establish occurrence reporting interfaces with contracted MOs (their own reporting duty is not replaced) and internal safety reporting interfaces with subcontractors.

### 2.4 Changes to the organisation (CAMO.A.130 + AMC/GM)
- **Prior approval required (a):**
  1. changes affecting the scope of the certificate or the terms of approval;
  2. changes to nominated persons under CAMO.A.305(a)(3) to (a)(5) and (b)(2);
  3. changes to reporting lines between those persons and the accountable manager;
  4. the procedure for changes not requiring prior approval.
- **(b)** Apply **before** the change and provide the relevant documentation. Implement only after **formal approval** (CAMO.B.330). Operate under any conditions the authority sets during the change.
- **(c)** Changes not needing prior approval are managed and notified per the agreed procedure (CAMO.A.115(b), approved under CAMO.B.310(h)).
- **AMC1 time frames:**
  - Application for amendment of the certificate at least **30 working days** before the intended change.
  - Planned change of a nominated person: inform the authority at least **20 working days** before.
  - Unforeseen changes: notify at the earliest opportunity.
- **AMC2:** manage the safety risk of changes via management of change (AMC1 CAMO.A.200(a)(3)(e)). For prior-approval changes, do a **risk assessment** and provide it to the authority on request.
- **GM1 CAMO.A.130(a)(1) examples of scope or terms changes:**
  1. name of the organisation;
  2. principal place of business;
  3. additional aircraft type, series or group;
  4. accountable manager;
  5. additional subcontracted organisation;
  6. M.A.201(ea) contracts with business grouping operators.
- **GM2:** a change of name requires a **new application as a matter of urgency**. If it is the only change, the previous documentation can be resubmitted.
- **GM1 CAMO.A.130(b): other changes needing prior approval:**
  - (a) AltMoC (CAMO.A.120(b));
  - (b) the CAME procedure for an airworthiness review under supervision of the organisation's authorised ARS (CAMO.A.310(c));
  - (c) the competency procedure (CAMO.A.305(g));
  - (d) the system for reporting safety performance and compliance to the authority when the oversight planning cycle is extended beyond **36 months** (CAMO.B.305(d));
  - (e) the indirect AMP approval procedure for Part-M aircraft (M.A.302(c)).
- GM1 CAMO.A.130 explains the structure: (a) lists specific prior-approval cases; (b) covers every case where the Regulation requires approval; (c) allows agreed changes without prior approval, depending on compliance and safety performance and the organisation's change management capability.

### 2.5 Continued validity and access (CAMO.A.135, CAMO.A.140)
- The certificate is valid while: the organisation complies (taking into account findings handling under CAMO.B.350); the authority is granted access; and it is not surrendered or revoked. There is **no expiry date**: validity is unlimited, subject to these conditions.
- For licensed air carriers, termination, suspension or revocation of the AOC automatically invalidates the CAMO certificate for the AOC registrations, unless the authority states otherwise.
- Exception for M.A.201(ea): the CAMO certificate is not invalidated, but the Appendix I (Part-M) contract becomes void. UK?
- Return the certificate **without delay** on revocation or surrender.
- **Access** "at any time" to any facility, aircraft, document, record, data, procedure or other material, whether contracted or subcontracted or not, for persons authorised by the competent authority, or by the authority acting under CAMO.B.300(d) or (e).

### 2.6 Findings (CAMO.A.150 + AMC/GM)
- After a finding is notified (CAMO.B.350):
  1. identify root cause(s) and contributing factors;
  2. define a **corrective action plan**;
  3. demonstrate implementation to the authority's satisfaction.
- All three must be done **within the period agreed** with the authority (CAMO.B.350). Level 1 and 2 definitions and time limits are in Section B, not in this chunk.
- AMC1: the action plan addresses the effects, root cause(s) and contributing factor(s), and as needed **correction/containment, corrective action and preventive action**.
- AMC2: if a finding (authority or internal) affects management system harmonisation under M.A.201(ea), inform the operator(s) through an agreed channel. If the group uses common standards, inform the group management board. UK?
- GM1 causal analysis: focus on **why**, not who. Consider systemic factors (regulatory, HF, organisational, technical). Avoid simple linear models such as a fault tree and "quick fixes". Peer review of the causal analysis is suggested. A system description of the organisation supports both causal and hazard analyses.

### 2.7 Immediate reaction and occurrence reporting (CAMO.A.155, CAMO.A.160)
- **Implement:** safety measures mandated by the authority (CAMO.B.135) and EASA mandatory safety information (e.g. ADs and EASA safety directives).
- **Occurrence reporting system** per Reg. 376/2014 and 2015/1018, as part of the management system.
- **Report to the competent authority AND to the organisation responsible for the design of the aircraft:** any incident, malfunction, technical defect, exceeding of technical limitations, occurrence showing inaccurate, incomplete or ambiguous Part 21 (or Part 21 Light) data, or other irregular circumstance that has or may have endangered safe operation, and has not resulted in an accident or serious incident.
- Reports use the authority's form and manner and contain all pertinent information known.
- **Timing: as soon as possible, and in any case within 72 hours** of the organisation identifying the condition, unless exceptional circumstances prevent this.
- **Follow-up report**, where relevant, giving preventive actions as soon as they are identified.
- AMC1:
  - With multiple certificates, an integrated reporting system is allowed.
  - Single reports are allowed only if they include all relevant perspectives, address all mandatory data fields, identify all certificate holders concerned, and the same authority covers all certificates and has agreed.
  - Assign one or more qualified persons to coordinate airworthiness occurrence actions and investigations. If several, designate a **single focal point** with a single reporting channel to the accountable manager.
- AMC2 (ED 2023/013): share relevant safety occurrence reports with the design approval holder (DAH) or the declarant of a declaration of design compliance, and liaise on service information. UK? (declarant wording comes from Part 21 Light)
- GM1: the 2015/1018 list is **not exhaustive**. See AMC 20-8.
- GM1 CAMO.A.160(b): the "organisation responsible for the design" is the holder of a TC, restricted TC, STC, ETSO authorisation, repair or change approval, or other approval or declaration.

### 2.8 Management system and safety management (CAMO.A.200 + AMC/GM)
- **CAMO.A.200(a): the system must include**
  1. clearly defined lines of responsibility and accountability, including the **direct safety accountability of the accountable manager**;
  2. a safety policy;
  3. hazard identification, risk evaluation and management, mitigation, and verification of effectiveness;
  4. trained and competent personnel;
  5. documentation of all key processes, including a process to make personnel aware of their responsibilities and a documentation amendment procedure;
  6. a **compliance monitoring function** with a feedback system of findings to the accountable manager;
  7. any additional requirements.
- (b) Proportionate to size, nature, complexity and risks.
- (c) May be integrated with other certificates under 2018/1139.
- (d) For licensed air carriers it must be integrated with the operator's management system.
- (e) Under M.A.201(ea) it must be harmonised with the business grouping operators' systems. UK?
- **GM1 CAMO.A.200:**
  - The framework (CAMO.A.200, .202, .205) covers the ICAO Annex 19 Appendix 2 SMS core elements.
  - Compared with the old Part-M Subpart G quality system, the new elements are the safety policy and objectives, just-culture safety reporting, and hazard identification and risk management.
  - **Key safety management processes:** hazard identification; safety risk management; internal investigation; safety performance monitoring and measurement; management of change; continuous improvement; immediate safety action and coordination with the operator's ERP.
  - Compliance monitoring outputs feed risk management, and risk management sets the focus areas for compliance monitoring.
- **AMC1 CAMO.A.200(a)(1): organisation**
  - A **safety manager** and a **safety review board (SRB)** are included in the structure.
  - The SRB is a high-level committee chaired by the **accountable manager**, made up of the persons nominated under CAMO.A.305(a) and (b).
  - The SRB monitors safety performance against the policy and objectives, the timeliness of safety actions, and the effectiveness of processes. It may also review compliance monitoring results and corrective and preventive action implementation. It ensures resources are allocated.
  - The safety manager (or a designee) may attend SRB meetings.
  - Small organisations may omit a formal SRB, subject to a risk assessment and authority agreement. The SRB tasks then go to the safety manager.
- **GM1 (a)(1): safety action group (SAG)**
  - Optional, standing or ad hoc, possibly several. It reports to the SRB.
  - Tasks: monitor safety performance, define risk controls, assess the impact of organisational change, ensure timely safety actions, and review the effectiveness of safety actions and promotion.
- **GM2 (a)(1):** accountability cannot be delegated; responsibility can be delegated.
- **AMC1 (a)(2): safety policy**
  - Content: commitment to safety and a positive safety culture; internal reporting principles encouraging reports of CAW errors, incidents and hazards; cooperation with compliance monitoring and investigations.
  - Endorsed by the accountable manager, communicated with visible endorsement, and periodically reviewed.
  - Commitments: comply with legislation and improve; provide resources; apply HF principles; safety as a primary responsibility of all managers; **just culture** (do not use occurrence information to blame front-line staff for actions commensurate with their experience and training, or for any non-safety purpose).
  - **Safety objectives** form the basis of safety performance monitoring, reflect continuous improvement, are communicated, and are periodically reviewed.
  - GM1: just culture principles come from Reg. 376/2014 Art. 16(11).
- **AMC1 (a)(3): key processes**
  - (a) **Hazard identification:** a reporting scheme for reactive events and proactive hazards (collect, record, analyse, act, give feedback). Include HF hazards and hazards from organisational set-up or complex contracting and subcontracting chains.
  - (b) **Risk management:** analysis (probability and severity), assessment (tolerability), and control (mitigation) to an acceptable level. Specify the management levels that have authority to decide risk tolerability.
  - (c) **Internal investigation:** of errors and near misses under just culture. The scope goes beyond CAMO.A.160 reportables to include CAMO.A.202(b) internal reports.
  - (d) **Safety performance monitoring and measurement:** against policy and objectives. May include safety reporting (including compliance status), safety reviews and trend reviews (on new products, equipment, procedures or organisational change), safety audits, and safety surveys.
  - (e) **Management of change:** a documented process to identify external and internal changes with adverse safety effect, using the existing hazard and risk processes.
  - (f) **Continuous improvement:** via external audits, assessments (including of safety culture and SRM effectiveness), staff and cultural surveys, monitoring the recurrence of incidents, SPI evaluation, and lessons learnt.
  - (g) **Immediate safety action:** a procedure to act promptly on concerns with immediate flight safety effect, with clear instructions on who to contact at the owner or operator and how, **including outside normal business hours**. If applicable, a procedure to support the operator's **ERP** when it is triggered.
- **GM1 (a)(3): interfaces in complex arrangements**
  - Identify all parties, including independent experts and non-approved organisations.
  - Assess across all subcontract levels: coordination and interfaces, procedures, communication and reporting channels, task allocation and authorities, and key personnel qualifications.
  - Aim for one party responsible per aspect with no overlaps, clear reporting lines, and the ability of staff to notify the organisation directly of an obviously unacceptable risk.
  - Hold regular communication among the parties.
- **GM2 (a)(3): management of change**
  - Any change, large or small, triggers hazard identification and risk management.
  - Examples: organisational structure; a new aircraft type in the terms of approval; more aircraft of the same or similar type; significant personnel changes or high turnover; new or amended regulations; security arrangements; economic or financial pressure; new schedules, locations, equipment or procedures; new subcontractors.
  - Consider HF impact (including computer system changes) and the transition period.
  - Use formal hazard identification and risk assessment for major changes and changes in key personnel.
  - Review previous risk assessments.
- **AMC1 (a)(4): safety communication**
  - Ensure awareness of SMS activities, convey safety-critical information, and explain why actions are taken or procedures changed. Regular staff meetings are one means.
  - GM1: safety promotion = training + communication + information sharing.
- **GM1 (a)(5): documentation**
  - The policy, objectives and key processes can be in a separate Safety Management Manual or Management System Manual, or in CAME Part 2.
  - Separately kept documents must be referenced and are integral to the management system documentation.

### 2.9 Compliance monitoring (AMC1 to AMC4 CAMO.A.200(a)(6), GM)
- **Independence:** audits and inspections are done by personnel **not responsible for** the functions, procedures or products audited.
- **Independent audit:**
  - Routine sample checks of all aspects, including some **product sampling**.
  - An **audit plan** shows when and how often Part-M, Part-ML and Part-CAMO activities are audited.
  - **All aspects of Part-CAMO compliance are verified every year (12 months), including all subcontracted activities.** This can be one exercise or spread over the year.
  - A common procedure does not need checking against each product line if it has been verified yearly without findings. Where findings exist, check it against other product lines until the findings are closed.
- **Extension:** with no safety-related findings, the audit planning cycle may be increased by **up to 100%** (i.e. up to 24 months), subject to a risk assessment and/or mitigation and authority agreement.
- **Multiple approved locations:** each location is audited every year, or at a risk-assessed interval agreed by the authority that does not exceed the applicable audit planning cycle.
- **An audit report is issued for each audit:** what was checked and the non-compliance findings.
- **Contracted auditors:** they work under the compliance monitoring manager's responsibility. The organisation ensures their knowledge and experience, including in compliance monitoring, and retains ultimate responsibility, especially for corrective action follow-up.
- **Feedback system:**
  - It must **not be contracted out**.
  - For each non-compliance: root cause(s) and contributing factors, and corrective actions defined. Define who addresses it and the escalation procedure if the corrective action is overdue.
  - Audit reports go to the relevant departments with **target closure dates** agreed with them before confirmation. The departments implement and report status back.
  - Unless the SRB reviews compliance monitoring results, the accountable manager holds regular progress meetings. These can be delegated day to day to the compliance monitoring manager, but the accountable manager meets senior staff **at least twice per year** and receives **at least a half-yearly summary report** of non-compliance findings.
  - Audit and feedback records are kept for the CAMO.A.220(b) period (5 years) or as long as needed to support changes to the audit planning cycle, **whichever is longer**.
- GM1: the compliance monitoring function itself is subject to independent compliance monitoring.
- GM1 (a)(6) and CAMO.B.300: remote audits using ICT follow GM1 145.A.200(a)(6) and 145.B.300 (not reproduced).

### 2.10 Integration and harmonisation (GM1 CAMO.A.200(e)) [ED 2022/017, UK?]
- A "management system" is a set of interrelated organisation policies, procedures, standards and processes under an overarching safety culture.
- An integrated system (AOC + CAMO) is linked to the operator's accountable manager's corporate authority, possibly with a common SMM.
- Harmonisation in a business grouping uses common standards and needs continuous exchange of hazard, risk, decision, action, compliance and best-practice information.
- An M.A.201(ea) CAMO serves at least 2 operators, which are then harmonised with each other.

### 2.11 Information security (CAMO.A.200A)
- An ISMS per Reg. 2023/203, in addition to the management system, to manage information security risks that may affect aviation safety. **Applicable from 22 February 2026.** UK? Likely not retained; the UK has its own cyber oversight approach (guess).

### 2.12 Internal safety reporting scheme (CAMO.A.202 + AMC/GM)
- Collects and evaluates CAMO.A.160 occurrences **and** internally reported errors, near misses and hazards.
- Identifies causes and contributing factors and addresses them through SRM. Evaluates all known information, including inability to follow procedures, and circulates it.
- **Access must be given to any subcontracted organisation.**
- Cooperate on safety investigations with organisations contributing significantly to the CAMO's safety.
- AMC1 elements:
  - **confidential**, encouraging free and frank reporting;
  - aims and objectives with corporate commitment;
  - a just culture policy and implementation procedures;
  - a process to select reports for investigation, investigate all causal factors (technical, organisational, managerial, HF), and analyse trends where proportionate;
  - corrective actions;
  - initial and recurrent training for investigators;
  - cooperation with the owner or operator on investigations.

### 2.15 Airworthiness review staff (ARS) (CAMO.A.310 + AMC)
- **Qualifications (a):** ARS issuing ARCs or recommendations, and PtFs if applicable, must have:
  1. **at least 5 years' experience in continuing airworthiness**;
  2. an appropriate **Part-66 licence**, an **aeronautical degree** or a national equivalent;
  3. **formal aeronautical maintenance training**;
  4. a **position with appropriate responsibilities** in the approved organisation.
- (b) (a)(2) can be replaced by **5 additional years** of CAW experience, i.e. 10 years in total.
- (c) An authorisation is issued only after **formal acceptance by the authority** following a satisfactory **airworthiness review under supervision** of the authority, or of the organisation's authorised ARS, per a CAME procedure approved by the authority.
- (d) Recent experience: ARS must show appropriate recent CAW management experience.
- AMC1 (a):
  - ARS are needed only for the (e) and (f) privileges.
  - "Experience in continuing airworthiness" means any appropriate combination of experience in aircraft maintenance, CAW management and/or surveillance of such tasks.
  - A person meeting AMC1 CAMO.A.305(c)(e) is treated as holding the equivalent of an aeronautical degree.
  - **Appropriate Part-66 licence:** B1 or L licence in the subcategory of the aircraft reviewed; or B2 or C; or for **piston-engine non-pressurised aeroplanes of 2 000 kg MTOM and below**, B3. Part-66 experience requirements need not be met at the time of the review.
  - **Appropriate position** means independent of the airworthiness management process, or having overall authority over the CAW management of complete aircraft.
    - Independence can be achieved by: reviewing only aircraft the person did not manage (e.g. a different type); MO staff of a CAMO that also holds an MO approval who are not involved in managing that aircraft and **did not release it to service** (except maintenance during or arising from the physical survey); or compliance monitoring department staff.
    - Overall authority can be achieved by: the AM or the nominated post holder; or reviewing only aircraft for which the person is responsible for the complete CAW process. In one-man organisations the person always has overall authority.
- AMC1 (a)(3) **formal aeronautical maintenance training**, with evidence, on: initial and continuing airworthiness regulations; operational requirements if applicable; the organisation's CAME; a relevant type sample (at least Part-66 Appendix III Level 1 General Familiarisation from a Part-147, the manufacturer or an accepted organisation); and maintenance methods.

  - been involved in CAW management activities for **at least 6 months in every 2-year period**, **or**
  - conducted **at least one airworthiness review in the last 12-month period**.
  To **restore** validity: conduct a satisfactory airworthiness review under supervision of the authority or, if the authority accepts, of another currently authorised ARS of the same CAMO, per an approved procedure.

## 4. Defined Terms (GM1 to Annex Vc, plus terms defined elsewhere in the chunk)

- **Audit:** "a systematic, independent, and documented process for obtaining evidence, and evaluating it objectively to determine the extent to which requirements are complied with". It may include inspections.
- **Alternative means of compliance (AltMoC):** means proposing an alternative to an existing AMC, or new means where no AMC exists.
- **Assessment:** in performance monitoring and oversight, a planned, documented evaluation by competent personnel of the level of performance and maturity against policy and objectives. It looks at the whole organisation to find strengths and weaknesses. Distinct from risk assessment.
- **Base maintenance / Line maintenance:** see AMC1 145.A.10 (not here).
- **Competency:** "a combination of individual skills, practical and theoretical knowledge, attitudes, training, and experience".
- **Correction:** "the action to eliminate a detected non-compliance".
- **Corrective action:** the action to eliminate or mitigate the root cause(s) and prevent recurrence of an existing detected non-compliance or undesirable condition. Proper root cause determination is crucial.
- **Error:** an action or inaction by a person that may lead to deviations from accepted procedures or regulations.
- **Hazard:** "a condition or an object with the potential to cause or contribute to an aircraft incident or accident".
- **Human factors:** anything that affects human performance; principles seeking a safe interface between the human and other system components.
- **Human performance:** human capabilities and limitations affecting safety and efficiency.
- **Inspection:** in compliance monitoring, an independent documented conformity evaluation by observation and judgement, with measurement or testing as appropriate. May be part of an audit (e.g. product audit) or stand alone (e.g. to verify closure of a finding).
- **Just Culture:** see Reg. 376/2014 Art. 2.
- **Near-miss:** an event in which a mandatorily reportable occurrence (376/2014) was narrowly averted. Example: CAMO staff find on recheck that an AD, AWL or CMR task was not properly processed in the AMP or record system, which would have led to it not being done on time.
- **Organisational factor:** a condition affecting the effectiveness of safety risk controls, related to culture, policies, processes, resources or workplace.
- **Oversight planning cycle:** the time frame within which the authority reviews all areas and processes of the approval by audits and inspections.
- **Oversight programme:** the authority's detailed schedule: number, scope and duration of audits and inspections, product audits, locations and tentative timing.
- **Post holder:** the person nominated under CAMO.A.305(b)(2), i.e. the air carrier's CAW manager.
- **Preventive action:** the action to eliminate the cause of a **potential** non-compliance or undesirable situation.
- **Risk assessment:** an evaluation by engineering and operational judgement and/or analysis to establish whether the achieved or perceived risk is acceptable or tolerable.
- **Safety Culture:** an enduring set of values, norms, attitudes and practices concerned with minimising exposure to hazards. A positive culture promotes shared concern, commitment and accountability.
- **Safety risk:** "the predicted probability and severity of the consequences or outcomes of a hazard".
- **Safety training:** dedicated training supporting safety management policies and processes, including HF training. Safety *management* training is for staff in CAMO.A.305(a)(5) or CAMO.A.200(a)(3) functions.
- **Working days:** "days between and including Monday to Friday not including public holidays".

Other terms defined in the AMC/GM:
- **Accountability vs responsibility** (GM2 CAMO.A.200(a)(1)): accountability cannot be delegated; responsibility can.
- **Line managers** (AMC3 CAMO.A.305(g)): departmental heads or persons responsible for operational units directly delivering the basic CAW management services.
- **Authority** in the safety management context (GM1 CAMO.A.305(g)): the management level needed to decide risk tolerability.
- **Relevant engineering degree** (AMC1 CAMO.A.305(c)): aeronautical, mechanical, electrical, electronic, avionics or other studies relevant to maintenance or CAW.
- **Relevant sample** (AMC1 CAMO.A.305(c), AMC1 CAMO.A.310(a)(3)): courses covering typical aircraft and systems within the scope of work.
- **Experience in continuing airworthiness** (AMC1 CAMO.A.310(a)): any appropriate combination of maintenance, CAW management and/or surveillance of such tasks.

### 5.2 Alerts and deadlines
- Organisation change: a **30 working day** lead time for certificate amendment and **20 working days** for a planned nominated person change. Use a working-day calculator excluding weekends and **public holidays** (UK bank holidays by region).
- Occurrence report: **72-hour** countdown from "condition identified". Follow-up report reminder.
- Finding deadlines: those agreed with the authority. Internal target closure dates. Overdue escalation per the feedback system procedure.
- Audit plan: each area, subcontractor and location due within 12 months (or an extended cycle ≤ 24 months).
- AM reviews: at least **2 meetings per year** with senior staff on compliance monitoring, and a **half-yearly** non-compliance summary report to the AM (auto-generate it).
- ARS recency: warn when the "1 AR in 12 months" and "6 months of CAW involvement in 2 years" conditions are approaching failure. Lapse automatically.
- Initial safety training due within **6 months** of joining. Recurrent safety training at least once per **2-year** period.
- Competency assessment before unsupervised work. Continuous control reviews.
- Contracted MO approval validity and scope checks (compliance monitoring must verify).
- Subcontractor change notifications go to the authority.
- New AM: CAME statement signature due "at the earliest opportunity".
- Safety policy and objectives periodic review. Staffing analysis review on significant change.
- Backups: verify backup within 24 hours of a new entry and offsite storage (a system health check).
- Record retention timers: personnel = leaving date + 3 years; management system and contracts = 5 years from creation or last amendment; aircraft CAW work, ARC and PtF = permanent transfer date + 3 years; default 3 years.
- Part-IS (EU only) applicability from 22 Feb 2026.

### 5.3 Workflows
1. Initial CAMO application pack: pre-audit checklist against Part-M, ML and CAMO, CAME draft generator (AMC1 layout), Form 2 data, change procedure, ATL description (CAT / commercial), subcontract technical content.
2. Change management: classify, risk assess, submit, approve, implement, notify, then update the CAME lists automatically.
3. CAME revision control with an approval route (indirect vs authority) and distribution / read-and-acknowledge by staff ("familiar with relevant parts").
4. Audit cycle: plan, audit, report, findings, CAP with agreed dates, implementation, verification, AM summary.
5. Authority finding: root cause (5-why / systemic factors template), CAP, evidence submission, closure.
6. Internal safety report: triage, investigation, hazard log, actions, feedback, training input.
7. Occurrence: draft, report to the authority + DAH within 72 h, follow-up.
8. ARS nomination: qualification check, supervised AR, CAME list update, authority acceptance, authorisation; then recency monitoring, and restoration if needed.
9. Competency: job description, assessment, supervision level, training plan, reassessment.
10. Maintenance contracting: MO selection agreed with the operator, contract with a responsibilities matrix, ongoing monitoring; work order path for line and component work only.
11. Non-mandatory SB/SL assessment with an SRM decision record (CMPA / air carrier).
12. Immediate safety action: out-of-hours contact directory for owners and operators, ERP support procedure.
13. Termination of CAMO operations or loss of a customer: record transfer workflow to the owner or the new CAMO (records export pack).

### 5.4 Permissions
- Role-based access control (RBAC) aligned to roles: AM (approves the safety policy and sees all summaries), CAW nominated persons, CMM (audit module; independence enforced; cannot be assigned audits of own area), SM (hazard log, ISRS), ARS (AR/ARC issue only within authorised scope and while recency is valid), ARC extension persons, PtF signatories, planners, engineers, technical records, contracted staff, and subcontractor users (scoped access to their tasks plus ISRS reporting access).
- An authority/inspector read-only role, to support "access at any time" to records and data.
- Confidential safety reports: only the SM and investigators can see the reporter's identity. Just culture: no use for disciplinary purposes. Restrict exports.
- Immutable audit trail on records (protection from alteration) and versioning. "Retention starts at last amendment" means amendment timestamps must be stored.
- Customer-owned data: segregated per contract.

### 5.5 Validation rules summary
- (f) PtF requires (e). (e) for a type requires (d) for that type.
- Subcontracted tasks must be from the allowed list, the subcontractor must be on the certificate, and no sub-subcontracting.
- The feedback system cannot be assigned to an external party. External auditors are allowed under the CMM.
- Base maintenance cannot use a work order only.
- An AMP for an ML aircraft can be approved by the CAMO (d)(5). A Part-M AMP needs authority approval or the indirect procedure.
