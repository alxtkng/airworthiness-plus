# Chunk 04: Part-M Section A Subpart G, Continuing Airworthiness Management Organisation (CAMO)

Source: EASA Easy Access Rules for Continuing Airworthiness (Reg. (EU) No 1321/2014), Annex I (Part-M), Section A, Subpart G, points M.A.701 to M.A.716, with their AMC and GM. I read the whole chunk (455 lines). It ends at M.A.716(b). The chunk holds no M.A.716(c)-(e) text and no Appendices (V, IX, XI, XII, XIII, Appendix II to AMC M.A.711(a)(3)). Those are referred to but not reproduced.

**Status warning (important for product design):** In the EASA system, Subpart G is a *legacy* regime. M.A.715(a) (as amended by Reg. (EU) 2021/700) says a Subpart G approval "shall remain valid until 24 March 2022". After that date, EASA CAMOs operate under Annex Vc (Part-CAMO), brought in by Reg. (EU) 2019/1383.

**UK guess (not verified):** Part-CAMO's EU application date fell after the end of the Brexit transition period (31 Dec 2020). So UK-retained 1321/2014 very probably *still uses M.A. Subpart G* as the live CAMO regime. M.A.715 is then likely reworded to give unlimited validity with no 2022 sunset. Expect the following:
- UK forms renamed as CAA Forms (CAA Form 2, 4, 14, and the Appendix VI certificate as a CAA/UK Form 14).
- "Member State" read as "the United Kingdom".
- "competent authority" meaning the UK CAA.
- Reg. (EC) 1008/2008 read as its UK-retained version.

The UK may also have kept or changed some Part-ML and Part-CAO references, depending on which 2019/1383 provisions applied before the transition ended. Part-ML and Part-CAO applied from 24 March 2020, so they are probably present in the UK version. Check against the CAA's UK Reg 1321/2014 consolidated text and CAP 1747 or the CAA "UK Part-M" guidance.

## 1. Scope (every IR point)

| Point | Plain summary |
|---|---|
| M.A.701 Scope | Sets the requirements an organisation must meet to get, or keep, an approval to manage aircraft continuing airworthiness. |
| M.A.702 Application | You apply for a new CAMO approval, or a change to one, on the form and in the manner the competent authority sets (AMC: EASA Form 2). |
| M.A.703 Extent of approval | The approval is shown on the Appendix VI certificate. For licensed air carriers (Reg. 1008/2008) it forms part of the AOC for the aircraft operated. The scope of work is set out in the CAME. |
| M.A.704 CAME | The CAMO must provide a Continuing Airworthiness Management Exposition with 9 mandatory contents. The authority approves the CAME and its amendments. Minor amendments may go through an approved indirect approval procedure. |
| M.A.705 Facilities | Suitable office accommodation at appropriate locations for M.A.706 personnel. |
| M.A.706 Personnel requirements | Accountable manager; nominated person or group (for air carriers, a nominated post holder); restriction on the post holder working for a contracted Part-145; enough qualified staff; qualifications recorded; persons authorised to extend ARCs; names kept current in the CAME; competence control for complex motor-powered aircraft (CMPA) and air carrier aircraft. |
| M.A.707 Airworthiness review staff (ARS) | Experience, licence or degree, and training requirements for ARS, split by aircraft class. Acceptance by the authority after a supervised review. Recent experience. Listed in the CAME with an authorisation reference. Records kept until 2 years after the person leaves. |
| M.A.708 Continuing airworthiness management | Management must follow Part-M or Part-ML Subpart C. There are 10 tasks per aircraft managed. A written maintenance contract is needed when the CAMO lacks a maintenance approval for CMPA, CAT, commercial SPO, commercial ATO or commercial DTO aircraft. Work orders are allowed instead for unscheduled line maintenance and component or engine maintenance. |
| M.A.709 Documentation | Hold and use current maintenance data (M.A.401 or ML.A.401). The data may come from the owner or operator under contract. Baseline or generic AMPs may be developed for initial approval or scope extension (non-air-carrier aircraft). |
| M.A.710 Airworthiness review | Reviews by an M.A.711(b) organisation are done under M.A.901 or ML.A.903. |
| M.A.711 Privileges | (a) Manage continuing airworthiness (CAW) of listed aircraft; subcontract limited CAW tasks; extend ARCs; approve Part-ML AMPs. (b) Perform airworthiness reviews, issue and extend ARCs, and issue recommendations. (c) Issue permits to fly (21.A.711(d)) for aircraft it may issue ARCs for, under an approved procedure. |
| M.A.712 Quality system | Quality system plus a quality manager, with feedback to the accountable manager. Three monitoring functions. Records kept at least 2 years. The system may be combined with another Part's quality system, or integrated with the operator's for air carriers. Small organisations may use organisational reviews instead, with exceptions. With no quality system, no subcontracting is allowed. |
| M.A.713 Changes | Notify the authority *before* changes to name, location, additional locations, accountable manager, M.A.706(c) persons, or facilities, procedures, work scope or staff that could affect the approval. Unforeseen personnel changes are notified at the earliest opportunity. |
| M.A.714 Record-keeping | Record all work. Keep M.A.305, ML.A.305 and M.A.306 records. Keep copies of ARCs, recommendations, extensions and permits to fly until 2 years after permanent withdrawal from service. Protect records; store backups off-site. Records move with a CAMO change and go to the owner if the CAMO stops operating. |
| M.A.715 Continued validity | EASA text: valid until 24 March 2022, subject to compliance (findings handled per M.B.705), authority access, and no surrender or revocation. The certificate is returned on surrender or revocation. |
| M.A.716 Findings | Level 1 = significant non-compliance that lowers the safety standard and seriously hazards flight safety. Level 2 = non-compliance that could lower the safety standard and possibly hazard flight safety. (Only (a) and (b) are in this chunk.) |

## 2. Obligations (who, what, when)

### Applicant or CAMO (organisation)
- **Apply** for an initial or changed approval on EASA Form 2 or equivalent (M.A.702 and AMC). One Form 2 can cover several approvals: Subpart F, Part-CAO, Part-CAMO, Part-145 and Subpart G.
- **Provide a CAME** with the M.A.704(a) contents. Get the CAME and its amendments approved (M.A.704(b)). For minor amendments, an indirect approval procedure must be defined in the CAME and approved by the authority (M.A.704(c)).
- **Facilities:** suitable office accommodation (M.A.705), including an adequate technical library and room for document consultation (AMC).
- **Personnel (M.A.706):**
  - Appoint an accountable manager with corporate authority to finance and carry out CAW activities (a). For air carriers, this is the same person who has corporate authority for the AOC operations (b).
  - Nominate a person or group responsible for compliance with CAW management, airworthiness review and permit-to-fly requirements. They are ultimately responsible to the accountable manager (c).
  - Air carriers: the accountable manager designates a nominated post holder for CAW (d). This person must not be employed by a contracted Part-145 unless the authority specifically agrees (e).
  - Have sufficient qualified staff (f). Point (c) and (d) persons must show relevant knowledge, background and experience (g).
  - Record the qualifications of *all* CAW management personnel (h).
  - Nominate persons authorised to extend ARCs, subject to authority approval (i).
  - Keep the names and titles of the (a), (c), (d) and (i) persons current in the CAME (j).
  - For CMPA and air carrier aircraft: establish and control the competence of CAW management, airworthiness review and quality audit personnel, using a procedure and standard agreed with the authority (k).
- **Airworthiness review staff (M.A.707).** These are needed only if the CAMO wants M.A.711(b) or (c) privileges.
  - **Air carrier aircraft, and aircraft > 2 730 kg MTOM except balloons:**
    - "at least 5 years' experience in continuing airworthiness"
    - an appropriate Part-66 licence, or an aeronautical degree, or a national equivalent
    - formal aeronautical maintenance training
    - a position with appropriate responsibilities
    - The licence or degree can be replaced by "5 years of experience in continuing airworthiness additional" (so 10 years in total).
  - **Non-air-carrier aircraft ≤ 2 730 kg MTOM, and balloons:**
    - "at least 3 years' experience"
    - licence, degree or national equivalent
    - appropriate aeronautical maintenance training
    - a position with appropriate responsibilities
    - The licence or degree can be replaced by "4 years of experience ... additional" (so 7 years in total).
  - The authorisation may be issued by the CAMO only after formal acceptance by the authority. That acceptance follows a satisfactory airworthiness review done under the supervision of the authority, or of the organisation's ARS under an approved procedure (b).
  - The CAMO must ensure ARS can show recent CAW management experience (c). Each ARS is listed in the CAME with their authorisation reference (d).
  - ARS records are kept until **2 years after the staff member has left** (e).
- **CAW management (M.A.708).** Detailed in section 5. The CAMO must be satisfied *before the intended flight* that all required maintenance has been properly carried out (AMC1 M.A.708(c) para 2).
- **Maintenance contract (M.A.708(c))** is required for CMPA, CAT, commercial specialised operations, commercial ATO and commercial DTO aircraft when the CAMO holds no Part-145, Subpart F or Part-CAO approval.
  - The contract is written "in consultation with the operator" and placed with a Part-145, Subpart F or Part-CAO organisation, or with another operator.
  - It covers the M.A.301(b), (c), (f), (g) functions, or ML.A.301(b) to (e), and defines support for the M.A.712(b) quality functions.
  - Individual work orders are acceptable instead for (1) unscheduled line maintenance and (2) component maintenance, including engines (M.A.708(d)).
  - Base maintenance always needs a written contract, even if ordered case by case (AMC M.A.708(d)).
- **Documentation (M.A.709):**
  - Hold and use current maintenance data.
  - If the data comes from the customer, it is kept only for the contract duration. The exception is the M.A.714 requirement to keep a copy of the customer data used during the contract (AMC).
  - Baseline or generic AMPs do not remove the need for a proper M.A.302 or ML.A.302 AMP "in due time before exercising the privileges".
- **Quality system (M.A.712).** Designate a quality manager. Monitor compliance and the adequacy of procedures, with feedback to the accountable manager. Quality records are kept for **at least 2 years**. Details are in section 7.
- **Changes (M.A.713).** Notify the authority **before** the change takes place. For personnel changes not known in advance, notify "at the earliest opportunity". When the accountable manager changes, the new AM must sign the CAME statement "at the earliest opportunity". AMC says failure to do so *invalidates* the approval or AOC.
- **Records (M.A.714).** See section 3. Computer backup must be updated **within 24 hours** of any new entry (AMC).
- **Surrender or revocation:** return the approval certificate to the authority (M.A.715(b)).

### Accountable manager
- Signs the CAME compliance statement (M.A.704(a)1; model wording in AMC M.A.704(a)(1)).
- Receives quality feedback. Holds regular rectification-progress meetings. In large organisations this may be delegated day to day to the quality manager, but the AM must still meet senior staff **at least twice per year** and receive **at least a half-yearly summary report** of non-compliance findings (AMC M.A.712(a) para 5).

### Airworthiness review staff
- Carry out both the documentary review and the physical survey. They have the final word on the depth of sampling and may go beyond the CAME depth.
- Produce a compliance report listing all items checked and the outcome (GM M.A.710).
- **Validity of the ARS authorisation (AMC M.A.707(c)).** The ARS must have either:
  - been "involved in continuing airworthiness management activities for at least six months in every two year period", or
  - "conducted at least one airworthiness review in the last twelve month period".
- **Restoring a lapsed authorisation:** carry out a satisfactory airworthiness review under supervision of the authority, or (if the authority accepts) of another currently valid ARS of the same CAMO under an approved procedure.

### Owner or contracted CAMO
- Responsible for the aircraft being fully airworthy "at any time". The ARC certifies airworthiness only at the time of the review (GM M.A.710).

### Subcontracted organisation (AMC M.A.711(a)(3))
- Must notify the CAMO of contract-affecting changes "as soon as practical". The CAMO then informs the authority. If it does not, the authority's acceptance of the contract may be invalidated.

Storage rules (M.A.714 and AMC):
- Records must be protected from damage, alteration and theft.
- Backup hardware is kept at a different location from the working data.
- Accepted media: paper, computer, microfilm or optical disc. Records must stay legible for the whole retention period, and paper must be robust.
- At least one computer backup, updated within 24 hours. Terminals must have safeguards against alteration by unauthorised personnel.
- On transfer of CAW management, all records move to the new organisation or person, and the retention periods continue.
- If the CAMO terminates its operation, records go to the owner.
- If the maintenance organisation keeps records on the CAMO's behalf, the CAMO is still responsible for them.

## 4. Defined Terms

- **Accountable manager:** "normally intended to mean the chief executive officer of the CAMO, who by virtue of position has overall (including in particular financial) responsibility". May serve as AM for more than one organisation. Need not be technical. If not the CEO, must have direct access to the CEO and sufficient CAW funding (AMC M.A.706(a)).
- **Nominated person or group of persons / nominated post holder:** M.A.706(c) and (d). Recommended qualifications (AMC M.A.706 para 4):
  - "five years relevant work experience of which at least two years should be from the aeronautical industry"
  - a relevant engineering degree or AMT qualification. This can be replaced by 5 further years of experience.
  - type knowledge at least "Part-66 Appendix III Level 1 General Familiarisation". For balloons and ≤ 2 730 kg MTOM this may be replaced by demonstrated knowledge.
  - knowledge of quality systems, regulations, maintenance methods and the CAME.
- **Relevant engineering degree:** "an engineering degree from aeronautical, mechanical, electrical, electronic, avionic or other studies relevant to the maintenance and continuing airworthiness of aircraft/aircraft components". A person qualified to AMC M.A.706 4.5 is treated as holding the equivalent of an aeronautical degree (AMC M.A.707(a) para 3).
- **Relevant sample (of types):** courses "should cover typical systems embodied in those aircraft being within the scope of approval".
- **Experience in continuing airworthiness:** "any appropriate combination of experience in tasks related to aircraft maintenance and/or continuing airworthiness management and/or surveillance of such tasks". For ≤ 2 730 kg and balloons it can be "full-time or part-time, either as professional or on a voluntary basis".
- **Appropriate licence (Part-66) for ARS:**
  - B1 in the subcategory of the aircraft reviewed, or
  - B2 or C, or
  - B3 for piston-engine non-pressurised aeroplanes ≤ 2 000 kg MTOM, or
  - L licence in the appropriate subcategory for sailplanes, powered sailplanes, ELA1 aeroplanes, balloons and airships.
  - The Part-66 experience requirements need not be met at the time of the review.
- **Position with appropriate responsibilities (ARS):** independent from the airworthiness management process, *or* holding overall authority over the airworthiness management process of complete aircraft.
  - Independence examples: reviewing only aircraft one has not managed; Part-145, Subpart F or Part-CAO maintenance staff not involved in management or release to service of that aircraft (except physical-survey tasks); quality department staff.
  - Overall-authority examples: the AM or nominated postholder; a person responsible for the complete CAW of those aircraft; the sole person in a one-man organisation.
- **Formal aeronautical maintenance training** (air carrier and > 2 730 kg aircraft): evidenced training (internal or external) in initial and continuing airworthiness regulations, operational requirements, the CAME, relevant type sample (≥ Part-66 App III Level 1) and maintenance methods.
- **Appropriate aeronautical maintenance training** (≤ 2 730 kg, balloons): *demonstrated knowledge* of the same subjects. Type knowledge may come from training and/or work experience.
- **Baseline maintenance programme:** "a maintenance programme developed for a particular aircraft type following, where applicable, the [MRB] report, the type certificate holder's [MPD], the relevant chapters of the maintenance manual or any other maintenance data containing information on scheduling."
- **Generic maintenance programme:** "a maintenance programme developed to cover a group of similar types of aircraft" (e.g. Cessna 100 Series: 150, 172, 177).
- **Indirect approval procedure:** an authority-approved procedure in the CAME that defines which class of (minor) amendments can be made without the authority's prior consent.
- **Independent audit:** "an objective process of routine sample checks of all aspects of the CAMO ability to carry out continuing airworthiness management to the required standards", including product sampling. It must be done by people not responsible for the function being checked.
- **Small organisation (M.A.712(f)):** "up to 5 full-time staff (including all M.A.706 personnel) or equivalent proportional number when using part-time staff". Complexity, aircraft mix, utilisation and number of locations are also considered.
- **Level 1 finding:** "any significant non-compliance ... which lowers the safety standard and hazards seriously the flight safety".
- **Level 2 finding:** "any non-compliance ... which could lower the safety standard and possibly hazard the flight safety".
- **Licensed air carrier:** an air carrier licensed under Reg. (EC) 1008/2008. This is the key branching attribute throughout the Subpart.
- **Secondary operator/CAMO:** another operator or CAMO without a maintenance approval that is contracted as the maintenance contractor or primary contractor (AMC2 M.A.708(c)).
- **CDCCL:** Critical Design Configuration Control Limitations (fuel tank safety).
- Abbreviations used: CAME, AMP, ARC, ARS, CMPA, CAT, ATO, DTO, MRB, MPD, EDP, PtF, AOC, MTOM, ETOPS, SGHA.

## 5. Software Implications

### Core entities
- **Organisation (CAMO)**
  - approval reference; approval certificate (Appendix VI) or AOC linkage
  - approval scope: aircraft types or groups; privileges flags `manage_CAW` (711(a)1), `manage_air_carrier` (711(a)2, requires listing on both the certificate *and* the AOC), `subcontract_tasks` (711(a)3), `extend_ARC` (711(a)4), `approve_ML_AMP` (711(a)5), `airworthiness_review` (711(b)), `permit_to_fly` (711(c))
  - approved locations
  - other approvals held (Part-145, Subpart F, Part-CAO), which drive the contract requirement and the combined exposition
  - `quality_system_type` (quality system or organisational review)
  - status (active, suspended, limited, revoked, surrendered); validity
  - regime: EASA Subpart G legacy, EASA Part-CAMO, UK Part-M Subpart G
  - Validation rule: privilege 711(b) for a type requires 711(a) for that type; 711(c) requires 711(b).
- **Exposition (CAME)**: versioned document with the 9 required sections as a checklist, amendment log, amendment class (direct or indirect approval), approval status and date, authority approval reference, and the person responsible for amendment (default: quality manager). It references the list of AMPs and the list of contracted maintenance organisations and subcontractors.
- **Person / role assignment**
  - roles: Accountable Manager, Nominated Person / Post Holder (M.A.706(c)/(d)), ARC-extension authorised person (706(i)), Quality Manager, ARS, PtF signatory, planning, technical records
  - fields: Form 4 status and date; qualification record; training records (initial, type, continuation, recurrent, fuel tank safety / CDCCL); employer conflicts (post holder employed by a contracted Part-145 needs an authority-agreement flag)
- **ARS authorisation**
  - authorisation reference; aircraft class scope (> 2 730 kg / air carrier vs ≤ 2 730 kg / balloons); permitted types
  - independence basis (independent vs overall authority); aircraft excluded for conflict of interest
  - Form 4 acceptance date; supervised initial review record; status (pending acceptance, valid, lapsed, restored, withdrawn); date left the organisation
- **Aircraft (managed)**
  - MTOM; type and class (balloon, sailplane, ELA1 etc.); used by licensed air carrier yes/no; CMPA yes/no
  - operation type (CAT, commercial SPO, commercial ATO or DTO, non-commercial)
  - regime (Part-M or Part-ML); AMP link; contract link; mass and balance statement version
  - permanently-withdrawn-from-service date (drives the retention clock)
- **AMP:** type (baseline, generic, aircraft-specific); base programme plus per-registration Annex (tasks added, tasks not applicable); approval route (authority, indirect, CAMO under ML.A.302(b)(2)); reliability programme; copy-provided-to-owner flag and date.
- **Contracts**
  - CAMO–owner/operator contract (Appendix I)
  - maintenance contract (M.A.708(c)) with Part-145, Subpart F, Part-CAO or another operator; scope of M.A.301 functions; quality support clause
  - work orders (allowed only for unscheduled line maintenance or component/engine maintenance; flag base maintenance as contract-required)
  - subcontract (CAW tasks), limited to: AD analysis and planning, SB analysis, maintenance planning, reliability / engine health monitoring, AMP development and amendment, and other tasks agreed by the authority. Fields: Form 14 listing check, subcontractor change notification log.
- **Airworthiness review:** documentary review plus physical survey; sampling depth (CAME default, overridable by ARS); compliance report (items checked and outcome); resulting ARC, extension or recommendation; supporting documents attached.
- **Permit to fly:** linked to aircraft, approved flight conditions, signatory, 21A.729 copy.
- **Quality**
  - quality plan (annual audit schedule covering every Subpart G aspect and subcontracted activity, and every approved location each year)
  - audit (auditor must be independent of the audited function)
  - finding (level 1 / level 2 / observation; responsible department; target rectification date agreed with the department; status; closure evidence)
  - management review meetings (AM ≥ 2/yr); half-yearly summary report
  - organisational review (small orgs, Appendix XIII)
- **Change notification (M.A.713):** change type (name, location, additional location, AM, 706(c) person, facilities, procedures, work scope, staff); planned date; notified date; authority response; flags "unplanned personnel change" and "new AM statement signed".

### Workflows
- Approval application (Form 2), then CAME drafting against the 9-item checklist, then authority approval, then certificate.
- CAME amendment: classify as minor/indirect or authority approval, then route accordingly.
- Personnel nomination: Form 4, then authority acceptance, then CAME update.
- ARS onboarding: check qualifications by aircraft class, then a supervised airworthiness review, then Form 4 acceptance, then CAME listing (= authorisation).
- Change management: **block the effective date of a change until notification is logged**. Warn if a planned AM, nominated person, location or name change has no prior notification.
- Maintenance ordering: decide contract vs work order by maintenance type and aircraft category.
- Pre-flight check (CMPA, CAT etc.): the CAMO is "satisfied before the intended flight" that required maintenance was done, so the release-to-service/CRS check feeds aircraft status.
- Transfer of CAW management: export the full record set to the new CAMO and keep the retention clocks. Termination: export to the owner.

### Alerts and computed statuses
- **ARS currency:** valid if (CAW management involvement ≥ 6 months within each rolling 2-year window) OR (≥ 1 airworthiness review in the last 12 months). Alert before lapse, and require a supervised review to restore.
- **ARS eligibility calculator:**
  - > 2 730 kg / air carrier: ≥ 5 yrs + licence/degree, or ≥ 10 yrs.
  - ≤ 2 730 kg / balloons: ≥ 3 yrs + licence/degree, or ≥ 7 yrs.
  - Licence subcategory must match the aircraft (B1 subcategory, B2, C, B3 ≤ 2 000 kg piston non-pressurised, L subcategory).
- **Nominated-person eligibility:** 5 yrs (≥ 2 aeronautical) + degree/AMT, or 10 yrs.
- **Conflict-of-interest guard:** block assigning a review to an ARS who manages that aircraft (unless their basis is overall authority), or who released it to service (except physical-survey work).
- **Annual audit coverage:** alert if any Subpart G element, subcontracted activity or location has not been audited within 12 months. The interval may be extended by up to 100% (to 24 months) with authority agreement if there are no safety-related findings.
- **Finding rectification:** overdue target date escalates toward the AM.
- **AM half-yearly report due;** AM meetings ≥ 2 per year.
- **Backup freshness:** last backup must be < 24 h after the latest entry.
- **AMP must exist** for each aircraft before privileges are exercised (warn if an aircraft is on a baseline/generic programme with no aircraft-specific AMP or Annex).
- **Mass and balance currency** after modifications or repairs.
- **Small-organisation check:** staff ≤ 5 FTE. Organisational review is not allowed if the org issues ARCs for > 2 730 kg aircraft (other than balloons) or manages air carrier aircraft. With no quality system, block creation of subcontracts.

### Retention rules
- ARC, recommendation and PtF copies plus supporting docs: until permanent withdrawal + 2 years.
- ARS record: until leave date + 2 years.
- Quality records: ≥ 2 years.
- Customer-supplied maintenance data: contract duration, except copies used for CAW tasks (keep under M.A.714).
- M.A.305 / M.A.306 records: per the other chunk.

### Permissions (multi-tenant SaaS: AMC M.A.712(f))
Contracting a software tool for CAW data is acceptable even without a quality system if:
- "each organisation should have access to its own data only" (strict tenant isolation)
- "Introduction of data can only be performed by personnel of the continuing airworthiness management organisation" (only CAMO users can enter or edit; vendor and support staff read-only or none)
- "The data can be retrieved at any time" (full export)

These must be described in the CAME and approved by the authority. Also needed:
- role-based edit rights with protection against unauthorised alteration
- audit trail (alteration protection)
- off-site backup
- a full record handover/export package for CAMO transfer or termination

The product should publish a compliance statement for these points that CAMOs can reference in their CAME.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM (practical effect)

- **AM statement (AMC M.A.704(a)(1)):** standard wording may be used unchanged, and its intent must not be altered. If a new AM does not sign "at the earliest opportunity", this *invalidates* the approval or AOC. This makes it a hard compliance alert.
- **CAME layout (AMC1 M.A.704):** Part 0 General; Part 1 CAW procedures; Part 2 Quality system or organisational review; Part 3 Contracted maintenance; Part 4 Airworthiness review procedures. CDCCL compliance info is included. Electronic CAME is allowed.
- **Staffing analysis (AMC M.A.706):** the authority accepts staffing based on a man-hour task analysis, which must be updated on significant change. The quality function must be independent.
- **Nominated post holder at a contracted Part-145 (AMC M.A.706(e)):** acceptable only if they are "manifest[ly]" the only competent person within practical working distance. This does not apply where the 145 and the operator are the same organisation.
- **Fuel tank safety / CDCCL training (AMC M.A.706(f)):** applies to staff in CDCCL management, SB assessment, planning and AMP management. Modification and repair approval must consider CDCCL (AMC M.A.708(b)3).
- **ARS (AMC M.A.706(i)):** ARS are automatically recognised as able to extend ARCs. CAME approval of the 706(i) list = acceptance and authorisation.
- **ARS independence and conflict-of-interest options (AMC M.A.707(a)):** in practice, this allows one-man CAMOs and AM / post-holder ARS.
- **ARS currency (AMC M.A.707(c)):** 6 months in 2 years, or 1 review in 12 months. Restore by a supervised review.
- **CAMO responsibility (AMC1 M.A.708(c), GM M.A.708(b)(4)):** the CAMO decides what maintenance, when, by whom, and to what standard. It bears responsibility for airworthy condition and may inspect at contractor facilities. Maintenance organisation selection is agreed with the operator. Contracts must clearly cover SB decisions, on-time ADs and approved data for non-mandatory mods.
- **GM M.A.708:** the CAMO must know the design status: type specification, customer options, ADs, ALS/ICA limitations, mods, major repairs, operational equipment. For CS-25 this includes H25.4 and fuel tank ALS/CDCCL. **Software should model a per-aircraft design configuration status.**
- **Contract with a secondary operator/CAMO (AMC2 M.A.708(c)):** permitted, e.g. a primary component contractor, or dry-lease continuity. The CAMO keeps the same level of control, and the list of approved maintenance organisations goes in the CAME.
- **Work orders (AMC M.A.708(d)):** must be authorised in the CAME and be manageable in volume and complexity. Base maintenance always requires a written contract.
- **Customer-supplied data (AMC M.A.709):** the CAMO must ensure it is current (via procedure or contract clause) and keep copies of the data actually used.
- **Baseline/generic AMP plus per-registration Annex:** this is how customer-specific AMPs are built. Indirect approval may be used; the procedure must notify the authority when a customer-specific AMP is created. An existing approved customer AMP may be used as-is.
- **GM M.A.709:** documentation in M.A.709(a) covers CAW tasks, not airworthiness reviews (AR records are under M.A.714).
- **GM M.A.710 (ARS responsibilities):**
  - The ARS has the final say on sampling depth and must be free from pressure. A compliance report is mandatory.
  - The ARS is not responsible for undetected problems or AMP gaps versus DAH recommendations, unless their basis is overall authority, in which case responsibility comes from their M.A.706 role.
  - An ARC certifies airworthiness only within the review scope and at the time of the review.
- **AMC M.A.711(a)(3) subcontracting:**
  - The CAMO remains accountable. Control is by direct involvement and/or endorsing the subcontractor's recommendations.
  - Normally one subcontractor per aircraft type, except engines and APU; otherwise coordination controls must be shown.
  - No sub-subcontracting. Subcontractors are listed on EASA Form 14, and the authority oversees them through the CAMO approval.
- **AMC M.A.711(b):** AR privilege can be held without currently managing that type. Losing all customers of a type need not remove the type from the approval, provided the Subpart G requirements are still met. A CAMO without AR privilege may contract ARs to another CAMO approved for that type (it need not be linked to an AOC).
- **AMC M.A.711(c):** for air carrier and > 2 730 kg aircraft, a PtF is only for aircraft in a controlled environment managed by that CAMO. Otherwise it is for any aircraft within the 711(b) privilege.
- **AMC M.A.712(a):** procedures must be verified and validated before use. Findings go to the department with target dates agreed with it, and escalation leads to the AM. The AM meets senior staff ≥ 2 times a year and gets a half-yearly summary.
- **AMC M.A.712(b) independent audit:**
  - Annual coverage of all Subpart G aspects, including subcontracted activities and each location. It can be one exercise or spread across the year to a plan.
  - Common procedures need not be checked against every product line if they were clean the previous year. If findings arise, recheck across product lines until rectified.
  - Intervals can be extended by up to 100% with authority agreement if there are no safety-related findings.
  - A report is produced for every audit. Auditors are independent of the audited function. A quality plan acceptable to the authority is required.
- **AMC M.A.712(f):** small organisation = ≤ 5 FTE. Technical publisher subscriptions and **contracted CAW software tools** are *not* subcontracting, under conditions (own-data-only access; only CAMO personnel enter data; retrievable at any time; plus M.A.714(d) and (e)). Directly relevant to Airworthiness+.
- **AMC M.A.713:** prior notification lets the approval continue during negotiations about the change. Without it, the approval "would automatically be suspended in all cases".
- **AMC M.A.714:**
  - The CAMO must obtain a complete CRS from the maintenance organisation, M.A.801(b)(1) staff or pilot-owner.
  - The CAMO is still responsible for records held by the maintenance organisation on its behalf.
  - Accepted media: paper, database, microfilm, optical disc; records must stay legible.
  - Backup within 24 hours; terminal safeguards against unauthorised alteration.
