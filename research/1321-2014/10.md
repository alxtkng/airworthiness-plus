## 1. Scope (point by point)

### Section B: Authority requirements
| Point | Title | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| 145.B.005 | Scope | Sets the conditions for certification, oversight and enforcement, and the administrative and management-system requirements for the competent authority (CA) that implements and enforces Section A. |
| 145.B.115 | Oversight documentation | The CA must give its staff all legislative acts, standards, rules, technical publications and related documents. |
| 145.B.120 | Means of compliance | EASA develops AMC. Alternative means of compliance (AltMoC) may be used. CAs must inform EASA of the AltMoC that they or their organisations use. GM1 / AMC1 (b);(c) / GM1 (b);(c) cover AltMoC processing. |
| 145.B.125 | Information to the Agency | (a) Notify significant implementation problems **within 30 days** of becoming aware. (b) Send safety-significant information from occurrence reports (Reg 376/2014 national database) "as soon as possible". (c) [from 22 Feb 2026] Send safety-significant information from information security reports (IS.I.OR.230). AMC/GM1-3 cover this. |
| 145.B.135 | Immediate reaction to a safety problem | The CA must have a system to collect, analyse and disseminate safety information. EASA analyses the information and passes it on. The CA takes measures and **immediately** notifies the affected persons, EASA and, when combined action is needed, the other Member States. |
| 145.B.135A | Immediate reaction to an information security incident or vulnerability | [from 22 Feb 2026] Same pattern as 145.B.135 for information security incidents and vulnerabilities, with coordination with national cyber authorities. AMC1 covers confidentiality and careful choice of recipients. GM1 describes a two-step, two-part report (public part plus restricted sensitive part). |
| 145.B.200 | Management system | CA management system: documented procedures, enough personnel with availability planning, qualified and trained staff, facilities, a compliance monitoring function (internal audit plus safety risk management, with feedback to senior management), and a person responsible for compliance monitoring. Also: a person responsible for each field of activity, mutual information exchange with other CAs, procedures copied to EASA, and (e) Part-IS.AR [from 22 Feb 2026]. |
| 145.B.205 | Allocation of tasks | Tasks may be allocated to qualified entities (QEs), subject to an assessment system and a written agreement. Internal audit and safety risk management must cover QE tasks. (c) [2026] 145.A.200A (Part-IS) oversight may be allocated to a QE or to a national cyber authority. |
| 145.B.210 | Changes in the management system | The CA must have a system to identify changes affecting its capability, must update its system in a timely way when the regulation changes, and must notify EASA of changes affecting its capability. |
| 145.B.220 | Record-keeping | Full list of records. **Minimum 5 years** retention. Records available on request to other CAs or EASA. The CA keeps a list of all certificates it has issued. |
| 145.B.300 | Oversight principles | Verify compliance before issue, continued compliance, and implementation of mandated safety measures. Verification is based on assessments, audits, inspections and unannounced inspections. Scope is risk-based. Covers multi-State facilities, prior notice to the host CA before on-site audits, information collection, and (g) [2026] review of IS.I.OR.200(e) approvals. |
| 145.B.305 | Oversight programme | Risk-based programme. Cycle **not more than 24 months**, extendable to **36**, and to a maximum of **48 months**. Must include management system assessments, process audits, product audits, sampling of airworthiness reviews, unannounced inspections and accountable manager meetings. Records of due and done dates. Recommendation report at the end of each cycle. |
| 145.B.310 | Initial certification procedure | Verify compliance, hold at least one meeting with the accountable manager, record findings, confirm findings in writing, and close all findings before issue. Issue EASA Form 3-145 per Appendix II classes and ratings, and formally approve the MOE. Certificate is unlimited in duration. The CA approves the MOE procedure for changes not requiring prior approval. |
| 145.B.330 | Changes – organisations | Changes requiring prior approval: verify, set operating conditions during the change, then approve. Changes made without approval: consider suspend, limit or revoke. Changes not requiring prior approval: reviewed within continuing oversight. |
| 145.B.330A | Changes to the information security management system | [2026] IS.I.OR.255(a) notified changes are reviewed in oversight. IS.I.OR.255(b) changes need approval. |
| 145.B.350 | Findings and corrective actions; observations | Level 1, level 2 and observations. Level 2 corrective action period initially **not more than 3 months**, extendable with an agreed corrective action plan (CAP). If the CAP or the action fails, the finding is raised to level 1. |
| 145.B.355 | Suspension, limitation and revocation | Grounds for suspend, limit or revoke. |

## 2. Obligations (who, what, when)

### Competent authority (CA)
- **Oversight documentation (145.B.115):** give staff all regulatory documents.
- **AltMoC (145.B.120, AMC1):**
  - Evaluate AltMoC consistently over time.
  - If the CA issues an AltMoC, make it available to all relevant organisations and notify EASA "as soon as the AltMoC is issued".
  - If an organisation proposes an AltMoC, analyse the documentation (and inspect if needed). If it complies, notify the applicant that it is approved, agree when the MOE is to be amended, and notify EASA "as soon as" it is approved.
  - Information sent to EASA: summary, content, compliance statement, and a safety assessment against the corresponding EASA AMC. These form part of the 145.B.220 records.
  - An AltMoC used by one CA or organisation may be used by another only if it is processed again under 145.B.120 / 145.A.120.
- **Information to EASA (145.B.125):**
  - Significant implementation problems: "within 30 days from the time the authority became aware".
  - Safety-significant occurrence information: "as soon as possible".
  - Information security reports [from 22 Feb 2026]: "as soon as possible".
  - AMC1: appoint a coordinator as contact point.
  - GM3: occurrences concerning EASA-certified organisations or products (or Part 21 Light Subpart C declarations) go to EASA if the occurrence is reportable, the responsible organisation is EASA-overseen, and the CA concludes that the organisation was not informed or did not properly address it. Format must be ECCAIRS-compatible, with attachments.
- **Safety problems (145.B.135):** collect, analyse and disseminate. Take adequate measures. **Immediately** notify measures to those who must comply, to EASA, and to other Member States where combined action is needed.
- **Management system (145.B.200):** see Section 1. Initial and recurrent training for staff. Compliance monitoring with feedback of audit findings to senior management.
- **Inspector competency (AMC3 145.B.200(a)(3)):** "Assessments of an inspector's competency should take place at regular intervals that do not exceed 3 years". Results and follow-up actions must be recorded. Competency must be assessed before an inspector works unsupervised.
- **Inspector qualifications (AMC2 145.B.200(a)(3)):** "5 years of relevant work experience" before working independently. Also a relevant engineering degree or an AMT qualification with additional education, type training at least Part-66 Appendix III Level 1 General Familiarisation, and FTS training.
- **Safety risk management (AMC1 145.B.200(a)(5)):** triggered at minimum on initial hazard identification and whenever changes may affect capability. Record mitigation actions with status and owner. Follow up, assess effectiveness, and review periodically.
- **Allocation to qualified entities (145.B.205):** initial and continuous assessment of QE compliance with Annex VI to 2018/1139, documented. A written agreement signed at the appropriate management level covering: tasks; declarations, reports and records; technical conditions; liability coverage; and information protection.
- **Changes in CA (145.B.210):** update "in a timely manner" and notify EASA.
- **Records (145.B.220):** keep for "a minimum period of 5 years, subject to applicable data protection law". The retention period starts when the record is created (AMC1). Electronic backup "updated within 24 hours of any new entry", stored at a different location (AMC1 145.B.220(a)).
- **Oversight (145.B.300):**
  - Verify compliance before issue and continued compliance afterwards.
  - Give organisations the results of oversight.
  - Inform the host Member State CA **before** any on-site audit or inspection at a facility located in another Member State.
  - Inform the organisation of any agreement to delegate oversight to another State or EASA, and of its scope.
- **Oversight programme (145.B.305):**
  - Cycle ≤ 24 months.
  - Extension to 36 months if, during the previous 24 months: (1) the organisation effectively identifies hazards and manages risks; (2) it continuously complies with 145.A.85 and has full control over changes; (3) "no level 1 findings have been issued"; (4) all corrective actions were implemented within the accepted or extended time.
  - Further extension "to a maximum of 48 months" if, in addition, the organisation has an effective continuous reporting system on safety performance and compliance that the CA has approved.
  - The cycle may be shortened if safety performance decreases.
  - Records of due dates and done dates.
  - **Recommendation report at completion of each cycle** (AMC: a new EASA Form 6).
  - AMC1 145.B.305(a);(b): review the cycle and programme **annually**.
  - AMC1 145.B.305(d): for cycles over 24 months, at least **one focused inspection in each 12-month segment**. If performance drops, revert to a 24-month (or shorter) cycle. For cycles over 36 months, agree the format and content of continuous reporting.
  - AMC2 145.B.305(c): at least **one on-site audit per cycle**. All applicable requirements are audited within the cycle. The first cycle normally starts at the date of the first certificate issue, and may be shortened to align with the calendar year.
  - GM1 145.B.305(c): 24 months is a maximum, not a minimum (for example 12 months is possible).
- **Initial certification (145.B.310):**
  - Hold at least one meeting with the accountable manager during the investigation.
  - Record all findings, closure actions and recommendations.
  - Confirm findings in writing. AMC1 145.B.310(d): "within 2 weeks of the on-site audit".
  - All findings must be corrected before issue.
  - Issue Form 3-145 and formally approve the MOE.
  - The certificate reference number format is specified by EASA.
  - "The certificate shall be issued for an unlimited duration".
  - Approve the MOE procedure for changes not requiring prior approval.
  - AMC1 145.B.310(d) (refusal): inform the applicant of the right of appeal under national law.
- **Changes (145.B.330):**
  - Verify before approving.
  - Set the conditions for operating during the change, or suspend.
  - If a change requiring approval is implemented without approval, consider suspend, limit or revoke.
  - Review changes not requiring prior approval within oversight (AMC: audit the process and sample the changes).
  - AMC1: control nominee changes for 145.A.30(a), (b), (c), (ca) and (k) personnel. The CA may ask for a résumé or interview the nominee. Audit limited to the extent of the change. The CA may request the risk assessment under AMC2 145.A.85. Document the assessment on the relevant parts of Form 6.
  - GM1: a name change means the certificate is reissued after the MOE is amended, with no audit unless other aspects have changed.
- **Findings (145.B.350):**
  - Communicate findings in writing and request corrective action.
  - If a level 1 finding relates directly to an aircraft, inform the State of Registry CA.
  - Level 1: immediate action to prohibit or limit activities, and if appropriate revoke, limit or suspend in whole or part until corrective action succeeds.
  - Level 2: corrective action period appropriate to the nature of the finding, "initially not be more than 3 months", running from the date of the written communication. May be extended at the end of that period if a CAP has been agreed. The CA assesses and accepts the CAP and implementation plan.
  - Failure to submit an acceptable CAP, or to implement it in time, raises the finding to level 1.
  - Record all findings, enforcement measures, corrective actions and closure dates.
  - A CA overseeing on behalf of another CA (145.B.300(d)) informs the certifying CA and indicates the finding level.
  - Observations are communicated in writing and recorded.
- **Suspension, limitation, revocation (145.B.355):**
  - (a) Suspend when there are reasonable grounds that it is needed to prevent a credible threat to aircraft safety.
  - (b) Suspend, revoke or limit as required by 145.B.350.
  - (c) Suspend or limit if unforeseeable circumstances outside the CA's control prevent oversight over the cycle.
- **Audit conduct (AMC1 145.B.310(a), AMC2 145.B.305(b)):** inspectors are accompanied by a senior member of the organisation, normally the compliance monitoring manager. For subcontractor audits, a senior technical member of the Part-145 organisation accompanies them. All findings are given at the end of the audit.

### Maintenance organisation (obligations visible from this chunk)
- Correct all initial-certification findings before the certificate is issued.
- Submit a corrective action plan and implement it within ≤ 3 months for level 2 findings (or the extended period).
- Allow CA access. Two written requests refused counts as a level 1 finding.
- Keep the MOE scope of work matching the classes and ratings on the certificate.
- The accountable manager signs the exposition statement.
- Have a control procedure in the MOE for: temporary removal of components by A-rated organisations; installed-engine work by B-rated organisations; installed-component work by C-rated organisations; NDT without D1.
- Component capability list amendments may go through the 145.A.85(c) "no prior approval" procedure (Appendix II(k)).
- **FTS training (Appendix IV):**
  - Phase 1: before working unsupervised, "but not later than 6 months after joining".
  - Phase 2: "by 31 December 2010 or within 12 months of joining the organisation, whichever comes later". Multiple-choice exam with pass mark 75%. "A duration of 8 hours" is acceptable.
  - Recurrent: "in each 2-year period".

### Certifying staff under Appendix IV (non-Part-66)
- Hold a national licence or authorisation fully compliant with ICAO Annex 1. Scope must not exceed the more restrictive of the licence and the authorisation.
- Training in Part-66 modules 9 (human factors) and 10 (aviation legislation).
- Experience: **5 years** for line certifying staff, **8 years** for base certifying staff, **3 years** if the scope does not exceed Part-66 category A.
- Line certifying staff and base support staff: type training and exam at B1, B2 or B3 level for each type. Category A-scope staff may do task training instead.
- Base certifying staff: type training at C level, except the first type, which is at B1, B2 or B3 level.
- Protected rights: privileges held before Part-66 entry into force continue without points 1(c)-(f). Extending scope requires point 1, but additional type training does not require 1(c) and (d).

## 4. Defined terms
- **AltMoC:** alternative means of compliance, other than the EASA AMC. Usable by the CA or its organisations. Must be notified to EASA. Reusable elsewhere only if processed again.
- **Safety-significant information stemming from occurrence reports** (GM1 145.B.125(b)): (a) a conclusive safety analysis summarising occurrence data with in-depth analysis relevant to EASA safety action planning; (b) individual occurrence data where EASA is the CA, meeting the GM3 criteria.
- **Level 1 finding:** significant non-compliance with 2018/1139 and its acts, with the organisation's procedures and manuals, or with the certificate or terms of approval, "which lowers safety or seriously endangers flight safety". It also always includes:
  1. Failure to grant access to facilities (145.A.140) during normal operating hours "after two written requests".
  2. Obtaining or maintaining the certificate by falsified evidence.
  3. Malpractice or fraudulent use of the certificate.
  4. "the lack of an accountable manager".
- **Level 2 finding:** any non-compliance that is not level 1.
- **Observation:** issued when no level 1 or 2 finding applies: (1) an item whose performance is ineffective; (2) an item with the potential to cause a non-compliance; (3) suggestions or improvements of interest for safety performance. GM1: findings are for non-compliance, observations are for compliant organisations as continuous-improvement input. Mismanaging or ignoring observations may lead to a level 2 finding. The GM examples use tool control (145.A.40(b)):
  - Level 2: tools missing from the register, or a wrong P/N or S/N.
  - Observations: backlog of tools awaiting calibration; register software process not detailed enough; confusing colour of the unserviceable tag.
- **Oversight planning cycle:** the period within which all applicable requirements are audited. ≤ 24 months by default, extendable to 36 or 48 months. Normally starts at the first certificate issue date.
- **Focused inspection:** inspection of a specific area, element or aspect, required in each 12-month segment of a cycle longer than 24 months.
- **Qualified entity:** a body to which certification or oversight tasks are allocated (Annex VI to 2018/1139).
- **Category A class rating:** aircraft maintenance, including components and engines/APUs **only while fitted**. Temporary removal for access is allowed unless it creates maintenance the organisation is not approved for, under an MOE control procedure.
- **Category B class rating:** uninstalled engines and APUs, and their components while fitted to the engine or APU. Installed-engine work is allowed with an approved MOE procedure.
- **Category C class rating:** uninstalled components, excluding complete engines and APUs. Installed-component work is allowed with an approved MOE procedure.
- **Category D class rating:** self-contained specialised service (D1 NDT). Only needed when NDT is done as a task for another organisation. A, B or C holders can do NDT on their own products if the MOE has NDT procedures.
- **Base / Line:** subdivisions of category A. A line facility located at the main base still needs a "Line" approval.
- **Series:** a specific type series (for example Airbus 300/310/319, Boeing 737-300 series, RB211-524 series, Cessna 150 or 172, Beech 55 series, Continental O-200 series).
- **Type:** a specific type or model (for example Airbus 310-240, RB211-524 B4, Cessna 172RG). Any number of series or types may be quoted.
- **Group:** for example "Cessna single piston engine aircraft" or "Lycoming non-supercharged piston engines".
- **Relevant engineering degree** (AMC2 145.B.200(a)(3)): aeronautical, mechanical, electrical, electronic, avionics or other relevant studies.
- **Relevant sample** (inspector type courses): courses covering the typical aircraft or components within the scope of work.
- **CDCCL:** Critical Design Configuration Control Limitations. **FTS:** Fuel Tank Safety. **FRS:** Flammability Reduction System. **ALI:** Airworthiness Limitation Items. **ECCAIRS:** European Coordination Centre for Accident and Incident Reporting Systems.

**Validation rules:**
- Every rating must have a non-empty limitation (Appendix II(i)).
- Base and Line flags apply only to A1-A4. At least one must be YES for an A rating. A line station at the main base still needs Line = YES.
- The ARC privilege is allowed only on A2, A3 and A4, and only for aircraft covered by Part-ML. Block it on A1. Do not force a "No" display (GM1 Appendix III).
- An approval must have between one class and rating (with limitations) and all classes and ratings (with limitations).

No A1, B1, B3. The CA may limit further. Enforce these as hard limits when one_person_organisation is true.
- Scope match: the MOE scope of work (MOE 1.9) must match the classes and ratings. Flag mismatches. The MOE scope of work "defines the exact limits".
- Work-order eligibility check: when a work package is created for an aircraft, engine or component, check it against the ratings and limitations (series, type, group, task) and against Base/Line, depending on whether the work is base or line (MOE 2.33 procedure). For an A-rated organisation doing component work, check that the component stays fitted or is only temporarily removed under the MOE procedure. Installed-engine work by a B-rated organisation, or installed-component work by a C-rated organisation, needs the MOE procedure flag. NDT done for third parties needs D1. In-house NDT needs MOE NDT procedures.
- The component capability list can be amended through the "changes not requiring prior approval" procedure if the MOE says so (Appendix II(k)). Model the capability list as a versioned child list of C ratings.

**Certificate reissue rules:**
- An MOE revision that affects certificate content creates a new certificate revision, updating "date of last revision approved" and "revision no".
- An MOE revision that does not affect the certificate does not trigger reissue.
- A name change triggers reissue after the MOE is amended (normally no audit).

### 5.2 Findings
```
Finding
  id, approval_id, audit_id, source (CA oversight | other CA under 145.B.300(d) | other means)
  reference_point   // Form 6 Part 2 point (e.g. 145.A.40) or Part 3 MOE item (e.g. 2.5)
  level             // LEVEL_1 | LEVEL_2  (Observation is a separate entity)
  description
  aircraft_related: bool, aircraft_registration?, state_of_registry?  // L1 aircraft-related -> notify State of Registry CA
  date_raised, date_communicated_in_writing   // L2 clock starts at written communication
  cap_submitted_date, cap_status (Pending | Accepted | Rejected)
  implementation_plan
  due_date          // L2: <= communicated_date + 3 months (initial)
  extension[] {new_due_date, reason, cap_agreed: bool}   // extension only if CAP agreed
  status            // Open | CAP Submitted | CAP Accepted | Extended | Closed | Escalated-to-L1
  closed_date, closure_reference
  enforcement_measures[]   // limit / suspend (whole/part) / revoke / prohibit activities
```
**Rules and alerts:**
- **Level 1:** immediate. Generate a task for "immediate and appropriate action". The approval status may change to Suspended, Limited or Revoked, in whole or in part, until corrective action succeeds. Level 1 categories to select: significant non-compliance, access denied after 2 written requests, falsification, malpractice or fraud, no accountable manager.
- **Level 2:** default due date = written communication date + an appropriate period, **capped at 3 months initially**. An extension is only possible at the end of the period and only with an agreed CAP. Alerts at, for example, T-30, T-14 and T-7 days, and at overdue.
- **Auto-escalation:** if no acceptable CAP is submitted, or the action is not done by the accepted or extended due date, set level = LEVEL_1 and trigger the level 1 workflow.
- **Initial certification:** block certificate issue until all findings are Closed. Findings must be confirmed in writing within **2 weeks of the on-site audit** (AMC). Alert if not sent.
- Keep a closure date and reference for every finding (145.B.350(d)(4), AMC2 145.B.310(c)).
- An extended cycle requires that "all corrective actions have been implemented within the time period that was accepted or extended", so a dashboard KPI should track on-time closure rate.

```
Observation
  id, approval_id, audit_id
  type: INEFFECTIVE_ITEM | POTENTIAL_NON_COMPLIANCE | IMPROVEMENT_SUGGESTION
  description, date_communicated_in_writing, response?, status
```
Observations must be communicated in writing and recorded. Track how they are managed, because ignoring them can lead to a level 2 finding.

Note: Part-145 organisation users would mirror these findings as **incoming authority findings** in their own compliance monitoring module (MOE 3.8.3). The same Level 1 / Level 2 structure applies to Part-145 internal audit findings elsewhere in the regulation (145.A.95, not in this chunk).

### 5.3 Oversight cycle and programme
```
OversightCycle
  approval_id, cycle_no
  start_date          // first cycle normally = first certificate issue date; may be shortened to align to calendar year
  length_months       // default 24; allowed 1..24, 36 (conditions), 48 (conditions + approved continuous reporting)
  extension_basis {safety_risk_mgmt_effective, a85_compliance_and_change_control, no_level1_in_prev_24m, all_CA_on_time, continuous_reporting_approved}
  end_date
  annual_review_dates[]       // review cycle and programme annually
  planned_items[] -> OversightItem
  recommendation_report (Form 6 Part 5) at cycle end
```
```
OversightItem
  type: MANAGEMENT_SYSTEM_ASSESSMENT | PROCESS_AUDIT | PRODUCT_AUDIT | AIRWORTHINESS_REVIEW_SAMPLE | UNANNOUNCED_INSPECTION | FOCUSED_INSPECTION | ACCOUNTABLE_MANAGER_MEETING | SUBCONTRACTOR_AUDIT | CHANGE_AUDIT
  scope (Part-145 points / MOE items / class-rating / site)
  site, on_site: bool
  due_date, done_date
  inspectors[], accompanied_by (senior member/CMM; senior technical member for subcontractor audits)
  report (areas/processes audited + findings)
```
**Rules and alerts:**
- Cycle length ≤ 24 months unless all four extension conditions held over the previous 24 months. In that case up to 36 months. Up to 48 months only if a continuous reporting system has also been approved. Make the system check the conditions automatically from the findings history, for example any level 1 in the last 24 months blocks extension.
- If the cycle is longer than 24 months, require at least one FOCUSED_INSPECTION in each 12-month segment. Alert if a segment has none. If results show decreased performance, prompt a revert to ≤ 24 months.
- The cycle may be shortened when safety performance decreases.
- At least one on-site audit per cycle.
- Every applicable requirement (the Form 6 Part 2 list) must be audited within the cycle. Show a coverage heat map of points × class/product line (the five columns), with status tick, X, N/A or N/R. N/R items at cycle end are coverage gaps.
- Annual review of the programme. Alert 12 months after the last review.
- Accountable manager meetings within each cycle. At least one AM meeting during initial certification.
- Cross-border facility: record that the host CA was notified before the on-site audit. Block or alert if it was not.
- Integrated oversight for organisations holding several certificates (for example CAMO + 145): audit-item credit allowed only if the same item, with evidence on record, corrective actions closed, and no deterioration.
- Programme planning inputs (AMC1 145.B.305(b)): management system effectiveness, industry standards, scope of the changes-not-requiring-prior-approval procedure, AltMoC procedures, number of locations and their activities, subcontractors, and volume per A, B, C and D rating.
- Inputs from 145.B.300(f): occurrence reports, reports from one-off certification authorisations (145.A.30(j)(5)), ramp inspections (Part-ARO RAMP), product surveys (M.B.303 / ML.B.303), product audits (CAMO.B.305(b)(1) / 145.B.305(b)(1)), and physical surveys or partial airworthiness reviews (AMC M.B.901).

- Implementing a prior-approval change without approval leads to a flag that the CA must consider suspension, limitation or revocation (a level 1 risk).
- Changes not requiring prior approval: the organisation logs and notifies them per its MOE 1.11 procedure. The CA samples them within the cycle. Provide a CNA register that can be filtered and sampled.

### 5.5 Status transitions for the approval
- Active → Suspended (whole or partial) / Limited: on a level 1 finding; on a credible threat to aircraft safety; when unforeseeable circumstances prevent oversight; or during a change if the CA decides so.
- Suspended / Limited → Active: after successful corrective action.
- Any → Revoked.
- Active → Surrendered or Superseded (Form 3 condition 4).
- There is no expiry date. Do not model a renewal deadline. Instead model **continued validity** depending on compliance, an access check, and an accountable manager existing (no accountable manager = level 1).

### 5.6 Certifying staff and personnel modules
- **Appendix IV non-Part-66 certifying staff record:** national licence or authorisation (ICAO Annex 1 compliant flag); scope (the more restrictive of licence and authorisation); module 9 and 10 training evidence; maintenance experience in years (validate ≥ 5 for line, ≥ 8 for base, ≥ 3 for category-A scope); type training and exam level per type (B1, B2 or B3 for line certifying staff and base support staff; C for base certifying staff, with the first type at B1, B2 or B3; task training allowed for A scope); protected-rights flag with the date privileges were held. Scope extension re-triggers point 1 checks, but additional type training skips 1(c) and 1(d).
- **FTS training tracker** (for organisations maintaining CS-25 large aeroplanes certified after 1 Jan 1958 with ≥ 30 passenger seats or ≥ 7 500 lb (3 402 kg) payload, or with CS-25 amendment 1 or later in the basis, and fuel system components where the maintenance data is affected by CDCCL):
  - Roles: management, CMM, safety manager and compliance monitoring staff get Phase 1 only. Planners, performers, supervisors, inspectors and certifiers get Phase 1 + Phase 2 + recurrent.
  - Phase 1 due: before unsupervised work and ≤ 6 months after joining. Trainer signature required.
  - Phase 2 due: within 12 months of joining (or 31 Dec 2010, whichever is later; now historical). MCQ exam with pass mark ≥ 75%. 8 hours is acceptable. Record e-learning exam content and results.
  - Recurrent: every 2 years. The syllabus sits in MOE 3.9.
  - Credit: earlier ED Decision 2007/002/R Level 1 or Level 2 courses count towards Phase 1 or Phase 2 (except recurrent).
- **CA-side inspector qualification (if the app ever serves authorities):** ≥ 5 years of experience before independent work; degree or AMT qualification; type course at ≥ Part-66 Level 1; FTS training; initial training list; recurrent training; competency assessment every ≤ 3 years with recorded results.

### 5.7 MOE module
- Use the Form 6 Part 3 list as the **MOE template and checklist** (Parts 1, 2, L2, 3, 4, 5). Each section has a compliance status (tick, X, N/A, N/R) and links to findings.
- MOE revision log with a flag "affects certificate content" that drives Form 3 reissue.
- Accountable manager signed statement (MOE 1.1). Required before initial issue.
- Lists that should be live data, not static text: certifying, support and ARC staff (1.6); facilities (1.8); scope of work (1.9); commercial operators served (4.1); subcontractors (5.2); line stations (5.3); contracted organisations (5.4); AltMoC used (5.5).
- AltMoC register: summary, content, compliance statement, safety assessment, CA approval date, EASA notified date, MOE amendment date agreed.

### 5.8 Records and retention
- CA-side records: minimum 5 years from creation. Configure retention policies per record type.
- Electronic records: backup within 24 hours of a new entry, at a separate location; access controls that stop unauthorised alteration (audit trail); sensitive applicant data stored securely; and migration care so data stays accessible for the full retention period. This is a good baseline for app infrastructure: daily or real-time off-site backup, immutable audit log, and role-based access.

### 5.9 Permissions and roles
- CA inspector: raise findings, observations and audit reports.
- CA independent reviewer: sign the Form 6 review.
- CA manager: approve changes, issue or reissue certificates, enforcement.
- Organisation: accountable manager, compliance monitoring manager (accompanies audits), nominated persons (145.A.30 a/b/c/ca/k), and certifying staff.
- An organisation can view findings and observations addressed to it, submit CAPs and closure evidence, and submit change applications. It cannot edit finding level or due date.
- Information security reports (Part-IS): two-tier visibility. A public alert part, and a sensitive part restricted to need-to-know (GM1 145.B.135A). Use an agreed taxonomy.

### 5.10 Occurrence and safety information hooks
- Occurrence records should export in an ECCAIRS-compatible format with attachments.
- Safety measures or directives issued by the CA (145.B.135(d)) are notified "immediately" to affected organisations. The app could ingest them as mandatory actions with acknowledgement tracking.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM
- **GM1 145.B.120:** The CA must demonstrate that its AltMoC complies. An AltMoC can be for organisations or for the authority itself. GM1 (b);(c): where no EASA AMC exists, national guides may be developed and offered to EASA for transposition.
- **AMC1 145.B.200:** The organisational structure is sized to the number of certificates, organisations and industry growth. The CA must not delegate so that organisations "in effect, regulate themselves". Tasks must not rely solely on individuals (cover for illness or leave).

Planning data: audits per cycle, audit duration, prep/on-site/report/follow-up time, unannounced inspections, minimum inspectors. A spreadsheet is recommended.
