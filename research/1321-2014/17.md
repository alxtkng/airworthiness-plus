## 2. Obligations

### 2.1 Who is responsible (ML.A.201)

**Owner (registered owner, lessee or operator)**
- Ultimately responsible for continuing airworthiness.
- Must ensure there is no flight unless:
  1. the aircraft is airworthy;
  2. operational and emergency equipment is serviceable, or clearly identified as unserviceable;
  3. the airworthiness certificate is valid. For Part-ML this means a CofA together with a valid ARC; the ARC itself is in ML.A.901, outside this chunk;
  4. maintenance is per the AMP.
- Must give the authority access to the aircraft and its records (ML.A.201(g)).

**Lessee**
- Takes on the ML.A.201(a) duties if named in the registration document or the lease.

**Mandatory CAMO/CAO (ML.A.201(e))**

This applies to:
- commercial ATO/DTO aircraft;
- aircraft **not** operated under Part-NCO (for example SPO or commercial operations);
- balloons under Part-BOP Subpart-ADD (commercial balloon operators);
- sailplanes under Part-SAO Subpart-DEC.

The operator must:
- hold a CAMO or CAO (CAM privilege) approval, or contract one under Appendix I;
- use only maintenance organisations (Part-M Subpart F, Part-145 or Part-CAO). No independent certifying staff and no pilot-owner;
- ensure (a) is met.

**Optional CAMO/CAO (ML.A.201(f))**
- All other owners (private Part-NCO and similar) may contract.
- With a contract, the CAMO/CAO "shall assume responsibility for the proper performance of those tasks". A written contract per Appendix I is required.
- Without a contract, the owner does the management tasks. GM1 ML.A.201(f): the owner should "properly and realistically self-assess" their competence or seek expertise.

**AOC aircraft used non-commercially or for SPO (ML.A.201(h))**
- The AOC holder's CAMO/CAO must perform the tasks.
- GM: (h) is not a derogation, so the other operator still needs an Appendix I written contract with that CAMO/CAO.

**Others**
- Maintenance performers are responsible for the tasks they perform (ML.A.201(c)).
- The PIC is responsible for the pre-flight inspection (ML.A.201(d)).

**Summary matrix (GM1 ML.A.201), for balloons, sailplanes and other aircraft alike**

| | Mandatory column: Subpart-ADD / Subpart-DEC / non-Part-NCO, and commercial ATO/DTO | Optional column: other (private Part-NCO, non-ADD balloon, non-DEC sailplane, non-commercial ATO/DTO) |
|---|---|---|
| CAMO/CAO contract | Required | Not required (owner may choose) |
| AMP | Approved by the CAMO/CAO | Declared by the owner if there is no CAMO/CAO, or approved by the CAMO/CAO if contracted. No document needed if ML.A.302(e) is met. |
| Maintenance | Maintenance organisation only | Maintenance organisation, independent certifying staff, or pilot-owner ("in the limit of their privileges") |
| AR / ARC | Maintenance organisation (together with the 100 h/annual inspection), the contracted CAMO/CAO, or the competent authority | Maintenance organisation or independent certifying staff (both together with the 100 h/annual inspection), the CAMO/CAO if contracted, or the competent authority |

**Not a "commercial ATO/DTO" (GM1 ML.A.201(e)):**
- (a) A non-profit sport or leisure aviation ATO/DTO operating aircraft it owns or dry-leases, where flights involving non-members are only a "marginal activity".
- (b) An owner's Part-NCO aircraft used with an ATO/DTO instructor for training, where the contract and procedures allow it. Continuing airworthiness stays with the owner or the owner's CAMO/CAO.
- (c) Aircraft used for very limited training flights because of their specific configuration.

### 2.2 Occurrence reporting (ML.A.202)

- **Who reports:** any person or organisation responsible under ML.A.201. This is without prejudice to Part-145 and Part-CAMO reporting.
- **What:** "any identified condition of an aircraft or component which endangers flight safety".
- **Recipients:**
  - the competent authority of the state of registry, plus the authority of the state of the operator if different;
  - the TC/STC holder.
  - Where maintenance or the AR is done under a written contract, the contractor also reports to the owner and, if different, to the CAMO/CAO.
- **Timing:** "as soon as possible, but no later than 72 hours from the moment when the person or organisation identified the condition", unless exceptional circumstances apply.
- **Form and content:** in the manner the competent authority determines, with all pertinent known information.
- **AMC1:**
  - Ensure the DAH (or Part 21 Light declarant) gets adequate reports so it can issue service instructions.
  - Liaise with the DAH on whether existing or proposed service information resolves the problem.
  - See AMC 20-8.
- UK guess: reporting goes via the CAA Occurrence Reporting portal under UK Reg 376/2014.

### 2.3 Continuing airworthiness tasks (ML.A.301)

These are the task categories the app must track:
- pre-flight inspection;
- defect and damage rectification, taking the MEL and CDL into account if they exist;
- maintenance per the AMP;
- ADs, operational directives with a continuing airworthiness impact, EASA continuing airworthiness requirements, and immediate safety measures from the competent authority;
- modifications and repairs (ML.A.304 data);
- maintenance check flights (MCFs) when necessary.

**Defects and deferred defects in this chunk**
- ML.A.301(b) requires rectification of any defect or damage "affecting safe operation", with the MEL/CDL taken into account.
- ML.A.201(a)(2): unserviceable equipment must be "clearly identified as unserviceable".
- ML.A.305(d)(6): the records must hold "the current list of deferred maintenance".
- The detailed rules on deferral (ML.A.403, aircraft defects) are **not** in this chunk.

**Maintenance check flights (GM1 ML.A.301(f))**

Air-ops rules define MCFs, and they are under the operator's control. Maintenance involvement is agreed in advance. There are four scenarios:
1. **Maintenance data requires an MCF before the maintenance can be completed.**
   - Issue a certificate after incomplete maintenance (ML.A.801(f) / 145.A.50(e)). The aircraft flies on its airworthiness certificate.
   - Open a new ML.A.305 logbook entry for the MCF, with limitations, effects on equipment, the data reference and the post-flight actions.
   - After a successful MCF, complete the records and issue the CRS.
2. **Discretionary MCF** (reliability or quality reasons) after properly completed maintenance. The CRS is issued first and the certificate remains valid.
3. **MCF to confirm a fix after troubleshooting on the ground.** The CRS is issued before the flight. An open logbook entry requesting the flight may be recorded.
4. **Fault diagnosable only in flight, with no maintenance-data process.** The aircraft is not released, so a **permit to fly** (Reg 748/2012) is needed. After the flight and the work, it is released and returns to its CofA.

Where in-flight data is needed for the release, maintenance staff may appoint and brief crew to collect data and make statements on their behalf. The briefing covers scope, functions, process and post-flight reporting.

### 2.4 Aircraft Maintenance Programme (ML.A.302)

**Basic rules**
- Every aircraft has an AMP (a).
- **Declared or approved (b):**
  - Declared by the owner where no CAMO/CAO manages the aircraft.
  - Approved by the CAMO/CAO managing the aircraft.
  - Amendments follow the same route.
  - The declarer or approver "shall keep the AMP updated".
- Only **one AMP** applies at any given time (AMC1 (a)). Changing basis, for example from MIP to DAH ICA, may need extra transition maintenance.
- One AMP document may cover several registrations if the requirements for each registration are clearly identified (AMC1 (b)).

**Mandatory content (c)**
1. Identify the owner and the aircraft, including installed engines and propellers.
2. Basis, one of:
   - (a) the tasks of the applicable **MIP**;
   - (b) the **DAH ICA**;
   - (c) the **ICA of the declarant of a declaration of design compliance** (Part 21 Light; probably not in the UK).
3. It may include additional actions, or actions alternative to the DAH ICA, proposed by the owner, CAMO or CAO once declared or approved. Alternatives to the ICA "shall not be less restrictive than those set out in the applicable MIP".
4. It must include **all mandatory continuing airworthiness information**: repetitive ADs, the ALS of the ICA, and specific maintenance requirements in the TCDS.
5. It must identify additional tasks arising from the type, configuration and operation, considering at least:
   - installed equipment and modifications;
   - repairs;
   - life-limited and flight-safety-critical components;
   - non-mandatory maintenance recommendations (TBO, SBs, SLs);
   - operational directives and requirements on periodic inspection of equipment;
   - special operational approvals;
   - use of the aircraft and its operational environment.
6. It must state **whether pilot-owners are authorised to perform maintenance**.
7. **When declared:** a signed statement by the owner that this is the AMP for the particular registration and that the owner "is fully responsible for its content and, in particular, for any deviations from the DAH's recommendations".
8. **When approved:** signed by the CAMO/CAO, which retains records justifying any deviation from the DAH's recommendations.
9. **Annual review:** "reviewed at least annually in order to assess its effectiveness".
   - Done either (a) by the person performing the airworthiness review, in conjunction with the AR, or (b) by the managing CAMO/CAO when the review is not done with an AR. GM2 gives the example of an ARC extension.
   - If the review shows aircraft deficiencies linked to AMP content, the AMP must be amended.
   - If the reviewer disagrees with the amendment measures taken by the owner, CAMO or CAO, the reviewer informs the competent authority of the state of registry. The authority decides the necessary amendments, raises findings, and may act under ML.B.304.

**Minimum Inspection Programme (d)**

Intervals (d)(1):
- **Aeroplanes, TMGs and balloons:** "every annual or 100-h interval, whichever comes first, to which a tolerance of 1 month or 10 h may be applied. The next interval shall be calculated as from the time the inspection takes place."
- **Sailplanes and powered sailplanes other than TMGs:** "every annual interval to which a tolerance of 1 month may be applied." The next interval is calculated from the time the inspection takes place.
- AMC warning: using the 1-month tolerance on the annual inspection "may result in an expired ARC".
- GM1 (c)(3) remark: the tolerances are for **one-time** interval extensions only.
- GM1 ML.A.302 (g): the authority cannot authorise deviation from the AMP schedule beyond the (d)(1) tolerances. The owner may declare an amended AMP instead.

Content (d)(2), as applicable to type:
- (a) DAH servicing tasks;
- (b) markings inspection;
- (c) review of weighing records, and weighing per Reg 965/2012, 2018/395 and 2018/1976;
- (d) operational test of the transponder, if installed;
- (e) functional test of the pitot-static system;
- (f) for aeroplanes:
  - operational tests of power/rpm, magnetos, fuel and oil pressure and engine temperatures;
  - the published run-up procedure for automated engine control (FADEC);
  - for dry-sump, turbocharged and liquid-cooled engines, an operational test for signs of disturbed fluid circulation;
- (g) inspection of the condition and attachment of structural items, systems and components, by area:
  - **aeroplanes:** airframe; cabin and cockpit; landing gear; wing and centre section; flight controls; empennage; avionics and electrics; power plant; clutches and gearboxes; propeller; miscellaneous such as the ballistic rescue system;
  - **sailplanes and powered sailplanes:** airframe; cabin and cockpit; landing gear; wing and centre section; empennage; avionics and electrics; power plant (powered sailplanes); miscellaneous such as removable ballast, drag chute and water ballast;
  - **hot-air balloons:** envelope, burner, basket, fuel containers, equipment and instruments;
  - **gas balloons:** envelope, basket, equipment and instruments.

There is no MIP for **rotorcraft or airships**. Their AMP must be based on the DAH ICA.

**Derogation: no AMP document (e) and (f)**

No owner declaration, no CAMO/CAO approval and no AMP document are required if **all** of these hold:
1. all DAH ICA are followed without deviation;
2. all non-mandatory maintenance recommendations (TBO, SBs, SLs) are followed without deviation;
3. there are no additional tasks arising from equipment or modifications, repairs, life-limited or flight-safety-critical components, special operational approvals, or use and environment;
4. "Pilot-owners are authorised to perform Pilot-owner maintenance".
   - The derogation does not apply if the pilot-owner, or for a jointly owned aircraft any of the pilot-owners, is not authorised, because that has to be specified in a declared or approved AMP.
   - Note the odd drafting of the 2023/989 text. Practical reading: the derogation is only usable where the pilot-owner is to be authorised. Flag for legal check. The UK text may predate this amendment (pre-2023 EU text had different wording). UK difference is a guess.

Under the derogation, the AMP **consists of** (f):
- the DAH ICA;
- the TBO, SB and SL recommendations;
- the mandatory continuing airworthiness information (repetitive ADs, ALS, TCDS requirements);
- tasks from specific operational or airspace directives on instruments and equipment.

**Responsibilities (GM1 ML.A.302 and GM2 ML.A.302)**
- **CAMO/CAO contracted:**
  - develops and approves the AMP;
  - states its basis;
  - identifies the owner and the aircraft, engine and propeller;
  - includes mandatory information and the additional tasks from its assessment of the DAH instructions;
  - **justifies** deviations, which must not fall below the MIP;
  - customises the AMP per (c)(5);
  - keeps the justifications and **gives the owner a copy**.
  - Alternative tasks or escalations chosen by the CAMO/CAO need no competent authority approval (GM1 (c)(2)(b)).
- **No CAMO/CAO:**
  - the owner develops and declares the AMP and takes full responsibility, including for the consequences of deviations;
  - deviations **need not be justified** but **must be identified**;
  - the AMP must still meet (c), must not fall below the MIP, and must include mandatory information.
- **Treatment of the declared AMP:**
  - It "cannot be challenged up front" by the authority or by the contracted maintenance organisation.
  - It is the basis for maintenance planning, ARs and ACAM inspections (ML.B.303).
  - It is reviewed periodically at the AR.
  - Where the owner and the AR staff disagree, the authority requests a copy, decides the amendments, raises a finding, may act under ML.B.304, and may adapt its ACAM programme.
  - There is no obligation to send the AMP to the authority, but the authority can request it at any time (AMC1 ML.B.201).
  - ARs and ACAM inspections can focus on areas affected by deviations.
- **Mandatory continuing airworthiness information intervals** (ALI, CMR, LLP and so on) "cannot be extended by a CAMO/CAO. The escalation of such tasks is to be approved by the Agency" (GM1 (c)(4)).

### 2.5 Airworthiness directives (ML.A.303)

- "Any applicable AD must be carried out within the requirements of that AD unless otherwise specified by the Agency."
- Repetitive ADs must also be in the AMP (ML.A.302(c)(4)).
- AD status must be kept in the records (ML.A.305(d)(1)). The record must say why a type-applicable AD is not applicable to the particular aircraft or component. Superseded or cancelled ADs need not be listed (AMC1 ML.A.305(c)).
- AD status must be "sufficiently detailed to identify the complied AD and/or the due limit" (AMC1 ML.A.305(d)).
- UK guess: "Agency" is the CAA. EASA ADs adopted before 2021 remain, plus CAA-issued or adopted ADs. The UK has its own AD adoption process (state of design ADs are automatically applicable under UK practice). Verify.

### 2.6 Modifications and repairs (ML.A.304)

- The repairer must assess any damage.
- Data must be one of:
  - Agency-approved;
  - approved by a Part 21 DOA;
  - CS-STAN, under 21.A.90B (standard changes) or 21.A.431B (standard repairs);
  - Part 21 Light requirements (21L.A.62/102/202/222);
  - declared by a Part 21 Light declarant.
- Records: modification and repair status (ML.A.305(d)(2)), retained for 12 months after permanent withdrawal (ML.A.305(h)(6)).

### 2.7 Continuing airworthiness records, logbook, "technical log" (ML.A.305)

- **CRS entry deadline:** at completion of any maintenance, the ML.A.801 CRS is entered in the record system "as soon as possible but not later than 30 days after the day of the completion of the maintenance task".
- **Composition:** aircraft logbook; engine logbook(s) or engine module log cards; propeller logbook(s); and log cards for any service-life-limited component, as appropriate.
- **Aircraft logbook entries:** aircraft type and registration mark, and the date with total flight time, flight cycles and landings.
- **Current status the records must contain:**
  1. ADs and immediate safety measures from the competent authority;
  2. modifications, repairs and other DAH maintenance recommendations;
  3. AMP compliance;
  4. service-life-limited components;
  5. the current mass and balance report;
  6. the current list of deferred maintenance.
- **Component entries:** in addition to EASA Form 1 (Part-M Appendix II) or equivalent, the engine, propeller, engine module or life-limited part logbook or log card records:
  - the component identification;
  - the type, serial number and registration of what it is fitted to, with references to installation and removal;
  - the date with accumulated total flight time, cycles, landings and calendar time, as relevant;
  - the current status information of (d) applicable to the component.
- **Control:** the person or organisation managing continuing airworthiness (owner or CAMO/CAO) controls the records and presents them to the authority on request.
- **Entries:** "clear and accurate". A correction must clearly show the original entry. In software this means no destructive edits.
- There is **no separate technical log** requirement in Part-ML. Defect and deferral entries go in the aircraft logbook (see the MCF GM, which refers to the "ML.A.305 aircraft logbook").

### 2.8 Transfer of records (ML.A.307)

- On permanent transfer to a new owner, the transferring owner ensures the ML.A.305 records go too.
- On contracting a CAMO or CAO, the owner ensures the records are transferred to the organisation.
- The ML.A.305(h) retention periods continue to apply to the new owner, CAMO or CAO.

### 2.9 Contract with a CAMO or CAO: what transfers (from this chunk only)

- **Responsibility:** for the contracted management tasks, responsibility passes to the organisation ("shall assume responsibility for the proper performance of those tasks"). The owner remains the overall ML.A.201(a) responsible person.
- **Written contract:** required per **Appendix I** (contents not in this chunk).
- **AMP:** becomes "approved" by the CAMO/CAO rather than declared. The CAMO/CAO keeps the deviation justifications and gives the owner a copy.
- **Records:** transferred to the CAMO/CAO (ML.A.307(b)), which then controls them (ML.A.305(f)).
- **Reporting:** contracted maintenance or AR providers must report safety conditions to the owner and the CAMO/CAO (ML.A.202(c)).
- **Annual AMP review:** if not done with the AR, done by the CAMO/CAO.

### 2.10 Pilot-owner maintenance

- In this chunk:
  - the AMP must state whether pilot-owners are authorised (ML.A.302(c)(6));
  - the EASA Form AMP block 6 lists named pilot-owner(s) with licence number, signature and date. A list may be referred to for jointly owned aircraft;
  - it applies only to balloons not under Subpart-ADD, sailplanes not under Subpart-DEC, and other aircraft under Part-NCO. It is "not allowed for aircraft operated by a commercial ATO/DTO";
  - pilot-owner authorisation is a condition of the ML.A.302(e) derogation;
  - changes in the list of pilot-owners are an annual review input.
- Elsewhere: the task list (Appendix II) and ML.A.803 conditions are in other chunks.

## 4. Defined terms

- **Independent certifying staff** (ML.1(c)(1)): "certifying staff who does not work on behalf of an approved maintenance organisation" and who complies with either Part-66, or, where Part-66 does not apply, the certifying staff requirements of the state of registry.
- **Maintenance organisation** (ML.1(c)(2)): an organisation approved under Part-M Subpart F, Part-145 Section A, or Part-CAO Section A.
- **Owner** (ML.1(c)(3)): "the person responsible for the continuing airworthiness of the aircraft", which may be the registered owner, the lessee (under a lease) or the operator.
- **Competent authority** (ML.1(b)): the authority designated by the Member State of registry.
- **AMP:** Aircraft Maintenance Program(me) (ML.A.201(a)(4)).
- **MIP:** minimum inspection programme (ML.A.302(c)(2)(a), (d)).
- **ICA:** instructions for continuing airworthiness.
- **DAH:** design approval holder. Per GM1 ML.A.302(c)(2)(b), the holder of a TC, restricted TC, STC, ETSO authorisation, or a repair or change approval. DAH ICA do **not** include other OEM data unless the DAH ICA clearly reference it.
- **Declarant:** a natural or legal person who submitted a declaration of design compliance under Part 21 Light.
- **ALS:** airworthiness limitation section. **TCDS:** type certificate data sheet. **TBO:** time between overhaul.
- **MEL:** minimum equipment list. **CDL:** configuration deviation list.
- **AD:** airworthiness directive.
- **CAO(-CAM):** a CAO with continuing airworthiness management privileges (GM1 ML.A.201).
- **TMG:** touring motor glider.
- **ELA1 / ELA2:** used but not defined here; defined in Reg 748/2012.
- **LSA:** light sport aeroplane. **VLA:** very light aeroplane.
- **MCF:** maintenance check flight; defined in the Air Operations Regulation.
- **ACAM:** aircraft continuing airworthiness monitoring (ML.B.303).
- **Mandatory continuing airworthiness information** (GM1 (c)(4)): ALI, CMR, safe life items or limits, LLP, time limits, retirement life, mandatory (airworthiness) inspections, and fuel airworthiness or fuel tank safety limitations.
- **Operational test / check** (GM1 ML.A.302(d)(2)): determines that an item is operating normally. No quantitative tolerances.
- **Functional test / check:** a quantitative check that one or more functions perform within the limits in the maintenance data. "The measured parameter should be recorded." A functional test is more restrictive than an operational test.
- **Alternative maintenance actions** (GM1 (c)(3)): deviations from the DAH ICA when the ICA are the AMP basis, for example a less frequent interval or a different task type. They must not be less than the MIP in frequency or task type.
- **Pilot-owner:** used here; defined in ML.A.803, which is in another chunk.

- Additional-requirement categories, each yes/no with a task list (Appendix B):
  - equipment and modifications;
  - repairs;

- Revision history.

**Derogation checker (ML.A.302(e))**

A guided checklist of four booleans:
- all ICA followed with no deviation;
- all SB/SL/TBO recommendations followed;
- no additional-task triggers;
- pilot-owners authorised.

If it passes, generate the (f) composition automatically: ICA + recommendations + mandatory information + operational/airspace tasks. Invalidate it automatically when a modification, repair, LLP, special approval or deviation is added. Prompt for a formal AMP when any condition fails.

**Maintenance task / schedule item**
- Source type: MIP / ICA / AD / ALS-ALI / CMR / TCDS / SB / SL / operational requirement / modification / repair / LLP / owner addition / alternative.
- Mandatory flag. Mandatory items cannot be escalated by the CAMO; only Agency (UK: CAA) approval allows it.
- Interval: hours, calendar, cycles or landings, whichever comes first.
- Tolerance, with a one-time-use flag.
- Task type: inspection / check / operational test / functional test / GVI / servicing / replacement. Keep a restrictiveness ordering for validation, e.g. functional > operational.
- Measured-value fields for functional tests.

**MIP inspection event**
- Category template: aeroplane / sailplane-powered sailplane / TMG / hot-air balloon. Gas balloon has its content defined in the IR, but the AMC gives no specific gas balloon MIP table.

- For TMGs, separate hour tracking of aircraft, engine and propeller is allowed between 100 h inspections, but at the 100 h/annual all elements must be covered.

- AD number, revision, applicability to type.
- Applicability to this serial or component, with the **reason if not applicable**.
- Compliance method, date and hours.
- Repetitive flag, interval, next due limit.

- Description, date found, found by, affects safe operation: yes/no.
- MEL/CDL reference if one exists.
- Status: open / deferred / rectified.

### 5.2 Workflows

1. **Onboarding classification:**
   - Is Part-ML applicable?
   - Is a CAMO mandatory?
   - Which maintainers are allowed?
   - Is an AMP needed (derogation check)?
   - Is the AMP route declared or approved?
2. **AMP authoring:** follow EASA Form AMP blocks 1 to 9, then sign-off.
   - Owner path: declaration text, e-signature.
   - CAMO path: approval with the approval reference and a justification file shared with the owner.
   - Version every amendment and re-sign or re-approve it.
3. **Alternative task validator:** compare each alternative against the ICA and the MIP equivalent.
   - Block the alternative if its interval is longer than the MIP interval, or its task type is less restrictive (per the GM1 (c)(3) table).
   - Allow it if no relevant MIP task exists.
   - Require a justification if CAMO-approved.
   - Also apply a risk-assessment helper using the AMC1 ML.A.302(c) risk factors (advisory).
4. **Annual AMP review:**
   - Due at most 12 months after the last review.
   - Linked to the AR or, if there is no AR (e.g. ARC extension), assigned to the CAMO/CAO.
   - Inputs per AMC1 (c)(9): the year's maintenance results, AR results, revisions to the MIP or ICA, configuration or operation changes, pilot-owner list changes, ADs and mandatory items.
   - Auto-assemble the last 12 months of maintenance records, including unscheduled maintenance, for the reviewer.
   - Outcome: no change / amend / disagreement escalated to the competent authority.
5. **Next-due calculation:**
   - The MIP annual/100 h is "whichever first".
   - Next due is recalculated from the actual inspection date and hours, **not** from the original due date.
   - Tolerance of +1 month / +10 h (aeroplanes, TMGs, balloons) or +1 month (sailplanes) shown as a grace window.
   - Warn that using the tolerance may cause ARC expiry; cross-check with the ARC date.
6. **CRS-to-record:** a CRS raises a task to enter it in the logbook. Alert at intervals before the 30-day limit and flag as overdue after it.
7. **Occurrence reporting:** start a 72 h countdown when the condition is identified. Generate recipient lists automatically. Include a contractor-to-owner/CAMO notification.
8. **Records transfer:** an ownership change or new CAMO contract triggers export and handover of the full record set and an audit-trail entry. Retention clocks carry over.
9. **AD management:** import ADs by type, engine and propeller. Classify each as applicable or not applicable with a reason. Track repetitive intervals. Pull repetitive ADs into the AMP automatically.
10. **Pre-flight / dispatch check (ML.A.201(a)):** a "can fly" status requires:
    - airworthiness certificate valid (and ARC from another chunk);
    - no overdue AMP items, ADs or LLPs;
    - no open defects affecting safe operation unless properly deferred;
    - unserviceable equipment identified.

### 5.3 Alerts

- AMP items due or overdue by hours, date, cycles or landings; in tolerance; tolerance exhausted.
- Tolerance use that would breach the ARC expiry date.
- AMP annual review due.
- AMP derogation invalidated by a new modification, repair or deviation.
- CRS not entered within 30 days.
- Occurrence report deadline of 72 h.
- New AD applicable, AD due, repetitive AD due.
- Life-limited component approaching its limit.
- Mass and balance report not current after a modification.
- Retention: block deletion before the retention period ends:
  - 36 months after release for detailed maintenance records;
  - 12 months after permanent withdrawal for total time, AD status and modification/repair details;
  - "until superseded" for AMP status and time since last scheduled maintenance.
- Balloon: fuel cylinder periodic inspection validity (e.g. 10 years). Fire extinguisher and first aid kit expiry. Sailplanes: oxygen bottle hydrostatic test date; wing strut re-inhibit every 3 years.
- ELT/PLB annual test (SIB 2019-09) and transponder test every 2 years (SIB 2011-15) as suggested AMP Appendix B items.

### 5.4 Permissions

- **Owner (no CAMO):** can author and declare the AMP and sign the declaration; manages records; records pre-flight inspections; can authorise pilot-owners in the AMP.
- **CAMO/CAO user:** can approve the AMP with its approval reference; owns the deviation justifications, which the owner can read; performs the annual review when it is not done with an AR; controls the records after transfer.
- **Owner (with CAMO):** read access to the AMP and justifications; must not be able to "declare" an AMP while a CAMO contract is active.
- **AR staff:** read access to the AMP, records and the last 12 months of maintenance. Can record the annual review outcome and escalate a disagreement to the authority. They are not responsible for the completeness of Appendix B but may sample.
- **Maintainers:**
  - maintenance organisation, independent certifying staff and pilot-owner can raise CRS entries;
  - independent certifying staff and pilot-owner are blocked when the aircraft is in the ML.A.201(e) mandatory regime;
  - the pilot-owner is only permitted if named in the AMP.
- **Competent authority (optional portal):** read and export of the AMP and records on request (ML.A.201(g), ML.A.305(f)).
- **Audit trail:** every edit preserves the original entry (ML.A.305(g)). An IT-only system needs at least one regularly updated backup and safeguards against unauthorised alteration (AMC1 ML.A.305(e)).

8. **Certification statement:** "I will ensure that the aircraft is maintained in accordance with this maintenance programme and that the maintenance programme will be reviewed and updated as required."

   - Fields: name or approval number, address, telephone/fax, email, signature/date.
9. **Appendices attached:** A, B, C and D, each YES/NO.

**Appendices**
- **Appendix A:** custom MIP tasks, only if not using the AMC1 ML.A.302(d) MIP.
- **Appendix B:** additional requirements.
  - Columns: task description, references, interval, and a tick box where the interval differs from the referenced document.
  - Only tasks included in the AMP are listed. Repetitive tasks not included, or interval differences, are kept with justifications in the CAMO/CAO files (Appendix D optional).
  - The owner, CAMO or CAO remains responsible for considering all instructions even if they are not adopted.
  - The AR person is not responsible for Appendix B completeness but may sample.
  - Pre-filled examples: "Emergency locator transmitters and personal locator beacon — annual testing, EASA SIB 2019-09, 1 Year"; "(if not using MIP or equivalent ICA task) Transponder test, EASA SIB 2011-15, 2 Years".
- **Appendix C:** alternatives to the ICA.
  - Columns: task description, recommended interval, alternative inspection/task, amended interval.
  - When contracted, the CAMO/CAO keeps the justifications and gives the owner a copy.
- **Appendix D:** optional additional information, for example the full task list or the SBs considered.

### AMC1 ML.A.302(c): risk-based approach to deviations
Applies to TBO extension or omission of DAH tasks. Factors are rated higher / medium / lower risk:

| Factor | Higher risk | Medium risk | Lower risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ops approval | commercial operation or commercial training | association training, non-commercial SPO | private |
| Flight rules | IFR | VFR night | VFR day |
| Weight | other than ELA1 | ELA1 other than LSA/VLA/sailplanes | LSA, VLA, sailplanes, powered sailplanes |
| Who manages | owner | — | CAMO/CAO |
| Who maintains | pilot-owner | independent certifying staff | maintenance organisation |
| Time in service | very high | medium | low |
| Utilisation | less than 50 h/yr | about 200 h/yr | more than 400 h/yr |
| ACAM findings | numerous (including ramp) | few | rare |
| Redundancy | single-engine | — | multi-engine |
| Supplementary measures | none | — | oil analysis, engine monitoring, borescope, corrosion inspections |
| Failure consequence | helicopter engine failure | aeroplane engine failure | sailplane or powered sailplane |

This is useful to CAMOs, AR staff and owners. An owner declaring the AMP still does not have to justify deviations.

**Product idea:** an advisory risk score when the user enters a TBO extension or other deviation.

### AMC1 ML.A.302(d): standard MIPs
There are three MIPs: aeroplanes ≤2730 kg; ELA2 sailplanes and powered sailplanes; ELA2 hot-air balloons. They comply with (d) but must still be customised per (c)(5), for example via the Form AMP.

**Aeroplane MIP** (annual/100 h, whichever first, with 1 month / 10 h tolerance). Notes: use the manufacturer's maintenance manual; check backup and secondary systems.
- **General:**
  - open panels and clean;
  - lubrication and servicing;
  - markings (side and underwing registration, alternate-display exemption, NAA identification plate);
  - weighing review, and weighing as required by Part-NCO or Part-SPO;
  - **service life limits** check in the records;
  - **software/firmware and database status** for engine and equipment.
- **Airframe:** fabric/skin, including composite impact damage; fuselage structure for corrosion and cracks; systems; pitot-static (drain condensation); loose items; tow hooks (service life, operational test).
- **Cabin:** seats and belts (service life); windows and canopy jettison; instrument panel and markings per the AFM; controls; speed/weight/manoeuvre placard; all systems.
- **Landing gear:** oleo fluid, bungees, attachments, retraction (operational check), linkages, steering, hydraulic leaks, wiring chafing, wheels and bearings, tyres, brakes (operational test), floats and skis.
- **Wing and centre section:** connections, play, bolt and bushing wear.
- **Flight controls:** rods, cables and stops; surfaces with full range and free play; trim.
- **Empennage.**
- **Avionics:** battery; radios (ground function test); wiring; bonding; antennas; lights operational check.
- **Power plant (non-turboprop):**
  - leaks; studs; **cylinder compression, recorded per cylinder**; oil filter and screens for metal;
  - mounts; dampeners; controls; hoses; exhaust;
  - turbo and intercooler, MP/density controller, wastegate;
  - **cabin heat exchanger with CO concentration check**;
  - liquid cooling; electronic engine control; accessories; cowling and flaps; baffles.
- **Turboprop:**
  - incoming power check per the EMM graphs; inertial separator functional check; cowling; leaks;

  - exhaust duct; starter-generator brushes; ignitors; overspeed governor; beta valve; propeller; fire detector functional check;
  - power check recorded; oil level within 10 minutes of shutdown.

- **Clutches and gearboxes:** filters and chip detectors, leaks, shaft play.
- **Propeller:** assembly, bolts and safety, control, anti-ice.
- **Miscellaneous:** ballistic rescue system (pyrotechnic inspection validity, parachute repack intervals); other items.

**Sailplane / powered sailplane MIP**
- Intervals: 100 h/annual for TMGs; annual for the rest. Tolerance 1 month or 10 h "as applicable".
- TMG note: aircraft, engine and propeller hours may be controlled separately between inspections, but all are covered at the 100 h/annual.
- **General:** clean; inspect; lubrication; markings; weighing.
- **Airframe:** gel coat; structure; nose fairing impact; **release hooks (operational life, test from all positions)**; pitot/ventilator; pitot-static; bonding and drains.
- **Cockpit:** debris; canopy, locks and **jettison test**; seat and energy-absorbing cushions; harness; rudder pedals; panel (readings consistent with ambient conditions, CB and fuse marking); AFM markings; **oxygen system (bottle hydrostatic test date expiry; installation recorded on the W&B schedule)**; control colour-coding; placards.
- **Landing gear:** skid and nose wheel (hard landing evidence, tyre pressure); main wheel and brake (hose life, lining and drum wear, fluid, asbestos caution); suspension; retraction test; tail skid; brake circuit and rigging with the air brake; parking brake.
- **Wing:** centre section; attachments, pins and main bolts; winglets; aileron circuit and stops; air brake circuit and locking; **struts: re-inhibit internally every 3 years or per the manufacturer**; mainplane; ailerons and flaperons; air brakes and spoilers; flaps; **control deflections, free play and cable tensions recorded on worksheets**.
- **Empennage:** tailplane (de-rigged) and connections; rudder and balance weights; rudder circuit ("S" tube liners); elevator circuit; trimmer; deflections recorded.
- **Avionics:** wiring and fuses; battery (correct main fuse; capacity test recommended for gliders with radio used cross-country, in controlled airspace or in competition); radio (call-sign placard, record radio type); ASI leak and functional check (calibration if malfunction); altimeter QNH; pitot-static leak check; transponder operational check.
- **Miscellaneous:** removable ballast (conspicuous colour, loading placard); drag chute (packing intervals); water ballast (bladder expiry).
- **Power plant:** electric or jet engines follow the DAH. Otherwise:
  - pylon and mountings, fire sealing; gas strut; stops and restraint cables; actuator; wiring at bend points; limit switches;
  - fuel tanks and gauges; pipes and vents; fuel cock; pumps and filters; decompression valve;
  - ignition, including magneto timing; propeller, folding, brake and stow sensors; doors; springs;
  - **extension/retraction times within limits**, interlocks; exhaust;
  - **compression test recorded (No 1 left/front, No 2 right/rear)**;
  - **oil and filter change**;
  - engine instruments and self-test; engine battery and **capacity test**; placards; leak check with the engine serviced.

**Hot-air balloon MIP** (100 h/annual, whichever first, with 1 month / 10 h tolerance)
- **Envelope:** identification plate; crown ring; crown line length; load tapes; fabric porosity; **grab test** per the manufacturer (flight test if porosity is suspected, but only after a grab test shows it is safe); flying cables (heat damage); karabiners; **melting link / tempilabel max temperature recorded**; control lines (length); pulleys.
- **Burner:** identification type/serial verified; frame welds, tubes, fasteners, lugs; gimballing; **leak check**; fuel hoses (service life), O-rings; pressure gauges; pilot, whisper and main valves; coils and jets.
- **Basket:** identification; walls; wires and swaging; karabiners; floor; runners; rawhide; rope handles; cylinder straps (approved type); edge trim; burner support rods and covers; equipment; pilot restraint; **fire extinguisher expiry**; **first aid kit completeness and expiry**.
- **Fuel cylinders:** identification; **periodic inspection validity per cylinder (e.g. 10 years)**; body; liquid valve and O-rings; level gauge; contents gauge; vapour valve, regulator, QR coupling; padded cover thickness; **pressure relief valve service life**; leak test of joints; functional test.
- **Additional equipment:** instruments functional check; quick release; radio; transponder operational check.

**Gas balloon:** no AMC MIP table; only the IR area list (envelope, basket, equipment, instruments).

### AMC1 ML.A.305: record keeping
- (a) Any form is acceptable instead of a logbook or log card: paper, spreadsheet or IT system. This legitimises an electronic-only system.
- (b) Log card and status for components other than engines and propellers may be combined.
- (c) and (d) AD rules: not-applicable ADs listed with reasons; superseded or cancelled ADs need not be listed; status must identify the complied AD and/or the due limit.
- (e) **If the IT system is the only record-keeping system:** "at least one backup system, which should be regularly updated", and each terminal has safeguards against unauthorised personnel altering the database. This is a direct product requirement for Airworthiness+: backups, access control and an immutable audit log.
