### 2.2 Appendix II: Aircraft type practical experience and OJT, list of tasks

**Part 2) List of tasks for OJT.** The procedure for selecting the minimum task list is:
1. "(i) Filter the ATA chapters (or sub-chapters, when required) applicable to the specific aircraft type (add others if they are missing)."
2. "(ii) Identify relevant and significant tasks for each required category of INS, FOT, SGH, R/I, MEL and TS."
3. "(iii) Retain the required percentage for each task category, and throughout ATA chapters, as much as relevant to the particular aircraft type."

Further rules:
- Tasks that are critical and complex take precedence. The criteria are: difficulty to execute; interpretation of procedures and work instructions; specific tools and equipment; teamwork; human factors (accessibility, HMI); and safety impact on the aircraft and crew.
- "Removal and installation tasks include the final confirmation test, if required."
- "Credit may be given for similar tasks between ATA systems (e.g. pneumatic valves in ATA 21, 30 and 36), but this should be kept to a minimum."
- "Some tasks may be performed on another aircraft type as long as both the system and the task are similar."
- The table is "an acceptable method of selection of OJT tasks for B1 and B2 AML categories ... as required by point 6.2(e) of Appendix III to Annex III."

### 2.3 Appendix III: Evaluation of competence

- **Scope:** "applies to the competence assessment performed by the designated assessors (and their qualifications)."
- **1) What competence means:**
  - It covers three factors: **Knowledge, Skills, Attitude**. Knowledge is normally assessed by examination. This appendix deals with skills and attitude after training that contains practical elements.
  - Evaluation is based on the learning objectives:
    - the (observable) desired performance;
    - the (measurable) performance standard, as tolerances, constraints, limits, rates or qualitative statements;
    - the conditions of the demonstration.
  - Areas of focus:
    - environment awareness;
    - systems integration;
    - areas of special emphasis or novelty (including domains not covered by Part-66 Appendix I and practical elements that simulators cannot teach);
    - using reports and indications;
    - finding and handling aircraft documentation;
    - performing maintenance actions;
    - aircraft final close-up and report, including signing maintenance records and logbooks.
- **2) How to assess:**
  - Use objective, observable criteria linked to the learning objectives and the passing level.
  - Good assessment is "objective, flexible, acceptable, comprehensive, constructive, organised and thoughtful".
  - Suggested questions cover: success factors, criteria, expected expertise, the pass mark ("Go-no go", points or minimum score, "Must know/execute" vs "Good to know" vs "Don't expect ... expert"), minimum or maximum time, how many failures are allowed, and how the trainee is prepared.
  - Assessment type: diagnostic (before the course), formative, or summative (partial or final). It may be done task by task, as a group of tasks, or as a final assessment. Self-assessment followed by discussion is suggested.
  - "A 'box-ticking' exercise would be pointless."
  - The assessor should also consider stress and environmental conditions, test difficulty, evaluation history (progress or sudden poor performance), and the time needed to build competence.
- **3) Who should assess.** The assessor should:
  - be proficient in human performance and safety culture, the aircraft type ("necessary to have the certifying staff privileges in case of CRS issuances"), training/coaching/testing skills, and the instructional tools;
  - understand the objective and content of the practical elements being assessed;
  - have interpersonal skills;
  - "Be ultimately designated by the organisation to carry out the assessment."
- **Combining roles:**
  - The assessor may also be the instructor for the practical elements of type rating training, or the supervisor for OJT. This is allowed provided both roles' objectives are understood and both sets of competence criteria are met.
  - "Whenever possible (depending on the size of the organisation), it is recommended to split the roles (two different persons) in order to avoid any conflicts of interests."

## 3. Records and Documents

No official form (such as an EASA Form) is set out in this chunk. The record formats it implies are:

- **Type rating list entry (Appendix I).** Fields:
  - holder (TC or STC holder, or "WITHOUT TC HOLDER - ORPHANED");
  - model;
  - type of structure (sailplanes and powered sailplanes);
  - Part-66 type rating endorsement text (STCs);
  - note (ELA1/ELA2, tethered, volume in m³, STC number, "(electrical)", "powered variants", series reference);
  - MTOM band (≤2T / >2T).
- **Practical experience record.** Tasks are taken from the Appendix II lists per aircraft category, with counted items such as "Five annual inspections" (sailplanes), "Eight annual inspections (covering at least three different types)" (hot-air balloons), and so on.
- **OJT programme or task list.** Each task needs: task no; ATA chapter or sub-chapter; task description; applicable licence category (B1/B1.1/B2); task category (INS/FOT/SGH/R/I/MEL/TS). The programme should meet the percentage per category, include the ATA 05 minimums (1 from (a), 2 from (b), 3 from (c)), and record any cross-ATA credit or tasks done on another, similar aircraft type.
- **Competence assessment record (Appendix III).** Fields:
  - trainee;
  - assessor, who must be designated by the organisation and hold the required qualifications, including certifying privileges on the type where CRS issue is involved;
  - whether the assessor also acted as instructor or supervisor;
  - assessment type (diagnostic, formative or summative);
  - scope (task, group of tasks, or final);
  - criteria and pass mark;
  - result, number of attempts, and feedback (what was done well, what was done poorly, how to improve);
  - optional trainee self-assessment.
- Maintenance records referenced as content: aircraft maintenance log, AD compliance records, component life-limit records, weight and balance amendment or weighing report, equipment list, MEL/CDL items, dent and buckle chart, annual inspection documentation, and FDR data retrieval.

## 4. Defined Terms

The chunk does not formally define any terms. Terms used, and their meaning in context:

- **ELA1 / ELA2**: European Light Aircraft classes, used as the Note value for Group 4 balloons and airships and for the Group 3 STC entries.
- **Orphaned ("WITHOUT TC HOLDER - ORPHANED")**: a type that no longer has a TC holder.
- **Tethered gas balloon**; **Mixed Gas / Hot-Air Balloons** (Rozière).
- **INS, FOT, SGH, R/I, MEL, TS**: the OJT task categories. They are not expanded in this chunk. They are normally Inspection; Functional/Operational Test; Servicing/Ground Handling; Removal/Installation; MEL (deferral); Troubleshooting. Confirm against Appendix III to Annex III, point 6.2.
- **OJT**: On-the-Job Training.
- **AML**: Aircraft Maintenance Licence.
- **Competence = Knowledge + Skills + Attitude**. "'Attitude' is indivisible from the 'skill'."
- **Assessment types**: diagnostic, formative, summative.
- **Assessor, instructor, supervisor**: three distinct roles that may be combined under conditions.
- Other terms: i.a.w. AMM, SRM, MEL/CDL, HIRF, ESDS, FADEC, CMU, THS, PTU.

## 5. Software Implications

A licence, training or competence feature would need the following.

**Type rating catalogue**
- Import Appendix I as reference data with these fields:
  - `group` (1–4) and `subgroup` (sailplane, powered sailplane, gas balloon, hot-air balloon, gas airship, hot-air airship, Group 3 STC);
  - `holder`, `is_orphaned`, `model`;
  - `structure_type` (enum: metal, composite, wood, metal-tube+wood, mixed);
  - `ela_class` (ELA1/ELA2/none), `tethered` (bool), `envelope_volume_m3`, `mixed_gas_hot_air` (bool), `electric` (bool), `has_powered_variants` (bool);
  - `series_ref` (a range of member models);
  - `stc_number`, `endorsement_text`, `mtom_band`, `source_ed_decision`.
- Use these fields to map a fleet aircraft, plus any embodied STC, to the right AML endorsement.
  - Engine-conversion STCs (Thielert, Technify, SMA, Rotax, Porsche) change the endorsement. The aircraft record should capture embodied STCs.
  - Some STC rows lack an STC number or an MTOM mark, so make those fields nullable.
- **Size:** about 1,550 Group 4 and STC rows in this chunk alone. Import them; do not type them in by hand. Record the ED Decision so the data can be refreshed when EASA revises it.

**Practical experience logbook**
- Store the task templates per aircraft category (A, B, C), with a balloon/airship applicability matrix (hot-air, gas free, gas tethered, hot-air airship, gas airship).
- Support counted requirements with a type-diversity rule, for example 8 annual inspections across at least 3 types for hot-air balloons. A trainee progress engine needs `count_required` and `min_distinct_types`.

**OJT programme builder**
- Inputs: the aircraft type's applicable ATA chapters (a filter, with the option to add chapters); licence category (B1 or B2, with GM1 covering B1.1 and B2).
- The 6 task categories with these percentage targets:

  | INS | FOT | SGH | R/I | MEL | TS |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 75 % | 50 % | 50 % | 50 % | 25 % | 25 % |

- Validation:
  - Check coverage per category against the X-marked cells for each ATA row.
  - Enforce the ATA 05 rule (≥1 from (a), ≥2 from (b), ≥3 from (c)).
  - Flag cross-ATA credit, which should be kept to a minimum.
  - Allow tasks performed on another type, with a justification field.
- Seed the task library from the GM1 448-task list, using ATA, description and B1.1/B2 applicability.
- Each completed task record needs: date, aircraft registration and type, task reference (AMM), category, supervisor, and signature.
- Weight task selection by critical or complex attributes (difficulty, special tools, teamwork, HF, safety impact).

**Competence assessment**
- Assessor register:
  - designation by the organisation (date and scope);
  - qualifications (HF/safety culture, type knowledge, certifying privileges on the type where CRS is involved, instructional skills);
  - role-combination flags (assessor+instructor or assessor+supervisor), with a warning when roles are combined in larger organisations (conflict of interest).
- Assessment record:
  - type (diagnostic, formative or summative) and granularity (task, group or final);
  - criteria tied to learning objectives, pass mark model (go/no-go, points threshold, must-know vs good-to-know);
  - time limits, attempt count and failure policy;
  - self-assessment and assessor comparison;
  - free-text feedback (what went well, what went poorly, how to improve);
  - competency areas from the list of 7 (environment awareness, systems integration, special emphasis, reports and indications, documentation, maintenance actions, close-up and records).
- Avoid a pure checkbox user interface. The guidance says box-ticking is pointless, so support narrative evidence and grouped skills.

**UK context.** UK CAA Part-66 retains equivalent AMC, but its type rating list and ED Decision amendment status may differ from EASA's. Make the catalogue jurisdiction-aware (EASA vs UK CAA source).
