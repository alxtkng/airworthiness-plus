## 1. Scope (point by point)

### General
- **66.1 Competent authority**. (a)1: the authority of the Member State where the person **first applies** for an AML. (a)2: another Member State's authority, by agreement with the first. In that case the old licence is revoked, the 66.B.20 records are transferred and a new licence is issued from those records. (b): **the Agency defines the list of aircraft types and which airframe/engine combinations fall in each type rating.** That list is the Appendix I to AMC "List of Type Ratings".

### Section A, Subpart A: Aircraft Maintenance Licence (technical requirements for persons)
- **66.A.1 Scope**. Defines the AML and sets the requirements for applying for it, getting it issued and keeping it valid.
- **66.A.3 Licence categories and subcategories**. Lists categories A, B1, B2, B2L, B3, L and C, with their subcategories and B2L system ratings.
- **66.A.5 Aircraft groups**. Groups 1, 2 (2a, 2b, 2c), 3 and 4, used for ratings.
- **66.A.10 Application**. Applications go on EASA Form 19 to the competent authority (CA). A change goes to the CA that issued the licence. If adding a basic category, submit the original licence too. Cross-border routes are covered under 66.B.100 and 66.B.105. Applications must be supported by evidence of knowledge, training and experience.
- **66.A.15 Eligibility**. The applicant must be **at least 18 years of age**.
- **66.A.20 Privileges**. (a) Privileges for each category. (b) Conditions for exercising them: compliance with Part-M, Part-145, Part-ML and Part-CAO; recency of **6 months in the preceding 2 years**; adequate competence; and language ability.
- **66.A.25 Basic knowledge requirements**. Examination against Appendix I (A, B1, B2, B2L, B3, C) or Appendix VII (L). Exams are run by Part-147, the CA, or (L only) another organisation agreed by the CA. Exams are valid for **10 years**. Credits are possible. Adding a category needs the extra modules.
- **66.A.30 Basic experience requirements**. Years of experience by category and training route; C-licence routes; extension experience (Appendix IV); recent experience; recognition of non-approved experience; a **10-year window**.
- **66.A.40 Continued validity of the AML**. The licence becomes invalid **5 years after its last issue or change** unless it is submitted to the CA for verification. Submission is on Form 19, or through a Part-145 or Part-CAO organisation that has an exposition procedure for it. Certification privileges end when the licence becomes invalid. The licence is valid only if issued or changed by the CA **and signed by the holder**.
- **66.A.45 Endorsement with aircraft ratings**. Sets which ratings are needed for each category and group: type, manufacturer subgroup, full subgroup or full group. Covers type training, OJT, the type evaluation route, subgroup and group endorsement rules, and limitations for B1 Group 3, B3 and L.
- **66.A.50 Limitations**. Limitations are exclusions from certification privileges. 66.A.45 limitations affect the whole aircraft. They are removed by experience or a CA practical assessment. 66.A.70 limitations are removed by exams set out in the conversion report.
- **66.A.55 Evidence of qualification**. Certifying staff and support staff must produce their licence **within 24 hours** of a request by an authorised person.
- **66.A.70 Conversion provisions**. Pre-Part-66 national certifying staff qualifications are converted without further exam, under the conditions of Subpart D. Limitations are added to reflect differences (route (c)) or to preserve existing privileges (route (d), for non-air-carrier, non-CMPA aircraft and for balloons, sailplanes, powered sailplanes and airships).

### Section B: Procedures for competent authorities
- **Subpart A, General**
  - **66.B.1 Scope**. Sets the CA procedures for implementing and enforcing Section A.
  - **66.B.2 Means of compliance**. EASA issues AMC. Alternative means of compliance (AltMoC) may be used, and CAs must tell EASA about them.
  - **66.B.10 Competent authority**. The Member State designates the CA for issue, continuation, change, suspension and revocation. The CA must have an organisation, resources and documented procedures.
  - **66.B.15 Information security management system**. Required under Part-IS.AR (Reg. 2023/203), **applicable from 22 February 2026**.
  - **66.B.20 Record-keeping**. Traceability of issue, revalidation, change, suspension and revocation. Sets the list of records and the retention periods (see section 3).
  - **66.B.25 Mutual exchange of information**. Under Art. 72(1) of Reg. 2018/1139. CAs assist each other on multi-State safety threats.
  - **66.B.30 Exemptions**. Art. 71(1) exemptions must be recorded and retained.
- **Subpart B, Issue of an AML**
  - **66.B.100 Issue by the CA**. Check Form 19 for completeness and the experience claimed. Verify exam status and credits against Appendix I or VII. Verify identity and date of birth. Issue the licence and keep the same information in CA records. Ratings endorsed at first issue are handled under 66.B.115.
  - **66.B.105 Issue via a Part-145 or Part-CAO organisation**. An authorised organisation may prepare the licence for the CA or make a recommendation. It must ensure 66.B.100(a) and (b) are met. **Only the CA issues the licence.**
  - **66.B.110 Adding a basic category, subcategory or B2L system rating**. The CA endorses it by stamp and signature, or reissues the licence, and updates its records. On request, a B2L licence is replaced by a B2 licence with the same aircraft ratings, after a difference exam (Appendix I) and the Appendix IV experience. Appendix IV tables give the modules and experience needed.
  - **66.B.115 Adding an aircraft rating or removing limitations**. The CA endorses or reissues, or removes limitations, and updates its records. If training was not by Part-147, the CA must be satisfied with it. If no OJT is needed, the endorsement is based on a Part-147 Certificate of Recognition (CoR). Split courses must cover the full scope and the interfaces. Differences training is covered. Practical element evidence is either records or a logbook (for directly approved courses) or a Part-147 certificate. **Ratings must use the Agency-specified type ratings.**
  - **66.B.120 Renewal of licence validity**. Compare the holder's licence with CA records and check for pending 66.B.500 actions. If they match, **renew for 5 years**. If they differ, investigate, inform the holder and any affected Part-M Subpart F, Part-145 or Part-CAO organisation, and act under 66.B.500.
  - **66.B.125 Conversion of licences including group ratings**. Old (pre-2018/1142) group ratings are converted to the new group and subgroup structure using a conversion table. Existing individual type ratings stay. Earlier 66.A.70 limitations remain.
  - **66.B.130 Direct approval of aircraft type training**. The CA may approve non-Part-147 type training under Appendix III, point 1. **Group 1 airship type training must always be directly approved by the CA.** EASA Form 149b CoR is used to record completion of the theory element, the practical element, or both.
  - **66.B.135 Approval of MBT courses**. For basic and type training in physical or virtual environments, compliance with Appendices I and III and the principles of Appendix IX.
- **Subpart C, Examinations**
  - **66.B.200 Examination by the CA**. Exam security and nominated persons and examiners. Basic exams follow Appendices I and II or VII and VIII, attested by **EASA Form 148b CoR**. Type exams and evaluations follow Appendix III, attested by **EASA Form 149b CoR**. New essay questions at least every **6 months**. Rules on exam papers and materials. Candidates proven to have cheated are **banned for 12 months**.
- **Subpart D, Conversion of certifying staff qualifications**
  - **66.B.300 General**. Converts only qualifications from the CA's own Member State that were valid before Part-66 applied. Conversion must follow a conversion report, either developed or approved by the CA, kept under 66.B.20.
  - **66.B.305 Conversion report for national qualifications**. Covers the scope and privileges of each qualification, with copies of the national regulations. For each qualification: the target licence, the limitations under (c) or (d), and the exam modules needed to remove limitations.
  - **66.B.310 Conversion report for approved maintenance organisation authorisations**. As 66.B.305, but for organisation authorisations, with copies of the organisation's procedures.
- **Subpart E, Examination credits**
  - **66.B.400 General**. Credit is granted only on a credit report, developed or approved by the CA, dated and kept. A credit report from another CA must be considered, and that CA consulted.
  - **66.B.405 Examination credit report**. Compares the Appendix I or VII modules, submodules and levels with the qualification syllabus, stating compliance and justification for each. Non-Part-147 credits can only be granted by the CA of the State where the qualification was obtained, unless agreed otherwise. Each module and submodule needs a statement of compliance. The CA must check regularly for changes.
  - **66.B.410 Examination credit validity**. The CA notifies credits in writing, with the credit report reference. **Credits expire 10 years after grant.** They can be extended by 10 years without reassessment if Appendix I or VII has not changed.
- **Subpart F, Continuing oversight**
  - **66.B.500 Revocation, suspension or limitation**. Grounds are a safety issue or clear evidence of: (1) falsifying documents to obtain the licence or privileges; (2) failing to carry out requested maintenance and not reporting it; (3) failing to carry out maintenance found necessary during one's own inspection and not reporting it; (4) negligent maintenance; (5) falsifying the maintenance record; (6) issuing a CRS knowing the work was not done, or without verifying it; (7) maintenance or a CRS while affected by alcohol or drugs; (8) issuing a CRS while not in compliance with the Regulation.

### 2.1 Licence categories and subcategories (66.A.3)
| Category | Subcategories or system ratings | Applicability |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | A1 Aeroplanes Turbine; A2 Aeroplanes Piston; A3 Helicopters Turbine; A4 Helicopters Piston | Line tasks endorsed on the 145.A.35 authorisation |
| **B1** | B1.1 Aeroplanes Turbine; B1.2 Aeroplanes Piston; B1.3 Helicopters Turbine; B1.4 Helicopters Piston | Mechanical |
| **B2** | None. "applicable to all aircraft" | Avionics and electrical |
| **B2L** | System ratings: com/nav, instruments, autoflight, surveillance, airframe systems. "shall contain, as a minimum, one system rating" | "all aircraft other than those in Group 1" |

| **L** | L1C composite sailplanes; L1 sailplanes; L2C composite powered sailplanes and composite ELA1 aeroplanes; L2 powered sailplanes and ELA1 aeroplanes; L3H hot-air balloons; L3G gas balloons; L4H hot-air airships; L4G ELA2 gas airships; L5 gas airships other than ELA2 | Group 4, plus L5 for Group 1 airships |
| **C** | None, but in practice "C for CMPA" and "C for other than CMPA" | "applicable to aeroplanes and helicopters". Base maintenance |

Clarifications from GM 66.A.3:
- L1 covers all sailplanes (composite, metal, wood). L1C covers composite only. The same split applies to L2 and L2C.
- Mixed gas and hot-air balloons need **both** L3G and L3H.
- "Powered sailplane" includes self-launching, self-sustaining and TMGs.
- ELA2 includes ELA1.
- A B2L system rating gives release privileges on the covered systems **plus electrical systems**.
- B2L system ratings can be applied for in any order or combination.

### 2.2 Aircraft groups (66.A.5, as amended by 2023/989)
- **Group 1**: complex motor-powered aircraft; multi-engine helicopters; "other than piston-engine aeroplanes, with maximum certified operating altitude exceeding FL290"; aircraft with fly-by-wire; gas airships other than ELA2. The Agency may move a lower-complexity aircraft down to Group 2, 3 or 4.
- **Group 2** (not Group 1):
  - 2a: single turboprop aeroplanes, plus turbine aeroplanes the Agency classifies here.
  - 2b: single turbine helicopters, plus multi-turbine helicopters the Agency classifies here.
  - 2c: single piston helicopters, plus multi-piston helicopters the Agency classifies here.
- **Group 3**: piston engine aeroplanes not in Group 1.
- **Group 4**: sailplanes, powered sailplanes, balloons and airships not in Group 1.

GM1 66.A.5 matrix (as far as the flattened table can be reconstructed):
- A, B1, C and B2: Group 1 (except airships), Groups 2 and 3.
- B2 also covers Group 4 and Group 1 gas airships. L5 covers Group 1 gas airships.
- B2L: Groups 2, 3 and 4.
- B3: non-pressurised ELA2 piston aeroplanes and ELA1 piston aeroplanes, within its 2 000 kg MTOM scope.
- L2 and L2C: ELA1 aeroplanes and Group 4 powered sailplanes.

### 2.3 Privileges (66.A.20(a))
- **A**: CRS after **minor scheduled line maintenance and simple defect rectification**, within the tasks endorsed on the **145.A.35 certification authorisation**. Restricted to work the holder **personally performed** in the organisation that issued the authorisation.
- **B1**: CRS, and acting as B1 support staff, for structure, powerplant, and mechanical and electrical systems, plus avionics work needing only simple tests and no troubleshooting. **B1 includes the corresponding A subcategory.**
- **B2**:
  - (i) CRS, and acting as B2 support staff, for avionic and electrical systems, and for electrical and avionics tasks within powerplant and mechanical systems that need only simple tests.
  - (ii) A-type CRS (minor scheduled line maintenance and simple defect rectification) within the 145.A.35 authorisation, for work personally performed, **limited to ratings already endorsed on the B2**.
  - "The category B2 licence does not include any A subcategory."
- **B2L**: CRS, and acting as support staff, for electrical systems and for avionics **within the endorsed system ratings**. With the **airframe systems** rating, also electrical and avionics tasks within powerplant and mechanical systems that need only simple tests. AMC: privileges are limited by both the aircraft group or subgroup and the system rating(s).
- **B3**: within the B3 aeroplane scope, the same pattern as B1: structure, powerplant, mechanical and electrical systems, plus avionics needing only simple tests and no troubleshooting. No A subcategory. GM: B3 may still release tasks typical of A1.2 within its limitations. (The text says A1.2, likely meaning A2.)
- **L**:
  - Structure, powerplant, mechanical and electrical systems.
  - Radio, ELT and transponder systems.
  - Other avionics needing only simple tests.
  - **L2 includes L1.** Any 66.A.45(h) limitation on L2 also applies to L1.
  - **L2C includes L1C.**
- **C**: CRS after **base maintenance**, for the aircraft in its entirety. C for CMPA includes C for other than CMPA. GM: the C holder issues a single CRS for the whole aircraft after checking all tasks were called up and signed by B1, B2, B2L, B3 and L support staff. Only C holders who also hold a B or L licence may perform both roles.
- GM: B2 with given aircraft ratings includes all B2L system-rating privileges for those ratings. B1.2 and B3 holders with the appropriate ratings may get L1 and L2 on application (66.B.110(d) per GM).

### 2.4 Conditions to exercise privileges (66.A.20(b)), quoted
1. In compliance with Part-M, Part-145, Part-ML and Part-CAO.
2. **Recency**: "in the preceding 2-year period he/she has, either had 6 months of maintenance experience in accordance with the privileges granted by the aircraft maintenance licence or, met the provision for the issue of the appropriate privileges".
3. "adequate competence to certify maintenance on the corresponding aircraft".
4. Able to "read, write and communicate to an understandable level" in the language of the technical documentation and procedures.

**Recency details (AMC 66.A.20(b)(2))**:
- **Duration**:
  - 6 months continuous in one approved organisation, or 6 months in blocks across one or more organisations.
  - The 6 months can be replaced by **100 days** of experience in line with the privileges, in an approved organisation, as independent certifying staff under M.A.801(b)1, or a mix.
  - For M.A.801(b)1 independent certifying staff, the CA may agree **in advance** to cut the days by **up to 50%**, for owners maintaining their own aircraft or for low-utilisation aircraft. This cannot be combined with the 20% reduction below. Days should be spread over the 6-month period.
  - **Up to 20%** of the duration may be replaced by training (as instructor, assessor or student), technical support or engineering, or maintenance management or planning, on similar aircraft.
- **Nature of the experience**: servicing, inspection, operational and functional testing, troubleshooting, repairing, modifying, changing components, supervising these, and releasing aircraft.
- **Per category**:
  - A: tasks under the authorisation on **at least one aircraft type per subcategory**.
  - B1, B2, B2L, B3 and L: for **every aircraft in the authorisation**, experience on that aircraft or a **similar aircraft** in the same (sub)category. "Similar" means the same propulsion type, flight control type (mechanical, hydro-mechanical or electro-mechanical), avionics (analogue or digital) and structure (metal, composite or wood).
  - B1 with (sub)group ratings: at least one type per (sub)group **and per structure type**.
  - B2 and B2L with (sub)group ratings: at least one type per (sub)group.
  - B3: at least one type per structure (metal, metal-tubing and fabric, composite, wood).
  - C: at least one of the endorsed types.
  - Combined categories: some activity in each category.
- **GM 66.A.20(b)2**: meeting all the requirements for the rating endorsement within the last 2 years (for example Group 1 theory, practical and OJT) counts instead of the 6 months for the first 2 years. After that the 6-in-24 rule applies.

### 2.5 Basic knowledge (66.A.25)
- Exam against Appendix I (A, B1, B2, B2L, B3, C) or Appendix VII (L). Standard is Appendix II or VIII.
- Conducted by Part-147, the CA, or (L only) another organisation agreed by the CA.
- **Validity**: "The basic knowledge examinations shall have been passed within 10 years prior to the application for an aircraft maintenance licence or the addition of a category or subcategory." This applies **per module**, except modules already used for an issued licence category.
- **Credits** (66.A.25(d)) for exams older than 10 years, or for equivalent national training, are granted via an approved **examination credit report** (Subpart E).
- A basic course without Modules 1 and 2 counts as a full Part-147 course only once those modules are examined and credited.
- Adding a category or subcategory: complementary exams per Appendix IV.
- AMC: academic degrees and military or apprenticeship exams may be credited where equivalent.
- GM: A requires a "limited but adequate" level of knowledge. B1, B2, B2L and B3 require a "complete" level.

### 2.6 Basic experience (66.A.30(a)), quoted figures
| Category | No relevant technical training | Skilled worker (CA-recognised technical trade training) | Part-147 approved basic course |
|---|---|---|---|
| A, B1.2, B1.4, B3 | 3 years | 2 years | 1 year |
| B2, B1.1, B1.3 | 5 years | 3 years | 2 years |
| B2L (covering the system ratings) | 3 years | 2 years | 1 year |
| L | 2 years covering a representative cross section of the subcategory; **or 1 year** with the 66.A.45(h)(ii)(3) limitation (no complex tasks, standard changes or standard repairs) | | |

- **Adding a B2L system rating**: "3 months of practical maintenance experience relevant to the new system rating(s) shall be required for each system rating added."
- **C for CMPA**:
  - (i) 3 years exercising B1.1, B1.3 or B2 privileges as support staff (or support and certifying staff) under 145.A.35, at an organisation working on CMPA, "including 12 months of experience as base maintenance support staff".
  - (ii) 5 years for B1.2, B1.4 or L5, with the same 12 months.
  - (iii) Academic degree: 3 years in a maintenance environment, "including 6 months of participation in the performance of base maintenance tasks in operating CMPA".
  - (iv) Extending C (other than CMPA) to CMPA: 2 years as B1.x, B2 or L5 support staff, including 6 months base maintenance support staff. Academic route: 2 years including 3 months of base maintenance participation.
- **C for other than CMPA**:
  - (i) 3 years exercising B1, B2, B2L, B3 or L privileges as support staff (or support and certifying staff), "including 6 months of experience as base maintenance support staff".
  - (ii) Academic route: 3 years, including 6 months of base maintenance participation.
- The academic degree must be in a relevant technical discipline, from a CA-recognised institution.
- (b) Extensions: experience per **Appendix IV**.
- (c) The experience must be practical and a representative cross section.
- (d) **Recent experience**:
  - "At least 1 year of the required experience shall be recent maintenance experience on aircraft of the category/subcategory for which the initial aircraft maintenance licence is sought."
  - For additions: less than 1 year is possible, "but shall be at least 3 months".
  - AMC: at least **50% of the 12-month recent experience within the 12 months before application**, and the rest within **7 years**.
- (e) Experience outside Part-145 or Part-CAO may be recognised if equivalent, but additional approved-organisation experience is still needed. AMC1 66.A.30(e): **6 months for A and L; 12 months for B1, B2, B2L, B3 and C**. Examples are military, coast guard, police, national or third-country organisations, and manufacturing.
- (f) "Experience shall have been acquired within the 10 years preceding the application."
- AMC1 66.A.30(a):
  - Experience may be part-time or voluntary, and may be interleaved with Part-147 training like an apprenticeship.
  - For L, weekend-only experience is acceptable, with competency statements from the supervising organisation or certifying staff.
  - **Combining categories**: periods may overlap only if each category gets enough experience in the same period. For example, B1.1 plus B1.3 needs 5 years working on both, not 3 years plus 2 years.
- A **skilled worker** is someone who has completed CA-acceptable training in manufacture, repair, overhaul or inspection of mechanical, electrical or electronic equipment, including use of tools and measuring devices.

### 2.7 Validity periods (66.A.40, 66.B.120)
- "The aircraft maintenance licence becomes invalid 5 years after its last issue or change, unless the holder submits his/her aircraft maintenance licence to the competent authority that issued it."
- Renewal is a records comparison. If the records match and no 66.B.500 action is pending, "the holder's copy shall be renewed for 5 years".
- Submission is on EASA Form 19 plus the holder's copy, or through a Part-145 or Part-CAO organisation with an exposition procedure.
- "Any certification privilege based upon a aircraft maintenance licence becomes invalid as soon as the aircraft maintenance licence is invalid."
- The licence is valid only when issued or changed by the CA **and signed by the holder**.
- GM 66.A.40: licence validity is **not** affected by recency. Recency affects only the 66.A.20 privileges.
- AMC 66.B.120: the CA does **not** check current practice at renewal. Continued validity of privileges is a matter for the Part-145, M.A. Subpart F or Part-CAO organisation, or for the M.A.801(b)1 certifying staff themselves. The CA may ask for 66.A.20(b) evidence during oversight audits or spot checks.
- Other periods:
  - Basic exam validity: 10 years per module.
  - Experience window: 10 years.
  - Credit validity: 10 years, extendable by 10 years.
  - Cheating ban: 12 months.
  - New essay questions: at least every 6 months.
  - Produce the licence within 24 hours.
  - Part-145 66.B.105 procedure audited at least every 12 months.

### 2.8 Aircraft ratings (66.A.45)
**Required rating by category and group:**
- **B1, B2, C**:
  - Group 1: type rating.
  - Group 2: type, **manufacturer subgroup** or **full subgroup** rating.
  - Group 3: type or **full group** rating.
  - Group 4: full group rating, **B2 only**.
- **B2L**:
  - Group 2: manufacturer subgroup or full subgroup rating.
  - Group 3: full group rating.
  - Group 4: full group rating.
  - No type ratings, and no Group 1.
- **B3**: the single rating "piston-engine non-pressurised aeroplanes of 2 000 kg MTOM and below".
- **L**: one fixed rating per subcategory ('composite sailplanes', 'sailplanes', 'composite powered sailplanes and composite ELA1 aeroplanes', 'powered sailplanes and ELA1 aeroplanes', 'hot-air balloons', 'gas balloons', 'hot-air airships', 'ELA2 gas airships'). **L5 needs an airship type rating.**
- **A**: no rating needed. Privileges depend on the 145.A.35 authorisation.

**Type rating routes:**
- (b) Category B1, B2 or C type training under Appendix III. For B2 or L5 gas airship type ratings, CA-approved training under 66.B.130.
- (c) For categories other than C, the **first type rating in a category or subcategory** also needs **OJT** under Appendix III (directly approved by the CA for gas airships).
- (d) **Groups 2 and 3 only**: alternative route of type **evaluation** plus, for B1 and B2, **practical experience** (a representative cross section). For an academic-route C holder, the first type evaluation must be at B1 or B2 level.

**Subgroup and group ratings:**
- **Group 2, B1 and C**:
  - Manufacturer subgroup: type rating requirements for **at least 2 types from the same manufacturer** that together are representative.
  - Full subgroup: **at least 3 types from different manufacturers** that together are representative.
- **Group 2, B2 and B2L**: manufacturer or full subgroup ratings by demonstrated practical experience (for B2L, relevant to the system ratings). **Full 2b gives entitlement to full 2c.**
- **Groups 3 and 4**: full Group 3 (B1, B2, B2L, C) and full Group 4 (B2, B2L) by demonstrated practical experience. B2L with full 2a or 2b is entitled to Groups 3 and 4.
- **Automatic limitations** apply unless the applicant shows experience. They are endorsed on the licence:
  - B1 Group 3: pressurised aeroplanes; metal-structure; composite-structure; wooden-structure; metal tubing with fabric.
  - B3: wooden; metal tubing with fabric; metal; composite.
  - L (not L5), sailplanes and powered sailplanes/ELA1: wooden with fabric; metal tubing with fabric; metal; composite.
  - L gas balloons: "other than ELA1 gas balloons".
  - L 1-year route: exclusion of complex maintenance tasks (Part-M Appendix VII), standard changes (21.A.90B, 21L.A.62, 21L.A.102) and standard repairs (21.A.431B, 21L.A.202, 21L.A.222).
  - GM: these structural limitations do **not** apply to L1C, L2C, L3H, L4H or L4G. Those ratings need experience covering the full scope.

**Practical experience thresholds (AMC 66.A.45(d);(e)3;(f)1;(g)1;(h)):**
- B1, B2, B2L, B3 and L1/L1C/L2/L2C: **50% of the Appendix II to AMC tasks**, covering every paragraph of the list.
- Balloons and airships (L3, L4, L5): **80% of the tasks, including all asterisked tasks**.
- Individual type ratings for Groups 2 and 3: second type of a manufacturer (sub)group **30%**, later types **20%**.
- The experience must be under the supervision of authorised certifying staff, and shown by records or a logbook.

**Other rating points:**
- **Manufacturer** means the TC holder in the type certificate data sheet (TCDS), as reflected in the type rating list. Joint TC holders such as AGUSTA / BELL HELICOPTER TEXTRON count as one manufacturer.
- GM 66.A.45(b): a type rating covers all models and variants in column 2 of Appendix I to AMC. If EASA adds variants, the holder's rating is amended **on request without extra training**. A course covering only some variants still gives the full rating. The holder and the organisation must still ensure competence (66.A.20(b)3, 145.A.35(a), M.A.607(a), CAO.A.040).

### 2.9 Limitations (66.A.50)
- Limitations are exclusions from certification privileges. Those from 66.A.45 apply to the **whole aircraft**.
- 66.A.45 limitations are removed by appropriate experience, or by a satisfactory CA practical assessment.
- AMC: the experience is a variety of tasks under certifying staff supervision, including annual inspection tasks. Alternatively, manufacturer theory and practical training with a recorded assessment. One representative type is enough. Experience on Annex I aircraft (for example ultralights) counts for the tube-and-fabric limitation. The application needs an experience record signed by certifying staff, or a manufacturer assessment.
- 66.A.70 conversion limitations are removed by passing the exams named in the conversion report.

### 2.10 Conversion (66.A.70, 66.B.125, Subpart D)
- Eligible qualifications are those valid before:
  - **28 September 2005** for aircraft above 5 700 kg MTOM.
  - **28 September 2006** for aircraft of 5 700 kg MTOM and below.
  - B3: before **28 September 2012**.
  - B2L: before **5 March 2019**.
  - L: before **1 October 2019**.
- There is no deadline for applying.
- Conversion covers the full scope of the qualification, even if privileges were not being exercised.
- Airworthiness review signatories do not qualify, because an airworthiness review is not a maintenance release.
- A qualification may be converted more than once, or to more than one category.
- 66.B.125 conversion table (old group to new group):
  - B1 and C:
    - Helicopter piston full group becomes full 2c, plus Group 1 type ratings. Manufacturer group becomes manufacturer 2c.
    - Helicopter turbine becomes 2b in the same way.
    - Single or multi piston aeroplane (metal, wood or composite), full or manufacturer group, becomes full Group 3, plus Group 1 types for multi-piston. For B1, add the structure limitations other than the structure held, and pressurised (except for metal).
    - Single turbine aeroplane becomes full or manufacturer 2a, plus Group 1 types.
    - Multi turbine aeroplane becomes individual type ratings.
  - B2 aeroplane becomes full 2a plus full Group 3 plus Group 1 types. B2 helicopter becomes full 2b and 2c plus Group 1 types.
  - C: the same as B2.
- GM 66.A.70(d) examples:
  - B1.2 or B3 with exclusions: air carrier aircraft (always), above 2 000 kg, pressurised, retractable gear.
  - Pilot-owner: "aircraft other than a Cessna 172" and "aircraft not owned by the licence holder".
  - L2 and L1C examples.

## 3. Records and Documents

### Documents and forms referenced
- **EASA Form 19**. Application for issue, change (category, subcategory, rating, limitation removal) or renewal (Appendix V, not in this chunk). For B2L it must state the system rating(s) and aircraft rating.
- **EASA Form 26**. The AML itself. Mentioned in GM 66.B.100 ("should be endorsed on the licence (EASA Form 26)"). The layout and pages (Appendix VI) are **not in this chunk**. Properties stated here:
  - It holds categories, subcategories, system ratings, aircraft ratings and limitations.
  - Validity is 5 years from last issue or change.
  - It must be signed by the holder.
  - It is endorsed by CA stamp and signature, or reissued. AMC 66.B.110: computer-generated licences are reissued, not stamped.
  - There is a "holder's copy" compared against CA records.
  - Type ratings use the standard codes in Appendix I to AMC (AMC 66.B.100 to 115).

- **EASA Form 149b**. Certificate of Recognition for type training or type evaluation, covering theory, practical or both.

- **Certificate of completion of skilled-worker or technical training**.
- **Detailed statement of non-civil (military) experience**, signed by the non-civil maintenance authority under conditions the CA sets.
- **Experience record or logbook** (AMC 66.A.10, AMC 66.A.20(b)2, AMC 66.A.45):
  - Not a bland "X years" statement, but a task-by-task account is not needed.
  - A logbook is "desirable", and some CAs require one. Form 19 may cross-refer to other documents.
  - It must be **countersigned**. The CA and the Part-145 organisation check the countersignature.
  - A Part-145 organisation may keep experience records in another form acceptable to the CA.
- **Recency logbook fields** (AMC 66.A.20(b)2): an individual logbook or any recording system, which may be automated, with:
  - Date
  - Aircraft type
  - Aircraft identification (registration)
  - ATA chapter (optional)
  - Operation performed (for example 100 FH check, MLG wheel change, SB embodiment, STC embodiment)
  - For Part-145: type of maintenance (base or line)
  - Type of activity (perform, supervise or release)
  - Subcategory used (A1 to A4, B1.1 to B1.4, B2, B2L, B3, C, L1, L1C, L2, L2C, L3G, L3H, L4G, L4H, L5)
  - Duration in days or part days
- **Type training practical evidence**: detailed practical training records or a logbook from the directly approved course provider, or a Part-147 training certificate. For directly approved courses, the student gets a certificate plus enough records to prove content, duration and exam or assessment.
- **Limitation removal evidence**: an experience record signed by authorised certifying staff, or a manufacturer assessment.
- **Credit notification**: written, with the credit report reference.
- **Conversion reports and credit reports**: dated, with change history.

### CA record-keeping (66.B.20), who keeps what and for how long
For each licence, the CA keeps:
1. The application and supporting documentation.
2. A copy of the licence and all changes.
3. Correspondence.
4. Exemptions and enforcement actions.
5. Reports from other CAs.
6. Records of CA-conducted examinations.
7. The conversion report used.
8. The credit report used.

- Items 1 to 5: "at least 5 years after the end of the licence validity".
- Items 6 to 8: "an unlimited period".
- AMC:
  - Records must be accessible and consistently organised.
  - Sensitive data must be secure, with controlled access.
  - **Backups must be in a different location.**
  - Data must stay accessible through hardware and software changes.
- AltMoC documentation is part of the 66.B.20 records.
- A B2L licence replaced by a B2 licence is retained by the CA.
- Exam question usage records are retained (66.B.200(e)).

### Who issues
- **Only the competent authority issues the licence** (66.B.105(c)).
- An authorised Part-145 or Part-CAO organisation may prepare the licence or recommend issue. AMC: the procedure is in **MOE Chapter 3.21**, audited every 12 months. It applies only where the same CA oversees both the Part-145 approval and the licence.
- For cross-State changes via a Part-145 or Part-CAO organisation, the organisation sends the licence and Form 19 to the 66.1 CA.
- On renewal, Part-145 and Part-CAO organisations may submit on the holder's behalf if their exposition has a procedure.

## 4. Defined Terms
- **Aircraft maintenance licence (AML)**: the Part-66 licence.
- **Competent authority** (66.1, AMC1 66.1(a)): a ministry, NAA or other designated body located in the Member State. There may be several, but only one per area of responsibility.
- **Electrical system** (GM1 66.A.20(a)): "the aircraft electrical power supply source, plus the distribution system to the different components contained in the aircraft and relevant connectors". Includes lighting. Covers continuity, insulation and bonding testing, crimping, connector pin removal and insertion, and wiring protection.
- **Avionics system**: "an aircraft system that transfers, processes, displays or stores analogue or digital data using data lines, data buses, coaxial cables, wireless or other data transmission medium". Examples are autoflight, com/radar/nav, instruments, IFE, IMA, on-board maintenance systems, information systems, fly-by-wire (ATA 27) and fibre optic control. Note: instruments are formally B2 or B2L (instruments rating), but electromechanical and pitot-static components may be released by B1, B3 or L.
- **Simple test**: a test in approved maintenance data meeting all of these:
  - Serviceability is verifiable using aircraft controls, switches, BITE, CMC, or external test equipment not needing special training.
  - A unique go/no-go result, which may be a single value or a value within a tolerance, with no interpretation.
  - **Not more than 10 actions**, excluding configuration steps. A push plus read counts as one step.
- **Troubleshooting**: "the procedures and actions necessary, using approved maintenance data, in order to identify the root cause of a defect or malfunction". May use BITE or external test equipment.
- **Line maintenance and base maintenance**: see AMC1 145.A.10.
- **System rating** (B2L): gives privileges for the systems covered plus electrical systems. The five ratings are com/nav, instruments, autoflight, surveillance and airframe systems. The scope of each is in Appendix I, paragraph 2 "Modularisation".
- **ELA1 aeroplanes, ELA2 gas airships, gas airships other than ELA2**: by reference to Art. 2(k) and 2(ka) of 1321/2014.
- **Powered sailplane**: self-launching, self-sustaining and TMG.
- **Complex motor-powered aircraft (CMPA)**: used throughout, defined elsewhere (Reg. 2018/1139).
- **Group 1, 2 (2a, 2b, 2c), 3, 4**: see 66.A.5.
- **Type rating, manufacturer subgroup rating, full subgroup rating, full group rating**: 66.A.45.
- **Manufacturer** (for manufacturer subgroup): the TC holder in the TCDS. Joint TC holders count as one.
- **Similar aircraft** (for recency): similar technology, construction and systems, judged on propulsion, flight controls, avionics and structure.
- **Maintenance experience on operating aircraft** (AMC1 66.A.30(a)): maintenance on aircraft actually operated by airlines, air taxi, clubs or owners, in a real environment rather than only a school.
- **Skilled worker**: see 2.6.
- **Recent experience** (AMC 66.A.30(d)): 50% within 12 months, the rest within 7 years.
- **Limitation**: an exclusion from certification privileges (66.A.50).
- **Certifying staff qualification** (GM 66.A.70): a qualification where the person's signature alone declared maintenance properly done and the aircraft fit for flight.
- **Met the provision for the issue of the appropriate privileges** (GM 66.A.20(b)2): met all rating requirements in the last 2 years.
- **OJT**: On-the-Job Training (Appendix III, Section 6). Only for the first aircraft rating in a (sub)category.
- **Airframe, powerplant and avionics/electrical type training courses** (AMC1 66.B.115):
  - Airframe: structure plus electrical and mechanical systems, excluding powerplant.
  - Powerplant: bare engine up to quick engine change unit build-up.
  - The engine/airframe interface is in one of the two (often the airframe course in GA).
  - Avionics: ATA 22, 23, 24, 25, 27, 31, 33, 34, 42, 44, 45, 46, 73, 77 "or equivalent".
- **AltMoC**: alternative means of compliance.
- **Credit report and conversion report**: Subparts E and D.
- **MBT**: multimedia-based training.

## 5. Software Implications (Airworthiness+)

### 5.1 Data objects
- **Person or Engineer**: id, name, date of birth (needed for the 18+ check and CA identity verification), nationality, contact details, languages and a self-declared proficiency flag (66.A.20(b)4).
- **Licence (AML)**:
  - licence_number, issuing_authority (Member State or UK CAA), regulatory_basis (EASA Part-66 or UK Part-66).
  - form_type (EASA Form 26 or national equivalent).
  - date_of_first_issue, date_of_last_issue_or_change, validity_expiry (= last issue or change + 5 years).
  - holder_signed (boolean, because an unsigned licence is invalid), status (valid, expired, suspended, limited, revoked).
  - computer_generated (boolean, because additions are then reissued rather than stamped).
  - A scanned copy of every page and version.
  - superseded_by and supersedes links for reissue, B2L to B2 replacement and transfer between authorities (66.1(a)2).
- **LicenceCategoryEndorsement**:
  - category: A, B1, B2, B2L, B3, L or C.
  - subcategory: A1 to A4, B1.1 to B1.4, L1C, L1, L2C, L2, L3H, L3G, L4H, L4G or L5.
  - for C: scope, either CMPA or other than CMPA.
  - date endorsed, and how (stamp or reissue).
  - Implied inclusions computed, not stored: B1.x includes the corresponding A subcategory; L2 includes L1; L2C includes L1C; C for CMPA includes C for other than CMPA; B2 includes all B2L system ratings for the same aircraft ratings.
- **B2LSystemRating**: com/nav, instruments, autoflight, surveillance or airframe systems. At least one is required.
- **AircraftRating**:
  - rating_type: type, manufacturer_subgroup, full_subgroup, full_group, B3_fixed or L_fixed.
  - type_rating_code: from the EASA "List of Type Ratings" (Appendix I to AMC). UK uses a CAA equivalent list.
  - group or subgroup: 1, 2a, 2b, 2c, 3 or 4.
  - manufacturer (TC holder), applicable category or subcategory.
  - basis: type training plus OJT, type evaluation plus practical experience, practical experience only, conversion, or derived (2b gives 2c; B2L 2a or 2b gives Groups 3 and 4).
  - evidence links: Form 149b CoR, OJT record, experience log.
  - date endorsed.
  - The type rating also needs a link to a **TypeRatingListEntry**: code, models and variants (column 2), airframe/engine combinations, group, and revision history, so that variant additions can be tracked.
- **Limitation**:
  - source: 66.A.45 (structure or pressurisation), 66.A.45(h)(ii)(3) (L 1-year route), 66.A.70 conversion, 66.B.500 CA limitation, or free text (for example "aircraft other than a Cessna 172", "aircraft not owned by the licence holder", "aircraft used by air carriers licensed under Reg. 1008/2008").
  - scope: applies to which category, subcategory or rating.
  - removal_condition, for example experience, CA practical assessment, or the modules listed in conversion report X.
  - removed_date.
  - These must be machine-checkable where possible: structure in {metal, composite, wood, metal-tube-fabric, wood-fabric}, pressurised (boolean), MTOM threshold, retractable gear, ELA1 gas balloon, complex tasks or standard changes and repairs, owned-by-holder, specific type.
- **BasicExamModule**: module or submodule number, category it counts for, pass date, exam body (Part-147 organisation, CA, or other organisation for L), Form 148b reference. Computed expiry is pass date + 10 years **unless already used for an issued licence category**, so store `used_for_issued_category`.
- **ExamCredit**: credit report reference, granting CA, modules credited, date granted, expiry (+10 years), extension history.
- **TrainingCertificate**: Part-147 basic training CoR, skilled-worker certificate, academic degree (discipline, institution, recognised flag), type training CoR (Form 149b: theory, practical or both), differences training, MBT flag.
- **ExperienceLogEntry**. The AMC 66.A.20(b)2 fields are directly usable as a schema:
  - date
  - aircraft type and type rating code
  - registration
  - ATA chapter (optional)
  - operation performed
  - maintenance type (line or base)
  - activity type (perform, supervise or release)
  - (sub)category used
  - duration (days or part days)
  - Also: organisation (with approval reference: Part-145, Part-CAO, Subpart F, or none for independent M.A.801(b)1 staff), supervisor or countersigner (with licence number), signature and attestation status, Appendix II task reference (for rating practical experience), and flags for activity counting towards the 20% (training, engineering, planning).
- **CertificationAuthorisation**: this sits in Part-145, Part-CAO and Part-ML and links to the licence. Category A privileges depend on it entirely, and the B2 A-type privilege relies on it.

### 5.2 Privileges check: "can person X certify task T on aircraft Y?"
1. **Licence valid**: issued or changed by the CA, signed, not past the 5-year date, not suspended or revoked.
2. **Category or subcategory matches the aircraft and work type**:
   - Aeroplane or helicopter, turbine or piston.
   - Work domain: mechanical; avionics or electrical; simple test versus troubleshooting; line minor scheduled versus base.
   - C only for a base maintenance final CRS.
   - B3 only for piston non-pressurised aeroplanes of 2 000 kg MTOM or less.
   - B2L only within its system ratings, and never Group 1.
   - The aircraft record must hold: group or subgroup (derived from CMPA status, engine count and type, FL290 ceiling, FBW, airship ELA2 status, and Agency reclassification), MTOM, pressurisation, structure material, ELA1/ELA2 status, and type rating code.
3. **Aircraft rating present** for the category, using the group rules in 2.8. Category A needs no rating, but needs the 145.A.35 authorisation task list.
4. **No limitation excludes this aircraft.** Limitations apply to the aircraft in its entirety.
5. **Recency**: 6 months (or 100 days, or 50 days with CA agreement for M.A.801(b)1 owners and low utilisation, which cannot be combined with the 20% reduction) in the last 24 months, on that aircraft or a "similar" one per (sub)category and per structure type for group ratings. Alternatively, a rating endorsed within the last 2 years.
6. **Competence flag**: type training variant coverage (for example A320 only on an A318-A321 rating), differences training for added variants, customer options. Organisation authorisation scope (145.A.35, CAO.A.040, M.A.607). Language.
7. **Organisational context**: Part-145, Part-CAO or Part-ML (M.A.801 independent certifying staff) rules apply too. These come from other chunks.

### 5.3 Alerts
- **Licence expiry**: last issue or change + 5 years. Remind at 6, 3 and 1 months. Note the renewal can be submitted through the organisation if it has an exposition procedure. Any change (new rating) resets the 5-year clock ("5 years after its last issue or change"), so recompute on each change.
- **Recency**: a rolling 24-month window per aircraft type or similar group, per (sub)category and per structure type. Forecast the date it will fall below 6 months or 100 days. Warn when the ratio of 20%-eligible activity is too high.
- **Basic exam module expiry**: 10 years after pass, for modules not yet used for an issued category. This is relevant to engineers working towards licence issue or adding a category.
- **Experience window**: experience older than 10 years drops out. Recent experience is 50% within 12 months and 100% within 7 years (initial), with at least 3 months for additions.
- **Credit expiry**: 10 years after grant.
- **Holder-signed check**: block privileges if the licence has not been signed.
- **New type rating list variants**: prompt the holder to request the amendment and flag a competence or differences training gap.
- **Licence production**: certifying and support staff must produce the licence within 24 hours, so offer a digital copy or export.
- **Licence status changes** (66.B.500 suspension or limitation): immediate block of the privileges check.

### 5.4 Application workflow support
- Build a Form 19 pack: experience summary (not a bland statement), cross-references to logbook entries, countersignatures, CoRs, credit report references, and for B2L the selected system and aircraft ratings.
- An experience calculator for 66.A.30. A wizard over category, training route (none, skilled worker, Part-147, academic) and C-licence routes. It should apply the combined-category rule (each category needs enough experience in the same period) and check non-approved experience (6 or 12 months of approved experience still needed).
- A rating practical experience tracker against Appendix II to AMC tasks: 50%, 80% with asterisked tasks for L balloons and airships, 30% or 20% for later types of the same manufacturer (sub)group, and coverage of every paragraph.
- Subgroup rating planner: 2 types from the same TC holder, or 3 types from different TC holders, covering flight control, avionics and structure technologies. Joint TC holders count as one.
- Limitation removal tracker.

### 5.5 Data protection and retention
- If the app acts for organisations (for example a Part-145 using the 66.B.105 route), follow similar retention. The CA keeps applications and correspondence for 5 years after licence validity ends, and exam, conversion and credit records indefinitely. Offer long retention and **off-site backup** as best practice.
- Sensitive personal data must have controlled access.

## 7. Notable AMC and GM
- **AMC 66.A.20(b)(2), recency**: the most important for software. It covers 6 months or 100 days, the 50% reduction for owners and low utilisation (with prior CA agreement, M.A.801(b)1 only), the 20% non-hands-on allowance (not combinable with the 50%), the similar-aircraft criteria, per-group and per-structure coverage, and the logbook fields.
- **GM 66.A.20(b)2**: a new rating within 2 years satisfies recency for the first 2 years.
- **AMC 66.A.20(b)3, competence gaps**: partial variant coverage, variants added without differences training, evolved models, customer options, grandfathered basic knowledge, (sub)group ratings based on representative samples, recency only on similar types, and converted licences with lifted limitations.
- **GM1 66.A.20(a)**: definitions of electrical system, avionics system, **simple test (10 actions or fewer, go/no-go)** and troubleshooting. Explains the C-licence role (single CRS, after B and L support staff have signed). B3 may do A-type tasks. B1.2 and B3 holders may get L1 and L2. B2 covers B2L.
- **AMC 66.A.20(a)(4)**: B2L privileges are limited by both aircraft group and system rating. The airframe systems rating is the only one covering electrical and avionics tasks in powerplant and mechanical systems.
- **AMC 66.A.10**: experience write-up standard. A logbook is desirable. Attach the Part-147 CoR or skilled-worker certificate.
- **GM 66.A.10(a)**: B2L application details. A system rating addition needs 3 months of experience per rating plus aircraft group experience. B2L to B2 needs a difference exam plus Appendix IV experience, and the B2L is then replaced.
- **AMC1 66.A.30(a)**: what counts as experience (part-time, voluntary, weekends for L). Skilled worker definition. **Combining categories** examples. C academic route content, including planning, QA, records, spares and engineering, at a Part-145, Part-CAO or Part-CAMO organisation or a CA. Non-EU experience needs at least 1 year in Part-145 or Part-CAO, including 6 months of base maintenance.
- **GM1 66.A.30(a)**: summary table of C-licence experience routes.
- **AMC 66.A.30(c)**: B2L "representative cross section" means tasks on the systems of the ratings applied for.
- **AMC 66.A.30(d)**: recent experience split 50% in 12 months, the rest in 7 years.
- **AMC1 66.A.30(e)**: additional approved-organisation experience of 6 months (A, L) or 12 months (B1, B2, B2L, B3, C).
- **GM 66.A.40**: licence validity is independent of recency.
- **GM1 66.A.45**: a large summary table of rating routes by group and category. OJT only for the first rating in a (sub)category. B3 and C cannot hold a B3-style rating on B2/B2L or C licences, because Group 3 covers those aircraft. Group 1 C needs type training (theory and exam only).
- **GM 66.A.45(b)**: a type rating includes all variants in the list. List amendments mean the rating is updated on request without training, but competence still applies. A partial-variant course gives the full rating.
- **AMC 66.A.45(d);(e)3;(f)1;(g)1;(h)**: 50%, 80% and 30%/20% task thresholds against Appendix II to AMC. Experience must be supervised and logged.
- **AMC 66.A.45(e)**: representativeness of subgroup ratings (flight controls, avionics, structure). Group 1 types from the same manufacturer may count. Manufacturer means the TC holder, and joint holders count as one.
- **GM1 66.A.45(h)(ii)**: L limitation applicability. The structure limitations do not apply to L1C, L2C, L3H, L4H or L4G.
- **AMC 66.A.50(b)**: removing limitations by supervised tasks (including annual inspection tasks) or manufacturer training plus assessment. One representative type is enough. Annex I aircraft experience is acceptable.
- **GM 66.A.70, 66.A.70(c), 66.A.70(d)**: conversion eligibility dates, who qualifies, and worked limitation examples (pilot-owner Cessna 172, own aircraft only).
- **GM 66.B.100**: what a licence must or may carry at issue. A has a subcategory and no type ratings. B1, B2 and C may have no ratings. B2L needs at least one system rating. B3 is always issued with its rating. L needs at least one subcategory and rating. **This gives the validation rules for a licence record.**

- **AMC 66.B.110**: computer-generated licences are reissued. A replaced B2L is retained by the CA.
- **AMC1 66.B.115**: split type courses (airframe, powerplant, avionics ATA list). Non-Part-147 training evidence. OJT acceptance across different CAs.
- **AMC 66.B.120**: the CA does not check current practice at renewal. The organisation or individual is responsible for privilege validity.
- **AMC 66.B.130**: direct approval contents (TNA, methods, instructors, records). Approval is case by case, not long term.
- **GM 66.B.200**: exam question design. MCQs have **3 alternative answers**, no calculators. Essays: 20 minutes, scored out of 100, 60% key points and 40% report style. Oral exams are not the primary means.
- **AMC 66.B.20**: secure records, off-site backup, continuity through system changes.
- **GM1 66.B.400(d)**: a licensing authority should accept another CA's credits unless they are non-compliant.
- **GM 66.B.410**: expired credits are reassessed only if Appendix I has changed.
- **AMC1, GM1 66.B.2**: AltMoC processing and notification to EASA.
