## 2. Obligations (who, what, when; numbers quoted exactly)

### 2.1 Maintenance programme (App I)
App I contains a scope note: "CAMO" here means CAMO **or CAO**, and "Part-145 organisation" means Subpart F **or Part-CAO** organisation.

- **Who prepares and owns it:** the owner, the operator or the CAMO managing the aircraft's airworthiness.
- **Signed statement:** the owner, operator or CAMO signs a statement that the aircraft "will be maintained to the programme" and that the programme "will be reviewed and updated as required" (1.1.4).
- **Utilisation and check periods (1.1.6):**
  - Check periods should reflect the anticipated utilisation.
  - The utilisation should be stated and include "a tolerance of not more than 25%".
  - Where utilisation cannot be anticipated, calendar time limits should also be included.
- **Escalation of check periods (1.1.7):** only where applicable and acceptable to the competent authority of registry.
- **Mandatory items (1.1.17):**
  - The programme cross-refers to EASA-approved documents holding the mandatory life and inspection limitations, the CMRs and the ADs.
  - These items should not sit in the main body of the programme, or in any planning control system, "without specific identification of their mandatory status".
- **Amendments (3):** made by the owner/CAMO to reflect:
  - changes in TC holder or declarant recommendations;
  - modifications;
  - service experience;
  - competent authority requirements.
- **Variation of periods (4):** periods may vary only:
  - with competent authority approval; or
  - through a procedure in the programme that the competent authority has approved.
- **Periodic review (5):**
  - The programme is reviewed periodically against current TC holder/declarant recommendations, MRB revisions, mandatory requirements and the aircraft's maintenance needs.
  - The detailed requirements are reviewed "**at least annually**" for continued validity in the light of operating experience (5.2).
- **CDCCL (2.4):** if the TC/STC holder has identified CDCCLs, maintenance instructions should be developed.
- **Competent authority (2.3):** may require changes to a proposed programme, such as extra tasks or de-escalation of check frequencies. A programme approved for one owner/CAMO is not automatically approved for another.

**Reliability programme: when required (6.1.1).** One is required when any of these applies:
- (a) the AMP is based on MSG-3 logic;
- (b) the AMP includes condition-monitored components;
- (c) the AMP lacks overhaul periods for all significant system components;
- (d) the MPD or MRB specifies one.

**Reliability programme: when not required (6.1.2).** It need not be developed when:
- (a) the AMP is MSG-1/2 based and contains only hard-time or on-condition items;
- (b) the aircraft is not a complex motor-powered aircraft (CMPA);
- (c) the AMP gives overhaul periods for all significant system components.

A "significant system" is one whose failure "could hazard the aircraft safety". A CAMO may set up a voluntary reliability programme anyway (6.1.3).

**Fleet size.**
- The requirement does not depend on fleet size (6.2.2).
- A "small fleet" is "less than 6 aircraft of the same type" (6.2.1). Small fleets should tailor the programme, use alert levels carefully and apply engineering judgement.
- Pooling data is recommended for small fleets.
- Where pooling is impossible (for example a new type entering service), the competent authority should restrict variations to MRB/MPD intervals (6.2.7).

**Contracting reliability work (6.4).** The CAMO may contract out:
- developing the AMP and the reliability programme;
- collecting and analysing data;
- reliability reports;
- proposing corrective actions.

The decision to implement a corrective action remains with the CAMO. A decision **not** to implement a proposed corrective action "should be justified and documented" (6.4.3).

**Corrective actions (6.5.7.2):** the documentation should include a "planned completion date for each corrective action".

**Submitted to the competent authority for approval (6.5.9):**
- the format and content of routine reports;
- the timescales and distribution of reports;
- the format and content of escalation/amendment reports.

**Reviews (6.5.10):**
- Routine and non-routine review periods should be detailed, for example "progressive, monthly, quarterly, or annual".
- Procedures are needed for when standards or alert levels are exceeded.
- Procedures and timescales are needed for revising the alert levels.

**Implementing changes before formal approval (6.5.11).** The competent authority may allow the CAMO to implement changes from reliability results before formal approval when:
- (a) the reliability programme monitors the AMP comprehensively; and
- (b) the Reliability Group procedures give the CAMO internal validation control.

**Pooling (6.6):**
- The competent authority's approval should be sought **before** any formal pooling agreement between CAMOs is signed.
- Commonality factors:
  - certification: TCDS variant and modification/SB status;
  - operational: environment, utilisation, fleet size, ETOPS/RVSM/AWO, MEL use;
  - maintenance: aircraft age, procedures, standards, lubrication, MPD revision or escalation.
- For a short-term lease "(less than 6 month)", the competent authority may grant more flexibility on the commonality criteria (6.6.3).
- Taking part in a manufacturer-run reliability programme is acceptable if the competent authority is satisfied with it (6.6.5).
- Inadequately qualified reliability personnel may lead the competent authority to reject the reliability programme and therefore the AMP (6.3.2).

### 2.2 Subcontracting CAM tasks (App II)

**CAMO controls (section 1):**
- Employ trained and competent staff in Subpart G disciplines to control the subcontractor.
- Conduct a **pre-subcontract audit**.
- Ensure the subcontractor has sufficient qualified staff, taking its existing commitments into account.
- Hold CAME procedures for managing the arrangement.
- Have a contract.

**The contract should require or provide that:**
- the subcontractor informs the CAMO of changes affecting its ability to fulfil the contract, and the CAMO then notifies its competent authority (1.5);
- the subcontractor's procedures may be amended only with the CAMO's agreement (1.8);
- the competent authority may monitor the subcontractor (1.11), and responsibilities for closing authority findings are allocated (1.12);
- the competent authority always has access (2.7).

**Subcontractor procedures:**
- If the subcontractor writes its own procedures, they must be compatible with the CAME.
- The competent authority accepts them as extended CAMO procedures, cross-referenced from the CAME.
- The CAMO keeps one current copy.
- If the CAME and the subcontractor's procedures conflict, the CAME prevails.
- The CAMO nominates a person to monitor and accept the subcontractor's procedures and their amendments.

**CAMO powers and duties:**
- The CAMO retains authority to override any subcontractor recommendation.
- If the CAMO fails to ensure the subcontractor keeps its expertise and resources, this "may invalidate the CAMO approval" (1.10).

**Task allocation (section 2):**
- Programme and amendments (2.2): the subcontractor may draft them. The CAMO assesses them and obtains the competent authority's approval.
- Programme variations (2.4): the subcontractor may prepare the justification. The CAMO accepts. Variations outside the programme's limits need competent authority approval.
- Base maintenance checks (2.5): the CAMO agrees the work specification case by case.
- Line checks (2.5): may be controlled day to day by the subcontractor, under CAMO liaison.
- ADs (2.9): assessment and planning may be subcontracted, but embodiment is done by a maintenance organisation. The CAMO remains responsible for timely embodiment, receives notification of compliance and agrees the means of compliance.
- Utilisation data (2.11): the subcontractor receives current FH/FC/landings/calendar data at a frequency set in the contract.
- Deferred defects (2.13):
  - The basis for deferral is the MEL/CDL.
  - Other defects go to the CAMO, which may defer them depending on the procedural authority the competent authority has granted.
  - The subcontractor assesses the cumulative effect of combined defects.
  - A contracted maintenance organisation may defer MEL/CDL items, subject to acceptance by the aircraft commander.
- Records (2.15): may be kept by the subcontractor, but the CAMO remains their owner. The CAMO receives the current AD/LLP/time-controlled component status and has unrestricted, timely access. Online access is acceptable.
- MCF (2.16): performed under the operator's control, in coordination with the CAMO.
- Urgent data (2.8): a "fast track" route for urgent maintenance data to the subcontractor.

### 2.3 KRE inspection items (App III): obligations restated as hard rules
- Changes to type design "shall be approved" (or declared) and, if embodied, "recorded with the reference to the approval" (or declaration).
  - Exception: changes under CS referred to in 21A.90B / 21L.A.62 / 21L.A.102 (standard changes).
- Repairs not defined in the appropriate maintenance manual need to be approved and recorded with the approval reference.
  - Exception: standard repairs under 21A.431B / 21L.A.202 / 21L.A.222.
- A third-country State of Design AD for an imported product "shall apply unless the Agency has issued a different Decision" before that AD enters into force.
- Life-limited components "must be permanently removed from service when, or before" their limit is exceeded. Their life is controlled at component level.
- The current status of life-limited parts "is to be maintained throughout the operating life of the part".
- Operations outside approved or declared data are allowed only under a Permit to Fly or the flexibility provisions (Art. 71 of Reg (EU) 2018/1139).
- AMP checks (C.1):
  - the AMP is valid for the aircraft and "reviewed annually";
  - tasks are done within the AMP and source-document values;
  - no task is omitted without competent authority acceptance.

### 2.4 MOM (App IV)
- The competent authority approves the MOM in writing, normally by approving the list of effective pages (LEP).
- Minor amendments, or amendments to a large capability list, may be approved indirectly under a procedure approved by the Member State.
- The organisation must prepare and get approved an amendment whenever the MOM no longer meets Part-M/Part-ML. Causes include:
  - a change in the rules;
  - a change in the organisation or its activities;
  - an inadequacy found by the organisational review;
  - any other reason.
- The MOM (or relevant parts) is distributed to all concerned staff and contracted organisations.

### 2.5 CAME (App V)
- **Accountable manager statement:**
  - Its intent must not be altered.
  - The procedures do not override new or amended regulations.
  - The competent authority may suspend, limit or revoke the approval if procedures are not followed.
  - For air carriers licensed under Reg (EC) 1008/2008, suspension or revocation of the Subpart G approval "would invalidate the AOC".
- **Changes needing prior notification (0.5):** the competent authority must be notified of changes to:
  - (1) name and location(s);
  - (2) the group of persons (0.3c);
  - (3) operations, procedures and technical arrangements that may affect the approval.

  These changes are not incorporated "until they have been assessed and approved by the competent authority".
- **Amendments (0.6):** minor amendments may be approved internally if the competent authority agrees (indirect approval). The CAME should define what counts as minor and major.
- **Distribution:** to management (and lower levels as needed), to the contracted Part-145/Subpart F maintenance organisations, and to the competent authority.
- **MEL deferrals (1.1b):** once the crew accepts one, the defect "must be rectified within the time limit specified in the MEL". A system must ensure this. The competent authority may allow overruns of MEL time limits under specific conditions.
- **Quality plan (2.1b):** covers "all the areas specific to Part-M in a definite period of time", stays dynamic, and audits subcontractors at the same frequency as the rest of the organisation.
- **Part-time auditors (2.6):** "must not be directly involved in the activity they are auditing".
- **Maintenance contractor selection (3.1b):** check the approval and also the industrial capacity, and run a contract review. The CAMO should agree the selection process with the operator before contracting.
- **ARC issue (4.6):** an ARC is issued "only after an airworthiness review has been properly carried out".
- **Permit to fly (4B.2):** completed on EASA Form 20b, with compliance with 21.A.711(d)/(e) checked before signing and 21.A.711(g) followed for revocation.
- **List reviews:** the lists of subcontractors (5.3) and contracted maintenance organisations (5.4) "should be periodically reviewed".

### 2.6 Contracted maintenance (App XI)
- The contract should specify:
  - the maintenance programme, which the CAMO should have approved by its competent authority (2.4);
  - quality surveillance and audit rights for the CAMO (2.5);
  - competent authority access (2.6).
- **Subcontracting (2.3):** reference M.A.615 and 145.A.75. The CAMO may require its approval before the maintenance organisation subcontracts. The CAMO gets access to subcontractor quality information.
- **Responsibilities:**
  - hours and cycles control is the CAMO's (2.10);
  - control of LLPs and time-controlled components is the CAMO's (2.11);
  - satisfying itself that a part meets approved data and is fit for installation is the maintenance organisation's (2.12).
- **Deferrals:**
  - Any deferral of a maintenance task by the maintenance organisation "has to be formally agreed with the CAMO" (2.14).
  - Deferral of any defect rectification goes to the CAMO (2.15).
  - Deviations beyond approved limits are managed by the CAMO under the AMP procedures (2.17).
- **Record-keeping:** if the CAMO has the maintenance organisation keep records, that counts as subcontracting under M.A.711(a)(3) (2.21).
- **Consistency:** where several maintenance organisations hold contracts (base, engine, line), the contracts should be consistent with each other.

### 2.7 Organisational review (App VIII: Subpart F organisations with fewer than 10 maintenance staff; App XIII: small CAMOs)
- **Responsible person:** by default the accountable manager. The accountable manager may delegate to an M.A.606(b) person (App VIII) or an M.A.706(c) person (App XIII).
- **Reviewer qualification:** thorough knowledge of the regulations and the procedures, plus audit knowledge gained through training or experience.
- **Frequency:** "Each item should be checked at least every **12 months**". This may be one full annual review or several partial reviews.
- **Level 1 findings** (M.A.619(a) for Subpart F, M.A.716(a) for CAMOs): notify the competent authority "**immediately**" and take all necessary action on in-service aircraft immediately.
- **Findings:** all findings are recorded and notified to the affected persons.
- **Corrective and preventive actions:**
  - approved by the person responsible for the review programme;
  - implemented "within a specified time frame";
  - closure recorded with a summary once the corrective action is shown effective.
- **Occurrence reports:** reviewed to find prior indicators and dismissed alerts.
- **Accountable manager:** notified of all significant findings, and regularly of overall results.
- **App VIII limit:** applies only to organisations with fewer than 10 maintenance staff. Larger organisations use an independent quality system.

### 2.8 Fuel tank safety training (App XII)

**Effectivity (A):** large aeroplanes (CS-25) that:
- were certified after **1 January 1958** with a passenger capacity of **30 or more** or a payload of **7500 lbs (3402 kg)** or more; or
- have CS-25 amendment 1 or later in their certification basis.

**Affected organisations (B):** CAMOs managing those aeroplanes, and the competent authorities overseeing them (M.B.704).

**Who is trained (C):**
- Phase 1 only: the quality manager and quality staff, and competent authority oversight staff.
- Phase 1, Phase 2 and continuation training: CAMO staff who manage and review the continuing airworthiness of the affected aircraft.

**Phase 1 (awareness):**
- Timing: before working unsupervised, and "not later than **6 months** after joining".
- Format: may be a training bulletin or self-study. The reader's signature is required.
- Credit: holders of the Level 1 course under ED Decision 2007/001/R already comply.

**Phase 2 (detailed):**
- Timing: by **31 December 2010** or "within **12 months** of joining the organization, whichever comes later". The flexible implementation period was not to extend beyond 31 Dec 2010.
- Format: not self-study. It ends with a multiple-choice exam with a pass mark of "**75%**".
- Duration: "A duration of **8 hours** for phase 2 is an acceptable compliance".
- E-learning criteria:
  - continuous evaluation of effectiveness;
  - intermediate questions before moving on;
  - exam content and results recorded;
  - access to an instructor.
- Classroom instructors must have attended a similar classroom course themselves.
- Credit: holders of the Level 2 course under ED Decision 2007/001/R (from a CAMO or a Part-147 organisation) already comply, except for continuation training.

**Continuation training:**
- "in each two years period" (every **2 years**);
- its syllabus sits in the CAME training policy;
- it is updated when new instructions are issued.

**Approval (F):** the CAMO's training programme and exam content are approved through a CAME change under M.A.704(b), implemented when the competent authority requests.

## 3. Records and Documents (structures in full)

### 3.1 Aircraft Maintenance Programme: content checklist (App I §1.1)
1. Type/model and registration of the aircraft, engines and (where applicable) APUs and propellers.
2. Name and address of the owner, operator or CAMO.
3. Reference, date of issue and issue number of the approved programme.
4. Signed statement of commitment (maintained to the programme; reviewed and updated).
5. Contents/LEP and revision status.
6. Check periods with stated anticipated utilisation (tolerance no more than 25%), plus calendar limits where utilisation cannot be anticipated.
7. Procedures for escalating check periods (if applicable and acceptable to the competent authority of registry).
8. Provision to record the date and reference of approved amendments.
9. Pre-flight maintenance tasks done by maintenance staff.
10. Tasks and periods (intervals/frequencies), with the type and degree of inspection, for every part of the aircraft, engines, APUs, propellers, components, accessories, equipment, instruments, electrical and radio apparatus, and associated systems and installations.
11. Periods for checking, cleaning, lubricating, replenishing, adjusting and testing components.
12. Ageing aircraft system requirements and sampling programmes (if applicable).
13. Structural programmes (if applicable):
    - (a) (S)SIP/(S)SID from the design approval holder or declarant;
    - (b) CPCP based on the baseline CPCP;
    - (c) for large aeroplanes, Part-26 point 26.370 ageing structure data (Reg (EU) 2015/640).
14. CDCCL details and procedures (if applicable).
15. Limit of validity of the structural programme, in total flight cycles / calendar date / flight hours (if applicable).
16. Overhaul/replacement periods.
17. Cross-reference to EASA-approved documents for mandatory life and inspection limitations, CMRs and ADs, kept separately identified as mandatory.
18. Reliability programme or statistical methods of continuous surveillance (details or cross-reference).
19. Statement that practices follow the TC holder's or declarant's maintenance instructions, or a reference to any approved different practices.
20. A definitions section defining each maintenance task quoted.

**Programme basis:** MRB report (where applicable) plus MPD or Chapter 5 of the maintenance manual (the manufacturer's recommended programme). The format may be restructured.

**Task sources (App III C.1):**
- mandatory: repetitive ADs, ALS (including CMRs);
- recommended: MRBR, MPD, SBs, other non-mandatory information;
- owner/CAMO additional or alternative instructions approved under M.A.302(e).

**Approval routes (App III C.1 item 11):**
- direct approval by the competent authority;
- indirect approval by the CAMO;
- self-declared maintenance programme.

### 3.2 Reliability programme document structure (App I §6.5)
1. Objectives (6.5.1):
   - at minimum: recognise the need for corrective action, establish what action is needed, and determine its effectiveness;
   - for MSG-3 programmes: monitor that MSG-3 tasks are effective and their periodicity is adequate.
2. Identification of items (6.5.2): for example by ATA chapter, with cross-references to separate programmes (structure, engines, APU sampling or life development).
3. Terms and definitions (6.5.3).
4. Information sources and collection (6.5.4). Procedures are set out in the CAME/MOE. Prime sources:
   - pilot reports;
   - tech logs;
   - AMAT/OMS readouts;
   - maintenance worksheets;
   - workshop reports;
   - functional check reports;
   - special inspection reports;
   - stores issues/reports;
   - air safety reports;
   - technical delay and incident reports;
   - other sources (ETOPS, RVSM, CAT II/III);
   - plus Part 21 / Part 21 Light continuing airworthiness information.
5. Display of information (6.5.5):
   - graphical, tabular or both;
   - rules for discarding data stated;
   - "nil returns" provision;
   - oriented to standards/alert levels.
6. Examination, analysis and interpretation (6.5.6). Methods:
   - comparison against standards;
   - trends;
   - repetitive defects;
   - confidence testing;
   - life-bands and survival;
   - reliability prediction.

   At least consider:
   - flight defects;
   - line and main base defects;
   - deterioration found in routine maintenance;
   - workshop findings;
   - modification evaluations;
   - sampling;
   - adequacy of equipment and publications;
   - effectiveness of procedures;
   - training;
   - SBs.

   Arrangements for data from contracted maintenance organisations.
7. Corrective actions (6.5.7). Possible forms:
   - procedure/technique changes;
   - AMP changes (escalation, de-escalation, adding, modifying or deleting tasks);
   - manual amendments;
   - modifications;
   - special inspections or fleet campaigns;
   - spares provisioning;
   - training;
   - manpower and equipment planning.

   Each action has a planned completion date. Some need competent authority approval.
8. Organisational responsibilities (6.5.8):
   - departments (Engineering, Production, Quality, Operations);
   - reliability group/committee;
   - competent authority participation.
9. Presentation of information to the competent authority (6.5.9).
10. Evaluation and review (6.5.10). Review criteria:
    - utilisation (high/low/seasonal);
    - fleet commonality;
    - alert level adjustment criteria;
    - adequacy of data;
    - reliability procedure audit;
    - staff training;
    - operational and maintenance procedures.
11. Approval of maintenance programme amendments (6.5.11).
12. Pooling arrangements (6.6).

## 4. Defined Terms
- **Small fleet (reliability):** "a fleet of less than 6 aircraft of the same type" (App I 6.2.1).
- **Significant system:** "a system the failure of which could hazard the aircraft safety" (App I 6.1.2).
- **CDCCL (Critical Design Configuration Control Limitations):** features of an installation or component "that should be retained during modification, change, repair, or scheduled maintenance for the operational life of the aircraft or applicable component or part" (App I 2.4).
- **Type design:** the part of the approved configuration (TCDS), or declared configuration (airworthiness data sheet), common to all products of that type. It consists of: drawings and specifications; materials, processes and manufacture information; the approved ALS of the ICA; and other comparison data (noise, fuel venting, emissions) (App III A.1).
- **Individual aircraft design:** the type design plus the type design changes (modifications) embodied on that aircraft.
- **Airworthiness limitation:** "a boundary beyond which an aircraft or a component thereof must not be operated, unless the instruction(s) associated with this airworthiness limitation is complied with" (App III A.2). Typical items:
  - SL ALI / LLPs;
  - DT ALI / structure;
  - CMR;
  - ASM incl. EWIS;
  - FTIP/FRM;
  - CDCCL;
  - ageing fleet inspections.
- **Airworthiness Directive:** "a document issued or adopted by the Agency, which mandates actions to be performed on an aircraft to restore an acceptable level of safety, when evidence shows that the safety level of this aircraft may otherwise be compromised" (21.A.3B / 21L.A.4).
- **Flight Manual:** a manual associated with the C of A, containing limitations, instructions and information for safe operation by the flight crew.
- **Defect management:** a documented system capturing faults, malfunctions, defects and occurrences that could adversely affect continuing airworthiness. It includes the MEL system, the CDL system and deferred defect management.
- **Aircraft Maintenance Programme:** a document describing (or incorporating by reference) the scheduled maintenance tasks and their frequency, the associated procedures and the standard practices. It includes the reliability programme when required.
- **Component control:** has two objectives: mandatory maintenance and recommended maintenance.
- **Life-limited component:** life controlled at component level; must be permanently removed at or before the limit.
- **Time-controlled components:** these include:
  - **Hard time** components: scheduled removal and restoration regardless of failure resistance; predictable deterioration.
  - **On-condition** components: inspected and left in service on condition they meet performance standards.
- **Condition-monitoring ("fly-to-failure"):** stays in service without preventive maintenance until functional failure; subject to unscheduled tasks.
- **Repair status:** list of embodied repairs still existing, plus unrepaired damage/degradations, with substantiating data (App III C.3).
- **Pooling:** collating reliability data from several CAMOs operating the same type (App I 6.6).
- **N/A vs N/R (Forms 6F/13):** N/A means not applicable; N/R means "applicable but not reviewed".
- **Abbreviations listed in App III:** A/C, ACAM, AD, ALI, ALS, AMM, AMP, APU, ASM, B-RNAV, CAMO, CDL, CDCCL, CMM, CMR, DT, ED, ETOPS, ETSO, EWIS, EZAP (Enhanced Zonal Analysis Procedure), FCOM, FDR, FM, FRM, FTIP, GA, ICA, IPC, KRE, LHIRF, LOPA (Layout of Passenger Accommodation), MCAI (Mandatory Continuing Airworthiness Information), MEL, MNPS, MRB, MRBR, MPD, NAA, OEM, OM, OM-B, PN, QRH, PWR, RVSM, SN, SB, SM (Service Manual), SRM, STC, TBO, TC, TCDS, TLB, TSO.
  - ACAM = "Aircraft Continuous Airworthiness Monitoring" in this list. Elsewhere it is "aircraft continuing airworthiness monitoring".
  - The text writes "IPCI" for the Illustrated Parts Catalogue (a typo).

    - list of amendments (date, reference, approval reference, approval level).
  - Child **AMPTask** fields:
    - task ID and definition;
    - source (AD repetitive / ALS-ALI / CMR / CDCCL / MRBR / MPD / SB / owner additional / alternative approved under M.A.302(e));
    - mandatory flag (must be visibly distinguished and protected from accidental edits per 1.1.17);
    - interval (FH/FC/landings/calendar, threshold and repeat, whichever-first logic);
    - tolerance;
    - applicability (zone, component PN, effectivity);
    - inspection type and degree;
    - pre-flight task flag;
    - pilot-owner task flag (M.A.803);
    - special operations linkage (RVSM, ETOPS, MNPS, B-RNAV);
    - structural programme items (SSID, CPCP, LOV in FC/FH/date).
- **AMPDeviation/Variation**
  - Fields: task, requested extension, justification, prepared by, CAMO acceptance, within or outside AMP limits, and competent authority approval if outside.
  - Covers maintenance organisation deferral requests needing CAMO agreement (App XI 2.14).
- **ReliabilityProgramme**
  - Fields:
    - required flag, with a computed trigger (MSG-3 / condition-monitored components / missing overhaul periods / MPD-MRB requirement; exempt if non-CMPA etc.);
    - fleet size, with a small-fleet (<6 same type) indicator;
    - items covered (ATA chapters);
    - data sources;
    - alert levels per parameter (value, method, last revision);
    - nil returns;
    - reports (format, frequency, distribution);
    - reliability group members;
    - pooling agreement (partners, competent authority approval before signing, commonality assessment, short lease <6 months flag);
    - manufacturer programme participation.
  - Child **ReliabilityCorrectiveAction** fields: type (one of the 6.5.7.1 list), planned completion date, needs competent authority approval, decision (implement / not implement plus mandatory justification), effectiveness monitoring.
- **Component / LLP**
  - Fields:
    - PN and SN;
    - control type (life-limited / hard time / on-condition / condition-monitored);
    - life limit;
    - TSN/CSN/landings/calendar;
    - remaining life;
    - installation history (per aircraft/engine type, hours/cycles per installation);
    - release document (Form 1 reference);
    - ETOPS/CDCCL flags;
    - physical marking verified.
- **TimeControlledTaskStatus:** task reference, threshold/interval, last done (date and component life), next due.
- **RepairRecord**
  - Fields:
    - repair file reference;
    - classification (minor/major);
    - item and precise location;
    - date;
    - item total FH/FC at repair or finding;
    - approved data reference (SRM/AMM/CMM, Part 21 approval, standard repair under 21A.431B/CS-STAN, or declaration);
    - status (embodied / unrepaired damage);
    - resulting AMP tasks or new ALS items.
  - Supports a "Repair status" report.
- **ModificationRecord:** approval or declaration reference, STC, standard change, effects on ALS/FM/MEL/WBM, and effects on noise and mass and balance.
- **ADRecord**
  - Fields:
    - AD number and revision;
    - issuing authority (State of Design / Agency);
    - applicability determination (applicable / not applicable, with reason; reviewable for wrong N/A);
    - means of compliance chosen and AMOC;
    - due date or limit;
    - repetitive interval;
    - linked AMP task;
    - accomplishment evidence (CRS, maintenance organisation);
    - emergency AD flag.
  - The closed-loop status states in §5.2 come from App V 1.4c.
- **Defect / DeferredDefect**
  - Fields:
    - source (PIREP, maintenance);
    - MEL/CDL reference and category with time limit;
    - crew acceptance;
    - non-MEL deferral (structural/cracks) with manufacturer liaison and CAMO authorisation;
    - cumulative-effect assessment;
    - rectification due;
    - extension/overrun approval;
    - repetitive defect link;
    - AOG flag.
- **UtilisationLog:** FH, FC and landings per flight/day, with a source (tech log). Feeds planning and is shared with contracted or subcontracted organisations at the contract-defined frequency.
- **AircraftDocuments:** CofR, CofA, noise certificate, ARC (validity), permit to fly and flight conditions, tech log, CRS, flight manual revision and supplements, mass and balance report (date, configuration basis), placards checklist.
- **Organisation**
  - Approval types: Subpart F / Part-145 / Subpart G / Part-CAMO / Part-CAO.
  - Fields: approval reference, Form 3 / Form 14 scope, staff count (to apply the <10 staff rule for MOM/organisational review), accountable manager, nominated persons (Form 4 acceptance records), facilities, tools (calibration due dates), maintenance data subscriptions.
- **ExpositionDocument (CAME/MOM)**
  - Section tree from the §3.3/§3.4 templates.
  - Revision and LEP management.
  - Amendment classification (minor via indirect approval / major via direct approval).
  - Distribution list with acknowledgements.
  - Pending changes that need prior competent authority approval (name, location, group of persons, procedures).
- **Contract**
  - Types: CAMO and owner (Appendix I to Part-M); CAMO and maintenance organisation (App XI); CAMO and CAM subcontractor (App II); work order (M.A.708(d)).
  - Clause checklist from App XI/App II headings.
  - Information-exchange matrix (what, when, how, who to whom).
  - Meetings schedule.
  - Pre-subcontract audit record.
  - Subcontractor procedure copies and amendment acceptance.
  - Periodic review dates for the subcontractor and contracted maintenance organisation lists.
- **AuditFinding / OrganisationalReview**
  - Checklist template (App VIII 11 items / App XIII 10 items / Form 6F / Form 13 rows).
  - Item results: tick / X / N/A / N/R.
  - Findings: level (1/2), cross-reference, corrective action, due date, closed date, summary.
  - Reviewer qualification.
  - Every item reviewed within 12 months.
- **TrainingRecord (FTS):**
  - person, role category (Phase 1 only vs Phase 1+2+continuation);
  - join date;
  - Phase 1 completion with signature (due within 6 months of joining);
  - Phase 2 completion, exam score (≥75%), duration, delivery mode (classroom/e-learning) (due within 12 months of joining);
  - continuation due every 2 years;
  - credit for ED Decision 2007/001/R Level 1/2 courses.
- **AirworthinessReviewStaff** authorisations (CAME 4.1, list 5.2) and **PermitToFly signatories** (4B.3: name, signature, scope).
- **RecordsRetentionSchedule** (CAME 1.3b): document family, document name, retention period, responsible person, place.

### 5.2 Statuses
- AD per aircraft:
  - `Not applicable` (with justification)
  - `Applicable – open, not overdue`
  - `Applicable – overdue` (grounding alert)
  - `Accomplished – terminating`
  - `Accomplished – repetitive, next due X`
- AMP: `Draft` / `Submitted` / `Approved (direct)` / `Approved (indirect)` / `Self-declared` / `Superseded`. Amendments use the same states.
- Corrective action: `Proposed` / `Accepted` / `Rejected (justified)` / `Awaiting CA approval` / `Implemented` / `Effectiveness verified` / `Closed`.
- Finding: `Open (L1/L2)` / `CA notified` (L1, immediate) / `CAP agreed` / `Overdue` / `Closed`.
- Deferred defect: `Open – within MEL limit` / `Extension requested/approved` / `Overdue` / `Rectified`.
- Organisational change: `Proposed` / `Notified to CA` / `Approved` / `Incorporated`.

### 5.3 Workflows
1. **AMP lifecycle:**
   1. Draft from MRB/MPD.
   2. Mark mandatory items.
   3. Owner/CAMO signed statement.
   4. Submit (direct) or indirect approval.
   5. Annual review task (auto-scheduled +12 months).
   6. Amendment triggers: new ICA revision, MRB revision, modification or repair embodied, AD, reliability corrective action, competent authority request.
   7. Record the amendment date and reference.
2. **Task variation/escalation:**
   1. Request (possibly prepared by a subcontractor or maintenance organisation).
   2. CAMO acceptance.
   3. If outside AMP limits, competent authority approval.
   4. Audit trail.
3. **Reliability cycle:**
   1. Data ingestion (PIREPs, tech log, shop reports, delays).
   2. Alert level computation, with a warning for small fleets that one event can breach a level.
   3. Reliability meeting.
   4. Corrective action with planned completion date.
   5. AMP amendment.
   6. Monitor effectiveness.
   7. Periodic alert level revision.
4. **AD closed loop:**
   1. Ingest a new or revised AD (multiple sources).
   2. Applicability decision per aircraft/component.
   3. Choose the means of compliance.
   4. Plan into a work package and link the AMP task if repetitive.
   5. Receive the compliance notification from the maintenance organisation.
   6. Close or set the next due.
   7. Emergency AD fast track.
5. **Work order / work package to maintenance organisation:**
   1. Include the job cards, scheduled component removal list, ADs and modifications to incorporate.
   2. The maintenance organisation returns: CRS, flight test report, modification list, repair list, list of ADs accomplished, visit report, test bench report, removed and installed list with Form 1s.
   3. The CAMO updates its records.
6. **Defect deferral:**
   1. MEL path: crew acceptance, then time limit tracking.
   2. Non-MEL path: CAMO assessment, cumulative check, manufacturer liaison.
   3. Escalate overruns to a competent authority-approved extension.
7. **Exposition amendment:**
   1. Classify minor or major.
   2. Minor: internal approval under the indirect procedure.
   3. Major: submit to the competent authority.
   4. Update the LEP and redistribute.
   5. Changes to name, location, group of persons or procedures are blocked until the competent authority approves.
8. **Organisational review / quality plan:**
   1. Schedule every checklist item within 12 months (full or partial).
   2. Record evidence.
   3. Record findings.
   4. Level 1: immediate notification to the competent authority and fleet action.
   5. CAP approved by the responsible person, with a time frame.
   6. Closure with summary.
   7. Accountable manager reporting.
9. **ACAM/airworthiness review preparation pack:** generate KRE-structured evidence (A.1 to C.4) for the authority or the CAMO's own airworthiness review:
   - configuration vs TCDS;
   - STC list;
   - ALS compliance;
   - AD status;
   - documents on board;
   - FM revision;
   - mass and balance validity;
   - placards;
   - operational equipment;
   - deferred defects;
   - AMP compliance;
   - component/LLP status;
   - repair status;
   - records integrity.
10. **Subcontractor onboarding:**
    1. Pre-subcontract audit.
    2. Contract with mandatory clauses.
    3. Procedure acceptance.
    4. Add to CAME 5.3.
    5. Periodic review and quality surveillance.
11. **Aircraft transfer:** a records transfer checklist (CAME 1.3d), including the LLP status with installation history.
12. **Permit to fly (if the CAMO has the privilege):**
    1. Check conformity with approved flight conditions.
    2. Form 20b, signed by an authorised signatory.
    3. Local authority interface.
    4. Records.

### 5.4 Alerts
- AMP annual review due/overdue.
- Utilisation drifting beyond the stated anticipated utilisation ±25% (prompt an AMP check period review).
- AD due, overdue or repetitive due; new AD received but not yet assessed.
- LLP remaining life thresholds; hard-time due; LOV approaching.
- MEL deferral limit approaching or exceeded; non-MEL deferral expiry.
- Aircraft with expired items (AD, AMP task, LLP, deferred maintenance, ARC validity): flag as **should be grounded** (App XIII item 2).
- Flights logged with overdue maintenance (App XIII item 6).
- Maintenance recorded without CAMO notification.
- Corrective action planned completion date passed.
- Level 1 finding raised: immediate notification task.
- Organisational review item not checked within 12 months.
- FTS training: Phase 1 at 6 months, Phase 2 at 12 months after joining, continuation every 2 years.
- Tool calibration due (MOM calibration frequencies).
- Contracted maintenance organisation approval (Form 3) copy out of date; subcontractor and contracted maintenance organisation list periodic review due.
- Mass and balance report not updated after a modification or repair.
- Flight manual supplement missing for an embodied STC.
- Reliability alert level exceeded.

### 5.5 Permissions and roles
- Roles:
  - owner / operator;
  - accountable manager;
  - nominated postholder for continuing airworthiness;
  - group of persons (contracts / technical / planning);
  - quality manager / auditors;
  - airworthiness review staff;
  - permit to fly signatories;
  - certifying staff (maintenance organisation);
  - subcontractor users;
  - read-only competent authority inspector.
- Rules:
  - Mandatory AMP items: editable only by authorised engineering roles, with change justification.
  - Only the CAMO can accept variations and corrective actions (not the subcontractor or maintenance organisation).
  - Auditors cannot audit their own activity (independence check, CAME 2.6).
  - A subcontractor may prepare drafts but not approve them. The CAMO can override recommendations.
  - Competent authority access to subcontractor and maintenance organisation records.
  - Records owned by the CAMO even if a subcontractor stores them.
  - Online access is acceptable (App II 2.15), which supports a shared portal model.
- Records integrity: protection against alteration and backups (CAME 1.3c), so use an immutable audit log.

### 5.6 Document generation
Templates to generate:
- CAME (App V);

- AMP skeleton (App I list);
- maintenance contract clause checklist (App XI);
- CAM subcontract (App II);
- organisational review checklists (App VIII/XIII);
- CAA/EASA Form 2 and Form 4;

The authority completes Forms 6F/13 itself, but they are useful for pre-audit self-checks.

## 7. Notable Guidance (changes practice)
1. **Mandatory items segregated (App I 1.1.17).** ALS, CMR and AD tasks should be cross-referenced and clearly flagged as mandatory in the AMP and in any planning system. This is a direct requirement on planning software.
2. **Utilisation basis.** AMP check periods are tied to a stated utilisation with ≤25% tolerance. Calendar limits are added where utilisation is unpredictable, which matters for GA and seasonal use.
3. **Annual AMP review is the norm (App I 5.2; App III C.1).** ACAM inspectors check that the AMP "is reviewed annually".
4. **Reliability programme requirement is independent of fleet size, but proportionate.** Under 6 aircraft of a type counts as a small fleet. Pool data where possible, but only with competent authority approval before signing. Without pooling, expect the competent authority to restrict interval changes.
5. **Pre-approval implementation.** The competent authority may let the CAMO implement reliability-driven AMP changes before formal approval if the reliability group gives robust internal validation (6.5.11).
6. **CAMO accountability is non-delegable.** In App II and XI, subcontractors and maintenance organisations may draft, analyse, plan and propose, but the CAMO decides and accepts:
   - corrective actions;
   - variations;
   - AD means of compliance;
   - deferrals.

   A rejected corrective action must be justified in writing. The CAMO owns the records wherever they are stored, and online access is acceptable.
7. **Deferral control.**
   - The MEL is not a CAMO document. Crew accept MEL deferrals in the tech log.
   - Defects outside the MEL/CDL (cracks, structural) need a CAMO deferred-defect procedure with manufacturer liaison.
   - Maintenance organisation deferrals of scheduled tasks need formal CAMO agreement.
   - MEL time-limit overruns only if the competent authority allows them.
8. **AD closed loop.** Every new or revised AD, for every aircraft, must end in one of three states. Inspectors specifically look for ADs wrongly marked non-applicable and sample physical compliance.
9. **ACAM KRE checklist (App III) is the authority's survey playbook.** Building the app's aircraft status views around KREs A.1 to C.4 means owners and CAMOs see what inspectors see. Notable items:
   - LOPA check;
   - mass and balance vs the previous report;
   - undocumented part swaps during troubleshooting (compare physical vs recorded SNs);
   - maintenance after abnormal events (hard landing, overspeed, overweight, turbulence, FM exceedances);
   - pilot-owner task identification.
10. **LLP status must include installation history** where remaining life depends on it (for example different engine or aircraft types with different cycle counting). The status is maintained for the life of the part and can be requested at each transfer.
11. **Repair status must include unrepaired damage**, not just repairs, with FH/FC at the time of finding.
12. **Small organisations may run organisational reviews instead of a quality system.** This applies to Subpart F organisations with fewer than 10 maintenance staff and to small CAMOs per M.A.712(f). Every checklist item is checked at least every 12 months. Level 1 findings are notified immediately. The CAMO checklist explicitly checks that aircraft with expired items are grounded, and that owners do not fly with overdue maintenance or have maintenance done without telling the CAMO. These are strong product features.
13. **Change notification is blocking.** Name, location, group of persons and approval-affecting procedures are not incorporated until competent authority approval (CAME 0.5).
14. **Contract review before a contract takes effect,** plus defined meetings (contract review, work scope, technical, quality, reliability), with authority participation in reliability meetings provided for.
15. **FTS training numbers are fixed:**
    - Phase 1 within 6 months of joining;
    - Phase 2 within 12 months, 75% multiple-choice exam, 8 hours acceptable;
    - continuation every 2 years;
    - not self-study for Phase 2.

    It applies only to CAMOs managing the large aeroplanes described in App XII A.
16. **Inconsistencies in the source text** (worth noting when building templates):
    - App V 1.2 refers to "Appendix I to AMC M.A.302(a) and M.B.301(d)", but App I's title cites AMC M.A.302 and AMC M.B.301(b).
    - App V's TOC lists 3.1 as "Maintenance contractor selection procedure", but the body titles 3.1 "Procedures for contracted maintenance" with a) contract development and b) selection. Form 13 follows the body.
    - App XI 2.5 cites "2.22 'Meetings'" and 2.8 cites "2.23"; Meetings is actually 2.23.
    - CAME 1.13 is "Maintenance check flight (MCF) procedures"; Form 13 says "Check flight procedures".
    - App IV MOM Part E lists maintenance locations before Part-145 organisations; Form 6F 5.2 to 5.4 reorders them and adds subcontractors and Part-CAO.
    - App III repeats KRE A.1 to C.4 in a short table and then in full; the short version of B.6 says "may include" where the full version says "includes".
    - App V's manpower example is dated "As of 28 November 2003".
17. **UK flags (guesses, not facts):**
    - UK forms are CAA Form 1/2/3/4/13/14.
    - The UK CAA may have its own CAME/MOM templates and its own AMC numbering. UK CAA guidance such as CAP documents may supplement or replace these appendices.
    - Part 21 Light and declared-design language (from ED Decision 2023/013/R) is likely absent in the UK.
    - The UK permit to fly process differs (no EASA Form 20b; UK permits and the ANO), so CAME Part 4B may differ.
    - References to "the Agency" issuing ADs become the CAA. The UK has its own AD adoption process, including how State of Design ADs apply.
    - Part-26 point 26.370 was added by a 2020 amendment; whether and when it applies in the UK may differ.
    - The FTS training dates (31 Dec 2010) are historical and the same.
    - With Part-CAMO in both regimes, the Subpart G-based appendices (V, VII, XIII) may be legacy or re-based to Part-CAMO AMC in UK guidance.
